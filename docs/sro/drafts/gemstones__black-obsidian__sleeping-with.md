# Sleeping with Black Obsidian: What to Know

Placing black obsidian under a pillow is physically risky: the stone is brittle, fractures conchoidally, and produces razor-sharp edges when chipped. A tumbled or smooth piece on a bedside table is safer and is the placement most commonly suggested in crystal practice. In various traditions, black obsidian is used near the bed for grounding and protection during sleep.

## Physical Considerations

Black obsidian is a volcanic glass with a Mohs hardness of 5 to 5.5, no cleavage, and a conchoidal fracture — meaning it breaks along smooth curved surfaces that leave very sharp edges. These are the same properties that made obsidian the preferred material for ancient blades.

Under a pillow, a piece of obsidian may:

- Chip or break under the weight and movement of a sleeping person, creating sharp glass-like shards in the bedding.
- Scratch or cut skin if a chipped edge contacts the face or neck during sleep.
- Scratch or dull its own polished surface against harder bedding materials.

A small, well-tumbled sphere or oval stone with no chips or sharp edges presents a lower physical risk under a pillow than raw or irregularly shaped pieces, but the risk is not zero. If a piece is already chipped, do not place it under a pillow.

The safer placement is on a bedside table, windowsill, or on a dry shelf near the bed. This eliminates the risk of breakage while keeping the stone within the sleeping space, which satisfies most crystal-practice intentions.

## Traditional Uses During Sleep

Crystals are a complementary practice, not a treatment for sleep disorders or any medical condition. The following reflects tradition within crystal communities:

Black obsidian is traditionally associated with protection, grounding, and the clearing of negative energy. In crystal practice, placing it near the bed is often described as creating a protective boundary during sleep and facilitating grounding through the night. Some practitioners also associate it with vivid or revealing dreams, connecting to its historical use in scrying.

Because the stone is traditionally associated with shadow work — examining difficult patterns or unconscious material — some crystal practitioners suggest it may surface unsettling dream content for some individuals. This is a matter of personal belief and individual sensitivity, not a documented effect. People who find their sleep disturbed after introducing any new element to their sleep environment, including crystals, can simply move the stone to another room.
