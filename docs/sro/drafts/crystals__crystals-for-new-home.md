# Crystals for a New Home: Which Stones Practitioners Use

The stones most commonly used when moving into a new home are black tourmaline, selenite, clear quartz, rose quartz, smoky quartz, jade, citrine, and amethyst. The tradition has two distinct stages: first clearing the space of any energy left by previous occupants, then setting an intention for what the home should feel like. Different stones suit each stage, and some suit specific rooms. Crystals are a complementary practice; they do not affect the physical environment.

## What Practitioners Look For in a New-Home Crystal

Evaluation in crystal tradition centers on the purpose (clearing versus setting intention) and the placement (fixed in a room versus moved in ritual use).

**Clearing vs. setting intention:** Most practitioners begin with a clearing stone — something that absorbs, neutralizes, or dispels whatever energy lingers from previous occupants. Smoky quartz, black tourmaline, and selenite are the most commonly used for this purpose. Once the clearing is done, stones associated with the intended atmosphere of the home are placed: rose quartz for warmth and relationships, citrine for energy and abundance, jade for stability.

**Fixed placement vs. active ritual use:** Some stones are placed and left — corner placement of black tourmaline, selenite plates in rooms. Others are moved through the space intentionally during an entry ritual before being placed. Clear quartz points and selenite wands are most commonly used in the active-ritual role because of their traditional association with directing and clearing energy.

**Physical safety in a home:** If children or pets are present, stones with sharp edges (raw crystals, obsidian), small tumbled pieces that could be swallowed, or fragile specimens that could shatter need to be placed out of reach. Soft stones like selenite can be damaged by curious hands.

## Black Tourmaline

[Black tourmaline](/gemstones/black-tourmaline) (schorl) is a complex boron silicate, Mohs 7–7.5, with no significant cleavage. It is physically robust and suitable for floor-level placement, which is where practitioners typically use it in a home.

In tradition, black tourmaline is the primary protective boundary stone for a home. Placing a piece at each corner of the home, or at each external entry point (front door, back door, garage entrance), is the most common new-home practice. Practitioners describe this as creating a protective grid around the space. It is also placed at windowsills facing the street. Raw pieces are common for this use — size matters less than consistent corner placement.

**Best for:** Entry points and corners; people whose primary concern with the new home is feeling secure; the first stone to place before any others.

## Selenite

[Selenite](/gemstones/selenite) is a variety of gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O), Mohs 2. That low hardness means a fingernail can scratch it — it is not suitable for placement where it will be handled frequently or knocked. **Do not clean selenite with water:** it dissolves slowly in contact with water and will lose its surface. Use dry methods — see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

In tradition, selenite is associated with clearing stagnant or heavy energy and with bringing a bright, still quality to a space. Large selenite plates placed in main living areas, or wands moved through rooms as part of a clearing ritual, are the most common new-home uses. Some practitioners place selenite above door frames for ongoing clearing. Its traditional reputation as a self-cleansing stone makes it popular for people who want a low-maintenance choice for fixed placement.

**Best for:** Space clearing ritual; living room and bedroom placement; people who want an ongoing clearing presence; must be kept dry; place where it will not be knocked.

## Clear Quartz

[Clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz) is SiO2 in colorless transparent form, Mohs 7. It is one of the most durable and widely available stones. Points and clusters are common forms for home use.

In tradition, clear quartz is associated with amplifying intention — placing it in a new home with a set intention (warmth, peace, creativity) is said to reinforce that intention throughout the space. Practitioners also use clear quartz points to direct energy in a room, pointing them toward a window or door to move stagnant energy out, or arranging them in simple grids. Single large clear quartz clusters are placed in a central room as a general energy brightener.

**Best for:** Setting and amplifying the overall intention for the home; central room placement; practitioners who prefer to work with directed intention; pairs well with any other stone.

## Rose Quartz

[Rose quartz](/gemstones/rose-quartz) (SiO2, Mohs 7) is durable for everyday placement and handling. Its pink color comes from microscopic inclusions or lattice-level defects. Some specimens fade with extended direct sunlight.

In tradition, rose quartz is associated with creating a warm, nurturing, emotionally safe home atmosphere. It is the most recommended stone for bedrooms and for shared living spaces where relationships matter. Practitioners place it in the bedroom to foster emotional openness between partners, and in family gathering spaces to encourage harmony. A larger rose quartz sphere or tumbled piece on a mantelpiece or bookshelf is the typical placement.

**Best for:** Bedroom and living room; new homes shared with a partner or family; people who want the home to feel emotionally welcoming; anyone for whom the new home represents a fresh relational start.

## Smoky Quartz

[Smoky quartz](/gemstones/smoky-quartz) is SiO2 colored gray to brown by irradiation, Mohs 7. Durable for any placement. Some commercial material is artificially irradiated — this does not affect physical properties.

In tradition, smoky quartz is associated with clearing and transmuting negative energy. In a new-home context, practitioners use it specifically in the initial clearing stage — moving a smoky quartz point through each room before unpacking is one method, then placing pieces in corners that feel heavy or unpleasant to address lingering energy. It is also placed long-term in rooms where arguments tend to happen or where stress accumulates.

**Best for:** Initial clearing of a new space alongside selenite; rooms with persistent heavy atmosphere; people who find pure black stones too intense; pairs well with black tourmaline in corner placement.

## Jade

[Jade](/gemstones/jade) refers to two distinct minerals: jadeite (NaAlSi2O6, Mohs 6.5–7) and nephrite (a calcium magnesium iron silicate, Mohs 6–6.5). Both are tough minerals with interlocking crystal structures that make them resistant to breaking. Most jade sold in Western markets is nephrite unless explicitly labeled as jadeite. Dyed or simulated jade (serpentine, aventurine sold as "new jade") is common — green aventurine and serpentine are legitimate stones in their own right but are not jade.

In tradition, jade is associated with good fortune, prosperity, stability, and harmonious family life. It has a particularly strong tradition in East Asian cultures as a household protective and prosperity stone. Practitioners place jade near the entrance of a home to invite prosperity, or in the living room as a stability stone. It is one of the most culturally specific stones in this category and is especially meaningful to practitioners with connections to Chinese, Japanese, or Korean traditions.

**Best for:** Prosperity and stability intention; entrance or living room placement; practitioners who value its cultural depth; those who want a traditional good-luck home stone.

## Citrine

[Citrine](/gemstones/citrine) is SiO2 colored yellow to orange by iron impurities or irradiation, Mohs 7. Most commercial citrine is heat-treated amethyst or smoky quartz that has been turned yellow — this is standard in the trade. Natural citrine (paler yellow) commands a higher price. Color can fade in extended direct sunlight.

In tradition, citrine is associated with energy, optimism, and abundance — practitioners call it a stone that does not accumulate negative energy and does not need regular cleansing in the same way other stones do (though cleansing is still generally recommended). It is placed in areas where activity and vitality are wanted: kitchens, home offices, and social spaces. A citrine cluster or tumbled piece near the stove or in the kitchen is a common recommendation in feng shui-adjacent crystal practice.

**Best for:** Kitchen, home office, and social spaces; people who want to bring an uplifting quality to the home; those starting a new chapter and want energy and optimism associated with the space.

## Amethyst

[Amethyst](/gemstones/amethyst) is purple quartz (SiO2), Mohs 7, colored by Fe3+ ions and natural irradiation. Color fades with prolonged direct sunlight — avoid sun-facing windowsills.

In tradition, amethyst is associated with calm, spiritual protection, and clear thinking. In a new home context, practitioners place it in bedrooms and meditation or reading spaces where a quiet, reflective atmosphere is wanted. Large amethyst geodes are traditional statement pieces that serve both as decorative objects and as room stones in this tradition.

**Best for:** Bedrooms and quiet rooms; people who want calm over vitality; those who already own amethyst as a starting stone; significant statement pieces as geodes.

## Room-by-Room Placement Guide

| Room | Suggested stones | Purpose in tradition |
|---|---|---|
| Front entrance | Black tourmaline, jade | Protective boundary; prosperity welcome |
| Living room | Clear quartz, rose quartz, citrine | Amplify intention; warmth; energy |
| Bedroom | Rose quartz, amethyst | Emotional warmth; calm |
| Kitchen | Citrine | Energy; abundance tradition |
| Home office | Clear quartz, citrine, amethyst | Clarity; energy; focus |
| Any corner or perimeter | Black tourmaline, smoky quartz | Protective grid; clearing |
| Window or shelf | Selenite, clear quartz cluster | Ongoing clearing; light |

## Practical Notes for Setting Up

The traditional sequence is: clear the space first, then place intention stones. Begin with selenite or smoky quartz moved through each room, then set your corner stones (black tourmaline), then place room-specific stones as you unpack.

For homes with children or pets, avoid small tumbled stones at floor level — larger pieces on shelves or mantlepieces are safer. Raw sharp specimens should always be placed out of reach.

Most stones here are water-safe for cleansing: black tourmaline, clear quartz, rose quartz, smoky quartz, jade, citrine, and amethyst can all be briefly rinsed. Selenite must not be wet. See [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals) for water-free alternatives.
