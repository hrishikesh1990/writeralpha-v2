# How to Identify Real Botswana Agate

Genuine Botswana Agate has tight, fine parallel banding in a muted gray-pink-peach-white palette. The main impostors are dyed agates from other origins, glass, and generic banded stone sold as Botswana material. None requires laboratory equipment to detect — visual inspection, a hardness test, and knowing what the genuine palette looks like will catch most fakes.

## What Genuine Botswana Agate Looks Like

Botswana Agate is characterized by:

- **Fine, tight parallel banding.** The bands are typically narrow and closely spaced, with a precise, layered look — more refined than the broad, irregular banding of Brazilian agates.
- **Muted color palette.** Authentic material runs through grays, soft pinks, peach, apricot, and white. Occasional purplish-red banding exists in some specimens. The tones are quiet and organic — not candy-bright, not uniformly saturated.
- **Warm undertone.** Even gray-dominant pieces tend to have a warm gray-pink cast rather than a cool or steely gray.
- **Opacity or translucency.** Botswana Agate ranges from opaque to semi-translucent. Holding a thin slab to light may reveal internal banding glowing through; thicker pieces are typically opaque.
- **Conchoidal fracture.** If a chip is visible on an unpolished edge, it breaks with a smooth, shell-like curve — characteristic of all quartz family minerals.

## Step-by-Step Identification

### 1. Examine the banding

Look at the pattern closely:

- Natural banding is organic — bands vary slightly in width and are not perfectly uniform. Micro-variations in band width, color shade, and spacing are normal and a mark of authenticity.
- Dyed material or glass will often show uniform, flat color zones without organic variation.
- Generic "gray agate" from Brazil may have broader, less tightly spaced bands and lack the warm pink undertone.

### 2. Check the color palette

Hold the stone in natural light:

- Genuine Botswana Agate reads quiet and nuanced. The pink is soft, almost dusty. The gray is warm, not cool or blue-toned.
- **Red flag:** vivid, saturated, candy-bright banding in pink, blue, purple, or neon tones is a strong indicator of dye. Botswana Agate is not naturally bright.
- **Red flag:** uniform single color throughout with no visible banding is not Botswana Agate — it may be dyed agate or glass.

### 3. Look for dye at cracks and surfaces

Dyed agate concentrates color at surface pores and any micro-fractures. On a dyed piece:

- Color pools along visible cracks or surface irregularities rather than being evenly distributed through the mineral.
- The color bleeds unnaturally at edges.
- Color concentration looks deeper at a scratched or chipped spot than on the polished surface.

Natural Botswana Agate's color is integral to the silica mineral itself — a scratch or chip reveals the same tones as the surface.

### 4. Test hardness

Botswana Agate has a Mohs hardness of 6.5–7. It will:

- Scratch a piece of glass (Mohs ~5.5) without difficulty.
- Not be scratched by a copper coin (Mohs ~3.5) or a steel knife blade (Mohs ~5.5).
- Be scratched by a topaz or sapphire (Mohs 8–9) or a diamond (Mohs 10).

To test: press the point of a steel file or the back of a steel blade against an inconspicuous edge and draw it across. Genuine agate will resist. Glass (Mohs 5.5) will be scratched by agate, but glass will not scratch agate. Note: glass can appear to leave a gray mark on agate — rub the mark off first to confirm whether the glass surface or the agate surface was damaged.

### 5. Temperature test

Natural stone feels distinctly cool to the touch initially, even in a warm room, and warms slowly in the hand. Glass mimics this somewhat but tends to hold warmth differently once handled. This is a suggestive test, not a definitive one.

### 6. Weight

Botswana Agate has a specific gravity of approximately 2.6, similar to quartz. A genuine piece feels heavier than an identically sized piece of glass (specific gravity ~2.5) or a plastic imitation (SG ~1.2–1.4). The difference is subtle between stone and glass but noticeable against plastic.

## Common Imitations

| Imitation | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| Dyed agate (other origin) | Over-saturated color; dye pooling at cracks; missing warm undertone |
| Glass | May have bubbles visible under magnification; uniform color; scratched by a steel file; no banding |
| Plastic | Much lighter weight; warmer to touch; often shows mold lines |
| Brazilian gray agate | Broader, less regular banding; cooler tone; genuine stone but not Botswana-origin material |
| Sardonyx | Reddish-brown and white banding with sharper contrast; no pink undertone |

## Buying Advice

Reputable dealers disclose origin and treatment status. Ask for both. Botswana Agate is one of the varieties most often sold untreated — the natural palette is the point.
