# How to Cleanse and Recharge Tree Agate

Tree Agate is a quartz-based chalcedony with a Mohs hardness of 7 and no solubility risk, so most cleansing methods are physically safe. A brief rinse under cool running water, smoke cleansing, sound, moonlight, and dry earth burial all work without damaging a natural, untreated piece. The one caveat: if your stone is dyed, skip prolonged water soaking and direct sunlight, as both can lift or uneven the color treatment.

## Before You Begin: Know Your Stone

A natural, untreated Tree Agate is SiO₂ — silicon dioxide — with green mineral inclusions. Water will not corrode it, it has no hygroscopic surfaces to trap moisture, and common cleansing agents pose no chemical risk to the mineral itself.

Dyed Tree Agate (where the white body or inclusions have been enhanced with colorants) is more sensitive. Extended soaking, solvents, ultrasonic cleaning, and prolonged strong sunlight can all affect dyed surfaces. If you are unsure whether your piece is dyed, treat it as dyed for the purposes of water and light exposure.

For a dedicated explanation of water safety for this stone, see [can Tree Agate go in water](/gemstones/tree-agate/can-go-in-water). For general guidance across all stones, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Water Rinse

Hold Tree Agate under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 to 30 seconds, then dry it immediately with a soft cloth. This is the fastest physical cleaning method and removes surface oils and dust.

Do not soak the stone in standing water for extended periods — not because the mineral will corrode, but because prolonged soaking is unnecessary and can affect dyed pieces, metal settings, strung beads (the cord absorbs water), and any resin or adhesive used in assembled jewelry.

Salt water is not recommended for Tree Agate in settings. Salt is mildly abrasive and can dull a polished surface; as the water evaporates, salt crystals can lodge in micro-fractures. A plain water rinse achieves the same cleaning result without those risks.

## Smoke Cleansing

Pass Tree Agate through the smoke of burning herbs or incense for 30 to 60 seconds, moving the piece so all surfaces are exposed. This is widely used in crystal practice and has no physical effect on the stone. Ensure the room is ventilated. This method works equally well for natural and dyed pieces.

## Sound

Place Tree Agate next to a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to carry through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing requires no physical contact with any medium, so it is safe for all finishes and settings. It is particularly convenient for pieces that cannot easily be moved through smoke or moisture.

## Moonlight

Set Tree Agate on a dry surface outdoors or on a windowsill on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check conditions before placing it outside: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture. This method is traditionally used for a combined cleanse-and-recharge in one step.

## Dry Earth Burial

Bury Tree Agate in a small pot of dry indoor soil or sand for 24 hours. Place the stone, cover it loosely, and retrieve it the next day. Brush off soil with a dry cloth. Use indoor potting mix rather than outdoor garden soil, which may be damp. In crystal tradition this method is associated with grounding and with restoring an earth-connected stone to its elemental source.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In most crystal traditions, cleansing and recharging are treated as distinct steps. After cleansing Tree Agate, place it in morning sunlight for 15 to 30 minutes (brief exposure at moderate intensity poses no risk to an opaque mineral; avoid prolonged midday sun if the piece is dyed), near a cluster of clear quartz, or on a dry selenite slab.

For a full guide to recharging methods and timings, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals). The [Tree Agate hub page](/gemstones/tree-agate) covers all physical properties in one place.

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions offer varying guidance. A common recommendation is to cleanse Tree Agate when it arrives, after prolonged or intense use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a mandatory schedule — this is a matter of personal practice.
