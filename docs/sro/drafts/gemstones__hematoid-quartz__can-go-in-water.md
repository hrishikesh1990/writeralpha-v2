# Can Hematoid Quartz Go in Water?

Yes, Hematoid Quartz can go in water for a brief wash. The stone's host mineral is quartz (SiO2), which is Mohs hardness 7, chemically inert in water, and used routinely in wet environments. The qualification is the iron oxide (hematite, Fe2O3) inclusions: these are stable under short contact with plain water, but prolonged soaking or salt water can eventually cause surface rust staining.

## Why the Quartz Component Is Water-Safe

Quartz sits at Mohs 7 on the hardness scale, well above the threshold for water damage, which typically affects stones at Mohs 5 and below. The SiO2 crystal lattice does not react with water at ordinary temperatures and does not dissolve. Clear quartz, smoky quartz, amethyst, and citrine — all quartz varieties — are routinely washed without damage.

Hematoid Quartz inherits this stability. A rinse under running water, a brief soak for cleaning, or a quick wash with mild soap presents no physical risk to the quartz host material. For a broader guide to which stones are water-safe, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## The Iron Inclusion Qualification

The hematite inclusions in Hematoid Quartz are iron oxide (Fe2O3). Iron oxide is not water-soluble and does not react rapidly with plain water the way metallic iron does. However, prolonged or repeated exposure to water — especially standing water, salt water, or high humidity — can gradually cause the iron-bearing zones to develop surface rust staining. The reaction is slow and mainly aesthetic, but it can discolor the stone's red and orange zones over time.

The practical guidelines:

- **Brief rinsing and washing:** Safe. A wash lasting a minute or two with fresh water and mild soap, followed by thorough drying, poses no meaningful risk.
- **Prolonged soaking:** Not recommended. Avoid leaving Hematoid Quartz submerged for hours or overnight.
- **Salt water:** Not recommended. Salt is mildly abrasive, and salt residue sitting on iron-bearing zones can accelerate surface staining. For guidance on which stones are safe in salt, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).
- **Ocean or mineral-rich water:** Avoid. The combined abrasive and mineral load adds unnecessary risk.

## How to Clean Hematoid Quartz in Water

1. Rinse the stone briefly under cool or lukewarm running water.
2. If removing surface grime, apply a small amount of mild dish soap and work it gently with a soft cloth or soft-bristled brush.
3. Rinse completely to remove all soap residue.
4. Dry the stone immediately and thoroughly with a soft cloth, including any crevices on natural points or rough specimens.
5. Store in a dry location away from steam or condensation.

For more cleansing methods beyond water — including smoke, sound, and moonlight — see [how to cleanse Hematoid Quartz](/gemstones/hematoid-quartz/how-to-cleanse).

## Sun After Washing

After washing and drying Hematoid Quartz, brief exposure to sunlight for 15 to 30 minutes is safe and is used in crystal tradition as a recharging step. The stone's red-orange color comes from iron oxide inclusions, which are opaque and do not fade in sunlight the way dye or certain colored minerals (such as amethyst or rose quartz) might. For a guide to which stones are sun-safe and which are not, see [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals).

## Storage After Washing

Store Hematoid Quartz in a cool, dry location after washing. Avoid bathrooms, kitchen windowsills, or any area with regular steam or condensation. A dry cloth pouch or a display case with a small silica gel packet will help control ambient humidity around iron-bearing specimens.
