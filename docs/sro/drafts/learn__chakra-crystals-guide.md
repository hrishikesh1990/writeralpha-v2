# Chakra Crystals: A Guide to the Seven Chakras

In Hindu and yogic tradition, the chakras are seven energy centers located along the body's central axis, each associated with specific physical areas, psychological states, and elemental qualities. Crystal practitioners use stones whose traditional color or energetic association corresponds to a given chakra, placing them on or near the body during meditation or carrying them as personal anchors. The assignments below are tradition, not physical fact, and vary between lineages and practitioners.

For an explanation of what crystals, minerals, and gemstones are as physical objects, see [gemstone vs crystal vs mineral](/learn/gemstone-vs-crystal-vs-mineral). For guidance on identifying genuine chakra sets sold commercially, see [how to know if a chakra bracelet is real](/learn/how-to-know-if-a-chakra-bracelet-is-real).

## The Root Chakra (Muladhara)

The root chakra is traditionally located at the base of the spine and is associated with physical stability, grounding, safety, and connection to the earth. Its traditional color is red or deep earth tones.

Stones traditionally linked to Muladhara include [red jasper](/gemstones/red-jasper) (a microcrystalline quartz with iron-oxide red color, Mohs 7), [black tourmaline](/gemstones/black-tourmaline) (a complex boron silicate, Mohs 7–7.5, traditionally used for protective energy), garnet (iron-bearing silicate group, Mohs 6.5–7.5), obsidian (volcanic glass, Mohs 5–5.5), and smoky quartz. In practice, red jasper and black tourmaline are the most commonly used stones for root-chakra work.

Crystal practitioners traditionally hold or place these stones during grounding exercises, carry them when feeling anxious or disconnected, or keep them in a workspace as stabilizing anchors. The stone is said to reinforce connection to present physical reality.

## The Sacral Chakra (Svadhisthana)

The sacral chakra sits below the navel and is associated in tradition with creativity, sensuality, emotion, and relationships. Its traditional color is orange.

Stones associated with Svadhisthana include [carnelian](/gemstones/carnelian) (SiO2 chalcedony with iron-oxide color, Mohs 7), [tiger's eye](/gemstones/tiger-eye) (SiO2 pseudomorph after crocidolite, Mohs 7), orange calcite, and sunstone. Carnelian is the most widely used choice for sacral chakra practice. It is traditionally carried or placed on the lower abdomen during meditation.

## The Solar Plexus Chakra (Manipura)

The solar plexus chakra is located at the upper abdomen and is associated with personal power, confidence, willpower, and transformation. Its traditional color is yellow.

Stones traditionally linked to Manipura include [citrine](/gemstones/citrine) (yellow-to-orange quartz, Mohs 7; most commercial citrine is heat-treated amethyst), yellow jasper, [tiger's eye](/gemstones/tiger-eye), pyrite, and yellow calcite. Citrine is the most commonly recommended stone for this chakra in contemporary crystal practice. Practitioners traditionally place citrine at the solar plexus during meditation or carry it to support confidence and clarity.

## The Heart Chakra (Anahata)

The heart chakra is located at the center of the chest and is associated with love, compassion, emotional healing, and connection. Its traditional colors are green and pink.

Stones traditionally associated with Anahata include [rose quartz](/gemstones/rose-quartz) (a massive pink quartz colored by fibrous mineral inclusions, Mohs 7, traditionally called the stone of unconditional love), [aventurine](/gemstones/aventurine) (quartz with fuchsite inclusions giving a green sheen, Mohs 7), [rhodonite](/gemstones/rhodonite) (manganese silicate, pink with black veins, Mohs 5.5–6.5), jade, and emerald. Rose quartz and green aventurine are the most commonly used heart-chakra stones.

Crystal practitioners traditionally place these stones at the center of the chest during meditation, carry them when working on emotional healing, or place them in shared living spaces. Crystals are a complementary practice and are not a substitute for professional mental-health support.

## The Throat Chakra (Vishuddha)

The throat chakra is located at the throat and is associated with communication, expression, truth, and the ability to speak and listen clearly. Its traditional color is blue.

Stones associated with Vishuddha include [aquamarine](/gemstones/aquamarine) (blue-green beryl, Mohs 7.5–8), [sodalite](/gemstones/sodalite) (blue sodium aluminum silicate, Mohs 5.5–6), [lapis lazuli](/gemstones/lapis-lazuli) (a rock aggregate of lazurite, calcite, and pyrite, Mohs 5–6), blue lace agate, and turquoise. Aquamarine and lapis lazuli are the most frequently recommended choices for throat-chakra practice.

## The Third Eye Chakra (Ajna)

The third eye chakra sits between the eyebrows and is associated in tradition with intuition, insight, perception, and inner vision. Its traditional color is indigo or deep violet-blue.

Stones traditionally linked to Ajna include [amethyst](/gemstones/amethyst) (violet quartz colored by iron-based color centers, Mohs 7), [lapis lazuli](/gemstones/lapis-lazuli), [lepidolite](/gemstones/lepidolite) (lilac mica containing lithium and manganese, Mohs 2.5–3), kyanite, and labradorite. Amethyst is the most widely used third-eye stone in contemporary crystal practice. Practitioners traditionally place it on the forehead during meditation or use an amethyst cluster near a meditation space.

## The Crown Chakra (Sahasrara)

The crown chakra is located at the top of the head and is associated with spiritual connection, consciousness, wisdom, and the integration of all chakra energies. Its traditional colors are violet and white.

Stones associated with Sahasrara include [clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz) (transparent SiO2, Mohs 7, described in tradition as the universal amplifier), [moonstone](/gemstones/moonstone) (a feldspar with adularescence caused by alternating layers of orthoclase and albite, Mohs 6–6.5), selenite (gypsum, Mohs 2, sometimes used as a cleansing and amplifying stone), and amethyst. Clear quartz is the most commonly recommended crown-chakra stone.

## Using Chakra Stones in Practice

Crystal practitioners use chakra stones in several ways:

- **Lying meditation:** Place one stone for each chakra on the corresponding body area and lie still for 10–20 minutes. This is the most direct form of chakra crystal work.
- **Carry stones:** Select one or two stones for the chakra you are focusing on and carry them in a pocket or pouch throughout the day as a tactile reminder of your intention.
- **Chakra sets:** A standard seven-stone chakra set includes one stone per chakra, often sold as tumbled stones. See [how to know if a chakra bracelet is real](/learn/how-to-know-if-a-chakra-bracelet-is-real) for what to check when purchasing a set.
- **Display and environment:** Place chakra stones on a desk, altar, or nightstand as environmental anchors for the qualities associated with that chakra.

Chakra work is a practice drawn from Hindu and yogic traditions and adopted widely in contemporary crystal and wellness communities. The associations between stones and chakras are cultural and traditional. No controlled clinical evidence demonstrates that placing crystals on the body produces measurable physiological or psychological outcomes; these practices complement but do not replace conventional medical or therapeutic care.
