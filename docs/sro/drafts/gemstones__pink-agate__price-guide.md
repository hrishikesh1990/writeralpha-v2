# Pink Agate Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Natural Pink Agate tumbled stones (AA grade, 1–1.5 inches, Botswana origin) retail for $4–$6 per piece at specialist crystal retailers as of September 2026. Dyed pink agate — the most common material in the trade — retails for $1–$3 per piece for similar sizes. The gap between those ranges reflects the single biggest price driver: natural versus treated color.

## What Pink Agate Is

Pink Agate is a banded chalcedony — a microcrystalline quartz mineral (SiO2) with a Mohs hardness of 6.5–7. As a common mineral with no rarity premium attached to the raw material, its price is driven almost entirely by color quality, treatment status, origin, size, and polish. Unlike precious gemstones, pink agate is not priced per carat in the retail crystal market; it is sold by the piece (tumbles), by weight (bulk lots), or by item (jewelry). See [the Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale) for context on how hardness relates to durability and everyday value.

## What Drives Price

### Natural vs dyed color

This is the primary driver. Natural, untreated pink agate — where the pink hue comes from trace iron oxide deposited during formation — is less common than dyed material and commands a higher price. Much of the pink agate in the market is white or gray chalcedony that has been artificially colored, a standard trade practice when disclosed. Sellers of natural material typically state the treatment status explicitly. If a listing does not mention treatment status, treat it as unknown. See [how to identify Pink Agate](/gemstones/pink-agate/how-to-identify) for ways to check treatment yourself.

### Origin

Pink Botswana Agate — naturally banded material from the Bobonong district of Botswana — is considered the most desirable origin for natural pink agate and tends to command the highest prices. Brazilian and Indian material is more abundant; much of it is dyed. Origin labeling in the crystal trade is not always verified, so "Botswana agate" from an undisclosed supplier should be evaluated on treatment status and banding quality rather than the label alone.

### Size and banding quality

Larger pieces with well-defined, contrasting banding cost more than small or uniformly colored material. In tumbled stones, pieces in the 1.5–2-inch range tend to cost 50–100% more than small pieces from the same batch.

### Polish quality

Crystal retail grades (often labeled A, AA, AAA) reflect polish quality and visual appeal rather than any standardized gemological criterion. Higher grades cost more but the grading is seller-specific.

## Where Overpaying Happens

**Paying natural-stone prices for dyed material.** If a retailer is selling pink agate at $4–$6 per piece but does not disclose treatment status, the material may be dyed Brazilian agate available elsewhere for $1–$2. Ask directly: "Is this natural or dyed?"

**Inflated metaphysical branding.** Stones marketed with specific claims about their energetic properties sometimes carry price premiums that reflect the brand rather than the material. The stone itself is the same regardless of how its tradition is described.

**Marketplace platforms with unverified origin claims.** "Botswana pink agate" listed on general marketplace platforms at $0.50–$1 per piece is almost certainly dyed material from another source. Genuine Botswana material at that price point is not consistent with current wholesale reality.

## What Fair Value Looks Like

A fair purchase for a single natural pink agate tumble is $4–$6 from a retailer who states origin (Botswana) and treatment status (natural/untreated). A fair purchase for dyed material is $1–$3 per piece, with treatment disclosed. Bulk purchases for display or craft use can bring per-piece cost down significantly even at retail.
