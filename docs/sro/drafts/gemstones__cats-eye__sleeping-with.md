# Sleeping with Cat's Eye: What to Know

Keeping Cat's Eye chrysoberyl by the bed or nearby during sleep is physically safe. The stone has a Mohs hardness of 8.5, contains no soluble or toxic elements, and poses no chemical hazard. The traditional practice of sleeping with it comes primarily from Vedic astrology, where it is linked to Ketu and is used for protection and clarity during rest — but individual suitability in that system is not universal.

## Is It Physically Safe?

Cat's Eye chrysoberyl (cymophane, BeAl2O4) is non-toxic. It contains no lead, arsenic, or other elements of concern at surface contact levels. Its hardness of 8.5 means the polished surface is durable and does not crumble or shed dust under normal handling. Beryllium is present in the chemistry, but the mineral form is stable; beryllium compounds are hazardous when ground into dust (as in industrial settings), not in handling a polished cabochon. Wash hands after handling any stone as a routine precaution.

No scientific evidence links gemstone proximity to changes in sleep quality, dream content, or health outcomes. Crystal and gemstone sleep practices are a complementary tradition, not a treatment for sleep disorders or any health condition.

## The Traditional Practice

In Vedic astrology and gemstone therapy traditions, Cat's Eye is the prescribed stone for Ketu, the South Node of the Moon. Ketu is associated with past experience, spiritual insight, detachment, and protective influence. Practitioners in this tradition sometimes keep a cat's eye near the bed or under the pillow to work with Ketu's protective quality during sleep, particularly during periods they consider Ketu-dominant in their chart.

In contemporary crystal practice, Cat's Eye is associated with mental sharpness and protective energy. Some practitioners place it on a bedside table with the intention of maintaining clear perception and guarding against disturbing dreams. This is a matter of personal practice with no physical basis.

Crystals are a complementary practice. They are not a substitute for medical care or treatment for sleep disorders.

## Practical Placement Advice

**On the bedside table.** The most practical option. The stone stays visible, accessible for a bedtime intention-setting moment, and out of the way during sleep. A small dish or cloth pouch keeps it dust-free and prevents it from rolling off the table.

**Under the pillow.** Possible, but not recommended for a faceted or large cabochon. A stone under the pillow can shift during the night and become uncomfortable, and a heavy stone can fall and be stepped on in the dark. If this is important to the practice, use a small tumbled or flat-bottom stone and place it inside the pillowcase against the outer edge.

**In the bedroom but not the bed.** Many practitioners place Cat's Eye on a small altar or shelf in the bedroom, which serves the same traditional purpose without the physical discomfort of under-pillow placement. This is the most common approach in Vedic gem therapy.
