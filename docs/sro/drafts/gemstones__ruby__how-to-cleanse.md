# How to Cleanse Ruby — Safe Methods and What to Avoid

Ruby is one of the safest gemstones to cleanse: untreated and heat-treated stones tolerate warm water, mild soap, ultrasonic cleaners, and steam. The important exception is fracture-filled ruby — stones enhanced with lead glass — which should only be wiped with a damp cloth and kept away from acids and ultrasonic equipment. Energetic or ritual cleansing methods (smoke, sound, moonlight) work for any ruby regardless of treatment.

## Know Your Ruby's Treatment Before Cleansing

The single most important factor in choosing a cleansing method is whether your ruby has been fracture-filled. Approximately 95% of commercial rubies are heat-treated, and a significant portion of lower-priced material is further treated with lead-glass fracture-filling to improve apparent clarity. GIA categorizes ruby treatments as follows:

| Treatment type | Water/soap | Ultrasonic | Steam | Acids |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Untreated | Safe | Safe | Safe | Safe (except boric acid) |
| Heat-treated only | Safe | Usually safe | Usually safe | Safe (except boric acid) |
| Fracture-filled (lead glass) | Damp cloth only | Not safe | Not safe | Not safe |
| Cavity-filled or dyed | Damp cloth only | Not safe | Not safe | Not safe |

If you are unsure, treat the piece as fracture-filled and use only dry or gentle methods. A reputable laboratory certificate (GIA, GRS, Gübelin) will specify treatment status. See [can ruby go in water](/gemstones/ruby/can-go-in-water) for a detailed explanation of water safety by treatment type.

## Physical Cleaning Methods

### Warm water and mild soap (untreated and heat-treated stones)

Place the ruby in a bowl of warm (not hot) water with a small drop of mild dish soap. Let it soak for two to three minutes, then gently scrub all surfaces with a soft-bristled brush — a clean toothbrush works well for set jewelry. Rinse under clean running water and pat dry with a lint-free cloth. This is the most thorough method for removing body oils, lotions, and surface grime from untreated or heat-treated ruby.

Avoid boric acid powder and household cleaners with acidic or abrasive ingredients, which can etch corundum surfaces.

### Damp cloth (fracture-filled and dyed stones)

For filled or dyed ruby, dampen a soft cloth with plain water or very dilute soapy water and wipe the surface gently. Do not soak, do not use ultrasonic equipment, and do not let the stone sit in liquid. Dry immediately.

### Ultrasonic and steam cleaners

These are generally safe for untreated and heat-treated ruby. They are not safe for fracture-filled, cavity-filled, or dyed material — the heat and vibration can damage the filler and worsen the stone's appearance.

## Energetic Cleansing Methods

Crystal traditions use several dry methods to "clear" accumulated energy from a stone. These are complementary practices, not replacements for physical cleaning where the stone needs it.

### Smoke cleansing

Pass ruby slowly through the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, ensuring all surfaces are exposed. This is one of the most widely used methods in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to ruby of any treatment type.

### Sound

Place ruby near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing is particularly useful for pieces that cannot easily be moved or touched.

### Moonlight

Set ruby on a dry surface outdoors or on a windowsill on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours. Check the forecast first: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity can dampen the stone. If your ruby is fracture-filled, keep it indoors near a window instead.

### Visualization and breath

Hold ruby in both hands, breathe slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while intending to clear accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This requires no materials and is used for a quick reset between sessions.

### Dry earth burial

Bury ruby in a pot of dry indoor sand or potting soil for 24 hours. Brush off with a dry cloth afterward. This is a grounding method used in crystal tradition; use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal practice, cleansing removes accumulated energy and recharging restores it. Ruby is traditionally associated with fire, vitality, and solar energy, so sunlight is frequently recommended for recharging. Brief exposure to morning sunlight — 15 to 30 minutes — poses no physical risk to an untreated or heat-treated ruby.

Other recharging options used in tradition include: placing ruby on a dry selenite slab, near a cluster of clear quartz, or setting an intention while holding the stone. For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions offer varying guidance. A common approach is to cleanse ruby when it arrives, after any period of intensive use or emotional significance, and approximately monthly during regular display or wear. How often you cleanse is a matter of personal practice; there is no physical requirement for a schedule.

Store ruby away from prolonged direct sunlight if the piece contains dyed or filled material, as some fillers can be affected by extended UV exposure. Untreated and heat-treated ruby is stable in sunlight.
