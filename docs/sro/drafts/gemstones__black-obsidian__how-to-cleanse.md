# How to Cleanse Black Obsidian

Black obsidian is an amorphous volcanic glass with no reactive chemistry, so a brief water rinse is safe — unlike pyrite or selenite, which water damages. Smoke, sound, moonlight, dry earth burial, and breath are all also safe and effective cleansing methods in crystal practice. Each method is explained below.

## Before You Start: What to Check

Black obsidian has a Mohs hardness of 5 to 5.5 and fractures conchoidally, producing sharp edges on chipped pieces. Before cleansing, examine your piece for chips or cracks: a damaged specimen should be handled with extra care during any physical method, and loose sharp fragments are an injury risk.

No method below poses a chemical risk to the stone. The practical risks are mechanical — chipping from impact, or dulling of polish from prolonged soaking.

## Water Rinse

Hold the black obsidian under cool, gently running water for 30 to 60 seconds, moving it so all surfaces are exposed. Do not use hot water (thermal shock can chip the stone). Pat dry immediately with a soft cloth and allow to air dry fully before storage.

A water rinse is safe for polished tumbled stones and smooth spheres. It is less suited to raw or fractured pieces with very sharp edges — handle those in a cloth or with gloves, and check afterward that no chips have formed. For a detailed breakdown of water safety for this stone, see [can Black Obsidian go in water](/gemstones/black-obsidian/can-go-in-water).

Do not soak black obsidian for extended periods (30 minutes or more), as prolonged immersion can dull a polished surface over time.

## Smoke Cleansing

Hold the black obsidian in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the piece slowly through the smoke so all surfaces pass through it. Ensure the room is ventilated. This method carries no physical risk to the stone and is one of the most widely used methods in crystal practice for obsidian specifically.

## Sound

Place black obsidian beside a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone does not need to be touched or moved. This is particularly useful for large or heavy obsidian pieces — raw chunks, substantial spheres — that are awkward to hold during smoke cleansing.

## Moonlight

Set black obsidian on a dry windowsill or outdoors on a dry surface on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the weather: dew, rain, or heavy overnight humidity should prompt you to keep the piece indoors. Bring it inside before morning condensation forms. In crystal tradition, the full moon is commonly used for a combined cleanse and recharge.

## Dry Earth Burial

Bury black obsidian in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or dry sand for 24 hours. Cover the piece fully, then retrieve and brush off loose soil with a dry soft-bristled brush or cloth. Use indoor soil rather than outdoor garden soil, which may be damp. Inspect the stone afterward for any scratches from grit in the soil; fine sand is gentler than coarse potting soil.

## Breath and Visualization

Hold the black obsidian in both hands, take a slow breath in, and exhale steadily across its surface while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and leaves no physical residue. It is used as a quick reset between sessions or when other methods are not available.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps. After cleansing, common recharging methods for black obsidian include brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes; the opaque glass is not harmed by sun), placement near a clear quartz cluster, or resting on a dry selenite slab. For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary widely. Common practice is to cleanse black obsidian when it arrives, after any intensive use, and roughly monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a personal practice decision. The stone does not degrade between cleansing sessions.
