# Can Sapphire Go in Water? Cleaning and Care Explained

Untreated and heat-treated sapphire can go in water without damage. At Mohs 9 with no cleavage and high chemical stability, corundum is among the most durable gemstone materials, and the GIA recommends warm soapy water as the standard cleaning method. The exception is fracture-filled or dyed sapphire: the glass filler used in fracture-filled stones can be attacked by even mild acids, so those stones should only be cleaned with a damp cloth.

## Why Sapphire Is Water-Safe

Sapphire is a variety of corundum (Al₂O₃), a mineral that does not react with water under normal conditions. It does not dissolve, corrode, or release harmful compounds on contact with water. Its hardness of 9 on the Mohs scale means the surface will not be scratched by water particles or normal water pressure. The GIA states that corundum is stable under normal wearing conditions and resistant to heat, light, and common chemicals.

This makes sapphire straightforwardly compatible with water-based cleaning and, incidentally, with incidental exposure during swimming, showering, or washing up — though prolonged soaking in heavily chlorinated water is worth avoiding because chlorine can degrade metal settings over time, even if the stone itself is unaffected.

Salt water is safe for the stone but can corrode or tarnish metal settings. If you clean sapphire in salt water or a saline solution, rinse the setting in plain water afterward. For guidance on which stones are safe in salt-water contexts, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## When Water Is Not Safe: Treated Sapphires

The water-safety picture changes for sapphires that have undergone certain treatments:

**Fracture-filled sapphires** have surface-reaching cracks filled with a high-lead-content glass to improve apparent clarity. The GIA notes that this glass can be damaged by even mild acids, including lemon juice and vinegar. Prolonged soaking or contact with acidic water would degrade the filler, clouding the stone's appearance. Clean fracture-filled sapphires only with a soft damp cloth — no soaking, no soap, and no ultrasonic cleaners.

**Dyed sapphires** are corundum that has had color introduced through a dye rather than heat or diffusion. Dye may bleed or fade with prolonged water exposure. Wipe with a damp cloth only.

**Cavity-filled sapphires** follow the same caution as fracture-filled: the filler material is susceptible to chemical damage.

If you have no documentation for your sapphire and bought it at a low price relative to its apparent quality, assume a treated stone and clean conservatively.

## How to Clean Sapphire in Water

For untreated and heat-treated sapphire:

1. Fill a small bowl with lukewarm water and a few drops of mild dish soap.
2. Place the sapphire in the water for a few minutes to loosen grime.
3. Brush gently with a soft toothbrush, paying attention to the area behind the setting.
4. Rinse under running lukewarm water.
5. Pat dry with a lint-free cloth and allow to air-dry fully before returning to storage.

Ultrasonic cleaners are also safe for untreated and heat-treated sapphire. Steam cleaners are generally safe by the same logic. Both methods should be avoided for fracture-filled, dyed, or cavity-filled material.

## Alternatives When Water Should Be Avoided

If your sapphire has been treated and water is not appropriate:

- **Dry soft cloth:** A microfiber cloth removes surface dust and oils without any moisture.
- **Smoke cleansing:** Passing the stone through herb or incense smoke is the most widely used ritual cleansing method for water-sensitive stones.
- **Sound:** A singing bowl or tuning fork carries vibration through the stone at a distance, requiring no contact with any liquid.

## Sun Charging After Water Cleaning

Sapphire is not prone to color fading in sunlight — it lacks the photosensitive chromophores found in amethyst or rose quartz. Brief exposure to morning sun after cleaning is physically harmless and is used in crystal tradition for recharging.
