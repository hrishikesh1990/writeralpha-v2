# Can Red Tiger's Eye Go in Water?

Red Tiger's Eye can go in water briefly without damage. As a quartz variety (Mohs 7), it is stable in water at room temperature and will not dissolve or become toxic from a short rinse. Prolonged soaking and salt water are not recommended: soaking can loosen jewelry adhesives and dull polish, while salt leaves abrasive deposits. A 30-to-60-second rinse is safe and sufficient.

## Why Red Tiger's Eye Is Generally Water-Safe

Tiger's Eye, including the red variety, is a fibrous quartz aggregate. Quartz (silicon dioxide, SiO2) has a hardness of 7 on the Mohs scale and is chemically inert in water at ambient conditions. It does not react with plain water, does not dissolve, and does not release harmful substances during normal handling in liquid.

The red color in most commercially available Red Tiger's Eye comes from iron oxide (hematite) produced by heat treatment of yellow-brown tiger's eye. Iron oxide itself is stable in water at room temperature; it is the mineral responsible for the reddish color of many naturally occurring rocks and soils without being degraded by rain or water contact. Brief water exposure does not alter or strip the color.

For a site-wide comparison of which crystals are water-safe and which are not, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## What to Watch For

### Prolonged soaking

Leaving polished Red Tiger's Eye submerged for hours or days is not recommended for two reasons. First, the adhesive used in glued cabochon settings can soften and weaken over prolonged water exposure. Second, while quartz itself is stable, long soaking can gradually affect the polish and surface finish on tumbled stones, leaving them appearing slightly dull. There is no need to soak this stone; a rinse achieves the same physical cleaning result.

### Salt water

Salt does not chemically attack quartz, but salt water is more abrasive and corrosive to metal settings and stringing material (elastic cord, wire) than plain water. Salt residue left in surface features or the fibers of the stone after drying can also attract humidity over time. For cleaning purposes, plain cool water is preferable. If you want to use a salt-based cleanse for ritual purposes, dry salt in a bowl placed near (not touching) the stone achieves the traditional purpose without physical contact. For more on salt-safe crystal guidance, see [salt safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

### Jewelry settings and stringing

Immersing a Red Tiger's Eye bracelet or pendant repeatedly in water accelerates wear on the elastic cord, wire, or metal clasps of the setting more than it risks the stone itself. Remove jewelry before swimming, bathing, or washing hands if prolonged or daily water exposure is likely.

## How to Clean Red Tiger's Eye with Water

1. Hold the stone under cool to lukewarm running water.
2. Gently rub the surface with your fingers or a very soft brush to dislodge dust or residue.
3. Rinse for 30 to 60 seconds total.
4. Pat dry immediately with a soft cloth and allow to air-dry fully before storing.

Do not use hot water, which may stress the stone structure in polished pieces, and do not use soap or chemical cleaners, which can leave a residue or affect the surface finish.

## Sun Drying After Water Cleansing

After rinsing, brief drying in natural light is fine. Avoid leaving Red Tiger's Eye in direct sunlight for extended periods as prolonged UV exposure may gradually fade the iron oxide color over time. A few minutes of sun-drying while you work is not a concern; hours of daily sun exposure is. For a full guide on sun-safe crystals, see [sun safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals).
