# How to Cleanse Blue Sandstone Without Damaging It

Blue Sandstone is a manufactured glass (aventurine glass) with a Mohs hardness of approximately 5.5. A brief rinse under cool running water is physically safe because the glass matrix does not dissolve and the copper particles inside are sealed. For energetic or ritual cleansing, smoke, sound, moonlight, and breath are all reliable options. Avoid prolonged soaking, salt baths, ultrasonic cleaners, and harsh chemical cleaning agents.

## What Makes Blue Sandstone Different to Cleanse

Blue Sandstone is not a natural mineral. It is a silica-based glass with metallic copper inclusions suspended inside a cobalt-tinted matrix — see the [Blue Sandstone hub page](/gemstones/blue-sandstone) for full physical properties. Because glass is chemically stable and non-porous, it does not absorb liquids the way porous stones such as selenite do, and it does not oxidize the way iron-bearing minerals such as pyrite do.

This stability means water is not the hazard it is for many other stones. However, glass at Mohs 5.5 is moderately soft, and abrasive cleaners or ultrasonic vibration can chip or scratch a polished surface. Salt — whether in solution or dry burial — can etch glass over time, particularly along micro-scratches, and is best avoided. For a focused look at water safety, see [can Blue Sandstone go in water](/gemstones/blue-sandstone/can-go-in-water).

## Physical Cleaning (Surface Dirt and Oils)

To remove everyday surface dirt or skin oils from Blue Sandstone jewelry or a tumbled stone:

1. Rinse briefly under cool or lukewarm running water — no more than 30 seconds.
2. If residue remains, use a soft cloth or cotton bud dampened with plain water.
3. Pat dry immediately with a lint-free cloth.
4. Allow to air-dry fully before storing.

Do not use:
- Salt water or ocean water (can etch the surface)
- Chemical jewelry cleaners or household cleaners (unnecessary and potentially abrasive)
- Ultrasonic cleaners (vibration can cause micro-fractures in glass)
- Steam cleaning (thermal shock can crack glass)

## Energetic Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold Blue Sandstone in the smoke of burning herbs, resin, or incense for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the piece slowly so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is the most widely used energetic cleansing method in crystal practice and poses no physical risk to the stone. Ventilate the room well; you will also breathe the smoke.

### Sound

Place Blue Sandstone near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to resonate for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing requires no contact with liquid or flame and is safe for all finished Blue Sandstone forms, including strung beads.

### Moonlight

Set Blue Sandstone on a dry windowsill or a dry outdoor surface on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours. Avoid leaving it outdoors where dew or rain could settle on it overnight. Bring the stone indoors before dawn to avoid condensation. This method also acts as a traditional recharge step.

### Visualization or breath

Hold the stone in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while mentally setting an intention to release any accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires nothing additional and is useful for a quick reset between uses.

## What to Avoid

| Method | Risk to Blue Sandstone |
|---|---|
| Salt bath or dry salt burial | Can etch the glass surface over time |
| Ultrasonic cleaner | Vibration can fracture glass |
| Harsh chemicals | May damage the polished surface |
| Extended soaking | Unnecessary; polished glass is stable but setting materials in jewelry may be affected |
| Steam | Thermal shock can crack glass |

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are separate steps. After cleansing, place Blue Sandstone in brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes — glass does not fade or degrade from short sun exposure), on a dry selenite slab, or near a clear quartz cluster. For a full guide to recharging options, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

For a broader overview of methods that apply across many stones, the [how to cleanse crystals guide](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals) covers each technique in detail.

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions offer varying guidance. A common approach is to cleanse Blue Sandstone on arrival, after extended wear or intensive use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical reason a polished glass stone requires any cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice, not stone maintenance. As a practical note, inspect the polished surface periodically for scratches, which appear when the stone contacts harder materials (anything above Mohs 5.5).
