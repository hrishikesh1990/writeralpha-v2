# How to Identify Real Citrine

Real citrine is a variety of quartz (SiO₂, Mohs 7). The main identification challenge is not spotting outright fakes — glass or plastic stand-ins — but distinguishing natural citrine from heat-treated amethyst or smoky quartz, which make up the majority of the commercial citrine market. Both are genuine quartz; neither is fraudulent if disclosed. The tests below cover all common scenarios, from casual visual checks to simple at-home tests.

## Understand What You Are Likely Buying

Before testing, it helps to know what is typically on the market:

- **Heat-treated amethyst or smoky quartz.** This accounts for most citrine sold at retail, particularly orange and reddish-orange pieces. Heating amethyst to roughly 470–560°C converts its purple color to yellow or orange. The result is genuine quartz with a different color from a different iron-oxidation state. It is not fraudulent unless sold as "natural citrine" without disclosure.
- **Natural citrine.** Rarer, typically paler — pale lemon yellow to golden yellow — and more evenly colored throughout the crystal. Main sources include Brazil (particularly the Chapada Diamantina region), Spain, Russia, and Madagascar.
- **Glass.** Manufactured glass is a genuine imitation risk, particularly for tumbled stones and beads at very low price points.
- **Dyed quartz or dyed glass.** Low-end products sometimes use dyed material to achieve the citrine color range.

For understanding price differences between these categories, see the [citrine price guide](/gemstones/citrine/price-guide). For the broader question of lab-created versus natural stones, see [lab-created vs natural gemstones](/compare/lab-created-vs-natural-gemstones).

## Visual Checks

### Color distribution

**Heat-treated amethyst in clusters:** Look at the base of a raw crystal cluster. Heat-treated amethyst clusters almost always have a white to milky base (the original host rock) with orange or burnt-orange color concentrated near the crystal tips. This white base is almost never present in natural citrine, which grew in a different geological environment.

**Heat-treated amethyst in single points:** Color is often uneven, more orange-red at the terminations and paler at the body, sometimes with a slightly "cooked" brownish tint at the edges.

**Natural citrine:** Color tends to be pale to golden yellow, evenly distributed, without the orange-red concentration at tips. Very deep reddish-orange even color in a single crystal is more characteristic of heat-treated material.

**Glass:** Perfectly uniform, bubble-free color throughout, with none of the slight internal variations or ghostly growth zones present in real quartz. Very uniform and "perfect" color in inexpensive pieces is a warning sign.

### Inclusions and internal features

Natural citrine and heat-treated amethyst are both real quartz and will often show faint growth zoning, wispy inclusions, or small natural features under magnification. Glass typically shows gas bubbles (round or elongated) under 10× magnification, and lacks the characteristic internal patterns of natural crystal growth.

## Simple At-Home Tests

### Temperature test

Real quartz feels noticeably cool to the touch compared with glass or plastic of the same size, because quartz conducts heat away from skin faster than glass does. This is a helpful but not conclusive indicator — some types of glass also feel relatively cool. Pick up the piece; if it warms up almost immediately in your hand, it is more likely glass or plastic.

### Hardness test

Citrine is Mohs 7. It will scratch glass (approximately Mohs 5.5) without difficulty, and a steel knife blade (approximately Mohs 5.5) will not scratch citrine. If a piece claiming to be citrine is easily scratched by a steel blade, it is not quartz. Perform any scratch test on an inconspicuous surface.

### Weight comparison

Citrine has a specific gravity of approximately 2.65. Glass can range from 2.2 to 4.8 depending on type, but typical glass used in imitation stones falls around 2.4–2.5. A piece of true quartz will feel slightly heavier for its size than most glass imitations of the same dimensions. This requires comparison and a feel for the difference; it is not a definitive test without accurate scales.

## Professional and Gemological Tests

If you have access to gemological equipment, the following are definitive:

| Test | Citrine result | Glass result |
|---|---|---|
| Refractive index (RI) | 1.544–1.553 | Varies; isotropic |
| Birefringence | 0.009 (weak double refraction) | None (isotropic) |
| Specific gravity | ~2.65 | ~2.2–4.8 (varies) |
| Polariscope | Anisotropic (shows ADR) | Isotropic (dark in all positions) |

The polariscope test is the most accessible: real quartz (anisotropic) shows a change as it is rotated under polarized light; glass (isotropic) stays dark. Many gem clubs and jewelry schools have polariscopes available.

## What "Real" vs "Natural" Means for Citrine

"Real" and "natural" mean different things in the gem trade:

- **"Real citrine"** means genuine quartz with a citrine-range color — natural or heat-treated both qualify. The stone is what it says it is, physically.
- **"Natural citrine"** means citrine whose color was not produced by heat treatment — it formed in nature with that color. Natural citrine commands higher prices; see the [price guide](/gemstones/citrine/price-guide).

When buying online, look for seller disclosure statements. Reputable sellers state whether the stone is natural or heated. For a general guide to evaluating online crystal sellers, see [are online crystals real](/learn/are-online-crystals-real).

## Red Flags When Buying

- Very orange or reddish-orange cluster with a white milky base sold as "natural citrine" without qualification — almost certainly heat-treated amethyst.
- Unusually low price for the claimed quality: heat-treated citrine retails for roughly $4–$12 per carat; natural untreated quality citrine starts around $40 per carat.
- No disclosure of treatment on a seller's listing.
- Perfect, flawless color with no internal variation in a tumbled stone under $5.
