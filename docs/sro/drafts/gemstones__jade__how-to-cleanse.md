# How to Cleanse Jade Without Damaging It

Jade — whether jadeite or nephrite — is generally safe to rinse under cool water for a short period, but prolonged soaking, salt, harsh chemicals, and ultrasonic cleaners can all cause damage, particularly on polymer-treated stones. Smoke, sound, moonlight, and selenite slab are safe for all jade types.

## What Jade Can and Cannot Handle

Jadeite has a Mohs hardness of 6.5–7; nephrite measures 6–6.5. Both minerals are chemically stable under normal conditions and resist surface damage from brief water contact. However, several factors increase the risk of harm:

- **Treatment**: Type B jadeite has been bleached and impregnated with polymer resin to improve apparent clarity. Soaking in water, especially with cleaning agents, can break down this polymer over time, causing the stone to become cloudy or develop surface crazing.
- **Wax coating**: many jade pieces receive a thin wax finish during polishing. Ultrasonic cleaners and steam cleaners strip this coating and can dull the surface.
- **Surface cracks**: jade with pre-existing fractures absorbs water and cleaning chemicals into those cracks, which may stain or weaken the material.
- **Salt**: dry or wet salt can abrade jade's surface (Mohs hardness below 7 means some common salts can mark softer examples) and salt dissolved in water concentrates as it evaporates, depositing residue in surface features.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Brief water rinse

Hold jade under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 to 30 seconds and pat dry immediately with a soft, lint-free cloth. Do not soak, do not use hot water, and do not add salt or cleaning chemicals. This method is physically low-risk for uncracked, untreated natural jade. For Type B (polymer-treated) pieces, limit rinsing to occasional use only and dry thoroughly.

### Mild soap and water

Dampen a soft cloth with plain water and a single drop of mild dish soap. Wipe the jade surface gently, rinse the cloth under water, wipe again to remove soap residue, and pat the stone dry. Avoid submerging the piece. This is the recommended physical cleaning method for removing oils and surface dirt from jewelry worn against skin.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold jade in the smoke of burning herbs, incense sticks, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, moving the stone slowly so all surfaces are exposed. Ventilate the room. This method carries no physical risk to the stone and is widely used in crystal practice as a ritual cleansing step. It also works for carved pieces and bangles where surface wiping is impractical.

### Sound

Place jade near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through or around it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance and requires no contact with liquids, making it suitable for all jade types including carved heirlooms.

### Moonlight

Set jade on a dry indoor windowsill or a covered outdoor surface on a clear night. Leave it for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast: avoid placing jade outside if rain or high humidity is expected. Bring the stone in before morning condensation can form on it. This method is used in crystal tradition as both a cleanse and a recharge in a single step.

### Selenite slab

Place jade on or against a dry [selenite](/gemstones/selenite) slab for several hours. Note that selenite itself is water-sensitive and must be kept dry; do not combine this method with any wet cleansing step. This is a fully dry method with no abrasion risk.

## Methods to Avoid

- **Ultrasonic cleaners**: vibration can enlarge pre-existing fractures and will degrade polymer fills in Type B stones.
- **Steam cleaners**: heat and moisture together strip wax coatings and can damage polymer-treated jade.
- **Salt baths (wet or dry)**: salt may abrade the surface and leaves residue in cracks. Salt is also hygroscopic, which draws ambient moisture toward the stone.
- **Strong chemical cleaners**: bleach, ammonia, and acidic cleaners can etch jade's surface or react with polymer fills.

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal tradition offers no universal schedule; a common guideline is to cleanse jade when it arrives, after periods of heavy use, and monthly during regular wear or display. As a physical maintenance note, inspect jade jewelry periodically for scratches or surface dullness. Pieces worn daily benefit from a gentle cloth wipe after each wear to remove skin oils before they build up.
