# Can Ruby Zoisite Go in Water?

Ruby Zoisite is safe for brief water contact. Neither the zoisite component (a calcium aluminum silicate) nor the ruby component (corundum, Al2O3) dissolves or reacts harmfully with plain water. The practical limit is prolonged soaking: extended immersion can allow water to penetrate the boundaries between the stone's three component minerals, and salt water should be avoided entirely.

## Why Ruby Zoisite Is Relatively Water-Tolerant

Ruby Zoisite, also called anyolite, is a metamorphic rock composed of three minerals: green zoisite (Mohs hardness 6–7), red ruby or corundum (Mohs 9), and black hornblende. Neither zoisite nor corundum is soluble in or reactive with plain water at normal temperatures. This distinguishes Ruby Zoisite from minerals that genuinely must never get wet, such as halite (table salt, which dissolves on contact) or selenite (soft gypsum that can lose surface luster and material over time).

The composite nature of the rock matters, however. Polished Ruby Zoisite looks uniform, but the interfaces between its three mineral components are potential weak points. Brief contact does not give water time to infiltrate those boundaries. Extended soaking does.

## What "Brief Contact" Means in Practice

A rinse of 30 to 60 seconds under cool running water is safe for a solid, polished, unfractured specimen. Wiping the surface with a damp cloth is also safe. The actions to avoid are:

- Soaking for more than 10 to 15 minutes
- Leaving the stone submerged overnight or for extended cleaning
- Any contact with salt water: salt crystals that lodge at mineral boundaries can generate internal stress as they dry, risking hairline fractures over repeated exposures
- Storing the stone in a damp environment after water contact

After rinsing, dry Ruby Zoisite promptly with a soft cloth and allow it to finish air-drying before storage.

## Cleaning Ruby Zoisite With Water

The recommended approach for physical cleaning is warm water with a small amount of neutral soap and a soft brush. Scrub gently, rinse thoroughly, and dry immediately. This is adequate for removing dust, oils, and handling residue. Avoid harsh detergents, bleach, and acid-based cleaners — these can attack the zoisite matrix. Ultrasonic and steam cleaners are not safe for Ruby Zoisite; vibration and heat can cause fractures along zoisite's perfect cleavage planes.

For ritual or energetic cleansing methods beyond physical washing, water is one option among several. For a full list of cleansing methods including dry alternatives, see [how to cleanse Ruby Zoisite](/gemstones/ruby-zoisite/how-to-cleanse).

## Salt Water and Gem Elixirs

Salt water is not recommended for Ruby Zoisite. As salt-saturated water dries at the mineral boundaries of the composite rock, salt crystals grow in micro-spaces and can exert outward pressure, contributing to surface damage over repeated exposures. This is the same reason salt water is avoided for many composite or naturally fractured stones.
