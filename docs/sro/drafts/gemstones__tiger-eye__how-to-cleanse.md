# How to Cleanse and Recharge Tiger Eye

Tiger Eye tolerates a brief rinse under running water but prolonged soaking risks rust-colored spots from its iron oxide content. The safest cleansing methods — smoke, sunlight, moonlight, sound, and selenite — carry no physical risk and cover the full range of what most practitioners use.

## Water and Tiger Eye: What the Iron Content Means

Tiger Eye is a quartz-family mineral with a Mohs hardness of 7. Its golden-brown color comes from iron oxide — specifically limonite and goethite — locked into the fibrous structure during geological formation. The quartz itself is water-resistant, but the iron inclusions are not chemically inert during prolonged water exposure: repeated soaking or leaving the stone wet overnight can eventually produce faint rust-colored spots or dull the polished surface over time.

A brief rinse of 15 to 30 seconds under cool running water is considered safe and will not damage the stone with a single exposure. Salt water accelerates iron oxidation and should be avoided entirely; salt is also abrasive to a polished surface. Dry Tiger Eye thoroughly with a soft cloth immediately after any water contact.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold Tiger Eye in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or sage for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating it so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is the most widely used method in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to the stone. Ventilate the room while doing this.

### Sunlight

Place Tiger Eye in direct morning sunlight for up to two hours. Unlike many colored stones, Tiger Eye does not fade in sunlight: its color comes from structural iron oxides rather than organic or light-sensitive pigments, so extended sun exposure is physically safe. Midday summer sun is best avoided for any polished stone as a general precaution. In crystal tradition, sunlight is also used as a recharging method in the same step, making it one of the most efficient options for Tiger Eye specifically.

### Moonlight

Set Tiger Eye on a dry windowsill or outdoors on a dry surface and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast first: dew, rain, or overnight condensation will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms. This method is popular in crystal practice for combining cleansing and recharging in one overnight step.

### Sound

Place Tiger Eye near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. The stone does not need to be touched or moved through any substance, which makes sound cleansing useful for larger or fragile specimens. It works equally well at close range.

### Selenite slab

Rest Tiger Eye on a dry [selenite](/gemstones/selenite) charging slab or alongside a selenite plate for several hours or overnight. Selenite is traditionally used in crystal practice to passively cleanse and recharge other stones. There is no physical risk to Tiger Eye from this method; keep the selenite itself dry and away from moisture, as selenite dissolves in water.

### Breath and visualization

Hold Tiger Eye in both hands, breathe in slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while mentally focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials, is available anywhere, and is used as a quick reset between sessions.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal traditions treat cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing, Tiger Eye is traditionally placed in morning sunlight, on a [clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz) cluster, or rested on a selenite slab for 15 minutes to two hours. For a full guide to recharging methods and timing, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

Tiger Eye is traditionally associated with confidence and courage — see [crystals for confidence](/crystals/crystals-for-confidence) for how it is used in broader crystal practice. Its hub page at [Tiger Eye](/gemstones/tiger-eye) covers all physical properties in one place.

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary. A common guideline is to cleanse Tiger Eye when it arrives new, after any period of intense daily use, and once a month during regular display. If carried daily, weekly cleansing is sometimes recommended. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — frequency is a matter of personal practice.

As a physical maintenance note: inspect Tiger Eye periodically for any rust-colored spots or surface dulling, which indicate moisture exposure rather than an energetic issue. Reducing ambient humidity in storage is the corrective step.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store Tiger Eye in a cool, dry place away from steam and condensation. Avoid bathrooms or kitchen windowsills. Tiger Eye at Mohs 7 can scratch softer stones and be scratched by harder ones such as sapphire or diamond — store pieces separately or wrap them to protect polished surfaces.
