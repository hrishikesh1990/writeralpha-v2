# Crystals for Healing: A Beginner's Wellbeing Set

Crystal healing is a complementary wellness practice in which people use stones as physical anchors for intention, relaxation, or ritual. It is not a medical treatment and does not replace professional healthcare. For people new to the practice, a five-stone general-wellbeing set — amethyst, rose quartz, clear quartz, selenite, and black tourmaline — covers the core tradition categories and is forgiving to learn with.

## What Crystal Healing Is (and Is Not)

Crystal healing encompasses a range of practices: holding or carrying a stone, placing stones on the body during rest, arranging them in a space, or incorporating them into meditation or breathwork. The connecting idea across traditions is that the physical form of a stone — its color, structure, weight, and texture — gives it symbolic or energetic qualities that interact with the user's state of mind or body.

Whether that interaction extends beyond the psychological effects of ritual, focus, and intention is contested. The evidence for and against the proposed mechanisms is covered at [crystal healing science](/learn/crystal-healing-science), where the site's full evidence stance is explained. This page treats crystal healing as a cultural and wellness practice — the associations described below are tradition, not medical claims.

Crystals are a complementary practice. They do not treat illness, manage symptoms, or substitute for medical care.

## How to Evaluate a Stone for General Wellbeing

Because "healing" is the broadest possible intention, practitioners typically organize beginner sets around a few overlapping criteria:

1. **Tradition range:** A good starter set covers calm/emotional support, energetic protection, clarity, and grounding — rather than loading all five stones with the same symbolic category.
2. **Physical safety and durability:** Stones that are easy to handle, water-safe for the most common cleaning methods, and free from surface toxicity concerns. A healing set will be handled frequently.
3. **Accessibility:** Common enough to source from reputable sellers, affordable enough to replace if lost or damaged.

The five stones below meet all three criteria. Practitioners new to the practice can start with any one of them and add the others as they explore what each tradition says.

## Amethyst

[Amethyst](/gemstones/amethyst) is purple quartz (SiO2), colored by Fe3+ ions under natural irradiation, Mohs 7. Its hardness makes it one of the most durable stones for everyday carry and repeated handling. Color can fade with prolonged direct sunlight; keep it out of south-facing windowsills.

In healing tradition, amethyst is associated with calm, emotional steadiness, and support during stress. It is the most frequently recommended stone for people seeking a general-purpose companion for anxious or unsettled periods. Practitioners carry it in a pocket, hold it during breathing exercises, or place it on a nightstand. It is widely available and among the most affordable stones in this set.

**Who it suits:** Anyone who wants a general calming stone; people new to crystal practice who want a single starting point.

## Rose Quartz

[Rose quartz](/gemstones/rose-quartz) is SiO2, Mohs 7, pink from microscopic inclusions or lattice-level defects depending on the specimen. Like amethyst it is durable for everyday carry. Some specimens fade with prolonged sun exposure.

In tradition, rose quartz is the primary stone for emotional support, self-compassion, and nurturing. Practitioners use it during periods of grief, relationship difficulty, or when they want to establish a daily self-care ritual. The stone's association with gentleness makes it a specific recommendation for people who find it hard to direct care toward themselves. It is commonly placed near a workspace or carried as a tumbled stone.

**Who it suits:** People working through emotional difficulty; anyone seeking a daily reminder of self-compassion; practitioners focused on relationship or heart-centered intention.

## Clear Quartz

[Clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz) is colorless SiO2, Mohs 7, and one of the most abundant minerals on Earth. It is durable, widely available, and inexpensive. In tradition it is called an amplifier — a neutral stone that practitioners use when they want to work with a clear, specific intention without adding the directional associations of a colored stone.

In a beginner healing set, clear quartz functions as a versatile substitute for any stone that is unavailable, and as an intensifier placed alongside other stones in a grid or arrangement. Practitioners also use it on its own when the intention is straightforward: to feel clearer, more focused, or more present.

**Who it suits:** Anyone who wants a versatile, low-cost anchor stone; practitioners building a first set on a budget who want one stone that covers many uses.

## Selenite

[Selenite](/gemstones/selenite) is a variety of gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O), Mohs 2. It is the softest stone in this set and requires different handling than the quartzes. **Do not submerge selenite in water** — it dissolves; see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals) for water-free methods. Do not use selenite as a regular hand-held carry stone; its surface scratches easily with daily contact.

In tradition, selenite is placed in a space rather than carried on the body. Practitioners use it to cleanse and reset a room's energy, to create a calm atmosphere for healing work, or as a charging plate for other stones. A wand or plate on a shelf, windowsill, or altar is the standard form. Its low maintenance reputation (some traditions say selenite self-clears and does not need cleansing) makes it popular for people who want a set-and-forget space anchor.

**Who it suits:** People who want to define a physical healing space at home; practitioners who prefer working with their environment rather than carrying stones.

## Black Tourmaline

[Black tourmaline](/gemstones/black-tourmaline) (schorl) is a complex boron silicate, Mohs 7–7.5, with no significant cleavage. It is the most durable stone in this set and handles daily carry and pocket wear without degrading. Schorl is also one of the most affordable black stones, which matters because look-alike substitutes (jet, black obsidian, black glass) are sometimes sold under the same name. Tourmaline's glassy-to-resinous luster and vertical striations on raw pieces are distinguishing features.

In tradition, black tourmaline is the primary protection stone. Practitioners associate it with creating a boundary between themselves and draining situations, environments, or other people's distress. It is placed at the corners of a room, carried in a bag or pocket when entering difficult settings, or worn as a bracelet. For people who work in healthcare, social services, or other high-contact roles, it is a common everyday carry stone.

**Who it suits:** People who want protection or boundary-setting energy; those in emotionally demanding occupations; anyone who finds that they absorb stress from their surroundings.

## A Simple Starter Arrangement

Using all five stones at once is not necessary. A beginner's general-wellbeing approach:

- Carry one tumbled stone daily (amethyst, rose quartz, or black tourmaline based on the day's intention).
- Place selenite in a fixed spot at home — a shelf, a windowsill, a charging station for other stones.
- Keep clear quartz nearby when setting a specific intention or when the other stones are unavailable.

This uses the set without requiring a structured ritual. As practice develops, the how-to article [how to start a crystal collection](/learn/how-to-start-crystal-collection) explains what to add next and how to prioritize on a budget.

Clean stones regularly — tumbled quartzes and black tourmaline can be rinsed briefly with water. For selenite and any stone you are uncertain about, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).
