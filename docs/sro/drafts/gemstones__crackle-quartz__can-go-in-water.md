# Can Crackle Quartz Go in Water?

Undyed Crackle Quartz can tolerate a brief rinse because the underlying mineral is quartz (SiO₂, Mohs 7), which does not dissolve or corrode in water. Dyed Crackle Quartz should not soak in water — the fracture network that produces the stone's sparkle lets water penetrate deep inside, where it can loosen and leach the dye. Saltwater is not recommended for any Crackle Quartz.

## The Mineral Base vs. the Treatment

Understanding water safety for Crackle Quartz requires separating the mineral from what has been done to it. The base mineral — quartz — is chemically inert in water, resistant to acids at normal concentrations, and rated Mohs 7, well above the threshold where brief water exposure causes surface erosion. If Crackle Quartz were simply untreated rock crystal, water would be a non-issue.

The treatment changes the picture. Quench-crackling creates a three-dimensional network of fractures throughout the interior of the stone. Those fractures are highly permeable to liquids: this is how dye is introduced in the first place, drawn in by capillary action during manufacture. When a dyed piece contacts water, the same capillary pathways allow water to travel into the stone and carry dye back out. Extended soaking can produce visible color loss, particularly at surface fractures.

## Undyed vs. Dyed Crackle Quartz in Water

| Type | Brief rinse | Extended soak | Saltwater |
|---|---|---|---|
| Undyed (clear or untinted) | Generally safe | Not recommended; may stress fractures | Not recommended |
| Dyed (any color) | Avoid; risk of dye bleed | Not safe | Not safe |

Most Crackle Quartz sold in the crystal and bead market is dyed. If the color is vivid — blue, red, purple, green, orange — assume dyeing unless the seller provides documentation to the contrary.

## Why Salt Is a Separate Concern

Saltwater combines the water-penetration risk with additional chemical stress. Salt draws moisture toward any surface it contacts (it is hygroscopic), and within a fracture network it can concentrate as the water evaporates, leaving deposits that can physically widen fracture planes over repeated cycles. Dry salt burial — a common cleansing method for harder, less porous stones — carries the same risk because salt absorbs ambient humidity and keeps the stone surface damp. For guidance on which stones tolerate salt, see [salt safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## How to Clean Crackle Quartz Without Water

If the stone needs physical cleaning — removing surface dust or handling residue — wipe it gently with a soft, dry or barely damp cloth, then dry immediately. Do not allow any moisture to sit on the surface or pool near surface fractures.
