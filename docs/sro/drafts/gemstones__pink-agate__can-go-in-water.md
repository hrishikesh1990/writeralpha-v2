# Can Pink Agate Go in Water?

Yes, Pink Agate can go in water briefly. As a chalcedony — a microcrystalline quartz mineral (SiO2) — it rates 6.5–7 on the Mohs scale and does not dissolve or react with plain water at room temperature. A quick rinse under lukewarm running water is safe. The practical limits are soaking time, temperature extremes, and whether the stone has been dyed.

## Why Pink Agate Is Water-Safe

Agate's composition — silicon dioxide (SiO2) — is the same mineral family as quartz and sand, both of which are stable in water. Minerals with Mohs hardness below about 5 or with soluble compositions (selenite dissolves, malachite reacts, pyrite oxidizes) are at risk in water. Pink Agate does not share any of those vulnerabilities:

- **No solubility**: SiO2 does not dissolve in plain water at room temperature.
- **No toxic leaching**: Agate contains no components that leach harmful compounds into water at ambient conditions.
- **Hard surface**: At Mohs 6.5–7, the polished surface resists scratching from water flow.

For a comparison of which crystals are and are not water-safe, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## What to Watch For

### Dyed material

A large proportion of the pink agate sold commercially is dyed — white or gray chalcedony that has been artificially colored. The dye is absorbed into agate's microscopic pores during processing. Repeated soaking can gradually leach dye from the stone, fading the color over time. A brief rinse (30–60 seconds) is unlikely to cause visible change; extended soaking or daily washing over months may dull the color in treated material.

To check whether your stone is dyed: examine any drill hole, chipped edge, or unpolished area — if the interior is substantially paler than the surface, the color is an added treatment. Natural pink agate shows consistent color throughout. The full identification guide is at [how to identify Pink Agate](/gemstones/pink-agate/how-to-identify).

### Temperature extremes

Avoid sudden temperature shifts: placing a cold stone under very hot water, or a warm stone into cold water, can stress natural fractures and cracks. Use lukewarm water and allow the stone to return to room temperature gradually.

### Salt water

Salt water is not recommended for Pink Agate, for two reasons. First, salt water is mildly abrasive over repeated exposure and can dull a polished surface. Second, salt particles can lodge in the stone's natural micro-pores and draw moisture over time if not fully rinsed. Plain fresh water is sufficient and carries less risk. For guidance on which stones tolerate salt, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

### Metal settings

If your Pink Agate is set in silver or plated jewelry, limit water exposure. Water accelerates tarnishing of silver and can damage plating or adhesive in set pieces. Loose tumbled stones and unset specimens are fine for rinsing.

## How to Rinse Pink Agate Safely

1. Use lukewarm running water from a tap.
2. Hold the stone under the flow for 30 to 60 seconds.
3. Pat dry immediately with a soft cloth.
4. Allow to air-dry completely before storing or setting.

Do not use soap on unset stones — residue can settle in surface pores and create a film that dulls the natural waxy luster. If cleaning jewelry, a small amount of mild soap is acceptable if rinsed very thoroughly.

## Sun Drying After Washing

Allow the stone to air-dry at room temperature rather than placing it in direct sunlight to speed drying. Prolonged UV exposure can fade dyed material. Indirect natural light or a warm room is sufficient. For more on sun safety for pink and dyed stones, see [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals).
