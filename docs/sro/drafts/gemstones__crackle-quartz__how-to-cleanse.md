# How to Cleanse Crackle Quartz Without Damaging It

Crackle Quartz is best cleansed with smoke, sound, moonlight, or breath. Water is risky for dyed pieces — the fracture network that gives the stone its sparkle is highly permeable to liquids, so water can penetrate and leach dye. Ultrasonic and steam cleaning are always off-limits.

## Why Crackle Quartz Needs Special Care

Crackle Quartz is natural quartz (SiO₂, Mohs hardness 7) that has been thermally shocked — heated and then rapidly quenched in cold water — to create a three-dimensional network of internal fractures. Those fractures produce the stone's signature sparkle but also make it more fragile than untreated quartz and highly permeable to liquids. Most pieces sold in bead and crystal markets have also been dyed, with color drawn deep into the fractures by capillary action during the quenching process.

These two characteristics — fragility and permeability — rule out cleansing methods that are safe for harder, intact minerals. ## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold or place the stone in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, turning it so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is the most practical cleansing method for Crackle Quartz: nothing touches the stone and there is no risk of moisture or mechanical stress. Ensure the space is ventilated.

### Sound

Place the stone near a singing bowl, tuning fork, or bell and let the vibration pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the piece stays stationary and undisturbed. This method is particularly useful for stones already set in jewelry or mounted pieces.

### Moonlight

Set the stone on a dry, stable surface — a windowsill or outdoor table — on a clear night. Leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast first: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it indoors before dawn condensation can form.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the stone gently in both hands, take a slow breath, and exhale steadily across its surface while focusing on releasing any accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and carries no physical risk.

## Methods to Avoid

| Method | Why to avoid |
|---|---|
| Running water | Penetrates fractures; leaches dye from dyed specimens |
| Saltwater soak | Salt compounds the damage; brine is more corrosive than plain water |
| Dry salt burial | Salt is hygroscopic and draws moisture toward the stone |
| Ultrasonic cleaner | Ultrasonic vibration can propagate the existing fractures and shatter the stone |
| Steam cleaner | Heat and moisture together risk further fracturing and dye loss |
| Prolonged direct sunlight | Repeated UV exposure may fade dye trapped in the fractures |

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal tradition treats cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing Crackle Quartz, place it beside a piece of [selenite](/gemstones/selenite), near a cluster of [clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz), or on a dry surface in full moonlight overnight. Some practitioners use brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes) for recharging; repeated direct UV exposure over weeks and months carries a small risk of fading dyed specimens. For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal practice varies. A common guideline is to cleanse the stone when it arrives, after any period of extended use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule; that is a matter of personal practice.

As a practical note: inspect dyed Crackle Quartz periodically for color loss along surface fractures, which can indicate moisture exposure. Store the stone in a dry location away from steam, condensation, and persistent humidity.
