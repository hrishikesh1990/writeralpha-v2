# Howlite Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Howlite is one of the most affordable stones in the crystal market. Single tumbled pieces retail for $2–$3 USD each; a one-pound bag of tumbled stones costs roughly $13–$26 USD. Prices checked at US online retailers in September 2026. The main risk is not overpaying for howlite itself, but paying turquoise prices for dyed howlite sold as turquoise.

## Typical Retail Prices

| Format | Price range (USD) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single tumbled stone | $2–$3 | Per piece; size 2–5 cm typical |
| 1 lb bag, tumbled | $13–$26 | Approx. 15–30 pieces depending on size |
| Cabochon, natural white | $3–$10 | Varies with shape and polish quality |
| Beaded bracelet | $5–$15 | Natural white; dyed colors similar range |
| Sphere (5–7 cm) | $10–$25 | More labor-intensive polish |
| Carving / decorative object | $10–$50+ | Highly variable with size and detail |

These prices reflect natural undyed white howlite from mainstream US crystal retailers. Dyed howlite (blue, red, or other colors) is priced similarly. Prices vary by grade, seller, and where the stone originates.

## What Drives Howlite's Price

### Supply

Howlite is abundant. Large borate deposits exist in California, Mexico, and Turkey, and the stone is mined commercially at volume. Its supply does not constrain price the way rare minerals do. This is the primary reason it is inexpensive.

### Hardness and durability

At Mohs 3.5, howlite is soft by gem standards (for context, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale)). Softer stones require more care in cutting and polishing to avoid surface damage, but they do not require the precision faceting work applied to hard transparent gems. The result is that polished howlite is labor-efficient to produce, which keeps costs low.

### Treatments

Howlite is commonly dyed — most often blue to imitate turquoise. Dye treatment does not substantially raise the price of howlite itself, but dyed howlite sold dishonestly as turquoise can be priced at multiples of its actual value. This is the market risk to understand. If you are paying more than $5–$15 for a tumbled piece or a bracelet labeled "turquoise," confirm with the seller whether the material is natural turquoise or dyed howlite. The identification methods are described at [how to identify howlite](/gemstones/howlite/how-to-identify).

### Polish and finish

Rough nodular howlite is the cheapest form. Tumbled stones cost a little more, cabochons and spheres more still, and carved pieces the most. Premium polishing and unusual sizes (very large spheres, matched sets) push prices toward the higher end of the ranges above.

### Origin and seller

Retail price varies meaningfully between large wholesale-backed online retailers (lowest prices), specialty metaphysical shops (mid-range), and artisan sellers with curated stock (highest, often for hand-selected or carved pieces). Buying from a reputable source that clearly labels treatments is worth a modest premium over the absolute cheapest option. See [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing) for broader guidance.

## When Howlite Is Overpriced

The main scenario is dyed howlite sold as turquoise. Natural turquoise of any quality costs substantially more per piece than howlite. A polished turquoise cabochon of 10–20 carats from a named deposit sells for tens to hundreds of dollars; a howlite piece of the same size should cost a few dollars. If a "turquoise" piece is priced like howlite, it almost certainly is howlite.

Sellers who disclose "howlite turquoise," "white turquoise," or "stabilized turquoise" may or may not be disclosing dyed howlite honestly — these are trade terms that require scrutiny. Ask the seller directly what the mineral is.

## Is Howlite Worth Buying?

Howlite is an excellent value as what it is: a soft, attractive white-veined stone with a consistent look and wide availability. For jewelry, it is best in low-wear settings (pendants, earrings, bracelets) rather than rings, given its softness. For crystal practice, it is a cost-effective entry to the calming stones associated with sleep and anxiety management — see [crystals for sleep](/crystals/crystals-for-sleep) and [crystals for anxiety](/crystals/crystals-for-anxiety). Its full profile is at [howlite](/gemstones/howlite).

The stone's affordability also makes it practical for buying multiple pieces for placement in different rooms or for pairing with other stones.
