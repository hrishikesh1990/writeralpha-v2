# Can Diamond Go in Water? Safe Cleaning Methods Explained

Diamond can go in water without any damage to the stone. Diamond is pure crystalline carbon (Mohs 10) and is chemically inert — it does not dissolve, oxidize, or react with plain water or salt water under normal conditions. Warm water with mild soap is the standard cleaning method recommended by GIA for diamond jewelry.

## Why Diamond Is Safe in Water

Diamond's exceptional stability comes from its crystal structure: carbon atoms bonded in a rigid cubic lattice with no incorporated elements that could react with water. Unlike pyrite (which oxidizes in moisture), selenite (which dissolves), or malachite (which is mildly soluble and toxic in solution), diamond is physically and chemically unchanged by water contact, even prolonged immersion.

Key properties that confirm water safety:

- **Mohs hardness 10** — the hardest natural substance; water carries no abrasive risk
- **Specific gravity 3.52** — dense, stable mineral with no porosity
- **Chemical formula: C** — pure carbon, no reactive metals, sulfides, or carbonates
- **No cleavage issues with water** — perfect octahedral cleavage exists but is not activated by liquid immersion

For context on which stones are and are not safe in water, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## The Right Way to Clean Diamond in Water

GIA recommends warm water with mild household detergent as the standard at-home method. The process:

1. Fill a small bowl with warm (not boiling) water and a few drops of dish soap.
2. Place the diamond (loose or set) in the solution and soak for five to ten minutes. This loosens body oils and grime that dull brilliance.
3. Use a soft toothbrush to gently scrub all surfaces, including the back of the stone and around prongs or bezels.
4. Rinse thoroughly under running warm water. Hold the piece over a cupped hand or a bowl if working near a drain.
5. Dry with a lint-free cloth or allow to air dry.

This routine, performed every one to two weeks, maintains a diamond's light performance. Oil from fingerprints is the primary reason a diamond's sparkle diminishes during wear.

## What to Avoid When Cleaning Diamond in Water

The stone itself tolerates most agents, but the metal setting does not. Specific things to avoid:

| Substance | Why to avoid |
|---|---|
| Chlorine bleach | Corrodes gold and platinum alloys in settings; weakens prongs over time |
| Abrasive cleansers or toothpaste | Scratches metal settings and softer side stones |
| Acetone, paint thinner | Can damage certain glue-set or resin-filled stones in the same piece |
| Boiling water | Thermal shock risk for fracture-filled diamonds or stones alongside other gems |

Ultrasonic and steam cleaners are effective in professional jewelry settings for most untreated diamonds, but GIA advises against home use because both can loosen stones from prongs. Fracture-filled diamonds (a clarity-enhancement treatment) should not go in ultrasonic cleaners — the vibration can disturb the filling material. If you do not know whether your diamond has been treated, use the warm-water soap method.

## Salt Water and Salt Cleansing

Salt water (ocean swimming, salt baths) does not harm the diamond itself. The physical risk is again to the setting: prolonged exposure to salt water can accelerate corrosion of base metals or weaken adhesives in glue-set stones. Removing fine jewelry before swimming is standard jewelry care advice.

Dry salt burial — used as an energetic cleansing method in crystal tradition — poses no chemical risk to diamond. Rinse the stone after to remove any salt residue from the setting, or use a salt slab or dish where the stone does not directly contact loose salt. For salt safety across different stones, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## Sunlight After Water Cleaning

Diamond is stable in sunlight: its color does not fade and its surface is not damaged by UV exposure. Allowing a diamond to air dry in sunlight after washing is harmless. For guidance on which stones are sun-safe, see [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals).
