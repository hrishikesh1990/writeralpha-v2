# Rose Quartz Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Rose Quartz is an abundant gemstone and one of the most affordable you can buy. Small tumbled pieces sell for $1–$5 each at retail, while deep-pink transparent faceted stones reach $15–$50 per carat or more. Form, color intensity, clarity, size, and origin are the five factors that move the price.

## Why Rose Quartz Is Generally Affordable

Rose Quartz is a variety of quartz (SiO2, Mohs 7) mined in large quantities in Brazil, Madagascar, India, and several other regions. Its abundance keeps base prices low. What drives the upper end of the market is a combination of rarity within the material — vivid, even color and good transparency together are less common — plus cutting and craftsmanship costs for finished pieces.

Rose Quartz also rarely undergoes treatments that inflate price, unlike some gemstones that are routinely heat-treated or fracture-filled. Most material sold is natural, untreated stone. If a seller cannot confirm treatment status, that is worth asking about — see [how to identify real rose quartz](/gemstones/rose-quartz/how-to-identify) for what to look for.

## Price by Form

Prices below are retail ranges checked in September 2026. Prices vary by grade, origin, and seller.

| Form | Typical retail price |
|---|---|
| Small tumbled stone (1–5 g) | $1–$5 per piece |
| Medium tumbled stone (5–15 g) | $3–$10 per piece |
| Rough/raw specimen (per kg, common grade) | $5–$20 per kg |
| Polished cabochon | $5–$20 per piece |
| Faceted gem, pale-to-medium pink (1–5 ct) | $5–$15 per carat |
| Faceted gem, deep pink, transparent (1–5 ct) | $15–$50 per carat |
| Star rose quartz cabochon (cat's eye / asterism) | $30–$80+ per piece |
| Carved heart or sphere (medium, 5–10 cm) | $20–$100 per piece |
| High-quality Madagascar carved heart (large) | $200–$500 per piece |
| Rose quartz in jewelry (silver setting) | $20–$150 per piece |
| Rose quartz in jewelry (gold or premium setting) | $100–$500+ per piece |

## The Five Price Drivers

**Color.** The single biggest factor. Deep, saturated, uniform pink commands a premium. Pale or uneven color is common and inexpensive. A washed-out or very milky piece may retail for $1–$2; a richly colored Madagascar stone of the same size may retail for ten times as much.

**Clarity and transparency.** Most rose quartz is translucent to semi-transparent. The clearer and more evenly lit a piece looks, the higher the price. Opaque or heavily included material is used for carvings and tumbles rather than faceted gems.

**Size.** Large, high-quality specimens are rarer. A 10-carat faceted stone with strong color is worth disproportionately more than two 5-carat stones of similar grade.

**Origin.** Madagascar material is broadly considered the finest for vivid color and clarity. Brazil produces consistent quality at moderate prices. Indian rose quartz tends toward darker saturation but can include more inclusions. Origin alone does not confirm quality; judge the stone by its characteristics, not just its label.

**Form and craftsmanship.** A rough chunk is the cheapest form. Tumbling, cabochon cutting, faceting, and artistic carving each add labor cost. A rose quartz sphere polished to a high finish costs more than the same weight in rough, regardless of grade.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

Compare across at least two or three sellers before buying. The market for tumbled rose quartz is competitive and prices are consistent enough that a retailer charging $15 per small tumbled piece is operating outside normal retail range for common grade material. Premium prices are justified by demonstrably better color, transparency, or large size — if you cannot see a difference, look elsewhere.

For any stone sold as high-quality or rare, ask the seller about origin, treatment status, and return policy. Reputable sellers will answer clearly. For guidance on sourcing practices, see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing).
