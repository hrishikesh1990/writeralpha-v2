# How to Cleanse and Recharge Larvikite

Larvikite can be cleansed with smoke, sound, moonlight, selenite, or a brief water rinse. Prolonged soaking or salt-water baths are not recommended because they can dull the stone's polished surface over time. Each safe method is covered below, followed by recharging options.

## What Larvikite Tolerates Physically

Larvikite is a coarse-grained igneous rock composed mainly of anorthoclase feldspar, with a Mohs hardness of 6 to 6.5. Anorthoclase feldspar is not water-soluble, so brief water contact does not dissolve or structurally damage the stone. However, polished larvikite can lose its surface sheen with repeated or prolonged contact with water, particularly salt water, which is mildly abrasive and can introduce mineral deposits into the grain boundaries of the rock.

Harsh household chemicals — bleach, acetone, ammonia-based cleaners — should be kept away from any polished stone, including larvikite. Ultrasonic cleaners are also not recommended: the vibration can enlarge micro-fractures in a coarse-grained rock over repeated use.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold larvikite in the smoke of burning herbs, incense sticks, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the stone slowly so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is the most straightforward method for larvikite: no moisture, no abrasive contact, and the stone's dark surface shows no residue. Ventilate the room; you are also breathing the smoke.

### Sound

Place larvikite near a singing bowl, tuning fork, or bell and allow the vibration to wash over it for one to two minutes. The stone does not need to be touched or moved. Sound cleansing is useful for large pieces, spheres, or specimens that are difficult to smudge, and it works equally well for polished and rough material.

### Moonlight

Set larvikite on a dry surface — a windowsill, a table outdoors, or a cloth — on a clear night and leave it for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast before placing it outside: morning dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it in before condensation forms. Full moon nights are traditional for this method in crystal practice, though practitioners use any clear night.

### Dry selenite slab or cluster

Place larvikite directly on a piece of [selenite](/gemstones/selenite) (a slab or a flat cluster) and leave it for several hours. Selenite is traditionally used for cleansing other stones in crystal practice. Physically, selenite is very soft (Mohs 2) and should not be used with abrasive cleaners, but as a static surface for placing another stone it works without any physical risk to either stone.

### Visualization or breath

Hold larvikite in both hands and exhale slowly across the surface while visualizing any accumulated energy leaving. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and is used as a quick reset between sessions or when other methods are unavailable. It is a practice of intention; no physical mechanism applies.

### Brief water rinse (with caveats)

A short rinse under running cool water — 10 to 30 seconds — will not harm polished larvikite, and some practitioners use this as a physical cleaning step before energetic cleansing. Do not soak, do not use salt water, and dry the stone promptly with a soft cloth. Repeated water contact over months can gradually dull highly polished surfaces.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as two separate steps. Cleansing is understood as releasing accumulated energy; recharging is understood as restoring the stone's vitality. Common recharging methods for larvikite:

- **Moonlight** doubles as a recharging method; leaving the stone overnight on a full moon is the most commonly recommended option for larvikite in the crystal community.
- **Morning sunlight** for 15 to 30 minutes is physically safe for larvikite (it is an opaque rock with no photosensitive color); longer sun exposure is not known to cause harm, but it is not considered necessary.
- **Selenite or clear quartz** placed alongside or beneath larvikite; both are traditional recharging companions in crystal practice.
- **Earth** — placing larvikite on soil or burying it in a small pot of dry indoor soil for 24 hours is a traditional grounding recharge for earth-associated stones.

For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary. A commonly cited guideline is: cleanse when the stone arrives, after heavy use or an emotionally intense period, and monthly during regular display or wear. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — the stone does not degrade without cleansing. This is entirely a matter of personal practice.

As a practical maintenance note, inspect polished larvikite periodically for any surface dulling or deposits at grain boundaries. If the stone looks less lustrous than when it arrived, a very gentle wipe with a barely damp cloth followed by thorough drying is a physical cleaning step, separate from energetic cleansing.
