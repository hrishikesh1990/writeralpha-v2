# Clear Quartz Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Clear quartz is one of the most affordable crystals because it is among the most abundant minerals on Earth. Tumbled stones typically retail for $1 to $5 each; small raw points for $3 to $20; clusters for $10 to $80 or more depending on size; large spheres and museum-grade specimens can reach several hundred dollars. Price is driven by size, clarity, formation type, and origin, not rarity.

## Why Clear Quartz Is Affordable

Silicon dioxide (SiO2) in its quartz form makes up a significant portion of the Earth's crust. Major gem and collector-quality sources include Brazil (the largest exporter), Madagascar, the United States (Arkansas in particular), and the Alps. The sheer volume of material on the market keeps prices low for common forms. Clear quartz is not treated to achieve its color (it is colorless naturally), and no artificial enhancement is needed to make it market-ready.

This abundance means that inflated prices are almost always a function of presentation, branding, or misrepresentation — not genuine scarcity. Understanding this is important when evaluating vendor claims.

## Price Ranges by Form (September 2026)

The ranges below reflect publicly observable retail pricing across online and in-store crystal vendors as of September 2026. Prices vary by grade, origin, and seller and may change over time.

| Form | Typical retail range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tumbled stone (small) | $1 – $5 per stone | Most common form; widely available |
| Tumbled stone (large) | $3 – $10 per stone | Size is the main driver |
| Raw point / single wand | $3 – $20 | Varies by clarity and termination quality |
| Polished wand | $10 – $30 | Shaped and polished; labor adds cost |
| Cluster (small) | $10 – $35 | Multiple crystals on matrix |
| Cluster (medium–large) | $35 – $80+ | Size, crystal definition, no damage |
| Sphere (5–8 cm) | $20 – $60 | Polishing labor; clarity and size |
| Sphere (10 cm+) | $60 – $200+ | Larger or high-clarity examples |
| Faceted gem stone (per carat) | $1 – $10/ct | Gem-quality faceted rock crystal |
| Museum specimen | $100 – $1,000+ | Exceptional formation, provenance |

Faceted gem-quality clear quartz (rock crystal) remains inexpensive per carat compared to almost any other transparent gem material, because supply vastly exceeds gem demand. Large, flawless faceted stones do not command the per-carat premiums seen in rarer gems — size alone does not dramatically increase per-carat price.

## What Drives Price Within Clear Quartz

**Clarity.** Internally clean, water-clear specimens command higher prices than milky or heavily included examples. Milky quartz (white from dense fluid inclusions) is less valued as a display piece, though it is equally quartz and serves the same purposes in practice.

**Formation type.** Well-formed, doubly terminated crystals, Lemurian seed crystals (with characteristic horizontal striations), elestial formations, and cathedral quartz all attract premiums above standard Brazilian points, primarily because of collector demand.

**Size.** Within any form, larger pieces cost more. A sphere's price scales roughly with volume, so doubling the diameter increases price substantially.

**Origin.** Arkansas quartz and certain Alpine specimens carry collector premiums for their reputation for clarity and formation quality. Brazilian quartz dominates the mass market and is generally very affordable.

**Polishing.** Shaped and polished forms (spheres, wands, palmstones, hearts) include labor cost in addition to material cost.

**Setting.** Clear quartz in silver or gold jewelry settings carries the price of the metalwork on top of the stone, which is usually the smaller cost element.

## What Does Not Justify High Prices

Because clear quartz is abundant and easy to source, there are few legitimate reasons for dramatic price premiums on standard forms. Watch for:

- **Vague rarity claims.** "Rare Himalayan quartz" or "special frequency quartz" without verifiable origin documentation is a marketing term, not a mineralogical category.
- **Healing certification markups.** The stone's traditional associations do not vary with vendor and are not something that can be certified.
- **Implausibly high prices for common forms.** A standard 3 cm tumbled stone should not cost $30. If it does, verify the seller's identification claims carefully — see [how to identify clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz/how-to-identify).

## How to Avoid Overpaying

- Buy from vendors who state origin, size, and grade clearly.
- Compare prices across at least two or three vendors for identical forms.
- Understand that clear quartz is durable (Mohs 7 — see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale)) and does not require specialist storage or handling, which means there is no cost basis for dramatic price markups related to "fragility."
- For ethical sourcing considerations — provenance, labor conditions, environmental impact — see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing).
