# Crazy Lace Agate Price Guide

Crazy Lace Agate is an affordable stone. Retail prices (checked September 2026) run approximately $2–10 per tumbled stone, $10–50 for polished slabs and cabochons of moderate quality, and $50–300 or more for exceptional pattern specimens or large display pieces. Pattern quality, color contrast, and piece size are the main drivers of price within each category.

## What Drives the Price of Crazy Lace Agate

Crazy Lace Agate is not priced by carat weight the way faceted gemstones are. The value of a piece comes primarily from visual appeal:

- **Pattern complexity and intricacy:** The most valued pieces have tightly folded, varied banding with well-defined lace-like loops, eyes, and color shifts. Simpler, broader banding commands less.
- **Color contrast:** Strong contrast between warm red or orange bands and pale cream or white bands is preferred in the market. Pale or washed-out color reduces value.
- **Polish quality:** Crazy Lace Agate requires a clean, bright polish to show its banding at its best. Orange-peel texture, dull patches, or scratches reduce appeal and price.
- **Size:** Larger pieces — whether tumbled, slabbed, or carved — cost more per piece simply because they contain more material. A large tumbled stone (40+ mm) costs more than a small one even if the pattern quality is comparable.
- **Heat treatment:** Some Crazy Lace Agate on the market has been heat-treated to intensify its red and orange tones. This is common practice and does not reduce durability, but untreated stones with vivid natural color may command a premium with knowledgeable collectors. Treatment is not always disclosed.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Compare pattern, not just price.** Two tumbled stones at the same price can differ significantly in pattern quality. Stones with vivid color and intricate banding are worth more than plain or washed-out pieces at any price point.

**Buy from sellers who show the actual piece.** Crazy Lace Agate varies so much from piece to piece that stock photos are a poor guide. Sellers who photograph the individual stone (or provide a close-up of the exact piece) allow informed comparison.

**Understand that supply is limited to one location.** Crazy Lace Agate comes from Chihuahua, Mexico. Its single-source origin means supply is inherently constrained. It is not a stone that can be freely substituted from another locality without a different pattern style.

**Be wary of very low prices on "Crazy Lace Agate" with uniform or suspiciously vivid color.** While outright faking of Crazy Lace Agate is uncommon, mislabeled lower-quality banded agates or heavily treated material is occasionally sold under the name. ## Durability Relative to Price

At Mohs hardness 7 and with no cleavage, Crazy Lace Agate is durable enough for most jewelry applications. Its durability relative to price makes it good value compared with softer stones at similar price points. For context on where hardness 7 sits on the scale, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale).

## Sourcing and Ethics

Crazy Lace Agate is mined commercially in Chihuahua, Mexico. The trade is established and the stone is plentiful enough that it is not considered endangered or under supply pressure, though as a single-locality material any sustained demand increase affects supply.
