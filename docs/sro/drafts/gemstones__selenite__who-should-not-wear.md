# Who Should Be Cautious About Wearing Selenite

Most people can wear or handle selenite without any physical risk — it is non-toxic. The real cautions are physical: selenite is extremely soft (Mohs 2.0) and water-soluble, which limits how it can be worn and by whom. Separately, some crystal traditions describe astrological incompatibilities, which are covered below and stated as tradition.

## Criteria for This Evaluation

Three categories of caution are assessed here:

1. **Physical safety** — hardness, solubility, fragility, toxicity
2. **Lifestyle situations** — occupations, habits, or activities that increase risk of damage or exposure
3. **Astrological and metaphysical tradition** — who practitioners traditionally advise against using selenite

## Physical Safety: Who Has Real Reason to Be Careful

### People who work with water or moisture

Selenite is calcium sulfate dihydrate (CaSO4·2H2O) and dissolves slowly in water. Wearing a raw or lightly polished selenite piece while washing dishes, gardening in wet soil, swimming, or bathing will degrade the surface. Even high ambient humidity, such as in a greenhouse or bathroom, can pit polished surfaces over time. Anyone whose daily routine involves regular hand-washing or wet environments should either choose a different stone or remove selenite before any water contact.

### People who do physical or abrasive work

At Mohs 2.0, selenite is softer than a fingernail (Mohs ~2.5) and far softer than most other crystals in common use. It scratches on contact with keys, coins, jeans rivets, and virtually any hard surface. It chips and fractures easily under impact. Wearing selenite jewelry in a workshop, construction environment, or during sport carries a high likelihood of damage to the piece.

### Children under supervision

Selenite is not toxic — calcium sulfate is used as a food additive (E516) and is generally recognized as safe — but its fragility makes loose pieces a concern for very young children. Pieces that splinter or chip into sharp fragments pose a minor injury risk. Selenite is not a good choice for unsupervised handling by toddlers.

### People sensitive to dust from cutting or grinding

In normal handling, selenite poses no inhalation risk. If you are cutting, grinding, or drilling selenite (e.g., for jewelry making), wear appropriate dust protection. Fine calcium sulfate dust is a respiratory irritant in quantity, as with most fine mineral dusts.

## Lifestyle Situations

| Situation | Risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Swimming / bathing | Surface dissolution | Remove before water contact |
| Physical labor / sport | Scratching, chipping | Remove or choose a harder stone |
| Humid storage environment | Pitting of polished surfaces | Store with silica gel; avoid bathrooms |
| Wearing against rough fabrics | Scratching | Mount in protective setting |
| Children's play | Fragmentation, sharp chips | Adult supervision recommended |

## Astrological and Traditional Cautions

Crystal practitioners note that selenite is linked to the Moon, Cancer, and Taurus in tradition. Some practitioners suggest it may feel overstimulating for highly sensitive individuals, particularly those who already experience vivid dreams or difficulty sleeping. This is a practitioner perspective, not a physical or medical claim. If you find selenite disruptive during nighttime use, see [sleeping with selenite](/gemstones/selenite/sleeping-with) for guidance on placement.

No credible astrological system formally prohibits any zodiac sign from wearing selenite. Practitioners sometimes suggest that fire signs (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius) may prefer more activating stones over the calm-leaning energies associated with selenite — but this is a stylistic preference in crystal tradition, not a contraindication.

Crystal practice is a complementary interest, not a substitute for medical advice.
