# How to Cleanse and Recharge Citrine

Citrine is a variety of quartz with a Mohs hardness of 7 and no solubility in water under normal conditions, making it one of the easier stones to cleanse. Safe methods include running water, smoke, sound, moonlight, and earth burial. One caution applies: prolonged direct sunlight can gradually fade citrine's iron-based color, so recharging in sun should be kept brief.

## Is Citrine Safe in Water?

Citrine is safe in water for short cleansing sessions. Quartz at Mohs 7 does not dissolve, corrode, or release harmful ions when rinsed under plain tap water or briefly submerged. For a full explanation of the chemistry and any nuances, see [can citrine go in water](/gemstones/citrine/can-go-in-water).

Salt water is not recommended for extended soaking. Plain water is sufficient, and salt does not add any practical benefit for stone cleansing beyond the ritual intention practitioners associate with it.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Running water

Hold citrine under cool or lukewarm running tap water for 30 to 60 seconds, turning the piece so all surfaces are rinsed. Dry it with a soft cloth immediately afterward. This is the most straightforward method and carries no physical risk to the stone for the durations typically used.

If you work with citrine frequently — carrying it daily, using it in sessions, or keeping it in a workspace — rinsing it once a week or after heavy use is a common guideline in crystal practice.

### Smoke cleansing

Pass citrine slowly through the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Keep the room ventilated. This method is widely used and has no physical effect on the stone itself; the benefit is entirely in the intention the practitioner brings to it.

### Sound

Place citrine near a ringing singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and let the vibration carry for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone does not need to be handled or moved through anything. This method suits large or display pieces that would be awkward to rinse or smudge.

### Moonlight

Set citrine on a dry windowsill or outside on a dry surface on a clear night and leave it for several hours or overnight. Moonlight cleansing is popular in crystal tradition as a combined cleanse-and-recharge step. Unlike sunlight, moonlight does not carry enough UV energy to fade the stone's color, making it a lower-risk overnight option.

Check the forecast before leaving citrine outside: dew or rain will wet the stone, which is not harmful but defeats the purpose if you intended a dry method. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms.

### Dry earth burial

Bury citrine in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil for 24 hours. Place it in a shallow hole, cover it, then retrieve and brush it off with a dry cloth. This method is used in crystal tradition for grounding and energetic reset. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp or contain residues.

### Breath and visualization

Hold citrine in both hands, take a slow breath in, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing on releasing any accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials, leaves no residue, and is used for a quick reset when other methods are not available.

## What to Avoid

**Prolonged direct sunlight.** Citrine gets its color from traces of ferric iron (Fe³⁺) incorporated in its quartz structure. This color can fade with sustained UV exposure over time — the same mechanism that fades amethyst when left in a sunny window for weeks or months. Brief sunlight (under 30 minutes) for recharging purposes is generally described as safe in crystal practice, but leaving citrine in a south-facing window all day, every day, risks gradual bleaching.

**Ultrasonic cleaners.** These are safe for sound citrine without fractures or inclusions, but any stone with internal cracks or extensive inclusions can fracture under ultrasonic vibration. If in doubt, skip this method.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps: cleansing removes accumulated energy; recharging restores the stone's intended vibrational state. After cleansing citrine, practitioners typically place it:

- In brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes), noting the fading caution above
- Near a cluster of clear quartz, which is traditionally described as an amplifier
- On a dry selenite slab, which is used in crystal practice as a charging surface

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary on frequency. Common guidelines: cleanse citrine when it arrives, after any intense or emotionally charged use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice. Inspect the stone occasionally for any surface dullness, which is usually just dust or skin oils and will clear with a water rinse.
