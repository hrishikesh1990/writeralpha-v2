# How to Cleanse Ruby Zoisite Safely

Ruby Zoisite tolerates brief water rinsing — neither ruby nor zoisite reacts harmfully with plain water. Prolonged soaking and salt water should be avoided, as water can infiltrate the interfaces between the stone's component minerals. Smoke, sound, moonlight, and dry earth are all reliable cleansing options with no physical risk to the specimen.

## Water Rinsing: What Is and Is Not Safe

Ruby Zoisite is a composite rock made of green zoisite (Mohs 6–7), red corundum or ruby (Mohs 9), and black hornblende. Neither zoisite nor ruby dissolves in or reacts chemically with plain water, making the stone much more water-tolerant than reactive minerals such as pyrite or selenite. A brief rinse of 30 to 60 seconds under cool running water is safe for polished, intact specimens.

Prolonged soaking is best avoided. The stone's three-mineral composition creates interfaces — boundaries where different minerals meet — and extended water exposure allows moisture to seep into those boundaries over time, potentially weakening the matrix. Salt water carries an additional risk: salt crystals can lodge in micro-cracks at mineral boundaries and generate internal stress as they grow, which can cause surface fractures.

Avoid ultrasonic and steam cleaners. These can cause fractures along zoisite's perfect cleavage planes. For a detailed explanation of water safety, see [can Ruby Zoisite go in water](/gemstones/ruby-zoisite/can-go-in-water).

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the Ruby Zoisite piece in the smoke of burning herbs or incense for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating it so all surfaces are exposed. Smoke cleansing is the most widely used method among crystal practitioners and carries no physical risk to the stone. Ensure the space is ventilated.

### Sound

Place Ruby Zoisite near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass over it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone does not need to come into contact with any liquid or abrasive material. This method is particularly useful for larger carved specimens.

### Moonlight

Set Ruby Zoisite on a dry surface outdoors or on a windowsill on a clear night and leave it for several hours or overnight. Confirm beforehand that no dew, rain, or high humidity is forecast. Bring the stone indoors before morning condensation forms.

### Dry earth

Bury Ruby Zoisite in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or sand for 24 hours. Retrieve the stone, brush off any loose soil with a dry cloth, and allow it to air. Practitioners use this method for a deeper energetic reset. Use indoor or oven-dried soil rather than damp garden earth.

### Visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, breathe slowly, and exhale steadily across its surface while focusing on clearing any accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. No materials are needed, and the method leaves no physical residue.
