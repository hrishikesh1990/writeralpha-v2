# Angel Aura Quartz Price Guide

Angel Aura Quartz is made from widely available quartz and a vacuum vapor-deposition process, so it is not rare. Small tumbled stones and points typically sell for $5–60 per piece; palm-sized tumbles run $12–45; clusters with clean quartz and even coating reach $80–250 or more. Prices checked September 2026. Variation within these ranges reflects quartz quality, coating evenness, size, and form.

## What Drives the Price

Angel Aura Quartz pricing depends on two layered variables: the quality of the quartz underneath, and the quality of the coating applied to it.

**Quartz quality** covers clarity (how transparent or included the base crystal is), form (sharp natural terminations versus broken or stubby points, cluster architecture), size, and origin. Brazilian and Arkansas quartz are common high-quality base materials. A clean, well-formed point starts the price higher before any coating is applied.

**Coating quality** covers evenness (a uniform rainbow across all faces versus patchy, cloudy, or scorched-looking zones), adhesion (whether the film shows early wear at edges), and depth of iridescence (some pieces produce a richer, more saturated rainbow; others are pale or predominantly one-toned). Well-applied coatings command a premium because the vacuum deposition process rewards careful surface preparation and controlled conditions.

**Form** matters for price per piece:
- Small tumbled stones (under 3 cm): commonly $5–25
- Standard points or tumbles (palm-sized): commonly $12–45
- Large single points or statement pieces: commonly $30–80
- Clusters (multiple points on matrix): $80–250+, higher for large or museum-grade pieces

These are retail single-piece prices. Wholesale and bulk purchases are substantially lower, which is why pricing well below the ranges above is possible from certain sellers — but it also warrants checking whether the piece is genuine coated quartz or a glass or dyed imitation.

## What Does Not Affect the Price (But Should Not)

Metaphysical associations and the "Angel Aura" trade name add perceived value but are not quality-controlled. The name covers a range of coating recipes and quartz grades. A piece labeled "Angel Aura Quartz" at $50 is not necessarily better than one at $18 — the difference may be the seller's margin, packaging, branding, or the retail channel rather than any objective difference in the stone.

The Mohs hardness of 7 for the quartz substrate is a durability asset for wearable forms (pendants, earrings), but the metallic coating is softer and will show wear on rings and bracelets regardless of price. For a full account of what hardness means for daily wear, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale).

## How to Avoid Overpaying

1. **Establish what form you want** (tumbled, point, cluster) and compare across at least three sellers before buying.
2. **Examine coating quality.** Ask for photos in direct light from different angles. Patchy, cloudy, or one-toned pieces are lower quality regardless of marketing language.
3. **Verify it is quartz, not glass.** Glass imitations and dyed fakes are sometimes sold as Angel Aura Quartz at price points that still seem plausible. The [how to identify Angel Aura Quartz](/gemstones/angel-aura-quartz/how-to-identify) page covers the tests you can do on a piece before or after purchase.
4. **Be skeptical of very low prices.** Single tumbled stones at $1–3 from a seller who does not disclose the treatment process are a flag for glass or dyed material. Legitimate retail for a well-coated natural quartz tumble rarely falls below $5–8.
5. **Be skeptical of very high prices without proportionate quality.** Angel Aura Quartz is not rare. A $200 tumbled stone needs to be large, exceptionally clear, and have an unusually even coating to justify the price. Rarity claims and "healing grade" or "premium vibration" language are marketing, not quality criteria.
