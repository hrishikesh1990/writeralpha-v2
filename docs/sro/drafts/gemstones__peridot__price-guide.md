# Peridot Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Peridot ranges from $5 to roughly $400 per carat at retail in 2026, depending on color saturation, size, origin, and clarity. Commercial-grade stones at the low end are widely available and affordable; fine Pakistani or Burmese material at the upper end commands collector prices. Peridot is never heat-treated or irradiated, so what you buy is always natural.

## What Drives Peridot's Price

Color accounts for roughly 60 to 70 percent of a peridot stone's value. The most prized color is a vivid, pure grass green — sometimes described as lime green — with no yellow or brown undertones. Because peridot's color comes directly from its iron content rather than from trace impurities, it cannot be improved by heat treatment. Stones with higher iron content trend brownish or yellow and trade at significant discounts.

Size matters because color becomes more saturated and uniform in larger crystals. Stones under 3 carats often show a lighter, more yellowish tone that is normal for the mineral but less desirable in the trade. Fine vivid green is uncommon below 5 carats and genuinely rare above 10 carats.

Clarity is assessed by eye: most commercial peridot contains "lily pad" inclusions — small disk-shaped features around chromite crystals — that are visible under magnification. Eye-clean stones with no visible inclusions command a premium. Origin also affects price: Pakistani material (from the Kohistan and Kaghan Valley regions) is widely regarded as producing the finest color, while Arizona's San Carlos Reservation produces the majority of commercial supply at more accessible prices.

Peridot at 6.5 to 7 on the [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale) is moderately hard — durable enough for pendants and earrings, but requiring care in rings. This affects its value relative to harder stones like sapphire (9) or ruby (9).

## Retail Price Ranges (September 2026)

Prices below are retail ranges gathered from gem-trade sources as of mid-2026. Actual prices vary by seller, cut quality, and individual stone characteristics.

| Grade | Typical size | Color | Price per carat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial | Under 1 ct | Pale yellow-green | $5–$20 |
| Good / A | 1–3 ct | Medium yellow-green, eye-clean | $20–$65 |
| Fine / AA | 3–10 ct | Vivid lime green, minimal inclusions | $65–$150 |

For reference: a 2-carat fine green peridot at $65–$80/ct would retail for roughly $130–$160 for the stone alone, before setting. Calibrated beads and tumbled stones for crystal-healing use sell by the piece rather than by carat and are widely available for $2–$20 each.

## Treatments to Know About

Peridot is one of the few colored gemstones sold almost entirely untreated. The GIA states explicitly that no heat treatment for peridot is commercially known. Sellers who market peridot as "unheated" are technically accurate but making a meaningless claim — all peridot is unheated.

A small fraction of commercial stones with surface fractures may be oiled or resin-filled to improve apparent clarity. Ask any seller directly: "Has this stone been clarity-enhanced?" Reputable sellers will answer plainly. Synthetic peridot (lab-grown forsterite) exists but is uncommon at retail; it sells at a fraction of natural stone prices and should be disclosed.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

Inspect color in multiple lighting conditions. Peridot looks vivid under incandescent light; daylight and fluorescent light reveal yellow or brown undertones that photos often suppress. Examine the stone face-on for windowing — a pale, washed-out center visible when you tilt the stone — which indicates a shallow cut that wastes material and dulls the color.

Ask for the exact carat weight. Any reputable seller states a specific number (e.g., "2.47 ct"), not a range or estimate. For stones above $150 in total value, a report from GIA, AGL, or AGTA confirms identity and origin claims. Claims of "Egyptian peridot" are almost always Arizona material with a premium markup: Zabargad Island's ancient deposits are essentially exhausted, and genuine provenance requires documentation.
