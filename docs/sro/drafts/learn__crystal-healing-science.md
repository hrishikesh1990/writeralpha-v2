# Crystal Healing: What Does the Science Say?

No controlled study published in the scientific literature demonstrates that crystal healing produces effects beyond what can be explained by expectation and the placebo response. The most cited evidence comes from two studies led by psychologist Christopher French, presented at conferences in 1999 and 2001, which found no measurable difference between real and fake crystals in producing the sensations practitioners attribute to crystal energy.

## What the French Studies Found

Christopher French, head of the Anomalistic Psychology Research Unit at Goldsmiths, University of London, conducted two studies of crystal power in collaboration with student researchers.

**The first study** (French & Williams, presented at the European Congress of Psychology, Rome, 1999) recruited 80 volunteers. Half were primed by reading material describing the sensations they might expect — tingling, warmth, improved mood — while holding a crystal. All participants then held a crystal for five minutes and reported any sensations. Crucially, half the group within each condition held a genuine quartz crystal; the other half held a fake made of glass, identical in appearance. The results: the vast majority of participants reported at least one sensation. Primed participants reported stronger sensations than unprimed ones. Whether they held a real or fake crystal made no measurable difference to the sensations reported.

**The follow-up study** (French & O'Donnell, presented at the British Psychological Society Centenary Annual Conference, Glasgow, March 2001) used an improved double-blind methodology with another 80 volunteers. Sensations were again reported by most participants regardless of whether they held genuine or fake crystals. In this study, scores on the sensation index correlated with participants' level of paranormal belief and with their measured hypnotic susceptibility — both known markers of suggestibility — but were unaffected by whether the crystal was real or fake.

French's conclusion, stated at the 2001 conference, was direct: "The power of suggestion, either explicit or implicit, seems to be the not-so-mysterious power that may convince many that crystals have the potential to work miracles."

These studies were not published in peer-reviewed journals, which French has acknowledged as a significant limitation. He noted in 2021 that, as far as he could determine, no other researcher had empirically tested crystal healing efficacy since, which he considered "unfortunate" from a consumer protection standpoint.

## The Placebo Response: What It Actually Is

The French studies demonstrate that the sensations people report from crystals are consistent with expectation effects — but expectation effects are not trivial. The placebo response is a genuine physiological and psychological phenomenon, not simply self-deception.

Ted Kaptchuk, Professor of Medicine at Harvard Medical School and director of the Program in Placebo Studies, has published more than 100 papers on the subject. His research and others' show that placebo responses can produce measurable changes in pain perception, stress hormones, immune markers, and subjective wellbeing — even when participants are aware they are receiving a placebo in some conditions. A 2014 Kaptchuk study found that a placebo was approximately 50% as effective as real migraine medication at reducing post-migraine pain.

Applied to crystal use, this means: if holding a crystal while meditating, breathing slowly, and focusing attention on a positive intention produces reduced stress or a calmer state, those effects are real. The mechanism is the ritual, the attention, the breathing, and the expectation — not a property unique to the crystal.

## Why Many People Find Crystals Meaningful

The evidence position does not require dismissing the experiences of people who find value in crystal practice. Several well-understood mechanisms can explain why crystals may support wellbeing for some individuals:

**Mindfulness and focused attention.** Crystal practice typically involves pausing, handling an object, and directing attention — behaviors that overlap with mindfulness meditation, which has documented effects on stress and mood in controlled trials.

**Ritual and intention-setting.** Rituals that mark transitions, intentions, or commitments can influence behavior and mood regardless of whether they have supernatural properties. Selecting a crystal for a particular purpose, placing it intentionally, or returning to it regularly creates a behavioral anchor.

**Aesthetic and tactile experience.** Handling a visually appealing, textured object can be intrinsically calming. The sensory properties of crystals — weight, temperature, texture, color — are real, even if the "energy" attributed to them is not measurable.

**Therapeutic context.** Crystal healers often spend extended time with clients in an empathetic, attentive environment. The quality of that therapeutic encounter — listening, validating experience, offering structure — has documented benefits independently of any treatment applied.

None of these mechanisms requires crystals to emit measurable energy or to differ from one another in their physical effects on the body.

## What This Means Practically

Crystal use as a complementary practice — alongside, not instead of, medical care — does not carry known physical risks for most people and may provide genuine comfort through the mechanisms described above. Crystals are not a treatment for any medical condition. They should not be used as a replacement for medical diagnosis, prescription medication, or professional mental health care.

The main practical risk is financial: investment in high-priced stones on the basis of specific healing claims is not supported by evidence. A person who finds value in handling a piece of quartz during meditation is unlikely to find additional benefit from paying significantly more for a "higher-vibration" stone.

For practitioners and collectors who use crystals as part of a personal or spiritual practice, the honest position is that the stones offer real comfort to many people through well-understood psychological pathways, rather than through mechanisms science has failed to detect despite the opportunity to test them.
