# Who Should Not Wear Ruby — Cautions Explained

Ruby is physically safe to wear for almost everyone: it ranks 9 on the Mohs scale, has no cleavage, and is chemically stable under normal conditions. The meaningful cautions are practical (avoiding fracture-filled stones near acids or heat) and traditional — in Vedic astrology, ruby is linked to the Sun, and several ascendants are cautioned against it. No crystal or gemstone is a substitute for medical advice; see a qualified practitioner for any health concern.

## Physical Safety: Who Needs to Take Care

Ruby (corundum, Al2O3) is one of the most durable gemstones available. Untreated and heat-treated rubies tolerate warm soapy water, normal daily wear, and most environments without damage.

Two groups need to be more careful:

**People with fracture-filled or cavity-filled rubies.** A significant proportion of commercial rubies are fracture-filled with lead glass to improve apparent clarity. Mild acids — including lemon juice, vinegar, and some household cleaners — can etch and discolor the filling, permanently damaging the stone's appearance. If you own a filled ruby, avoid contact with acidic substances and do not use ultrasonic or steam cleaners. GIA notes that such stones should only be cleaned with a damp cloth.

**People who handle ruby dust or freshly cut material.** Corundum dust is an abrasive and a lung irritant. Lapidary work on ruby should always be performed with water cooling and respiratory protection. For finished, polished pieces worn as jewelry or held as specimens, there is no dust risk.

Children wearing small ruby pieces as loose stones rather than set jewelry present a choking hazard. Jewelry settings eliminate this risk.

## Vedic Astrology: Traditional Contraindications

In Vedic (Jyotish) astrology, ruby is the gemstone of the Sun (Surya). The tradition holds that wearing ruby amplifies the Sun's significations — authority, ego, vitality, leadership — whether they are well-placed or problematic in the birth chart. These are traditional beliefs, not verified outcomes.

The recommendation to wear or avoid ruby in Vedic tradition follows the ascendant (lagna) — the rising sign at birth — not the Western sun sign. Traditional guidance consistently cautions five ascendants:

| Ascendant | Traditional position | Traditional reason |
|---|---|---|
| Taurus (Vrishabha) | Generally avoid | Sun is a natural enemy of ascendant lord Venus |
| Virgo (Kanya) | Avoid without expert guidance | Sun rules the 12th house (losses) |
| Libra (Tula) | Generally avoid | Sun is debilitated in Libra; enemy of Venus |
| Capricorn (Makara) | Avoid | Sun rules the 8th house; enemy of Saturn |
| Aquarius (Kumbha) | Generally avoid | Sun rules the 7th house; enemy of Saturn |

Ascendants for which ruby is traditionally considered beneficial include Leo (Sun is lagna lord), Aries (Sun rules 5th), Sagittarius (Sun rules 9th), and Cancer (Sun rules 2nd).

These are traditional frameworks. Whether a specific person should wear ruby depends on the individual chart, the Sun's placement and strength, current planetary periods (dasha), and personal experience. Vedic tradition recommends a trial period of three to twenty-one days for any new gemstone, removing it if adverse effects appear.

## People Traditionally Advised to Exercise Caution

Several groups are traditionally counseled to consult a qualified astrologer before wearing ruby:

**People prone to anger, aggressive behavior, or high blood pressure.** Ruby is held in tradition to amplify Solar energy, which includes assertiveness and heat. If these qualities are already pronounced, practitioners advise caution. This is a belief-based concern with no clinical basis; however, if ruby causes increased agitation in practice, removing it is reasonable.

**People with a weakened or afflicted Sun in the birth chart.** If the Sun is debilitated (placed in Libra), combust, or placed in a dusthana house (6th, 8th, or 12th) without offsetting factors, Vedic tradition suggests the amplification from ruby may express the Sun's challenging dimensions rather than its constructive ones.

**Children under 12 and pregnant women.** Traditional Vedic practice considers high-carat ruby too intense for children whose chart is still forming, and advises against starting new gemstone protocols during pregnancy. Wearing an existing piece of ruby jewelry is generally treated differently from deliberately adopting the stone for astrological purposes.

## Western Tradition

In Western crystal practice, ruby is associated with passion, courage, and vitality and is not generally subject to specific contraindications. Its association with the fire element means some practitioners suggest that people who already feel overwhelmed, overheated, or prone to anxiety try gentler stones first — but this is a personal-practice suggestion, not a rule. See [ruby meaning](/gemstones/ruby/meaning) for the full range of traditional associations.

## Practical Guidance

If you are buying ruby primarily as jewelry or a display stone, the physical cautions above are all that apply: avoid fracture-filled stones in environments with acids or ultrasonic cleaners, and keep loose stones away from small children.

If you are adopting ruby for astrological or energetic purposes, Vedic tradition recommends consulting a qualified astrologer first, particularly if your ascendant is among those listed above. Crystal healing is a complementary practice, not a medical treatment.
