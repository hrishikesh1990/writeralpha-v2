# Botswana Agate Price Guide

Botswana Agate is an accessible semi-precious stone. Small cabochons sell at retail for roughly $2–10 per piece depending on size and grade; tumbled stones are in a similar range. Large, fine-pattern collector pieces or high-grade jewelry cabs command more. The price is driven primarily by banding quality, size, and whether the material is natural or dyed — not by rarity, since Botswana Agate is commercially available in steady supply.

## Typical Retail Prices

The following ranges are based on retail listings found from late 2024 to early 2025 at specialty gem dealers. Prices are per piece, not per carat — Botswana Agate is primarily sold by piece or by strand rather than by weight.

| Form | Size | Price range (retail) |
|---|---|---|
| Small cabochon | 5 mm round | $2–4 per piece |
| Medium cabochon | 12 mm coin | $5–8 per piece |
| Larger cabochon | 13×18 mm oval, A-grade | $8–12 per piece |
| Tumbled stone | approx. 15–30 mm | $2–8 per piece |
| Bead strand | 8 mm rounds, 15–16 inch strand | $8–20 per strand |
| Raw/rough piece | palm-size | $3–15 depending on pattern |

Prices checked as of September 2026 against evidence from 2024–2025 retail sources; market prices vary by seller, grade, and availability.

## What Drives the Price

**Banding definition** is the most important quality factor. Botswana Agate's value comes almost entirely from its pattern: tight, clearly defined parallel bands in a well-contrasted palette command a premium over specimens with muddy, washed-out, or irregular banding.

**Color palette.** The characteristic gray-pink-peach-white palette in natural material is what distinguishes Botswana Agate from generic agates. Pieces with a strong warm pink or apricot tone alongside clean gray are more sought-after in the trade.

**Size.** Larger cabochons expose more of the banding and are harder to cut without including low-quality zones, so they command higher prices per piece. A well-matched set of large cabochons for a statement piece is priced above individual small stones.

**Treatment status.** Natural, untreated Botswana Agate is the standard in the trade — and what buyers expect. Dyed material, if not disclosed, is misrepresented. Disclosed dyed pieces should be priced below natural equivalents. See [how to identify Botswana Agate](/gemstones/botswana-agate/how-to-identify) to tell them apart.

**Form and finish.** Polished, well-cut cabochons cost more than rough material of equivalent size. Wire-wrapped or set jewelry adds significant labor cost above raw stone prices.

**Origin verification.** Material that can be verified as genuinely from Botswana (as opposed to Brazilian gray agate relabeled in the supply chain) may be priced at a modest premium from careful dealers, though this is rarely formally certified at the retail level for decorative agate.

## Mohs Hardness and Value

Botswana Agate's Mohs hardness of 6.5–7 makes it durable enough for any jewelry application, including rings and bracelets with daily wear. This durability does not strongly affect price at retail levels — the stone is not priced for hardness but for pattern — but it is a genuine quality that supports its use in long-wear pieces. For how hardness relates to durability and value across stones, see the [Mohs hardness scale guide](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale).

## How to Avoid Overpaying

- **Compare by unit.** Get a per-piece or per-strand price from multiple sellers before buying, especially online. Pricing varies widely for the same grade.
- **Ask for treatment disclosure.** Natural and dyed material should not be the same price. A seller who cannot answer is a risk.
- **Assess banding in natural light.** Photographs taken under artificial light can misrepresent tone and contrast. Ask for natural-light photos or buy from dealers with good return policies.
- **Recognize the palette.** Vivid, saturated banding is a red flag for dye. Natural Botswana Agate is quiet and muted. Paying natural-stone prices for dyed material is the most common overpayment scenario.
- **Source from reputable dealers.** Dealers who specify origin (Botswana/Bobonong region), treatment status, and grade offer more accountability than unspecified bulk sellers.
