# How to Identify Cherry Quartz and Spot Fakes

Cherry Quartz is man-made glass — it is not a natural mineral. This changes the identification question: you are not distinguishing a natural stone from a fake, but rather identifying quality, composition, and whether a seller is misrepresenting the material. Genuine Cherry Quartz is silica-based glass with a distinctive translucent-to-opaque cherry-red or pink color; common substitutes are lower-quality resin or ordinary dyed glass sold under the same name.

## What "Real" Cherry Quartz Actually Means

Cherry Quartz is sold legitimately as man-made glass. It is produced by melting silica and adding coloring agents — traditionally cinnabar (mercury sulfide), though most commercial production today uses synthetic red dyes because they are cheaper and avoid any mercury-handling issues. The finished material has an approximate Mohs hardness of 7, a conchoidal (shell-like) fracture, vitreous luster, and a specific gravity of approximately 2.65.

A piece can be legitimately described as Cherry Quartz if it is silica-based glass with the characteristic pink-to-red coloration. What is sometimes sold under the same name — and represents poorer value — is lower-density plastic resin, painted stone, or poorly colored glass that chips or scratches easily. ## Visual and Physical Tests You Can Do at Home

### Color and pattern

Genuine Cherry Quartz has a consistent cherry-red to pink color throughout, often with visible swirls, plumes, or cloud-like internal patterns. The coloration is diffuse within the glass, not sitting on the surface as a coating. If the color looks painted on, rubs off, or appears only in a thin outer layer visible at chips or scratches, the piece is dyed or coated rather than through-colored glass.

### Hardness scratch test

Glass with a Mohs hardness of approximately 7 will scratch most metals, ceramic, and porcelain. Cherry Quartz should scratch the unglazed back of a ceramic tile. A piece that scratches easily with a copper coin (Mohs 3) or a steel key (Mohs 5–6.5) is likely resin or low-quality soft glass rather than silica glass. Use this test on an inconspicuous edge of the piece, not on its face.

### Temperature feel

Glass and stone feel cold to the touch and warm slowly in the hand. Plastic resin warms quickly and never feels as cold initially as glass does. Hold the piece in your palm for 10 seconds: genuine silica glass should feel noticeably cool at first contact.

### Weight

Cherry Quartz glass has a specific gravity of approximately 2.65, similar to quartz. It should feel heavier than a piece of clear plastic of the same size. A piece that feels unusually light is likely resin.

### Bubbles and inclusions

Silica glass often contains small air bubbles or flow lines visible under magnification. These are normal manufacturing artifacts. Resin fakes tend to show different internal structures — denser uniform color or air pockets with a different character. Bubbles in genuine glass are rounded and evenly distributed; resin tends to show larger, irregular voids.

### Conchoidal fracture

If you examine a chip or the edge of a piece, genuine glass shows a conchoidal (shell-like, curved) fracture surface. Resin fractures more irregularly and may show a rougher, less smooth edge.

## Red Flags When Buying

- Prices significantly below the normal range for glass-quality pieces (see [Cherry Quartz price guide](/gemstones/cherry-quartz/price-guide) for typical retail ranges).
- Sellers describing Cherry Quartz as a "natural" or "mined" stone — it is not. It is always man-made.
- Uniform, intense color with no internal variation or pattern.
- A piece that scratches easily or feels warm immediately on first touch.
- No information about origin or manufacturer.

## What Cherry Quartz Is Not

Cherry Quartz is not the same as natural strawberry quartz (a naturally occurring quartz variety with hematite or goethite inclusions), red jasper, or carnelian. All three are natural minerals with different physical properties, identification features, and price points. Sellers occasionally mislabel these materials, or mislabel Cherry Quartz as a natural stone. The key distinguishing factor is always origin: Cherry Quartz is manufactured, never mined.
