# How to Identify Real Blue Tiger's Eye

Real Blue Tiger's Eye is a quartz mineral with chatoyancy — a moving band of reflected light — caused by parallel fibrous inclusions locked inside the stone. The key tests are chatoyancy depth and direction of movement, weight relative to size, visible fibrous texture under magnification, and temperature. Glass and dyed imitations fail at least one of these. Dyed regular quartz passes some tests but lacks the fibrous depth of genuine hawk's eye.

## What Real Blue Tiger's Eye Looks Like

Blue Tiger's Eye, also called hawk's eye, is a variety of tiger's eye in which the crocidolite fibers were only partially replaced by quartz, preserving the original blue-gray to blue-green color rather than developing the golden-brown of classic tiger's eye. Authentic material shows:

- **Chatoyancy**: When you rotate a cabochon or tumbled stone under a single light source, a bright, silky band of light moves across the surface. The effect is three-dimensional — the band appears to sit inside the stone, not on its surface.
- **Silky, fibrous texture**: Hold the stone at an angle in good light. You should see parallel, fine fiber-like lines running beneath the surface. Under a 10x loupe, these are distinctly linear, not granular or random.
- **Color**: Genuine material ranges from steel blue to blue-gray with subtle hints of gold or green depending on how much iron oxidation occurred. The color is muted and layered, not uniformly flat.
- **Temperature**: Natural quartz takes time to warm up in your hand. Hold it for 30 seconds — it should feel noticeably cool at first and warm slowly.

## Step-by-Step Identification

1. **Test chatoyancy under a point source.** Use a single light — a phone flashlight or a lamp with a small, bright bulb. Move the stone side to side. A real stone shows a distinct, moving band. Glass can show reflectivity but the band is flat and surface-level, not deep.

2. **Check the weight.** Blue Tiger's Eye has a specific gravity of 2.64–2.71. Hold a piece you know to be genuine alongside the suspect piece. Genuine quartz feels noticeably denser than glass of the same volume. Glass and acrylic are usually lighter (glass SG ~2.5, acrylic ~1.2).

3. **Look for fibrous depth with a loupe.** At 10x magnification, real Blue Tiger's Eye shows aligned fibrous inclusions running in one direction. Glass shows bubbles, swirls, or a completely homogeneous interior.

4. **Check the temperature response.** Place the stone on your bare inner wrist for 10 seconds. Real quartz holds cold longer than glass or resin. It warms up slowly and evenly; glass warms faster.

5. **Look at the color distribution.** Real material has color variation following the fiber direction — stripes and variations are organic and follow the internal structure. Dyed imitations often show color concentrated in surface pits, cracks, and grain boundaries, or a uniformly saturated hue with no variation.

6. **Check the price as a signal.** A very low price ($1–2 for a bracelet, for example) should prompt closer inspection. While genuine Blue Tiger's Eye is not expensive in absolute terms, extremely cheap material is more likely to be dyed glass or synthetic. ## Common Imitations and How They Fail

| Imitation | How it mimics real | How to spot the difference |
|---|---|---|
| Blue dyed glass | Silky appearance, similar color | No chatoyancy depth; bubbles under loupe; warms faster |
| Dyed regular quartz | Similar weight and temperature | No fibrous texture; color in surface cracks; no chatoyancy |
| Synthetic cat's eye glass | Has a chatoyant band | Band is always centered, flat; no fibrous inclusions under loupe |
| Blue-dyed tiger's eye | Has chatoyancy and fiber structure | Color in cracks and surface; may bleed color in warm water |
| Labradorite (sometimes mislabeled) | Blue-gray color | Shows labradorescence (color in broad patches), not chatoyancy |

## Confirming with Gemological Tests

If you have ongoing concerns about a significant purchase, a gemologist can confirm identification with:

- **Refractive index**: Blue Tiger's Eye shows RI of approximately 1.544–1.553, matching quartz.
- **Spectroscope**: Can identify dye absorption bands not present in natural material.
- **Specific gravity measurement**: Float test in heavy liquid or water displacement confirms SG within the quartz range.

For most buyers purchasing tumbled stones or beaded bracelets, the chatoyancy test combined with the loupe check and temperature response is sufficient.
