# Red Agate Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Red Agate is one of the more affordable gemstones in the crystal market. Tumbled pieces typically sell for $1 to $5 each, small carved shapes and palm stones range from $6 to $30, and larger freeforms or spheres run $20 to $100 depending on size and color quality. Retail prices checked in September 2026 confirm Red Agate sits firmly in the accessible tier. Treatment disclosure, color depth, and polish quality are the main value drivers.

## Typical Price Ranges by Form

| Form | Typical retail range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tumbled stones | $1–$5 per piece | Most common form; dyed and heat-treated both available |
| Bead strands (15–16 in.) | $6–$8 per strand | 33–95 beads per strand depending on size (4mm–10mm) |
| Small carved shapes | $6–$15 each | Hearts, points, spheres under 3 cm |
| Palm stones | $8–$30 each | Price rises with size and color uniformity |
| Larger freeforms | $20–$60 | Rough or lightly polished |
| Larger spheres and points | $30–$100+ | Size and color quality-dependent |

Prices vary by seller, origin, and grade. These ranges represent common retail prices in the US market as of September 2026; wholesale and bulk buying reduce per-unit cost considerably.

## What Drives Red Agate Prices

**Color depth and saturation.** Deeper, more uniform red commands higher prices. The richest red tones require either high-quality iron-bearing rough or heat treatment, and both cost more to source and process. Very deeply saturated red at very low prices ($1 or less per tumbled piece) sometimes signals dyed agate with poor treatment disclosure rather than naturally rich material.

**Treatment status and disclosure.** This is the most important value variable specific to Red Agate. Naturally colored Red Agate — where the red comes from iron oxide inclusions without additional treatment — is rarer and commands a premium. Heat-treated material is genuine Red Agate with deepened color; it is widely available and fairly priced. Dyed material can be attractive and affordable but should be priced accordingly and disclosed clearly. Paying natural-material prices for undisclosed dyed agate is the main way buyers overpay.

**Size.** Larger pieces require better quality rough and more lapidary work, and larger polished specimens are simply rarer. Price scales upward with size in all categories.

**Polish and finish quality.** Well-polished material with no chips, scratches, or uneven color shows significantly better in jewelry settings and commands higher prices than lesser-grade material sold in bulk.

**Origin.** Origin affects price at the margins. Brazilian heat-treated material and Zimbabwean Red Agate dominate the commercial market and are priced competitively. Indian material from Gujarat is also widely available. Provenance from specific deposits rarely adds meaningful value at retail except in collector markets.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Ask for treatment disclosure.** Any reputable seller can tell you whether a piece is naturally colored, heat-treated, or dyed. If they cannot, that is a gap in their supply chain knowledge. Buying undisclosed material at natural-stone prices is the most common route to overpaying.

**Compare color to price.** Suspiciously uniform, deeply saturated red at prices below $1 to $2 per tumbled stone typically indicates bulk-dyed material from opaque supply chains. This is not necessarily a bad purchase for decorative purposes, but it should not carry a premium.

**Check polish and surface condition.** Surface chips, uneven polish, and inclusions visible on the surface reduce the stone's utility in jewelry and should be reflected in lower prices.

**Know the Mohs hardness context.** Red Agate's hardness of 6.5 to 7 (for full context on hardness and what it means for durability, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale)) means it is suitable for all jewelry types, including rings — which increases its value relative to softer stones.

For guidance on identifying genuine vs. dyed or imitation material, see [how to identify Red Agate](/gemstones/red-agate/how-to-identify). For sourcing considerations that go beyond price, see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing).

## Is Red Agate Good Value?

At its price point, Red Agate is genuinely good value: it is durable, widely available, attractive in a range of settings, and historically significant as a worked gem material. The main caveat is the disclosure issue. Buyers who understand the treatment landscape — and buy from sellers who disclose it — consistently get what they pay for.
