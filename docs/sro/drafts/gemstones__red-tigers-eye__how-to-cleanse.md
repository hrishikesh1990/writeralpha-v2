# How to Cleanse Red Tiger's Eye Without Damaging It

Red Tiger's Eye can be cleansed safely with smoke, sound, moonlight, dry soil burial, or a brief rinse under cool running water. The main physical caution is heat: the red color in most commercial Red Tiger's Eye comes from iron oxide produced by heat treatment, and sustained heat or prolonged direct sunlight can alter or fade the color over time. Extended soaking is also unnecessary and best avoided.

## What Makes Red Tiger's Eye Vulnerable

Red Tiger's Eye is a quartz variety (Mohs hardness 7) with subparallel iron-oxide-rich fibers that create its chatoyant shimmer and red color. Most Red Tiger's Eye sold commercially has been heat-treated: heating yellow-brown tiger's eye oxidizes the limonite in the fibers to hematite, shifting the color to red or red-brown.

Because the color depends on an iron oxide that was changed by heat, two things can theoretically affect it:

- **Sustained high heat or direct sunlight for prolonged periods** can continue altering the iron minerals, causing gradual color change or fading.
- **Harsh chemical cleaners** can etch the polish on quartz-based stones and should be avoided regardless of the stone's composition.

Brief water exposure carries no risk — quartz is stable in water at ambient temperatures. The full water-safety question is covered at [can Red Tiger's Eye go in water](/gemstones/red-tigers-eye/can-go-in-water).

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the Red Tiger's Eye piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating it so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is one of the most widely used methods in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to the stone. Ensure ventilation for the room; you are breathing the smoke too.

### Sound

Place Red Tiger's Eye beside or inside a singing bowl, ring a bell nearby, or hold a tuning fork close to the stone and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing requires no physical contact with the stone and is especially useful for multiple pieces at once or for polished spheres and carvings where surface contact is undesirable.

### Moonlight

Set Red Tiger's Eye on a dry windowsill or an outdoor surface on a clear night and leave it in moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity may leave moisture on the stone. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms. Full moon nights are traditional in crystal practice for a combined cleanse-and-recharge.

### Dry soil burial

Bury Red Tiger's Eye in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or clean sand for 24 hours. Dig a shallow hole, place the stone, cover it, and retrieve it after a day. Brush off loose soil with a dry cloth. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp. This method is used in traditions that associate earth burial with a deep physical grounding reset.

### Brief running water

Hold the stone under cool running tap water for 30 to 60 seconds, then dry it immediately and completely with a soft cloth. Do not soak it. This is adequate for removing surface dust or handling residue and is safe given the quartz base at Mohs 7. For a longer explanation of water safety, see [can Red Tiger's Eye go in water](/gemstones/red-tigers-eye/can-go-in-water).

### Breath and visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, take a slow breath, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This needs no materials and is used as a quick reset between sessions or when other methods are unavailable.

## Methods to Avoid

**Prolonged direct sunlight:** A brief few minutes while drying poses no known risk. Setting the stone on a south-facing windowsill for hours daily over weeks may gradually fade or alter the heat-treated color. For recharging after cleansing, morning sunlight for 15 to 30 minutes is a common guideline; avoid multi-hour sun sessions.

**Salt water or dry salt burial:** Salt does not chemically attack quartz, but leaving salt against the surface of a polished stone can scratch softer settings or string material in jewelry, and salt is hygroscopic — it draws ambient moisture, which then sits against the stone. Dry salt burial is unnecessary when soil or sound cleansing work equally well.

**Steam cleaners or ultrasonic cleaners:** These can loosen adhesive in set pieces and are not suitable for stones with included fibrous materials, even at Mohs 7.

Crystal traditions treat cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing Red Tiger's Eye, common recharging options include setting it on a dry selenite slab, near a cluster of clear quartz, or in brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes). ## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary widely on this. A common guideline is to cleanse new pieces when they arrive, after heavy intentional use, and once a month for stones in regular display or wear. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice.
