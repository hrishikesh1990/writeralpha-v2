# Can Peach Moonstone Go in Water? Safety and Cleaning Guide

Peach Moonstone can briefly contact plain water without being dissolved or chemically damaged — feldspar (KAlSi3O8) does not react with water the way pyrite or selenite does. The real risk is physical: its two planes of perfect cleavage mean prolonged soaking, salt water, or thermal shock from steam can infiltrate and widen hairline fractures in the stone. A short rinse under cool running water is safe; soaking overnight or using salt water is not.

## Why Peach Moonstone Is Not Fully Water-Safe

[Peach moonstone](/gemstones/peach-moonstone) is a variety of orthoclase feldspar with Mohs hardness 6–6.5 and two directions of perfect cleavage at approximately right angles. The cleavage is the key issue. In nature and in cutting, cleavage planes produce flat, smooth breaks — they are structural weak points that run through the stone. Even in a specimen that looks solid, microscopic separations along these planes are common.

When water enters these separations and then evaporates, it can deposit dissolved minerals or cause repeated expansion-contraction cycles that gradually widen the fracture. This is not a rapid process in plain water, but repeated soaking accelerates it. A solid, well-cut cabochon with no visible cracks handles a brief rinse without problem; a stone with visible inclusions or surface cracks should be wiped with a damp cloth rather than rinsed at all.

The stone's hardness of 6–6.5 also means sand, grit, or salt particles in the water can abrade the polish over time.

## What Happens in Different Types of Water

| Water type | Risk to peach moonstone | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Plain cool tap water, brief rinse | Low — generally safe | Keep under 30 seconds; dry immediately |
| Bottled or distilled water, brief | Low — generally safe | Marginally lower mineral deposit risk |
| Salt water (sea water, salt bowl) | Moderate — not recommended | Salt is mildly abrasive; salt crystals can deposit in cracks |
| Prolonged soaking (any water) | Moderate to high | Infiltrates cleavage planes over time |
| Hot water or steam | High — avoid | Thermal expansion propagates along cleavage; steam cleaners can split cabochons |
| Ultrasonic cleaner | High — avoid | Vibration travels along cleavage planes |

## How to Clean Peach Moonstone Physically

For surface dirt, fingerprints, or oil: wipe with a soft, lint-free cloth slightly dampened with plain cool water. If more cleaning is needed, use a very small amount of mild, non-detergent soap on the cloth — not directly on the stone. Wipe gently, then wipe again with a clean damp cloth to remove any soap residue, and immediately dry with a soft cloth. This is the standard care method recommended for feldspar gems in gem-trade practice.

Do not drop moonstone into a bowl of water to soak. Do not use jewelry cleaning dips, ultrasonic baths, or steam jets, all of which are specifically contraindicated for feldspar gems due to cleavage risk.

For ritual or energetic cleansing methods that avoid water entirely, see [how to cleanse peach moonstone](/gemstones/peach-moonstone/how-to-cleanse).

## Why Salt Water Is a Special Concern

Salt-water soaking is a popular crystal-cleansing practice, but it is not appropriate for peach moonstone. Two mechanisms work against it. First, dissolved salts are mildly abrasive and can pit or dull the surface polish of a 6–6.5 Mohs stone over repeated exposure. Second, when salt water evaporates from inside a micro-crack, it deposits salt crystals. As those crystals grow, they exert pressure on the crack walls — the same process that splits rock faces in cold climates. Even a single soak carries some risk in a stone with existing hairline fractures.

For guidance on which crystals can safely go in salt water, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## Sun and Light After Washing

If you do rinse peach moonstone, dry it fully before placing it in sunlight. Wet feldspar surfaces in strong sunlight can experience slight thermal stress — not a severe risk, but unnecessary. Brief indirect sunlight poses no fading risk to the peach body color, which comes from trace mineral impurities in the feldspar structure rather than an organic dye. For extended sun charging, practitioners often limit exposure to 15–30 minutes of morning sun out of caution. See [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals) for broader context.
