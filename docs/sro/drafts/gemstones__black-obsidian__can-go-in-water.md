# Can Black Obsidian Go in Water?

Black obsidian can tolerate brief water contact — a quick rinse under running water will not harm a polished specimen. Prolonged soaking is not recommended: obsidian is a volcanic glass with a hardness of only 5 to 5.5 on the Mohs scale, and extended immersion can dull a polished surface and increase the risk of chipping along its conchoidal fracture edges.

## Why Black Obsidian Is Relatively Water-Tolerant

Black obsidian is an amorphous volcanic glass composed primarily of silica, formed when lava cools rapidly. Unlike iron-sulfide minerals such as pyrite, obsidian contains no chemically reactive components that break down on contact with water. It also has no cleavage planes and no soluble salts that dissolve or expand when wet. A brief rinse — 30 seconds to a couple of minutes under cool running water — is therefore generally safe for most polished tumbled stones or cabochons.

The principal physical risks of water exposure are mechanical, not chemical:

- **Hardness**: At 5 to 5.5 Mohs, obsidian is soft enough to scratch and abrade. Mineral content in tap water or sediment in natural water can scratch the surface over repeated exposures.
- **Conchoidal fracture**: Obsidian breaks along smooth curved surfaces with very sharp edges. Thermal shock from very cold or very hot water, or a knock while wet, can chip a specimen.
- **Surface polish**: Prolonged soaking softens and can lift residue from any wax or resin coating applied by sellers, and can gradually dull a high polish.

## Salt Water and Saltwater Baths

Salt water is more abrasive than plain water and is not recommended for black obsidian. Salt crystals drying on a glassy surface can scratch it, and the combination of salt and moisture can accelerate minor surface degradation over repeated cycles. For guidance on which stones tolerate salt, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## Safe Alternatives to Water Cleansing

If you prefer not to use water, several dry methods work without any physical risk to the specimen. Smoke cleansing, sound (singing bowl or bell), moonlight on a dry surface, and breath are all commonly used in crystal practice. For a full list of methods suited to black obsidian, see [how to cleanse Black Obsidian](/gemstones/black-obsidian/how-to-cleanse).

## Practical Cleaning Guidelines

| Method | Safe? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brief rinse (30–60 sec, cool water) | Yes | Pat dry immediately; avoid prolonged contact |
| Soaking (30+ min) | No | Risks dulling polish; avoid |
| Salt water | No | Abrasive; can scratch glass surface |
| Dishwasher or hot water | No | Thermal shock can chip specimen |
| Dry cloth wipe | Yes | Preferred for daily dust removal |
| Soft brush, damp | Yes | For crevices on raw pieces; dry promptly |

After any water contact, dry the piece gently with a soft cloth and allow it to air dry fully before storage. Keep finished obsidian pieces away from prolonged humidity, not because of chemical risk but because sustained moisture can affect any wax or oil coating applied to enhance luster.
