# How to Identify Real Lepidolite

Real lepidolite is soft enough to scratch with a fingernail (Mohs 2.5–3), shows a pearly or silky mica sheen on polished surfaces, and often reveals a layered sheet-like structure on broken or unpolished faces. Dyed purple micas, charoite, sugilite, and glass all fail at least one of these tests. A step-by-step process follows.

## The Core Physical Properties to Test

Genuine lepidolite is a lithium-bearing mica with formula K(Li,Al)3(Si,Al)4O10(F,OH)2. Its properties are specific and testable without any specialist equipment:

| Property | Genuine Lepidolite | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mohs hardness | 2.5–3 | Scratches with fingernail; a copper coin scratches it easily |
| Cleavage | Perfect basal | Splits into flat sheets on broken edges |
| Luster | Pearly to silky on polished faces | Vitreous (glassy) on fresh breaks |
| Color | Pink to purple | Even tone or gentle gradation; no sudden color edges |
| Specific gravity | 2.8–2.9 | Feels light for its volume |
| Transparency | Opaque | No see-through areas |

## Step-by-Step Identification

### Step 1: Test the hardness

Press a fingernail firmly against an inconspicuous edge or base of the stone. Genuine lepidolite scratches. If the stone resists, it is harder than Mohs 2.5 and is not lepidolite — it may be a dyed mica (typically Mohs 3–4), charoite (Mohs 5–6), or sugilite (Mohs 6–6.5). This single test eliminates most substitutes immediately.

### Step 2: Look for mica texture

Examine the surface under natural light. Genuine lepidolite shows a characteristic pearly or silky sheen, sometimes with visible layered plates or a fine sparkle from the mica structure. On broken or unpolished areas, you should see flat cleavage planes rather than a rough or conchoidal fracture. If the surface looks uniformly glassy or matte with no trace of layering, the material is likely glass, resin-stabilized stone, or a non-mica substitute.

### Step 3: Check the color distribution

Real lepidolite's pink-to-purple color comes from lithium, iron, manganese, and rubidium within the crystal structure. The color is inherent to the material throughout, not painted or applied. On dyed stones, color tends to concentrate in surface pits and cracks while the underlying material is lighter or colorless. Wipe the stone with a slightly damp white cloth and check whether any color transfers — genuine lepidolite does not dye a cloth.

### Step 4: Assess weight and feel

Lepidolite has a specific gravity of 2.8–2.9, which makes it light relative to its volume — noticeably lighter than glass of the same size (glass specific gravity approximately 2.5, though some types approach 3). Sugilite is denser. The stone also feels slightly slippery on polished faces, characteristic of mica minerals.

### Step 5: Check the price

Genuine lepidolite commands a minimum market price that reflects careful hand-finishing (the softness makes polishing difficult and lowers yield). Tumbled pieces at $1–$5 each strongly suggest dyed mica or substitute material. See [lepidolite price guide](/gemstones/lepidolite/price-guide) for current retail ranges. A price far below the market floor is a reliable warning sign.

## Common Imitations and How to Distinguish Them

**Dyed purple mica.** Feels harder than genuine lepidolite on the scratch test. Color may transfer to a damp cloth or fingers. Lacks lepidolite's characteristic lithium-pink hue — dyed material often looks artificially vivid.

**Charoite.** Mohs 5–6, resists scratching. Has a distinctive swirling or fibrous purple-and-black pattern. Under magnification, shows interlocking fibrous structure absent in lepidolite. Does not show mica sheen.

**Sugilite.** Mohs 6–6.5, much harder. Opaque pink-purple but denser and without mica layering. Does not scratch under fingernail. Tends to be more uniformly purple without the lilac-pink gradation common in lepidolite.

**Glass or resin.** Conchoid fracture on broken surfaces rather than flat cleavage planes. Uniformly glassy appearance without mica sparkle. Feels noticeably heavier than genuine lepidolite of the same size.
