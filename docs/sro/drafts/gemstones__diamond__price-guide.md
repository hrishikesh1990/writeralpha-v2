# Diamond Price Guide: What Drives Cost and How to Buy Well

A 1 carat round brilliant diamond graded G-H color and VS2-SI1 clarity with an Excellent cut typically retails between $3,800 and $7,800 in 2026. A D-color, flawless 1 ct stone can exceed $15,000. Prices are driven by the 4Cs (cut, color, clarity, carat weight), shape, certification, and whether the stone is natural or laboratory-grown. Lab-grown diamonds sell for 70–85% less than comparable naturals at current market rates.

## How Diamond Pricing Works

Diamonds are priced per carat (1 carat = 0.2 grams). The price per carat is not fixed — it increases non-linearly with carat weight because larger gem-quality diamonds are exponentially rarer. A 2 ct diamond of the same quality does not cost twice as much per carat as a 1 ct stone; it costs considerably more.

The Rapaport Diamond Report, published weekly, is the primary benchmark used by the trade. Retail prices are negotiated as a discount or premium to the Rapaport list. Actual consumer prices vary by retailer, location, and individual stone characteristics.

## The 4Cs and Their Effect on Price

### Cut

Cut is the single largest driver of visual quality and affects price significantly. GIA grades round brilliants from Excellent to Poor. An Excellent cut commands a premium of 15–25% over a Good cut of identical carat weight, color, and clarity. Cut determines how light moves through the stone — a poorly cut diamond of high color and clarity will appear dull compared to a well-cut stone of average grades.

### Color

GIA grades diamond color from D (colorless) to Z (light yellow or brown). The jump from colorless (D-F) to near-colorless (G-J) is the most significant price step for most buyers. Near-colorless stones (G-H) appear colorless to most observers in face-up position and represent strong value in the consumer market. The premium for D-F over G-H color can be 40–100% depending on size.

### Clarity

GIA clarity grades range from Flawless (FL) to Included (I3). For everyday jewelry use, VS2 and SI1 stones are typically eye-clean — inclusions are not visible to the naked eye. A jump from SI1 to VVS2 can add 20–40% to price while producing no visible difference.

### Carat weight

Prices jump at milestone weights — 0.50 ct, 1.00 ct, 1.50 ct, 2.00 ct — because demand concentrates at these round numbers. A 0.97 ct stone of the same quality as a 1.00 ct stone often costs noticeably less per carat with virtually no visible size difference face-up.

## Retail Price Ranges by Size (Natural Diamonds, 2026)

The following figures are illustrative retail price ranges for GIA-certified round brilliant diamonds with Excellent cut, checked against online retail data in September 2026. Actual prices vary by retailer and specific stone.

| Carat | G-H, VS2-SI1 | G-H, SI1 | D-F, VS1+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 ct | $1,200–$3,200 | $900–$2,400 | $1,800–$4,500 |
| 1.00 ct | $3,800–$7,800 | $3,200–$6,200 | $8,000–$16,000 |
| 1.50 ct | $7,500–$15,500 | $6,500–$12,500 | $16,000–$30,000 |
| 2.00 ct | $14,000–$32,000 | $11,000–$24,000 | $30,000–$60,000 |

Prices for fancy shapes (oval, cushion, pear, princess, emerald) are typically 20–40% lower than round brilliants of the same quality specification, because round brilliants have higher consumer demand and require more rough stone wastage during cutting.

## Natural vs. Laboratory-Grown Diamonds

Laboratory-grown diamonds are chemically and physically identical to natural diamonds — both are pure carbon, Mohs 10, with the same refractive index and optical properties. Lab-grown diamonds are produced by HPHT (high-pressure, high-temperature) or CVD (chemical vapor deposition) processes. In 2026, lab-grown diamonds retail at approximately 70–85% below comparable natural diamonds. A 1 ct G-H, VS2 lab-grown round may be available for $800–$2,000.

The difference matters in resale: natural diamonds retain more resale value. For buyers focused on crystal practice or personal use, lab-grown diamonds offer the same physical and optical properties at a fraction of the cost. To understand how to distinguish natural from lab-grown, see [how to identify real diamond](/gemstones/diamond/how-to-identify) and [lab-created vs. natural gemstones](/compare/lab-created-vs-natural-gemstones).

## Diamond for Crystal Practice

Diamonds used in crystal practice — loose rough chips, small uncut stones, or low-clarity faceted stones — occupy a very different price tier from jewelry-grade material. Small rough diamond chips (often used as grids anchors or meditation stones) can be found from specialist mineral dealers for $10–$100 for a few grams, depending on quality and size. These are typically not gem-quality stones and will not be graded by GIA. Specific retail data for this niche market is limited; prices vary significantly by seller.

## What Affects Diamond Value at Resale

Diamond retains more value than most gemstones when the stone is natural, certified by GIA, and of high quality. Factors that reduce resale value:

- Fracture-filled or laser-drilled treatments (lower buyback offers)
- No certificate or a certificate from an unrecognized lab
- Mounted in jewelry (mounting must be removed for re-grading)
- Very low clarity (I1 and below) where inclusions are visible to the naked eye
