# Selenite Meaning, Properties and Traditional Uses

Selenite is a transparent to translucent variety of gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) named after Selene, the Greek goddess of the moon. In crystal tradition it is used as a stone of clarity, calm, and energetic cleansing. Physically, it is one of the softest minerals sold in gem trade — Mohs 2.0 — and dissolves slowly in water.

## What Selenite Is: Physical Identity

Selenite is not a separate mineral species; it is the transparent crystalline variety of gypsum, a calcium sulfate dihydrate. Three other gypsum varieties are commonly sold alongside it: satin spar (fibrous, silky, the most common form sold as "selenite wands"), alabaster (fine-grained, massive), and desert rose (rosette clusters of gypsum blades). In practice, retailers often label satin spar as selenite, so buyers encounter both.

Key physical facts:

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | CaSO4·2H2O |
| Mohs hardness | 2.0 |
| Lustre | Vitreous to pearly |
| Transparency | Transparent to translucent |
| Color | Colorless to white; pale tints possible |
| Cleavage | Perfect in one direction |
| Water solubility | Slight; dissolves slowly, especially in warm water |

The name was coined by J.G. Wallerius in 1747 in his Mineralogia, drawn from the Greek σεληνη (the moon), referencing the pale, luminous reflections visible through thin selenite sheets used historically as window glazing.

For a broader discussion of what distinguishes a mineral, crystal, and gemstone, see [gemstone vs crystal vs mineral](/learn/gemstone-vs-crystal-vs-mineral).

## Where the Name Comes From

The moon connection is directly physical: ancient and medieval builders cut thin selenite sheets to glaze windows — the mineral's translucency admitted diffuse, moonlit-quality light. The Greek σεληνη was applied by Wallerius specifically for this luminous quality, not as a mystical attribution. It is this physical resemblance that later fed selenite's adoption into moon-linked spiritual traditions.

Selenite is found on every continent. Major sources include the giant-crystal caves of Naica, Chihuahua, Mexico (where single crystals grow to several meters), as well as Morocco, Australia, and the United States. Moroccan material dominates the retail trade in wands and charging plates.

## Traditional Metaphysical Meaning

In contemporary crystal practice, selenite is one of the most widely used cleansing and protective stones. Practitioners describe it as traditionally associated with:

- Mental clarity and focus
- Calm and inner peace
- Energetic cleansing of a space or other crystals
- Connection to lunar and higher spiritual energy
- Intuition and gentle insight

The site's summary subtitle is "The Stone of Clarity." In tradition it is linked to the element Air and the planet Moon. It is most often associated with the zodiac signs Cancer and Taurus — see the [Cancer zodiac page](/gemstones/zodiac/cancer) for stones associated with that sign.

These associations are tradition, not established physical or medical fact. Selenite has no demonstrated ability to cleanse, protect, or focus in a physical sense beyond its optical properties.

## What Selenite Is Traditionally Used For

The most common practical applications in crystal tradition:

**Cleansing other crystals.** Selenite is widely described as self-cleansing and capable of clearing energy from other stones placed on or near it. This is a stated tradition, not a physical phenomenon.

**Meditation.** Selenite wands and plates are used as focal objects or held during meditation sessions. See [crystals for meditation](/crystals/crystals-for-meditation) for how selenite fits into that practice.

**Calming anxiety.** Selenite appears in lists of stones used by people seeking calm. See [crystals for anxiety](/crystals/crystals-for-anxiety) for context.

**Affirmations.** Selenite's association with clarity makes it a traditional companion to spoken or written intentions. See [selenite affirmations](/gemstones/selenite/affirmations) for specific examples.

**Crystal grids and pairings.** Selenite is often placed at the center or perimeter of crystal grids, and paired with stones of different energetic qualities. See [selenite combinations](/gemstones/selenite/combinations) for pairing guidance.

## Physical Cautions

Because selenite is Mohs 2.0, it scratches with a fingernail and chips easily under modest impact. It must never go in water — even brief soaking causes surface dissolution. It should be stored away from harder stones and rough surfaces.
