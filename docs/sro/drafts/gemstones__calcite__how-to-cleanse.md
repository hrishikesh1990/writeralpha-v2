# How to Cleanse Calcite Without Damaging It

Calcite must not go in water. As a calcium carbonate mineral (CaCO3) it reacts with acids — including mild acids in water, sweat, and salt — and prolonged exposure can etch or degrade its polished surface. Safe cleansing methods are smoke, sound, moonlight (on a dry surface), breath and visualization, and placement on a dry selenite slab. Each is covered below.

## Why Water Is Off-Limits for Calcite

Calcite is slightly soluble in water (approximately 0.001 g per 100 ml at room temperature) and reacts readily with even dilute acids, producing carbon dioxide gas — the effervescence you see when vinegar touches limestone. Saltwater is more damaging than plain water: the combination of acidity and dissolved salts accelerates surface etching. Extended immersion in any water, including tap water, risks dulling the polish and slowly pitting the surface.

Dry salt burial — a popular cleansing method for some stones — carries the same risk for calcite. Salt is hygroscopic, drawing moisture from the air toward the specimen's surface, which can initiate the same acid reaction over time.

For a full explanation of what water does to calcite, see [can calcite go in water](/gemstones/calcite/can-go-in-water). The site's general guide at [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals) also notes which stones are water-sensitive.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold your calcite piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, turning it so all surfaces are exposed. This is the most widely used method for soft, water-sensitive stones and carries no physical risk to calcite. Work in a ventilated room so you are not breathing concentrated smoke yourself.

### Sound

Place calcite near — not touching — a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing keeps the stone stationary and dry. It works well for fragile raw specimens or large pieces that are awkward to move through smoke, and for clusters where individual surfaces are difficult to reach.

### Moonlight on a dry surface

Set calcite on a dry windowsill or on a clean, dry surface outdoors on a clear night. Leave it under moonlight for several hours. Check the weather before placing it: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Retrieve the piece before morning condensation forms. This method is popular in crystal tradition for a combined cleanse and recharge.

### Breath and visualization

Hold calcite in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale a steady breath across its surface while focusing your attention on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This requires no materials and is useful for a quick reset between uses or when other methods are unavailable.

### Dry selenite slab

Rest calcite on a dry selenite slab overnight. In crystal tradition, selenite is considered self-cleansing and capable of cleansing other stones resting on it. Physically, the selenite stays dry and does not interact with the calcite surface. Note that selenite itself should also be kept away from water, so store the pair in a dry location.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are separate steps. After cleansing calcite, you can:

- Place it in brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes). Most calcite varieties are opaque or only slightly translucent; short sun exposure is unlikely to cause fading, though brightly dyed pieces may be more susceptible to UV over long periods.
- Rest it beside or on a cluster of [clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz), which is traditionally considered an amplifier.
- Leave it overnight on the dry selenite slab mentioned above.

For a full guide to recharging approaches, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary. A common guideline is to cleanse when the stone first arrives, after any period of intensive use, and monthly during general display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — that is a matter of personal practice.

As a practical maintenance note: inspect calcite periodically for surface dulling, minor pitting, or white powdery deposits, which may indicate acid contact or humidity exposure rather than a need for spiritual cleansing. Address the physical cause by moving the stone to a drier, cooler location.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store calcite in a cool, dry spot away from bathrooms, kitchen steam, or outdoor humidity. Avoid storing it against harder stones that could scratch it (Mohs 3 means almost any other crystal will scratch calcite). A soft cloth pouch or padded box keeps the surface protected.
