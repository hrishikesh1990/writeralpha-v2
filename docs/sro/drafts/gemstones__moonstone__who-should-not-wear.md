# Who Should Be Cautious About Wearing Moonstone

Anyone who subjects their hands to regular impact, abrasion, or chemical exposure should be cautious about wearing moonstone in a ring. With a Mohs hardness of 6 to 6.5 and two directions of perfect cleavage, moonstone can crack from a single sharp blow and scratch from everyday contact with harder materials. Beyond physical risk, some astrological traditions identify signs or placements that may be poorly matched to moonstone's lunar associations.

## Physical Cautions: The Real Durability Limits

Moonstone sits at 6 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale, which means it can be scratched by quartz (hardness 7), most ceramic surfaces, and many tools. More critically, it has two directions of perfect cleavage — planes of atomic weakness that run through the crystal. A sharp knock can split a moonstone along these planes, regardless of how gently it is otherwise treated.

GIA notes that moonstone has poor toughness and recommends setting it in pendants, earrings, and pins rather than rings, which receive more knocks and contact. A ring setting does not make moonstone safer; the stone is still vulnerable to impact.

People for whom moonstone in a ring carries the most physical risk include:

- Mechanics, carpenters, gardeners, climbers, and anyone who works regularly with their hands around hard surfaces or tools
- Competitive athletes and gym-goers, where bars, weights, and floors pose impact risk
- People who wear rings 24 hours a day without removing them for manual tasks
- Anyone who cleans with harsh chemical products frequently, since chemical exposure can affect the finish over time

If you want to wear moonstone daily, pendants and earrings present far lower risk than rings. If a ring setting matters to you, choose a deep bezel or protective setting that shields the stone's edges, and remove the ring before physical tasks.

## Children and Stone Safety

Moonstone is not acutely toxic — it is an aluminium silicate feldspar and poses no chemical hazard from normal handling. It is not a stone to grind or inhale as dust, but casual handling is safe for adults and older children. Very small tumbled moonstones pose a choking hazard for young children and should be kept out of reach of children under three.

## Astrological and Traditional Cautions

Moonstone is not medically contraindicated, and crystals are a complementary practice, not a treatment. The cautions below are traditional associations held in various astrological systems, not verified clinical findings.

**Vedic astrology (Jyotish)** links moonstone to the planet Chandra (the Moon) and regards it as most beneficial for Cancer (Karka Rashi), whose ruling planet is the Moon. Vedic practitioners generally consider it favorable for other water and some earth signs and recommend that Aries, Leo, Scorpio, and Aquarius consult a qualified Jyotish astrologer before wearing moonstone, since the Moon's placement varies by individual chart. A strongly afflicted or debilitated Moon in the natal chart is sometimes cited as a reason for emotional instability when moonstone is worn, though opinions differ and the outcome depends on the individual reading.

**Western tradition** associates moonstone with Cancer, Libra, and Scorpio, and links it to the Moon, feminine energy, and emotional amplification. Some practitioners suggest that people who are already highly emotionally reactive, or who are going through a period of emotional overwhelm, take extra care with moonstone's traditionally amplifying associations. This is framed as a matter of personal readiness rather than an absolute contraindication. For more on those associations, see [moonstone meaning](/gemstones/moonstone/meaning).

**Fire-sign tradition:** Some crystal practitioners suggest that people drawn to grounding, stability, and high-energy activity may find that stones with solar or earth associations feel more resonant than moonstone. This is an aesthetic and practice fit, not a safety issue.
