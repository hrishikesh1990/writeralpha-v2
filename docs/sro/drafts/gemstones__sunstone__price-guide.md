# Sunstone Price Guide — What It Costs and What Drives Value

Oregon Sunstone ranges from roughly $20 per carat for pale, non-phenomenal material to $1,700 per carat for large, intensely colored red stones — a roughly 85-to-one spread that is almost entirely explained by color. Indian Sunstone (typically an oligoclase or orthoclase aventurine feldspar) is generally far less expensive, running from $1 to $30 per carat for translucent material. The sections below explain what moves the price and how to use that framework when buying.

## The Main Price Drivers

### Color

Color is the dominant variable in Sunstone valuation. The most sought-after colors — deep red, deep green, and strong bicolors — command the highest premiums. Pale yellow, champagne, and colorless stones occupy the bottom of the price range.

As of September 2026, retail benchmarks for faceted Oregon Sunstone (per carat) drawn from GemSociety, GemRockAuctions, and GemVal 2025 data:

| Color | Typical retail range (per carat) |
|---|---|
| Colorless / pale champagne | $20–$150 |
| Pink / tan with or without schiller | Up to $50 |
| Strong pink, orange, or light red | $50–$300 |
| Deep red or red-orange | $450–$1,000 |
| Rare green, teal, or blue-green | $600–$1,000 |
| Large (3+ ct) intense red | Up to $1,700 |

These are retail ranges from dealer listings and established gem trade sources; actual prices vary by cut quality, clarity, seller markup, and market timing. Verify against a live source before making a purchasing decision.

### Clarity and the Copper Inclusions

In most colored gemstones, inclusions lower value. In Oregon Sunstone, oriented copper platelets are a feature. They produce the aventurescence (metallic schiller) that makes the stone distinctive, and their presence is desirable unless they reduce transparency significantly. Fractures, however, do reduce value because they interrupt the color and structural integrity of the stone.

Non-phenomenal material — stones with copper so fine it produces a warm glow rather than visible schiller — is less sought by collectors but still commands a premium over plain feldspar.

### Cut

Oregon Sunstone rewards creative cutting. Faceted stones designed to catch the copper schiller across the table face bring higher prices than calibrated commercial cuts of comparable color and weight. Cabochons are the lowest-priced cutting style and are often used for translucent or moderately included material.

### Size (Carat Weight)

Price per carat increases with size. Most Oregon Sunstone from commercial mining runs one to ten carats. Stones over three carats in a saturated color command a disproportionate premium. Clean, faceted stones over five carats in red or green are genuinely rare from the Oregon deposits.

### Origin

Oregon Sunstone — mined from basalt deposits in Lake County and Harney County, Oregon — is the most valued variety. It is the official state gemstone of Oregon (designated 1987) and represents one of the only copper-bearing gem feldspars mined in the United States. Indian Sunstone from Andhra Pradesh, typically an oligoclase or orthoclase with hematite inclusions, is more abundant and considerably less expensive.

## Avoiding Overpaying

The identification page covers this in detail, but from a price perspective: genuine Oregon Sunstone is not cheap. If you see red or strongly colored "sunstone" offered at $5 to $20 per carat, it is almost certainly either Indian Sunstone, goldstone (glass), or copper-diffused andesine. See [how to identify real Sunstone](/gemstones/sunstone/how-to-identify) for the physical tests.

Hardness also affects wearability and therefore value over time. At Mohs 6.0 to 7.2, Sunstone is moderately durable — suitable for pendants, earrings, and occasional-wear rings, less ideal for daily-wear rings without protective settings. The [Mohs hardness scale guide](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale) explains what the numbers mean in practice.

## What Tumbled Stones and Rough Cost

Not all Sunstone buyers want faceted gemstones. For crystal practice and collection:

- **Tumbled Oregon Sunstone**: typically $5–$25 per stone for small pieces without strong color; higher for pronounced schiller or rich color.
- **Rough Oregon Sunstone**: a few dollars for small, low-grade pieces; up to several hundred dollars or more for larger, high-grade rough with good color.
- **Indian Sunstone tumbled**: commonly $1–$10 per stone in bulk.

These figures are general benchmarks from gem auction and retailer listings as of September 2026.

## Ethical Sourcing

Oregon Sunstone is mined domestically in the United States under regulated conditions. The Bureau of Land Management operates public collection areas where anyone can dig for personal use. Commercial mines such as Ponderosa, Spectrum, and Dust Devil offer documented provenance. For stones from other origins, supply chain transparency varies. The site's guide to [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing) covers what to look for.
