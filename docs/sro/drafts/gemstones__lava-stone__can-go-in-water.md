# Can Lava Stone Go in Water? What You Need to Know

Lava stone can go in water briefly without chemical harm — basalt does not react with or dissolve in water. The concern is physical: lava stone is porous, and prolonged soaking traps moisture in the vesicles (natural pores), which degrades elastic cord in bracelets and creates conditions for mildew if the stone is stored damp. A short rinse of 20 to 30 seconds is safe; soaking for minutes or hours is not recommended.

## Why Lava Stone Can Handle Brief Water Contact

Lava stone is vesicular basalt, a silicate-based igneous rock composed primarily of plagioclase feldspar, pyroxene, and olivine. None of these minerals react with plain water or dissolve at room temperature. Basalt used as flooring and countertop material routinely encounters moisture without chemical degradation. Its natural absorption rate is low compared with sedimentary stones like limestone, but the pores visible in lava stone beads do hold liquid.

This distinguishes lava stone from minerals that must never go in water. Pyrite oxidizes when wet, forming sulfuric acid on its surface. Selenite dissolves slowly in water. Malachite releases copper into solution. Lava stone does none of these; its water sensitivity is mechanical, not chemical.

## The Actual Risk: Porosity and Elastic Cord

The natural pores in lava stone beads absorb water. On a solid specimen or a tumbled piece this is manageable — dry the stone thoroughly and store it in a dry place. On a beaded bracelet the risk is greater: water absorbed into the beads sits against the elastic cord, and repeated wet-dry cycles accelerate cord degradation. Most lava stone bracelets use a single-strand elastic cord, which will weaken and eventually break faster if regularly soaked.

If you want to rinse a lava stone bracelet, hold it under cool running water for 20 to 30 seconds, then pat dry and allow it to air-dry for at least an hour in a ventilated spot before wearing or storing it.

## Salt Water

Lava stone can handle a brief salt water rinse without chemical damage, but salt is hygroscopic — it attracts ambient moisture. Salt residue left in the pores continues drawing dampness even after the surface appears dry. If you use salt water, follow with a plain water rinse, then dry completely. For guidance on which stones tolerate salt and which do not, see [salt safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## Swimming, Showering, and Bath Use

Showering with a lava stone bracelet occasionally is unlikely to cause visible harm, but it is not ideal practice for the cord. Chlorinated pool water and salt water from the ocean are more aggressive than plain water; they will not damage the basalt, but chlorine and salt residue in the pores are harder to clear without a thorough rinse and dry. Wearing lava stone in a bath — particularly with bath oils or salts — means the porous beads will absorb those substances, which may be desirable (aromatherapy) or not.

## When Not to Use Water

Avoid extended soaking (five minutes or more). Avoid leaving a wet lava stone bracelet in a closed bag or sealed container where moisture cannot escape. Avoid repeated soaking-and-drying cycles, as these accelerate elastic cord wear more than any other factor.

## Sun Drying After Water Contact

Lava stone is opaque and does not fade in sunlight, making sun drying a practical option. A short period in morning sun after a rinse dries the surface quickly.
