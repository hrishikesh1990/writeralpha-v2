# How to Identify Real Moss Agate

Real Moss Agate is a translucent variety of chalcedony (SiO2) containing branching mineral inclusions — most commonly chlorite, hornblende, celadonite, or iron and manganese oxides — that produce the distinctive moss, fern, or landscape patterns inside the stone. The most common imitations are dyed chalcedony with added coloring, glass, and lower-grade chalcedony sold as scenic Moss Agate. None of the at-home tests below is conclusive alone, but together they give a reliable picture.

## What Genuine Moss Agate Looks Like

Genuine Moss Agate has a translucent to semi-transparent body — holding it up to a light source should reveal light passing through the base material, with the inclusions appearing as three-dimensional structures inside rather than patterns on the surface. The inclusions branch organically: fern-like, tree-like, or filament-like forms with irregular edges. They typically appear in green (from chlorite or hornblende), brown to black (from iron or manganese oxides), or occasionally red. No two specimens are alike.

The term "agate" is applied loosely here: technically, agate requires banding, which Moss Agate lacks. It is mineralogically a dendritic chalcedony. This is not an indication of fakery — it is simply how the stone is classified by convention.

For what genuine material is prized for in practice, see [Moss Agate meaning](/gemstones/moss-agate/meaning).

## At-Home Tests

### Temperature test

Hold the stone for 30 seconds, then release it and pick it up again. Natural chalcedony is a poor conductor of heat and remains noticeably cool to the touch even after brief handling. Glass warms up quickly to hand temperature. This test is useful for ruling out glass imitations but does not distinguish Moss Agate from other natural stones.

### Scratch test

Moss Agate has a Mohs hardness of 6.5–7. A steel file (hardness about 6.5) will not scratch the surface of genuine material, or will only just mark it. Glass typically sits at Mohs 5–5.5 and scratches clearly with a steel file. Test an inconspicuous area and wipe clean before reading the result — surface powder can look like a scratch when it is not.

### Inclusion inspection

Under a loupe or magnification, examine the inclusions. In genuine Moss Agate, the mineral inclusions have depth — they appear to float at different levels within the stone body. Patterns that appear perfectly flat, printed-looking, or placed precisely on the surface suggest dyed material or a composite. Unnaturally uniform or vivid electric green coloring that does not vary across the inclusions may indicate dye.

### Weight and feel

Chalcedony has a specific gravity of approximately 2.58–2.64. Genuine Moss Agate will feel denser than same-sized glass or plastic. This is a rough guide, not a precise test without scales and water displacement.

## Common Imitations and How to Spot Them

| Imitation | How to spot it |
|---|---|
| Dyed chalcedony | Color concentrated in surface cracks; unnaturally uniform green throughout |
| Glass | Warms to hand temperature quickly; air bubbles sometimes visible under loupe |
| Plastic | Very light for its size; scratches easily with a fingernail |
| Tree agate | Very similar stone — also dendritic chalcedony, but typically opaque rather than translucent and white-based; both are genuine stones |

Tree agate is not a fake — it is a related stone. The comparison page at [tree agate vs moss agate](/compare/tree-agate-vs-moss-agate) covers the differences in detail.

## Buying Guidance

Price is a useful signal: Moss Agate is an affordable stone, and very low prices rarely indicate higher quality. A tumbled stone or small cabochon sold for under a dollar may be dyed lower-grade material. For expected price ranges by type and quality, see the [Moss Agate price guide](/gemstones/moss-agate/price-guide).

Buying from sellers who can describe the source region (India, Brazil, Montana, and the US Southwest are major sources) and who show individual stone photos rather than stock images reduces risk. For broader guidance on assessing crystal sellers, see [are online crystals real](/learn/are-online-crystals-real). For the distinction between lab-created and natural stone — a separate question from fake vs. real — see [lab-created vs natural gemstones](/compare/lab-created-vs-natural-gemstones).

If you need a definitive identification, a gemologist with a refractometer can confirm the refractive index (1.530–1.540 for chalcedony) and distinguish Moss Agate from glass or synthetic imitations conclusively.
