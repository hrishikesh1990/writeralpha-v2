# Crystal Storage: Best Ways to Store Your Collection

Store crystals separated by hardness to prevent softer stones being scratched by harder neighbors, keep humidity below 40% relative humidity for reactive minerals, and block direct UV light from color-sensitive stones such as amethyst and fluorite. A small collection can be stored safely with three materials: individual fabric pouches, a wood or plastic divided tray, and silica gel packets for humidity control.

## What You Need Before Setting Up Storage

**Prerequisites:**

- Cleanse all stones before storage. Storing a stone before cleansing seals in whatever it has been exposed to (handling oils, dust, residues from other environments). See [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).
- Know which stones in your collection are water-reactive, light-sensitive, or humidity-sensitive. The three reference pages cover this: [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water), [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals), and [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).
- Have storage containers ready before moving stones around. Handling stones repeatedly without a destination causes abrasion and drops.

## Steps for Organizing Crystal Storage

**Step 1: Sort your stones into hardness groups.**
Mohs hardness determines which stones can scratch which others. A harder stone will always scratch a softer one when they contact and slide. Group stones roughly as:

- Hardness 7 and above (quartz family, garnets, sapphire, ruby, topaz, diamond): can share a tray divider without scratching each other.
- Hardness 5–6 (obsidian, moonstone, labradorite, hematite, opal): keep separate from the harder group.
- Hardness below 5 (selenite, calcite, fluorite, howlite, lepidolite, malachite): store individually or in separate padded pouches.

**Step 2: Wrap or pouch each stone that cannot share a compartment.**
Use individual cloth pouches, squares of velvet, bubble wrap, or foam cut to fit. This protects both the stone being wrapped and any neighbor that might otherwise be scratched. Pointed crystals and raw specimens with sharp terminations should always be individually wrapped — both to protect adjacent stones and to prevent personal injury when reaching into a box.

**Step 3: Place reactive and delicate minerals in airtight or humidity-controlled containers.**
[Pyrite](/gemstones/pyrite) oxidizes when exposed to moisture above roughly 40% relative humidity; the reaction produces iron sulfates and, over time, sulfuric acid on the surface. [Selenite](/gemstones/selenite) (gypsum) slowly loses bound water and surface integrity in dry air, but is also damaged by high humidity because gypsum is slightly water-soluble. [Opal](/gemstones/opal) can craze if humidity fluctuates dramatically, as it contains up to 20% bound water. For these stones, store in a sealed container with a small silica gel packet to maintain moderate, stable humidity. Silica gel packets rated for museum storage are available from archival suppliers; replace them when they change color to indicate saturation.

**Step 4: Decide on a display versus dark-storage policy for light-sensitive stones.**
[Amethyst](/gemstones/amethyst), fluorite, smoky quartz, and rose quartz (crystalline variety) fade permanently under prolonged UV exposure because their color-causing mechanisms are radiation-induced color centers that UV light destroys. For display near a window, use UV-filtering glass or acrylic display cases. For long-term storage, a drawer, box, or cupboard away from light is sufficient. Dark-colored stones (obsidian, black tourmaline, garnet, carnelian) and heat-treated citrine are not photosensitive and can be displayed in sunny locations.

**Step 5: Choose a storage location with stable temperature and low humidity.**
Avoid:

- Bathrooms and kitchens (steam, high humidity).
- Window ledges with direct afternoon sun (UV exposure and thermal cycling).
- Uninsulated attics or garages where temperature swings are large.
- Locations near heat vents, radiators, or candles.

A bedroom shelf away from the window, a closed cabinet in a living area, or a dedicated display case in a climate-controlled room all work well.

**Step 6: Label pouches and compartments.**
A collection grows faster than memory. A small paper label inside each pouch or compartment with the stone's name, origin if known, and purchase date takes little time to create and prevents later identification confusion.

## Completion Check

Your storage system is working if:

- No stone shows new scratches after you retrieve it.
- Pyrite and selenite specimens show no white powdery deposits or surface softening after several months.
- Color-sensitive stones have not visibly lightened or greyed.
- You can identify every stone in your collection without guessing.

## Maintenance Schedule

- **After each use:** Wipe tumbled stones with a dry soft cloth to remove skin oils before returning to storage.
- **Monthly:** Check silica gel packets and replace or recharge them when saturated. Inspect reactive minerals (pyrite, marcasite, iron-rich specimens) for early oxidation (yellow or white powder on surface).
- **Seasonally or when restoring after storage:** Re-cleanse stones before returning them to active use. For definitions of what crystals, minerals, and gemstones are physically, see [gemstone vs crystal vs mineral](/learn/gemstone-vs-crystal-vs-mineral).

## Special Cases

**Tumbled versus raw stones.** Tumbled stones have polished surfaces that resist minor abrasion better than raw specimens. Raw specimens with natural faces and crystal planes have sharper edges that scratch adjacent stones and damage polish on tumbled pieces — keep them separated.

**Jewelry.** Store crystal jewelry in individual compartments in a jewelry box with a velvet lining. Chains and metal settings can scratch stone surfaces; beaded bracelets should be stored flat rather than hung, to reduce tension on the elastic or wire.

**Large pieces and clusters.** Geodes and large clusters are usually stable on their own base. Avoid stacking anything on top of them. A slight padding of folded cloth under the base reduces vibration transmission if the shelf is near a source of regular vibration (traffic, machinery).
