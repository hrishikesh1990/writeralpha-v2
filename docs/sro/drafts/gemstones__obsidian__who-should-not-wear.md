# Who Should Be Cautious About Wearing Obsidian

Physical caution applies to anyone handling raw obsidian: conchoidal fracture creates edges sharper than a scalpel, and dust from cutting is a silica inhalation hazard. For polished jewelry, physical risks are minimal. Traditional cautions suggest that people in acute emotional difficulty or who find obsidian's intensity overwhelming may want to start with brief sessions rather than continuous wear.

## Physical Safety

Obsidian is volcanic glass. When it breaks or is knapped, conchoidal fracture produces extremely sharp, curved edges. Anyone handling raw or freshly broken obsidian should treat it as they would broken glass: use eye protection, handle edges carefully, and avoid skin contact with freshly fractured surfaces.

Cutting, grinding, or breaking obsidian creates fine silica particles. Prolonged inhalation of crystalline silica dust is an occupational hazard (silicosis), but the dust from amorphous volcanic glass like obsidian is considered less hazardous than crystalline silica. Even so, use respiratory protection when generating dust from any glassy material. Wash hands after handling chipped or broken pieces and avoid touching eyes or mouth.

Polished obsidian jewelry — pendants, bracelets, tumbled stones — carries no significant physical risk to a healthy adult when worn or carried normally.

**Children:** Raw obsidian pieces should be kept out of reach of young children. The edges are a laceration hazard, and small polished pieces are a choking hazard.

## Traditional and Astrological Cautions

Crystal practice is a complementary activity, not medicine. The cautions below are matters of tradition, not established outcomes. See [crystal healing science](/learn/crystal-healing-science) for what the evidence shows.

**Highly sensitive or empathic individuals:** Some practitioners describe obsidian's energy as intense and confrontational — they associate it with surfacing buried emotions and demanding honest self-examination. People who find this overwhelming are sometimes advised to build up gradually: start with brief holds of a small tumbled stone rather than continuous wear, and pair it with a gentler grounding stone such as [hematite](/gemstones/hematite) or [black tourmaline](/gemstones/black-tourmaline).

**People in acute emotional distress or grief:** A subset of crystal practitioners cautions against wearing obsidian during very raw periods of grief, anxiety crisis, or depression without additional grounding support. The reasoning, in tradition, is that obsidian is believed to accelerate the surfacing of suppressed material — which can feel destabilizing when a person is already struggling. This is not a medical contraindication; obsidian has no pharmacological effect. If you are experiencing a mental health crisis, seek professional care.

**Astrological cautions:** Obsidian is most often linked to Scorpio and Sagittarius. Some astrological traditions suggest that signs ruled by very different energies — particularly air signs such as Gemini, Libra, and Aquarius — may find obsidian's heaviness an uncomfortable contrast. This is a traditional perspective, not a testable claim.

**Zodiac conflict:** The same astrological traditions sometimes caution that obsidian, associated with Pluto and deep transformation, may feel overwhelming for signs that emphasize lightness or mental agility. Again, this is traditional framing.

## Who Has No Particular Reason to Avoid It

Most adults wearing polished obsidian jewelry have no physical or significant traditional reason to avoid it. Its associations with protection and grounding (see [crystals for protection](/crystals/crystals-for-protection) and [crystals for grounding](/crystals/crystals-for-grounding)) make it one of the most commonly recommended protective stones.
