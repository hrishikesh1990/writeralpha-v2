# How to Cleanse Strawberry Quartz Without Damaging It

Strawberry Quartz can tolerate a brief rinse under plain water because its base mineral, quartz (SiO2), is rated 7 on the Mohs scale and is not water-soluble. However, salt water and prolonged soaking should be avoided as they can dull the surface and potentially affect the iron oxide inclusions over time. Safe cleansing methods include plain water rinse, smoke, sound, moonlight, and selenite slabs.

## What Makes Strawberry Quartz Relatively Robust

Strawberry Quartz is natural quartz with iron oxide inclusions — primarily hematite, goethite, or lepidocrocite — suspended within the crystal. The quartz host is chemically stable and hard enough (Mohs 7) to resist surface scratching from most everyday materials. The inclusions are iron oxides, which are insoluble under normal conditions and will not leach from a polished stone during casual handling or a brief water exposure.

The main physical risks during cleansing are:

- **Salt water or salt abrasion** — salt can work into micro-fractures and dull a polished surface over repeated use
- **Ultrasonic cleaning** — high-frequency vibration can stress inclusions and potentially cause micro-fractures within the quartz, particularly along planes where inclusions are dense
- **Steam cleaning** — rapid temperature change stresses any included stone
- **Prolonged soaking** — unnecessary for a hard stone and increases the chance of surface dulling

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Plain water rinse

Hold the strawberry quartz under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 to 30 seconds. Use your fingers or a soft cloth to wipe the surface gently. Dry it immediately with a soft lint-free cloth. This is the simplest physical clean and is safe for polished tumbled stones, cabochons, and beads. Do not soak the piece; a rinse is sufficient. Avoid water that is chemically treated or highly chlorinated if you prefer to be cautious, though brief exposure to tap water poses minimal risk to the stone.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the strawberry quartz in the smoke from burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, turning the piece so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This method carries no physical risk to the stone. Ensure the room is ventilated. Smoke cleansing is one of the most commonly used methods in crystal practice for stones of all kinds and is the safest choice for any quartz variety with inclusions.

### Sound

Place the strawberry quartz near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to wash over it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone is never exposed to water, salt, or heat. This method suits fragile pieces or any specimen where you want to avoid any physical contact.

### Moonlight

Set strawberry quartz on a dry surface — a windowsill, a plate, or a shelf — on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the weather beforehand: dew, rain, or high humidity means the stone should stay indoors. This is a popular traditional cleansing and recharging method that poses no physical risk when the stone is kept dry.

### Selenite slab or bowl

Place the strawberry quartz on a flat selenite slab or inside a selenite bowl for several hours. In crystal tradition, selenite is used to cleanse other stones through proximity. No moisture is involved, and the contact between the two minerals poses no risk at this hardness level (selenite is Mohs 2, much softer, so strawberry quartz will not be scratched by it, though the selenite may acquire surface marks if the quartz is dragged across it — simply place the piece and leave it still).

### Visualization and breath

Hold the strawberry quartz in both hands, breathe in slowly, and exhale gently across the surface while mentally releasing any accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This requires no materials, leaves no physical residue, and is used in crystal practice for a quick reset between uses.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal tradition treats cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing strawberry quartz, practitioners commonly place it:

- In morning sunlight for 15 to 30 minutes (brief exposure is safe; strawberry quartz resists fading better than amethyst or rose quartz because its color comes from mineral inclusions rather than trace elements, but prolonged direct sun exposure is still best avoided as a general principle)
- On a dry selenite slab
- Near a cluster of clear quartz

## How Often to Cleanse

There is no physical requirement for a cleansing schedule — quartz does not degrade from use. Crystal tradition commonly suggests cleansing when a stone first arrives, after any emotionally significant session, and roughly once a month during regular display or wear. If you use strawberry quartz daily, more frequent cleansing is a matter of personal practice.

As a maintenance point: inspect your stone periodically. Strawberry quartz is naturally durable, but any tumbled stone with surface micro-fractures can collect residue over time. A soft cloth wipe after handling keeps the surface looking its best.
