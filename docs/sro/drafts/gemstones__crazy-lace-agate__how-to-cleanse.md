# How to Cleanse Crazy Lace Agate Without Damaging It

Crazy Lace Agate is safe to cleanse with water. It is a stable silica mineral (SiO2) at Mohs hardness 7, meaning it does not react with water or dissolve. A brief rinse under lukewarm water is the simplest cleansing option; smoke, sound, moonlight, and sunlight are all equally safe and widely used in crystal practice.

## Why Crazy Lace Agate Is Easy to Cleanse

Crazy Lace Agate belongs to the chalcedony family — microcrystalline quartz with no cleavage and no soluble compounds. Unlike reactive minerals such as pyrite (which oxidizes in water) or selenite (which dissolves), Crazy Lace Agate does not break down on contact with water. Its polished surface is durable enough to handle gentle washing.

The main care consideration is the polished finish: harsh chemicals, abrasive scrubbing, or prolonged soaking in salt water can gradually dull the waxy-to-vitreous surface, and salt can crystallize inside any hairline fractures as the stone dries. ## Cleansing Methods

### Water rinse

Hold Crazy Lace Agate under lukewarm running water for 20 to 30 seconds. Rub the surface gently with your fingers if needed. Pat dry immediately with a soft cloth and allow it to air-dry fully before storing. Avoid hot water, which can stress the stone over repeated cycles, and avoid leaving it submerged for long periods. A quick wash with a drop of mild liquid soap is acceptable for physical cleaning; rinse thoroughly afterward.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the stone in the smoke from burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the stone slowly through the smoke so all surfaces are covered. Ensure the space is ventilated. This is a widely used traditional method and carries no physical risk to the stone.

### Sound

Place Crazy Lace Agate near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and let the vibration pass over it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone does not need to be touched or moved through anything liquid. This method is well-suited to multiple stones at once.

### Moonlight

Set the stone on a dry, clean surface — a windowsill or a plate outdoors — on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the weather: do not leave it outdoors if rain or heavy dew is expected. Bring it inside before morning condensation forms. In crystal tradition, moonlight is considered both a cleansing and a recharging method.

### Sunlight

Crazy Lace Agate is not photosensitive. Unlike amethyst or rose quartz, which can fade with prolonged sun exposure, Crazy Lace Agate's warm iron-oxide colors (reds, oranges, browns) are stable. A few hours in morning sunlight is safe and used in crystal tradition as a combined cleanse and charge. Avoid extended all-day exposure simply as good general stone care.

### Dry earth burial

Bury the stone in a small pot of dry indoor soil or sand for 24 hours, then brush off residue with a dry cloth. Use indoor potting mix rather than garden soil, which may be damp. This method is used in crystal practice for what is described as a grounding reset.

## What to Avoid

| Method | Risk to the Stone |
|---|---|
| Prolonged salt water soak | Can dull polish and crystallize salt in fractures |
| Harsh chemical cleaners | May etch or dull the polished surface |
| Ultrasonic or steam cleaners | Risk if the stone has surface-reaching fractures |
| Abrasive scrubbing | Scratches the polish |

Crazy Lace Agate at hardness 7 can be scratched by harder stones or materials — keep it away from diamonds, sapphires, and corundum in storage.

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal tradition varies. A common guideline is to cleanse when the stone first arrives, after heavy use or a significant emotional event, and roughly once a month during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule; this is a matter of personal practice.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Cleansing and recharging are treated as distinct steps in most crystal traditions. After cleansing, Crazy Lace Agate is commonly placed in brief morning sunlight, near a clear quartz cluster, or on a dry selenite slab.
