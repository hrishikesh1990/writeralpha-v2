# How to Cleanse Howlite Without Damaging It

Howlite is safe for brief contact with plain water but is porous and soft (Mohs 3.5), so prolonged soaking and salt water should be avoided. The most reliable methods are smoke cleansing, sound, moonlight, and dry visualization. Each is covered below.

## Why Howlite's Porosity Matters for Cleansing

Howlite is a calcium borosilicate mineral that forms as compact, fine-grained nodules. Its porous surface absorbs liquid readily — which is why it takes dye so easily in the trade — and that same absorbency means water, salt, and cleaning agents can penetrate the surface and weaken it over time. At Mohs 3.5, howlite is also soft enough that abrasive or corrosive treatments cause visible surface damage.

Salt water is doubly problematic: the salt itself is abrasive, and both salt and water together can get into surface pores and accelerate wear. Dry salt burial, sometimes recommended for harder stones, carries the same risk because salt is hygroscopic and draws moisture toward whatever it contacts.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Brief running water

Hold howlite under cool running tap water for 20 to 30 seconds, then dry it immediately and completely with a soft cloth. Do not soak it in a bowl of water, and do not use warm or hot water, which opens surface pores further. This method is acceptable for natural undyed howlite. For dyed specimens, skip water entirely: cheap dyes may bleed when wet, and even quality dyes can fade with repeated exposure.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold howlite in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, moving it slowly so all surfaces are exposed. This is the most widely used method for porous stones in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to the specimen. Ensure good ventilation; you are also breathing the smoke.

### Sound

Place howlite near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone never needs contact with anything liquid or abrasive. This method suits dyed howlite as well as natural specimens.

### Moonlight

Set howlite on a dry windowsill or outdoors on a dry surface overnight under moonlight. Check the forecast first: dew or overnight humidity will leave moisture on the surface. Bring the stone in before condensation forms. In crystal tradition, a full moon is considered the most effective setting.

### Dry earth burial

Bury howlite in a small pot of dry, indoor potting soil or fine sand for 24 hours. Retrieve it, brush off any loose material with a dry soft brush, and inspect the surface. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp. This method is not suitable for dyed specimens, as soil particles may scratch the surface.

### Breath and visualization

Hold howlite in both hands, take a slow breath in, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method is the gentlest option, leaves no physical residue, and works for any specimen, dyed or not.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps in most crystal traditions. After cleansing, place howlite in moonlight overnight, on a dry selenite slab, or near a clear quartz cluster. Brief morning sunlight (15 minutes or less) is also used, though howlite does not fade significantly with short exposure. ## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary, but common guidance is to cleanse howlite when it arrives, after any sustained period of use, and monthly if kept on regular display. If you carry it daily, more frequent cleansing is often suggested. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule; this is a matter of personal practice.

As a physical maintenance note: inspect dyed howlite periodically for color fading or pooling on the surface. Any sign of dye bleeding near water suggests the specimen should be kept fully dry going forward.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store howlite in a dry location away from perfumes, lotions, and cleaning sprays, which its porous surface readily absorbs. Keep it away from harder stones that could scratch it; a soft pouch or padded compartment is ideal. Do not stack it under heavy objects, as the soft surface marks easily.
