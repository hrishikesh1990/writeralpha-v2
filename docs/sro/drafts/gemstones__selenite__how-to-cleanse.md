# How to Cleanse Selenite Without Damaging It

Selenite must not go in water. The mineral is calcium sulfate dihydrate (CaSO4·2H2O) and dissolves slowly when wet — soaking or rinsing will pit the surface and eventually destroy the piece. Safe cleansing methods are smoke, sound, moonlight, breath or visualization, and placement near other crystals. Each is covered below, followed by recharging guidance.

## Why Water and Salt Damage Selenite

Selenite is Mohs 2.0 — among the softest minerals in common gem trade — and it is slightly water-soluble. Prolonged immersion causes surface dissolution; even brief contact with liquid water leaves a dull, pitted finish on polished pieces. High ambient humidity causes slower but cumulative deterioration.

Salt is equally problematic. Salt is hygroscopic (it draws moisture from the air), so placing selenite in a bowl of dry salt or sea salt draws moisture toward the stone's surface. Salt-water baths, common for harder stones, are doubly damaging because they combine two corrosive forces. Avoid both.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the selenite piece slowly through the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating so all surfaces are exposed. This is the most widely used method for selenite in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to the specimen. Ensure the room is well ventilated; you are breathing the smoke too.

### Sound

Place selenite near a singing bowl, tuning fork, or bell and allow the vibration to move through or around it for one to two minutes. The stone does not need to be touched or moved. Sound cleansing is particularly useful for very large selenite slabs and wands that are awkward to move through smoke.

### Moonlight

Set selenite on a dry windowsill or on a dry outdoor surface on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast first: rain, dew, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture damage. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms. In crystal tradition, a full moon is considered the most effective timing.

### Sunlight (brief only)

Short exposure to morning sunlight — 15 to 30 minutes — is physically safe for selenite. Prolonged direct sunlight may gradually bleach some specimens; to be cautious, limit sun exposure to brief periods and avoid leaving selenite in a south-facing window all day.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the selenite in both hands, take a slow deep breath, and exhale steadily across the surface while directing your attention toward releasing whatever the stone has accumulated. Repeat two or three times. This requires no materials and is used for a quick reset when other methods are unavailable.

### Placement on or near other crystals (tradition)

Selenite is widely described in crystal tradition as self-cleansing. Practitioners also use dry selenite slabs as charging plates for other stones, stating that a selenite slab cleanses and recharges nearby crystals without needing cleansing itself. This is a stated tradition and has no physical basis in mineralogy.

## Step-by-Step: Smoke Cleansing

1. Set the selenite piece on a heatproof surface or hold it in your non-dominant hand.
2. Light your preferred herbs or incense and let it catch a steady smoke trail.
3. Move the selenite slowly through the smoke for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating to expose all sides.
4. Set the piece on a dry cloth or tray.
5. Allow the smoke to dissipate before storing the stone.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps. After cleansing, practitioners commonly:

- Place selenite under moonlight overnight (the same session that cleanses also recharges)
- Set it on a dry clear quartz cluster for several hours
- Leave it in brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes maximum)

For a full guide to recharging methods and timing, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary on frequency. Common guidelines suggest cleansing selenite when it first arrives, after any period of intensive use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule; selenite does not accumulate substances through normal handling.

As a practical maintenance note: inspect selenite periodically for surface cloudiness or dullness, which may indicate humidity damage rather than energetic depletion. Store in a cool, dry location with relative humidity below 40% where possible.
