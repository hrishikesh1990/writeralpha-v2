# Can Yellow Calcite Go in Water?

Yellow Calcite should not go in water. As calcium carbonate (CaCO3) with Mohs hardness 3, it dissolves slowly in acidic water and prolonged soaking etches the surface. A brief wipe with a slightly damp cloth is tolerable if dried immediately, but soaking, rinsing under running water, and water-based cleansing are not safe for this stone.

## Why Calcite Reacts with Water

Calcite's chemistry makes it reactive in two ways. First, it dissolves directly in dilute acids: even carbonic acid — formed when carbon dioxide dissolves in water — is acidic enough to slowly etch calcite. This is the same process that creates limestone caves and dissolves eggshells in vinegar. Tap water pH varies by region, but rainwater is consistently slightly acidic (around pH 5.6) due to dissolved CO2, and any water carrying dissolved acids will attack calcite over time.

Second, calcite is brittle with perfect rhombohedral cleavage in three directions. Repeated wetting and drying cycles create thermal and mechanical stress that, in combination with surface etching, can cause chips to separate along cleavage planes — particularly on raw or unpolished specimens.

The classic test used to identify calcite in mineralogy relies on this exact reactivity: a drop of dilute hydrochloric acid on calcite produces vigorous fizzing as carbon dioxide is released. Vinegar (acetic acid) produces a slower but visible reaction. This tells you that even weak household acids are chemically active against this mineral.

For a broader guide to which crystals are and are not water-safe, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## Salt Water Is More Damaging Than Plain Water

Salt water combines the dissolution risk of water with the abrasive effect of salt crystals on a Mohs 3 surface. It should be avoided entirely. Salt-water baths are sometimes recommended in crystal practice as a cleansing method; yellow calcite is not a candidate for this.

Dry salt is also not safe. Salt is hygroscopic — it draws moisture from the surrounding air — and salt left in contact with a calcite surface acts as an ongoing moisture source. Whether the stone is buried in dry salt or salt crystals are placed on it, the practical result is prolonged moisture contact. See [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals) for more information on which stones tolerate salt-based cleansing.

## Tap Water and Brief Contact

A tumbled yellow calcite stone can tolerate a brief wipe with a slightly damp cloth provided it is dried thoroughly and immediately afterward. This is not the same as rinsing, soaking, or holding the stone under running water. The risk from a single brief contact is low; the risk accumulates with repeated or prolonged exposure.

If the stone has been in contact with acidic substances — perfume, body lotion, fruit juice — wiping with a damp cloth followed by immediate drying is acceptable as a cleaning measure. Do not rinse under a tap.

## Sun and UV After Water Contact

Some practitioners dry crystals in sunlight after water contact. For yellow calcite, brief drying in indirect light or air is preferable to prolonged direct sunlight. The yellow color in calcite typically comes from iron-bearing or organic impurities, and prolonged UV exposure can gradually bleach these pigments. A few minutes of morning sun to aid drying poses no visible risk; hours of direct summer sun may cause gradual color fading over time. For general guidance on sun-sensitive stones, see [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals).

## Safe Cleaning Methods

To physically clean yellow calcite without ritual intent:

1. Use a soft, dry cloth to remove dust and light surface grime.
2. For stuck-on material, breathe lightly on the surface to generate minimal condensation, then wipe with a dry cloth.
3. If a damp cloth is necessary, use it briefly and dry the stone immediately with a separate dry cloth.
4. Never use any cleaning product, soap, or chemical solution on calcite — most are either acidic or alkaline and will damage the surface.
