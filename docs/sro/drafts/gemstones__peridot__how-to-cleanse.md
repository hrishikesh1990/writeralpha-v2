# How to Cleanse Peridot Without Damaging It

Peridot can tolerate brief physical cleaning with warm water and mild soap, but it is damaged by acids, ultrasonic cleaners, steam, and prolonged soaking. For energetic cleansing in crystal practice, the safest methods are smoke, sound, moonlight, and breath — all of which leave the stone physically unharmed.

## Physical Cleaning (Removing Dirt and Oils)

To remove surface grime from peridot, use warm water, a drop of mild dish soap, and a soft cloth or very soft brush. Work gently, rinse thoroughly, and pat dry immediately. Do not soak the stone — prolonged water contact can weaken prong settings and, over time, may affect polished surfaces.

Never use ultrasonic cleaners on peridot. The vibrations can cause existing lily-pad inclusions (small disk-shaped fractures common in the mineral) to expand, and may crack the stone along those stress points. Steam cleaning is also off-limits: thermal shock from the sudden heat and pressure change can fracture peridot, which is sensitive to rapid temperature changes.

Avoid acids entirely. Even mild household acids — vinegar, lemon juice, some jewelry cleaning solutions — can etch peridot's surface over time. Pool chlorine and salt water carry the same risk; see [can peridot go in water](/gemstones/peridot/can-go-in-water) for a full breakdown of water safety. For general guidance on cleaning methods across many stones, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Energetic Cleansing Methods

Crystal traditions treat cleansing as removing accumulated or stagnant energy, separate from physical cleaning. The following methods are safe for peridot.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the peridot piece in the smoke of burning herbs or incense for 30 to 60 seconds, moving it slowly so all surfaces pass through the smoke. Ensure the room is ventilated. This is the most common energetic-cleansing method for peridot and carries no physical risk to the stone.

### Sound

Place peridot near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and let the vibration pass over it for one to two minutes. Because the stone never contacts liquid or heat, this method suits fragile faceted pieces as well as tumbled specimens.

### Moonlight

Set peridot on a dry surface — a windowsill or outdoor table — on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast: dew or rain would expose the stone to moisture. Bring it indoors before dawn condensation forms. In crystal tradition, moonlight is used for a combined cleanse and recharge.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the peridot in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale steadily across the stone while mentally focusing on clearing any energy you wish to release. Repeat two or three times. This method needs no materials and is used for a quick reset between sessions.

### Dry salt — use with caution

Some cleansing guides recommend burying stones in dry salt. Peridot tolerates brief dry contact with salt, but salt is hygroscopic — it draws moisture from the air — so extended salt burial risks exposing the surface to humidity. If you use this method, keep the stone in dry salt for no more than one hour and brush off any residue immediately with a dry cloth.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal traditions treat recharging as restoring or amplifying a stone's energy after cleansing. Common methods for peridot include placing it in morning sunlight for 15 to 20 minutes (brief, not prolonged: extended sun exposure can fade some stones, though peridot is not strongly susceptible), resting it on a dry selenite slab, or placing it near a clear quartz cluster. ## How Often to Cleanse

Cleansing schedules are a matter of personal practice with no physical basis. A common guideline is to cleanse when a stone arrives, after any period of frequent use, and monthly during regular display. Inspect peridot periodically for scratches: at 6.5 to 7 Mohs, it can be scratched by harder minerals stored alongside it. Store it in a separate soft pouch away from harder stones like sapphire or topaz.
