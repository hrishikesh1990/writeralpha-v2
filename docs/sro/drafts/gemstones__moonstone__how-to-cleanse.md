# How to Cleanse and Recharge Moonstone

Moonlight, smoke, and sound are the most recommended methods for cleansing moonstone in crystal practice. Brief contact with plain water is physically tolerable — GIA confirms warm soapy water is the standard jewelry-cleaning method — but extended soaking is not advisable because water can work into cleavage cracks over time. The steps for each method are below, followed by recharging guidance.

## What You Need to Know About Moonstone Before Cleansing

Moonstone is a feldspar mineral with a Mohs hardness of 6 to 6.5 and two directions of perfect cleavage. These cleavage planes make it more fragile than its hardness alone suggests: a sharp impact can split a moonstone along these internal weaknesses. This matters for cleansing because any method involving knocks, prolonged moisture, or thermal shock carries physical risk.

Moonstone is insoluble in water and most common acids, but extended water exposure can allow moisture to work into existing cracks and stress the stone further. It is stable to light, so sunlight does not fade it the way it fades certain colored stones. It is harmed by hydrofluoric acid, but this is not a household concern.

For a dedicated answer on whether moonstone can go in water, see [can moonstone go in water](/gemstones/moonstone/can-go-in-water). For a general overview of methods across all stones, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Moonlight

Set moonstone on a dry surface — a windowsill, a table by a window, or outdoors on a dry night — and leave it in moonlight for several hours or overnight. This is traditionally the most resonant method for moonstone given its lunar associations, and it carries no physical risk provided the surface is dry. Check the forecast before leaving it outside: dew or overnight rain will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it indoors before morning condensation settles. A full moon is often cited in crystal tradition as the most effective timing, though practitioners use any clear night.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold moonstone in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating it so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is a widely used, zero-risk method for the stone itself. Ensure the space is ventilated.

### Sound

Place moonstone near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork. Strike the instrument and allow the vibration to pass through the stone for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing is particularly useful for stones in settings or display pieces that cannot easily be moved through smoke or moonlight.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, take a slow breath in, and exhale steadily across its surface while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and is a practical option when others are not available.

### Dry selenite slab

Rest moonstone on a piece of [selenite](/gemstones/selenite) for several hours or overnight. In crystal tradition, selenite is regarded as self-cleansing and able to clear nearby stones. There is no physical risk to moonstone from this contact.

## Methods to Avoid

**Ultrasonic cleaners and steam cleaners** are not recommended for moonstone. GIA specifically flags both as unsuitable: ultrasonic vibration can stress cleavage planes, and steam heat can cause sudden temperature change, which risks cracking a stone with two perfect cleavage directions.

**Salt water baths** are not recommended. Salt is abrasive to polished surfaces and, when wet, acts as a moisture source that can work into surface cracks.

**Dry salt burial** is also not ideal for moonstone. Salt is hygroscopic and draws ambient moisture, which defeats the purpose of keeping the stone dry.

**Prolonged water soaking** should be avoided. Brief rinsing under lukewarm water followed by immediate thorough drying is tolerable, but leaving moonstone submerged for hours is not advisable.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps. After cleansing, place moonstone:

- Under moonlight for an extended overnight session (this both cleanses and recharges in one step)
- Near a cluster of [clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz), which is traditionally regarded as an amplifier
- On a dry selenite slab for several hours

Brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes) poses no physical risk to moonstone, but practitioners who work with its lunar associations often prefer moonlight as the recharging source for this specific stone.

For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Frequency is a matter of personal practice. Common guidelines in crystal tradition suggest cleansing when the stone arrives, after sustained use, and monthly during regular display. Moonstone used in grief support or emotionally intensive practice — as used by some people exploring [crystals for grief](/crystals/crystals-for-grief) — is often cleansed more frequently.

On the physical side, inspect moonstone periodically for surface dulling or micro-scratching, which respond to gentle cleaning with a soft damp cloth and mild soap, followed by thorough drying. Store moonstone away from harder gemstones (quartz, topaz, corundum) that can scratch it. A full property overview is at [moonstone](/gemstones/moonstone).
