# Can Green Goldstone Go in Water?

Green Goldstone can go in water. It is synthetic glass, and glass does not oxidize, dissolve, or leach harmful compounds into water at room temperature. A brief rinse under cool or lukewarm running water is the safest way to physically clean the surface. The only precautions involve avoiding thermal shock from very hot water and protecting metal fittings on jewelry pieces.

## Why Green Goldstone Is Water-Safe

Green Goldstone is a man-made glass — silica melted with copper oxide and chromium compounds, then cooled under controlled conditions to form metallic copper crystals throughout the green glass matrix. Glass is chemically inert to liquid water across a wide temperature range. It does not react the way iron-bearing minerals like pyrite do (which oxidize and corrode), nor does it dissolve the way evaporite minerals like selenite and halite do, nor does it release toxic copper dust into solution the way malachite can when abraded.

The copper inside green goldstone is sealed within the glass matrix. It does not leach into water during a rinse or short soak. The glass surface is non-porous, so it cannot absorb water or contaminants that would change its internal structure.

Green goldstone rates 5.5 to 6 on the Mohs hardness scale. Water has no hardness and causes no mechanical wear to the surface during a rinse.

## How to Clean Green Goldstone with Water

1. Hold the piece under cool or lukewarm running water.
2. Rub the surface gently with your fingers or a soft cloth to remove oils and dust.
3. Rinse for 20 to 30 seconds.
4. Dry immediately and thoroughly with a soft lint-free cloth.
5. Allow it to air-dry completely before storing, especially if it has metal fittings.

That is the complete process. No soaking is required unless you prefer it for intentional cleansing purposes, in which case a few minutes in a bowl of cool clean water is fine.

## When to Be Cautious with Water

**Very hot water:** Glass can experience thermal shock when the temperature difference between it and water is large. A sudden plunge from room temperature into very hot water — or the reverse — can stress the glass, particularly in thin beaded pieces. Use cool or lukewarm water and let the piece return to room temperature gradually.

**Metal jewelry fittings:** Water itself does not harm green goldstone, but clasps, wire wraps, and findings made from base metals (copper alloy, brass, plated steel) will corrode with repeated wetting and drying cycles. If your piece has metal fittings, wipe it dry quickly and keep water contact brief. Sterling silver and gold fittings resist corrosion better than plated metals.

**Adhesive settings:** Some cabochons are glued into bezels rather than set mechanically. Prolonged soaking may soften certain glues over time. A brief rinse is fine; avoid leaving glued pieces submerged.

**Scratching risk:** Water itself does not scratch green goldstone, but if sand or grit is present in the water or on the cloth used to dry it, that grit can scratch the polished surface. Use clean water and a soft cloth.

## Salt Water and Green Goldstone

Salt water does not chemically react with green goldstone the way it would with an iron-bearing mineral. However, salt is corrosive to metal fittings and can leave a salt crust on the glass surface if it evaporates before the piece is rinsed. If your green goldstone is a loose tumbled stone or sphere with no metal, brief contact with salt water is not harmful, and a plain water rinse afterward is sufficient. For jewelry pieces with metal settings, keep salt water away from the fittings. For guidance on using salt in crystal practice, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## Sunlight After Water Cleansing

Green goldstone is synthetic glass and does not fade in sunlight the way dyed stones or color-treated minerals can. Placing it in sunlight to dry after a water cleanse is fine and is used by many practitioners as a combined drying and recharging step.
