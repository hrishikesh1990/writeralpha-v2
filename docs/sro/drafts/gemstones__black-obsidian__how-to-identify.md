# How to Identify Real Black Obsidian

Real black obsidian is an opaque volcanic glass showing flow lines, tiny gas bubbles, or swirl marks when held to bright light. It fractures with curved, sharp-edged (conchoidal) breaks and has a Mohs hardness of 5 to 5.5. Bright, uniformly colored, or transparent "obsidian" sold online — especially in vivid blue, red, or crystal-clear forms — is almost certainly manufactured glass, not natural obsidian.

## What Real Black Obsidian Looks Like

Black is the most common color of natural obsidian, caused by microscopic inclusions and iron content. Genuine black obsidian is typically:

- **Opaque or very slightly translucent** in thin edges when held to a bright light source. Thick pieces are fully opaque.
- **Vitreous (glassy) luster**: the surface reflects light smoothly, like glass, not like a grainy or waxy stone.
- **Internally patterned**: when held under a strong light at an angle, look for torpedo-shaped gas bubbles, faint swirl marks from lava flow, or dark flow banding. These natural inclusions are formed during rapid cooling.
- **Smooth, non-crystalline texture**: obsidian has no visible crystal structure. It looks and feels like glass because it is a natural glass.

## The Conchoidal Fracture Test

If your piece has a broken or chipped edge, examine it. Real obsidian breaks conchoidally — the fracture surface shows smooth, curved, shell-like ridges. This produces very sharp edges (obsidian was historically used to make surgical blades). Manufactured glass fractures similarly, but if the chips look angular and granular rather than smooth and curved, the material is likely not glass-based.

Note: this test requires an already-chipped or broken specimen. Do not try to chip a piece to test it.

## The Light Test: Bubbles and Flow Lines

Hold the piece flat side up under a bright lamp or direct sunlight and look through or at the surface at a low angle. In genuine obsidian you will often see:

- Small elongated or round gas bubbles trapped in the glass
- Faint swirl patterns or banding from the original lava flow
- Subtle variations in the darkness of the material

A completely featureless, uniform interior with no bubbles or internal structure is more consistent with manufactured glass than natural obsidian. High-quality natural obsidian can be quite clean, however, so absence of visible inclusions does not definitively indicate fake.

## Common Fakes and How to Spot Them

Gemdat explicitly notes that many colored glasses are sold as "obsidian," particularly in obvious fakes that are brightly colored and flawless.

| Material sold as "obsidian" | How to tell |
|---|---|
| Blue "obsidian" | Natural blue obsidian essentially does not exist in commerce; vivid blue pieces are colored glass |
| Red "obsidian" | Very rare in nature; bright red, uniform pieces are glass |
| Clear or transparent "obsidian" | Natural black obsidian is opaque; water-clear pieces are glass or dyed glass |
| Green "obsidian" (bright) | Vivid uniform green is glass; natural green obsidian is grey-green and rare |
| Gold sheen obsidian | Genuine variety; sheen from gas bubble layers. Fakes exist with applied coatings |
| Rainbow obsidian | Genuine variety; iridescence from nanoparticle layers inside. Artificial coatings can mimic this; coating can be scratched off fakes |

## Hardness Check

Real obsidian has a hardness of 5 to 5.5 on the Mohs scale. A steel pocket knife blade (approximately Mohs 5.5) should be able to scratch the surface with firm pressure. A glass plate (approximately Mohs 5.5) should barely scratch or be scratched by obsidian at about the same level. This test is not definitive because common glass and obsidian have similar hardness, but it can rule out much harder imposters.

Do not scratch a finished, polished specimen in a visible area; use the base or back if testing at all.

## Weight

Black obsidian has a specific gravity of 2.35 to 2.60, comparable to common glass (2.4 to 2.5). An unusual weight — much heavier than expected for size — may indicate a different material.

## What About Dyed Obsidian?

Natural obsidian is not typically dyed; its color is intrinsic to its composition. Some sellers sell dyed or coated black glass as "black obsidian." A very high, mirror-like black sheen that looks almost metallic, combined with an unnaturally perfect appearance, can indicate a coating. Genuine polished obsidian has a glassy sheen but retains slight depth and variation when examined closely.

## Price as a Signal

Black obsidian is one of the most abundant and affordable semi-precious materials on the market. If a piece is priced far above typical market rates and is described as especially rare or premium, that claim warrants scrutiny. If a piece is extremely cheap and has suspicious coloring (vivid blue, red, or clear), it is likely glass. For price context, see [Black Obsidian price guide](/gemstones/black-obsidian/price-guide).
