# How to Cleanse Blue Lace Agate Without Damaging It

Blue Lace Agate (SiO2, Mohs 6.5 to 7) is safe for a brief rinse in plain water but should not be left in prolonged sunlight, which can permanently fade its pale blue color. Safe cleansing options include running water, moonlight, sound, smoke, and selenite. Each method is covered below, with notes on what to avoid.

## What Makes Blue Lace Agate Safe or Vulnerable

Blue Lace Agate is a chalcedony variety — microcrystalline silicon dioxide — with no cleavage and a conchoidal fracture. At Mohs 6.5 to 7, it is durable enough for daily jewelry wear and resists scratching from most household surfaces. It contains no soluble toxic compounds, so there is no chemical hazard from handling it wet or dry.

Two physical vulnerabilities are worth knowing before choosing a cleansing method:

**Color in sunlight.** The pale blue color of Blue Lace Agate is caused by Rayleigh scattering of light by fine inclusions or structural features within the chalcedony matrix — not by a pigment or dye. Prolonged exposure to direct sunlight can fade this color permanently. Brief outdoor exposure during moonlight cleansing on a clear night is not an issue, but leaving the stone on a sunny windowsill for extended periods is.

**Polish in salt water.** Blue Lace Agate is not water-soluble, but salt water over time can abrade a polished surface and, if the stone is set in metal, attack the setting. A brief dip is not harmful; routine salt-water baths are not recommended.

For a detailed discussion of water safety, see [can Blue Lace Agate go in water](/gemstones/blue-lace-agate/can-go-in-water). For a general guide to which stones are safe with each method, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Running water

Hold Blue Lace Agate under cool running tap water for 30 to 60 seconds. Visualize the water carrying away accumulated energy. Pat the stone dry immediately with a soft cloth rather than leaving it to air-dry in sunlight. This is the most straightforward method for a stone that is not water-sensitive.

### Moonlight

Place Blue Lace Agate on a dry windowsill or an outdoor surface on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the weather first: dew or rain will wet the stone without causing damage, but it is better to retrieve it before morning condensation forms if humidity is high. This method is particularly favored in crystal tradition because it combines cleansing and recharging in one step while avoiding the light-fading risk of sunlight.

### Sound

Place the stone near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so no contact with water or any other substance is required. This method is useful for multiple stones simultaneously and for specimens that are fragile or set in delicate metalwork.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold Blue Lace Agate in the smoke of burning herbs or incense for 30 to 60 seconds, moving it slowly so all surfaces are exposed. Ensure good ventilation. This method leaves no physical residue on the stone.

### Selenite slab or plate

Rest Blue Lace Agate on a dry selenite plate or beside a selenite wand for several hours or overnight. Crystal tradition treats selenite as self-cleansing and cleansing to other stones placed near it. Note that selenite itself is water-soluble and should not be rinsed; keep it dry.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, breathe in slowly, and exhale across its surface while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This requires no materials and provides a quick reset when other methods are not available.

## Methods to Avoid

| Method | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Prolonged direct sunlight | Avoid | Permanently fades the blue color |
| Salt water baths | Not recommended | Can dull polish and corrode metal settings over time |
| Boiling water or steam | Avoid | Thermal shock can crack or craze chalcedony |
| Dry salt burial | Use with care | Salt is hygroscopic and can draw moisture; brief burial in very dry conditions is low-risk but unnecessary |

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps. After cleansing, place Blue Lace Agate under moonlight (the preferred option, since sunlight risks fading), on a selenite slab, or near a clear quartz cluster. Avoid prolonged placement in direct sun. For a full guide to recharging options, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

No physical schedule is required. Crystal tradition commonly suggests cleansing Blue Lace Agate when it arrives, after a period of intense use, and roughly monthly during regular display. This is a matter of personal practice. As a practical maintenance note, inspect the stone periodically for surface dulling if it is worn as jewelry, and clean away skin oils and residue with a damp cloth followed by thorough drying. Store Blue Lace Agate away from prolonged direct light and in a soft pouch or separate compartment to protect the polish.
