# Green Opal Price Guide

Green Opal is a common (non-precious) opal variety and is among the more affordable crystal-market stones. Bulk tumbled pieces sell for roughly $13–$18 per pound (approximately $0.50–$0.90 per stone); retail single pieces run $2–$8 each. Faceted or high-quality cabochon material ranges from about $10 per carat (commercial grade) to $200 or more for exceptional specimens. Prices checked September 2026.

## What Drives Green Opal Prices

Green Opal is abundant in Madagascar, Tanzania, Peru, Australia, Brazil, and Oregon (USA), which keeps supply steady and prices moderate relative to precious opals or rarer colored stones. The following factors push individual pieces toward the higher or lower end of the range:

**Color intensity and evenness**: Vivid, uniformly saturated green commands more than pale, patchy, or mottled pieces. The most sought-after colors in current market listings tend toward bright apple green (associated with nickel-bearing Tanzanian material) and deep forest or moss green (associated with chlorite-inclusion material from Madagascar and Oregon).

**Clarity and inclusions**: High-clarity translucent material with no visible fractures or distracting inclusions typically commands a premium. However, specimens with attractive patterning — dendritic inclusions creating plant-like imagery, or interesting mottled moss patterns — can also fetch higher prices as "picture stones."

**Cut and polish quality**: Well-cut cabochons that maximize color and show a smooth, even dome are more valuable than stones with uneven polish or poor proportions. Faceted Green Opal is unusual given the stone's softness (Mohs 5.5 to 6) but exists at the collector end of the market.

**Size**: Larger specimens are rarer and priced higher per carat. The price per carat rises sharply for large, high-quality pieces.

**Origin**: Material from certain well-documented localities (Tanzanian nickel-bearing opal, Oregon material) sometimes carries a premium for traceable provenance.

**Treatments**: Opal can be treated by impregnation with oil, wax, or plastic resin to improve surface appearance. Treated material should be disclosed by sellers; untreated natural stone commands a premium where provenance is documented.

## Price Tiers with Evidence

**Bulk tumbled (wholesale)**: Madagascar Minerals lists 30–45 mm Green Opal tumbled stones at $14.95–$10.46 per pound (20–30 pieces per lb) depending on volume. Gem Avenue lists 1 lb bulk (~20–25 pieces) at $13. This puts individual wholesale tumbled pieces at roughly $0.50 to $0.90 each (September 2026 check).

**Retail tumbled stones**: Individual polished pieces at crystal shops and online retail typically run $2 to $8 per stone for common sizes (2–4 cm). Premium or larger pieces sit at $10–$20 individually.

**Commercial-grade cabochons (per carat)**: Approximately $10–$50 per carat for stones with good but not exceptional color, minor inclusions, or smaller size. Suitable for production jewelry.

**Mid-grade (per carat)**: Approximately $50–$200 per carat for vibrant, even color and good clarity; typical of quality artisan jewelry.

**High-grade / collector (per carat)**: Above $200 per carat for exceptional color saturation, clarity, attractive patterning, and significant size. Prices in this tier are highly variable and seller-dependent; treat any specific number as a guide only.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Compare like-for-like**: The price range for Green Opal is wide because quality varies enormously. A $50 piece from one seller may be genuinely different in color and clarity from a $5 piece from another — or it may simply be marketing. Compare similar sizes and qualities before committing.

**Know what you are buying**: The most common substitution in the budget market is dyed green quartz or chalcedony sold as Green Opal. Dyed material typically has more uniform, unnaturally vivid color with color concentrated in surface cracks. For full identification guidance, see [how to identify Green Opal](/gemstones/green-opal/how-to-identify).

**Ask about treatments**: Sellers of genuine, higher-end material generally disclose impregnation treatments. If a seller cannot or will not answer questions about treatments, this is worth noting.

**Check origin labeling**: "Green Opal" covers material from multiple countries with different properties and price points. Tanzanian nickel-opal and Oregon green opal are typically more expensive than bulk Madagascan material, so claimed origin should match the price.

**Durability affects use-case value**: Green Opal has a Mohs hardness of 5.5 to 6 and very poor to fair toughness — see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale) for context. Paying a premium for ring use requires careful consideration of whether the setting is protective. For pendants, earrings, and display pieces, durability is a smaller concern.
