# How to Identify Real Black Onyx

Real black onyx and a convincing fake differ in density, temperature, and surface behavior. The key fact first: nearly all commercial black onyx is dyed chalcedony — that is standard practice, not fraud. The actual identification question is whether you have genuine chalcedony versus glass, plastic, or a different black stone sold under the wrong name. Density, temperature, hardness, and a simple acetone test sort these reliably.

## The Distinction That Actually Matters

"Black onyx" in the trade refers to opaque black chalcedony, almost always dyed. True naturally black onyx (black chalcedony with no treatment) is rare; most specimens showing any natural banding command a premium. If you have purchased standard commercial black onyx, the dyeing is expected and legitimate — it does not make the stone worthless or fake. See the [black onyx price guide](/gemstones/black-onyx/price-guide) for how treatment status affects value.

What is genuinely problematic is:

- **Black glass** sold as onyx — heavier on the optical front but distinctive by feel and SG
- **Black plastic or resin** — clearly lighter and warmer
- **Other black stones** (black spinel, jet, black tourmaline, obsidian) labeled and priced as black onyx

For a broader look at authenticity across crystals, see [are online crystals real](/learn/are-online-crystals-real) and [lab-created vs natural gemstones](/compare/lab-created-vs-natural-gemstones).

## Step-by-Step Identification Tests

### 1. Temperature test (first, no tools needed)

Hold the stone for five seconds and release it. Black onyx (chalcedony) feels distinctly cool to the touch and warms slowly. Black glass behaves similarly. Black plastic or resin warms up noticeably faster, often already feeling room temperature or slightly warm straight away.

This test alone rules out plastic and resin imitations. It does not distinguish onyx from glass.

### 2. Heft test

Pick up the stone and estimate its weight relative to its size. Black onyx has a specific gravity of 2.55–2.70, meaning it feels heavier than plastic (SG ~1.2) and roughly similar to black glass (SG ~2.4–2.5). Jet — an organic material sometimes confused with onyx — is dramatically lighter (SG 1.10–1.35); if a "black onyx" piece feels almost weightless, it is almost certainly not onyx.

### 3. Acetone test (identifies dyed material)

Apply a small amount of acetone (nail polish remover) to an inconspicuous surface using a cotton swab. If color transfers to the swab, the stone is dyed. This is expected behavior for commercial black onyx — nearly universal — and does not indicate a fake. If no color transfers and the stone appears jet black throughout, it may be naturally colored material or a different stone entirely.

### 4. Hardness check

Black onyx has Mohs hardness 6.5–7 and will scratch a steel knife blade (Mohs ~5.5) and most glass (Mohs ~5.5–6). If a "black onyx" piece scratches easily with a fingernail or fails to scratch glass at all, it is likely softer material — possibly plastic, jet, or a resin.

Perform this test on an inconspicuous edge to avoid damaging the finish.

### 5. Surface inspection under magnification

Examine the surface with a loupe (10x) if available:

- **Black onyx (chalcedony)**: fine, even microcrystalline texture; no bubbles; conchoidal fracture on any chips
- **Black glass**: may show rounded gas bubbles just below the surface; conchoidal fracture; glassy, more uniform appearance
- **Jet**: smooth, slightly resinous surface; may show faint wood-grain striations
- **Plastic/resin**: molding lines possible; surface texture may look slightly grainy or have micro-swirls

### 6. Specific gravity (definitive, requires a scale)

Weigh the stone in air, then weigh it suspended in water. SG = weight in air ÷ (weight in air − weight in water). Black onyx chalcedony: 2.55–2.70. Black glass: ~2.4–2.5. Black spinel: ~3.60. Black tourmaline: ~3.06. Jet: 1.10–1.35. A result outside the chalcedony range points to a different material.

## Common Look-alikes at a Glance

| Material | SG | Mohs | Key distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black onyx (chalcedony) | 2.55–2.70 | 6.5–7 | Acetone lifts dye; cool touch; scratches glass |
| Black glass | ~2.4–2.5 | 5–6 | Gas bubbles under loupe; isotropic |
| Jet (organic) | 1.10–1.35 | 2.5–4 | Much lighter; warm to touch; may show grain |
| Black spinel | ~3.60 | 8 | Much heavier than onyx; vitreous luster |
| Black tourmaline | ~3.06 | 7–7.5 | Visible striations; heavier; pleochroic |
| Obsidian | 2.35–2.60 | 5–5.5 | Glassy; conchoidal fracture; softer |

## Price as an Indicator

Genuine black onyx (dyed chalcedony) is an affordable stone. If a price seems extremely low — loose stones for a few cents each — quality or authenticity deserves scrutiny. If a price seems extremely high with claims of "rare natural untreated black onyx," independent certification from a gemological laboratory is the appropriate response.
