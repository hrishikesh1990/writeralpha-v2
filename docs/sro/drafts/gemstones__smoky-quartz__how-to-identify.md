# How to Identify Real Smoky Quartz and Spot Fakes

Real Smoky Quartz is silicon dioxide (SiO2) at Mohs hardness 7, with a vitreous luster, conchoidal fracture, no cleavage, and no fluorescence under UV. The most common substitutes sold as Smoky Quartz are glass, dyed clear quartz, and — the hardest case — artificially irradiated rock crystal, which shares the same chemistry and cannot be told apart by standard consumer tests. The steps below let you screen out the obvious fakes.

## Physical Tests You Can Do at Home

### Hardness test

Smoky Quartz scratches ordinary glass (Mohs 5.5) cleanly. Hold a piece of flat glass and scrape the suspected specimen across it firmly. Real quartz leaves a clean scratch; glass does not. Be careful: glass dust from this test is an irritant, and the scratch on the glass may be reversible if it is actually glass powder from a glass imposter rubbing off rather than a true scratch. Run your finger across the mark — powder brushes away; a true scratch does not.

A steel knife blade is Mohs 5.5 to 6.5. Smoky Quartz should scratch the blade, not the other way around.

### Luster and surface quality

Genuine Smoky Quartz has a vitreous (glassy) luster on cut or polished faces and on fresh fracture surfaces. Glass imposters also have vitreous luster, so this test alone does not separate them. Look instead for:

- **Bubbles** inside the specimen — tiny spherical bubbles are a sign of glass, not quartz.
- **Swirl marks** under magnification — glass often shows flow lines; quartz crystal shows natural growth zoning or needle-like inclusions.
- **Perfectly uniform color** throughout — natural Smoky Quartz frequently shows color zoning, with darker zones in some areas and lighter in others.

### Temperature

Quartz conducts heat away from your fingertips faster than glass does. A real quartz specimen will feel cool to the touch and take longer to warm in your hand than a piece of glass of similar size. This is not a definitive test but is a useful quick check.

### Fracture

Break a chip off a disposable piece (never do this to a stone you want to keep): real quartz shows conchoidal fracture — smooth, curved, shell-like breaks. Glass also shows conchoidal fracture, so this test separates quartz from some other materials but not from glass.

## What to Look For in Genuine Smoky Quartz

Natural Smoky Quartz often shows:

- Color that grades from dark brown or gray at the tip to lighter toward the base (especially in crystal points)
- Natural internal features: fine needle-like inclusions, growth phantoms, faint lines parallel to crystal faces
- Slight dichroism — when viewed from different angles in polarized light, the color shifts between darker yellow-brown and lighter red-brown

Very dark, almost black material sold as Smoky Quartz (sometimes called Morion) is genuine at the deep end of the natural range but is also the color most likely to have been produced by artificial irradiation of plain clear quartz. Completely uniform near-black color with no gradient is a mild red flag.

## Artificially Irradiated Rock Crystal

The most difficult identification challenge is distinguishing naturally colored Smoky Quartz from rock crystal (colorless quartz) that has been artificially irradiated to produce the smoky color. Both are SiO2; both have the same hardness, refractive index (1.544–1.553), specific gravity (2.65), and optical properties. According to Lazzarelli's Blue Chart Gem Identification (2010), cited by Gemdat, artificial irradiation of rock crystal to produce smoky quartz color is not detectable by standard gem trade tests.

From a crystal practice standpoint, artificial irradiation remains a real physical process producing a physically identical stone — the question of natural versus treated is relevant to collectors and to buyers seeking unenhanced material. See [lab-created vs natural gemstones](/compare/lab-created-vs-natural-gemstones) for context on the broader treated vs natural distinction.

What sellers sometimes do is irradiate very low-grade or colorless Arkansas quartz in bulk. Nearly-black, uniformly dark, very inexpensive material from unknown sources is more likely to be treated. Fine, naturally-colored Smoky Quartz from documented localities (Brazil, Scotland's Cairngorm, Swiss Alpine deposits) commands a modest premium and is more likely to be sold with provenance information.

## Common Fakes and Red Flags

| What is sold as Smoky Quartz | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| Brown glass | Bubbles under magnification; does not scratch glass; usually lighter and warmer |
| Dyed clear quartz | Color concentrated in cracks or surface; fades with acetone on cotton |
| Artificially irradiated rock crystal | Cannot be distinguished by standard tests |
| Smoky topaz (trade name) | Actually Smoky Quartz; "smoky topaz" is a misleading trade name, not a separate mineral |

The label "smoky topaz" is a trade term used in some markets for brown smoky quartz. It is not topaz. Topaz has different properties (Mohs 8, different RI and SG). If a seller uses this label, it is usually either a marketing term for genuine smoky quartz or a sign of mislabeling — worth asking for clarification.
