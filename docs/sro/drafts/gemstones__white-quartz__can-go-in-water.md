# Can White Quartz Go in Water? Safety Explained

Yes, White Quartz is safe in water. It is silicon dioxide (SiO2) with a Mohs hardness of 7, no soluble components, and no chemical reaction with water at normal temperatures. A brief rinse or short soak poses no risk to a polished or tumbled stone. Two things to avoid: hot water, which carries a thermal shock risk, and prolonged soaking of raw, unpolished specimens.

## Why White Quartz Is Water-Safe

White Quartz is a variety of quartz — the same mineral family as clear quartz, amethyst, and citrine — sharing the formula SiO2. Silicon dioxide does not dissolve in water at normal temperatures and pressures, does not rust, and contains no soluble salts or toxic compounds that would leach into water. Its Mohs hardness of 7 means it is harder than most minerals that water exposure would damage.

The white opacity of milky quartz comes from microscopic fluid inclusions trapped inside the crystal during formation — tiny pockets of water or gas that scatter light. These inclusions do not make the stone fragile or water-reactive. The crystal formed in the presence of water-rich fluids and brief re-exposure to water does not disturb its structure.

Compare this to water-unsafe stones: selenite (gypsum, Mohs 2) dissolves in water; pyrite oxidizes and corrodes on contact with moisture; malachite is mildly toxic and should not be used in elixirs. White Quartz has none of these problems. For a site-wide reference on water-safe and water-unsafe stones, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## What Is Safe and What to Avoid

| Method | Safe? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brief rinse under cool tap water | Yes | Standard cleansing method |
| Short soak (up to 30 minutes) | Yes, for polished stones | Dry thoroughly after |
| Salt water, brief rinse | Yes, with caution | Rinse salt off immediately; do not leave residue |
| Moon water (direct immersion) | Yes | White Quartz is commonly used for moon water preparation |
| Hot water | No | Thermal shock can cause microfractures in any crystal |
| Prolonged soaking (several hours) | Not recommended | Not harmful chemically, but unnecessary; may dull surface over time |
| Raw, unpolished specimens, long soaks | Caution | Brief rinse is fine; micro-fragments possible with extended soaking |

## Salt Water

White Quartz is [salt-safe](/learn/salt-safe-crystals) as well as water-safe. A brief rinse in salt water or dry-salt burial will not damage the mineral. Prolonged soaking in salt water is not recommended for any stone, however: salt's abrasive quality can gradually dull a polished surface, and dried salt residue can draw ambient moisture into a stone's surface crevices. Rinse with plain water and dry thoroughly after any salt contact.

## Sunlight After Water Cleansing

White Quartz is also safe in sunlight — its white or colorless appearance has no UV-sensitive color centers to fade. After water cleansing, drying White Quartz in brief morning sunlight is a practical and traditionally recommended step. See [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals) for the site-wide guide on which stones tolerate sun exposure.

## Practical Cleaning Steps

For physical cleaning (removing dirt or dust):

1. Rinse briefly under cool or lukewarm running water.
2. Gently scrub with a soft toothbrush if needed — at Mohs 7, White Quartz will not be scratched by a soft brush.
3. Pat dry with a clean cloth.
4. Allow to air dry fully before storage or display.

For energetic cleansing by water, the process is the same: a short, mindful rinse under running water, then dry.
