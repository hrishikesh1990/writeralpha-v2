# Pyrite Price Guide: What It Costs and Why

Pyrite is one of the most affordable stones in the crystal market because it is geologically abundant. Tumbled stones and basic bracelets typically sell for a few dollars each at retail; collector-quality natural cubic specimens from premium origins can reach tens to hundreds of dollars. The form, luster, size, and origin of the specimen are the main price drivers.

## What Makes Pyrite Inexpensive Overall

Pyrite (FeS2) is the most common sulfide mineral in Earth's crust, found in sedimentary deposits, hydrothermal veins, and metamorphic rocks on every continent. That abundance keeps the raw material cost low. Unlike gemstones sold by carat — where weight is the primary unit — pyrite is almost always sold by the piece or by specimen type: tumbled stone, rough cluster, display cube, bead, or polished form.

Because pyrite is not rare, the price consumers pay reflects labor (cutting, polishing, beading), origin reputation, and visual quality more than raw material scarcity.

## Price Ranges by Form (September 2026)

The ranges below come from online retail and wholesale listings checked in September 2026. Prices vary by seller, country of purchase, and grade; treat these as orientation points, not fixed market rates.

| Form | Typical retail range | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Tumbled stones (small–medium) | $2 – $8 | per stone |
| Beaded bracelet (8 mm beads) | $5 – $25 | per bracelet |
| Rough cluster or nodule | $5 – $30 | per piece |
| Natural cubic display specimen | $15 – $80+ | per specimen |
| Collector cubic specimen (Navajun, Spain) | $30 – $200+ | per specimen |
| Polished sphere or palm stone | $10 – $40 | per piece |

Wholesale prices are substantially lower — bulk bracelet beads from trade suppliers were listed around $3 USD per bracelet at wholesale in 2025–2026 — which explains why retail markup varies widely across sellers.

## The Main Price Drivers

**Crystal habit and formation.** Pyrite forms in several habits: perfect cubes (the most prized), pyritohedra (twelve-sided forms), striated masses, nodular concretions, and fine-grained disseminated grains. Perfect natural cubes with sharp edges and no tarnish command the highest prices. Massive or fine-grained pyrite has minimal collector value.

**Luster.** A bright, mirror-like metallic surface indicates minimal oxidation and careful handling or storage. Dull, tarnished, or powdery surfaces indicate oxidation damage — a physical defect that lowers value and may indicate the specimen is actively degrading.

**Origin.** Navajun, La Rioja, Spain produces world-famous pyrite cubes embedded in marlstone matrix that have become the benchmark for collector-grade material. Peruvian pyrite clusters and Brazilian specimens are also common on the market at mid-range prices. Spanish Navajun cubes at display size carry a significant premium.

**Size.** Larger specimens cost more, but larger pieces of lower quality cost less than smaller pieces of exceptional luster and formation. A 2 cm flawless Spanish cube is typically worth more than a 5 cm oxidized cluster.

**Polish and form.** Cutting and polishing add labor cost. Spheres and palm stones require more work than tumbled stones, which in turn require more work than rough pieces. However, polishing hides surface quality; for rough and matrix specimens, clarity of the natural crystal is the quality signal.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

Check luster before buying. A bright, reflective surface is the clearest sign of quality. Dull or powdery surfaces can indicate pyrite decay in progress, which will worsen over time regardless of what you pay.

Compare the price to available alternatives. Because pyrite is common, a very high price without a clear justification — exceptional size, verified Spanish origin, pristine cube formation — is a signal to look elsewhere.

Know the difference between natural pyrite and imitations. Some sellers market glass, synthetic resin, or dyed material as pyrite. Learning to identify genuine pyrite by streak (greenish-black), hardness (6–6.5), and weight (notably heavy) is the best protection. ## Pyrite as a Metaphysical Purchase

Pyrite's traditional associations with prosperity and abundance — covered fully on the [pyrite hub page](/gemstones/pyrite) and in [crystals for money guides](/crystals/crystals-for-money) — have increased retail demand. Higher demand from the wellness market has pushed some retail prices above what the geology alone would suggest. A $25 tumbled pyrite stone is not a better mineral specimen than a $4 one from a reputable supplier; it may simply reflect a different sales channel, branding, or markup.
