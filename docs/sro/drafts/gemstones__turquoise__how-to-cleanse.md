# How to Cleanse Turquoise Without Damaging It

Turquoise should not be soaked or rinsed with water. The mineral is naturally porous and absorbs liquids readily, which can cause discoloration, chemical damage from soaps or salts, and — in untreated specimens — structural weakening over time. Safe cleansing options include smoke, sound, moonlight, dry earth burial, and breath or visualization.

## Why Water and Salt Are Risky for Turquoise

Turquoise (CuAl₆(PO₄)₄(OH)₈·4H₂O) is a hydrated copper-aluminum phosphate with a Mohs hardness of 5 to 6. The mineral is naturally porous, which is why most commercially sold turquoise has been stabilized with resin or wax to harden the surface and improve color stability. Even stabilized material can absorb moisture through micro-cracks in the treatment layer, and untreated or lightly treated specimens are more vulnerable still.

Water itself will not dissolve turquoise, but prolonged contact allows it to seep into the stone's pore structure. When it dries unevenly, it can carry dissolved salts or impurities into the stone and alter color. Soaps, lotions, and cleaning agents introduce chemicals that turquoise absorbs and cannot easily release. Salt water combines the risks of moisture with the corrosive effect of dissolved salts. Even brief contact with high-chlorine water — such as swimming pool water — can affect the surface of an untreated stone.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the turquoise piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, moving it so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is the most widely used cleansing method for turquoise in crystal practice and poses no physical risk to the stone. Ensure the room is ventilated; the smoke affects you as well as the stone.

### Sound

Place turquoise near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. The stone never needs to touch anything liquid, which makes this method particularly useful for set jewelry pieces where removing the stone is not practical.

### Moonlight

Set turquoise on a dry surface — a windowsill, a shelf near an open window, or outdoors on a clear night — and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the weather before placing it outside: dew or overnight humidity can deposit moisture on the surface. Bring the piece indoors before morning condensation forms. In crystal tradition, moonlight is considered a full cleanse-and-recharge in a single step.

### Dry earth burial

Bury turquoise in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or clean sand for 24 hours. Place the stone in a shallow hollow, cover it fully, then retrieve and brush off any loose material with a dry cloth. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp. In traditional practice this is used to ground and reset the stone's energy.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the turquoise in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale steadily across the stone's surface while focusing on releasing any accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and is the quickest option between longer sessions.

## What to Avoid

- Water soaking or rinsing (including brief rinses for set jewelry)
- Salt water or dry salt burial (salt is hygroscopic and draws moisture toward the stone's surface)
- Ultrasonic cleaners (vibration can fracture porous material and dislodge any existing resin treatment)
- Steam cleaning (heat and moisture combined)
- Household cleaning products, soap, or alcohol wipes

Wipe fingerprints and surface oils with a dry or very slightly dampened soft cloth only, and dry immediately if any moisture is used.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal tradition treats cleansing and recharging as two separate steps. After cleansing, turquoise can be placed in brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes — an opaque mineral is not at significant risk of fading from short exposure, though prolonged sunlight may affect some treated surfaces over time), beside a cluster of clear quartz, or on a dry selenite slab. ## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions offer varying guidance. A common recommendation is to cleanse turquoise when it arrives, after any period of intensive wear or use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice. As a practical maintenance note: inspect the surface periodically for any cloudiness or color change, which may indicate absorbed oils or moisture that should prompt a change in storage conditions rather than a water rinse.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store turquoise away from direct sunlight, heat sources, and humid areas such as bathrooms. Keep it separate from harder stones (diamonds, sapphires, corundum) that can scratch its 5–6 Mohs surface. A soft pouch or lined box is sufficient. Remove turquoise jewelry before swimming, showering, applying cosmetics, or using cleaning products.
