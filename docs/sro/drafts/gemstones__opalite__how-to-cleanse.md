# How to Cleanse and Recharge Opalite

Opalite is synthetic glass, which means it is water-safe and physically easy to cleanse — a brief rinse under cool running water will not damage it. Additional methods include smoke, sound, moonlight, and selenite. Each method is covered below, along with what to avoid.

## What Opalite Is Made Of

Opalite is a trade name for synthetic opalescent glass, not a natural mineral. It is produced from glass — sometimes combined with dolomite or resin — and has a Mohs hardness of about 5.5 to 6. Because it is glass, it does not react with water the way reactive minerals such as pyrite or selenite do, and it carries no toxic components. This makes the full range of common cleansing methods available without the restrictions that apply to many natural stones.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Water rinse

Hold opalite under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 to 30 seconds. Pat dry with a soft cloth immediately afterward. Avoid hot water, which can cause thermal shock in glass, and avoid prolonged soaking, which is unnecessary and may eventually affect any surface coating on specialty-treated pieces. Plain tap water at room temperature poses no risk to standard opalite glass.

Do not use ultrasonic cleaners. The intense vibration can create micro-fractures in glass and may disturb any added surface treatments or coatings.

### Smoke cleansing

Pass opalite slowly through the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, ensuring all surfaces are exposed. Ventilate the room while doing this. Smoke cleansing is widely used in crystal practice as a method for energetic clearing and carries no physical risk to glass.

### Sound

Place opalite near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and let the vibration wash over it for one to two minutes. Because sound works at a distance, the stone does not need to be touched, moved through liquid, or exposed to heat. This method is particularly convenient for carved or decorative opalite pieces that are awkward to move.

### Moonlight

Set opalite on a dry windowsill or outdoors on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Glass will not be damaged by overnight air exposure in dry conditions. Check that the surface will remain dry — avoid dew or rain contact as a precaution for any surface coatings. Bring the piece indoors before morning condensation forms.

### Selenite

Place opalite on a selenite slab or inside a selenite bowl for several hours. In crystal tradition, selenite is used to cleanse and recharge other stones without any physical contact method required. Selenite itself is a soft mineral (Mohs 2) and will not scratch opalite, but keep opalite away from coarser or harder stones that could abrade its surface.

### Sunlight

Brief sunlight exposure — 15 to 30 minutes — poses no physical risk to opalite glass. Prolonged direct sunlight over many months may gradually affect specialty coatings or dyes on treated pieces, but standard opalite glass is stable. Crystal tradition uses sunlight as a recharging method rather than a cleansing one; the two steps are covered separately below.

## Methods to Avoid

**Ultrasonic cleaners.** The vibration can cause micro-fractures in glass or damage surface treatments. Hand-cleansing methods work reliably for opalite and there is no need for ultrasonic cleaning.

**Harsh chemicals.** Household cleaning agents, bleach, or acidic solutions can damage surface coatings on specialty opalite pieces. Mild soap on a soft cloth, followed by rinsing and drying, is sufficient for any physical dirt.

**Salt water or dry salt burial.** While opalite glass is not chemically reactive with salt the way pyrite or malachite are, salt is abrasive and may scratch a polished glass surface. It is also hygroscopic and can draw moisture toward the piece. Plainer methods work just as well.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps. After cleansing, opalite is traditionally recharged by placing it in morning sunlight for 15 to 30 minutes, resting it on a selenite slab overnight, or positioning it near a clear quartz cluster. Moonlight overnight also serves as a combined cleanse-and-recharge in many practitioners' routines.

For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary widely on frequency. Common guidelines suggest cleansing opalite when it first arrives, after any period of intensive use, and roughly once a month during regular display. This is a matter of personal practice and has no physical basis. As a physical maintenance note, wipe opalite with a soft dry cloth periodically to remove dust and fingerprints, which can dull the iridescent surface over time.
