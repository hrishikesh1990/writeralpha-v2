# How to Cleanse Green Goldstone Without Damaging It

Green Goldstone is synthetic glass, which means water cleansing is safe — unlike reactive minerals such as pyrite or selenite. Rinse briefly under cool running water, or use smoke, sound, moonlight, or breath. The one thing to avoid is abrasive scrubbing and harsh chemical cleaners, which can dull the polished surface over time.

## Why Green Goldstone's Glass Composition Matters for Cleansing

Green Goldstone is a man-made glass consisting of a silica base tinted with chromium compounds, with metallic copper crystals grown through the glass by a controlled kiln process. Glass is chemically stable in water — it does not oxidize, dissolve, or corrode the way reactive minerals do. This makes green goldstone significantly easier to care for than many natural stones.

The material rates 5.5 to 6 on the Mohs scale. That means it is harder than a fingernail or copper coin but softer than quartz. Abrasive materials — including stiff brushes, scouring pads, and ultrasonic cleaners running abrasive solutions — can scratch the polished surface. The goal with any cleaning method is to remove surface oils and dust without mechanical abrasion. For more context on which crystals can and cannot be cleaned with water, see [what crystals can go in water](/gemstones/green-goldstone/can-go-in-water).

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Water rinse

Hold green goldstone under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 to 30 seconds. Rub the surface gently with your fingers to dislodge oils or dust. Dry immediately with a soft lint-free cloth. Do not use hot water: rapid temperature change (thermal shock) can stress glass, particularly in thinner pieces or beads. This is the quickest physical cleanse for pieces you handle frequently.

### Smoke cleansing

Pass green goldstone through the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a wood-based candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Rotate the piece slowly so all surfaces are exposed. Ensure good ventilation in the room. Smoke cleansing leaves no physical residue on the glass and poses no risk to the surface.

### Sound

Place green goldstone near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and sound it for one to two minutes. The stone does not need to be touched, making this method ideal for jewelry pieces where you want to avoid any moisture contact on metal fittings. Sound cleansing is also useful for multiple pieces at once.

### Moonlight

Set green goldstone on a dry windowsill or outdoors on a dry surface overnight under moonlight. Unlike porous natural stones, glass does not absorb moisture — a brief encounter with dew will not damage the piece, though wiping it dry afterward is still good practice. This method is popular in crystal tradition as a combined cleanse and recharge.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the piece in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires nothing else and is used when other options are unavailable or for a quick reset between uses.

## Methods to Avoid

**Abrasive cleaners:** Toothpaste, scouring powders, and stiff brushes will scratch the polished glass surface. Over time this dulls the aventurescence — the internal sparkle that defines green goldstone's appearance.

**Harsh chemicals:** Bleach, ammonia-based glass cleaners, and acetone can attack adhesives used in jewelry settings and may dull the surface of the glass itself. A plain water rinse is the safest physical cleaner.

**Dry salt burial:** Commonly used for natural minerals, dry salt is hygroscopic — it draws moisture from the air. If salt sits against a metal setting or finding attached to the stone, it will corrode the metal over weeks of contact. Avoid this method for set pieces.

**Prolonged hot water or steam:** A brief warm rinse is fine. Extended soaking in hot water or steam-cleaning, which is sometimes used for gemstones, risks thermal shock in thinner glass pieces and may loosen adhesives in glued settings.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps in most crystal traditions. After cleansing green goldstone, practitioners place it in morning sunlight (brief exposure poses no color-change risk to an opaque synthetic glass), near a cluster of clear quartz, or on a dry selenite slab. ## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary widely on frequency. A common guideline is to cleanse green goldstone when it arrives, after any period of intensive intentional use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical requirement to cleanse glass on any particular schedule — no oxidation or chemical change occurs from regular handling. As a maintenance note, inspect the polish periodically; if the surface looks dull, a water rinse and gentle dry buff with a microfibre cloth will restore it.
