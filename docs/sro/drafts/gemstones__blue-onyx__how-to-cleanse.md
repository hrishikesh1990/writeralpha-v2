# How to Cleanse Blue Onyx Without Damaging It

Blue Onyx is a dyed chalcedony with a Mohs hardness of 6.5–7 and no chemical water solubility. A brief rinse under clean water is physically safe, but prolonged soaking risks fading or bleeding the dye that gives most commercial specimens their blue color. The safest routine cleansing methods are smoke, sound, moonlight, and selenite — none of which risk the surface.

## What Blue Onyx Is Made Of

Blue Onyx is a banded variety of chalcedony, microcrystalline quartz (SiO₂). Its hardness (6.5–7 on the Mohs scale) means water does not scratch or dissolve it. However, the majority of commercial blue onyx is dyed to deepen or create the blue color, and dyes can degrade with extended water exposure, soap, or heat. Before choosing a cleansing method, consider whether your piece is dyed — if the blue is very uniform and saturated, it almost certainly is.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the Blue Onyx piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Move it slowly so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is physically harmless to the stone and carries no risk of dye damage or surface erosion. Ventilate the room; you are breathing the smoke too.

### Sound

Place Blue Onyx near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. The stone does not need to be touched or moved through any medium. Sound cleansing is particularly convenient for larger decorative pieces that are awkward to smudge.

### Moonlight

Set Blue Onyx on a dry surface — a windowsill or outdoor ledge — on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast: rain, dew, or high overnight humidity means the stone will be wetted, which is not ideal for dyed specimens left unattended. Bring it in before morning condensation forms.

### Selenite slab or cluster

Place Blue Onyx on or beside a dry [selenite](/gemstones/selenite) slab for several hours. This is one of the most popular low-effort methods in crystal practice and poses zero risk to the Blue Onyx surface. Note that selenite itself is water-soluble and must be kept dry.

### Brief water rinse (with caveats)

A 30-second rinse under cool, clean running water is physically safe for Blue Onyx given its hardness and insolubility. Use plain water only — soap and cleaning agents can affect the dye finish over time. Pat dry with a soft cloth immediately after rinsing. Do not soak, and do not use salt water. For how often this is appropriate versus the risks, see [can Blue Onyx go in water](/gemstones/blue-onyx/can-go-in-water).

### Breath and visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, take a slow breath in, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing on clearing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires nothing and leaves no physical residue — useful as a quick reset between uses.

## Methods to Avoid or Limit

| Method | Risk |
|---|---|
| Prolonged soaking | May bleed or fade dye |
| Salt water bath | Salt is mildly abrasive and corrosive to dye finish |
| Dry salt burial | Salt is hygroscopic; may draw moisture and affect dye |
| Extended direct sunlight | May fade dye with repeated or long exposures |
| Ultrasonic cleaner | Vibration can loosen dye from the chalcedony matrix |
| Steam cleaning | Heat and moisture together risk dye degradation |

## Recharging After Cleansing

Recharging is treated as a separate step in most crystal traditions. After cleansing, place Blue Onyx in brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes is commonly suggested — short enough not to risk dye fading), on a [clear quartz](/gemstones/clear-quartz) cluster, on a dry selenite slab, or on a windowsill under the moon. For a full guide to charging methods and their rationale, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary widely. Common guidance is to cleanse a stone when it first arrives, after periods of intense use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical reason a specific schedule is required — this is personal practice. As a routine care note, inspect your Blue Onyx periodically for any surface color change; fading usually indicates dye aging rather than anything that cleansing will fix, but keeping the stone out of prolonged sunlight and away from moisture will slow it.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store Blue Onyx in a cool, dry location away from direct sunlight and steam. A cloth pouch, lined box, or display case works well. Avoid storing it alongside selenite, rock salt, or other hygroscopic materials that may bring moisture. The full properties overview is at [Blue Onyx](/gemstones/blue-onyx).
