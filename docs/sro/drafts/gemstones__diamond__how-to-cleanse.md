# How to Cleanse Diamond Without Damaging It

Diamond is safe for water, mild soap, smoke, sound, moonlight, and sunlight. As pure carbon rated Mohs 10, it is chemically inert and the hardest natural material known, so most cleansing methods carry no physical risk to the stone itself. The main care considerations involve the metal setting and any treatments applied to the diamond, not the diamond itself.

## Why Diamond Is One of the Easiest Stones to Cleanse

Diamond (chemical formula: C) does not react with water, salt, smoke, or most cleaning agents under normal conditions. It does not oxidize, rust, dissolve, or fade. This makes it physically distinct from many other stones used in crystal practice — pyrite oxidizes in water, selenite dissolves, and many colored stones fade in prolonged sunlight. Diamond has none of these vulnerabilities.

The one caveat: some diamonds sold as crystal pieces have been fracture-filled (a clarity enhancement where resin is injected into surface-reaching cracks) or laser-drilled. GIA recommends avoiding ultrasonic cleaners and steam cleaners for fracture-filled diamonds, as heat and vibration can disturb the filling. If you are uncertain whether your stone has been treated, avoid those methods and use warm water and mild soap instead.

## Physical Cleaning: Removing Oils and Grime

Diamond's adamantine luster attracts oils from fingerprints and skin. Regular physical cleaning maintains the stone's brilliance and is effective alongside any energetic practice.

### Warm water and mild soap

Fill a small bowl with warm (not hot) water and a few drops of dish soap. Soak the stone for five to ten minutes, then gently scrub all surfaces with a soft toothbrush. Pay attention to the underside of the stone and around prongs where oils accumulate. Rinse under running warm water and pat dry with a lint-free cloth. This method is safe for loose stones and most settings.

**Avoid:** chlorine bleach, household abrasive cleaners, and toothpaste. Chlorine can damage metal settings (gold, silver) without harming the diamond itself.

## Energetic Cleansing Methods in Crystal Tradition

The methods below are used in crystal practice to reset the stone's energetic state. They are described as tradition; there is no scientific basis for energetic cleansing.

### Moonlight

Place diamond on a dry surface under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Diamond's chemical stability means no moisture concern outdoors on a calm night, though dew or rain will not harm the stone. Moonlight cleansing is one of the most commonly recommended methods in crystal tradition for a combined cleanse and recharge.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the diamond piece in the smoke of burning herbs or incense for 30 to 60 seconds, moving it slowly through the smoke so all faces are exposed. Diamond's hardness and inertness mean smoke poses no risk. Ensure the room is ventilated.

### Sound

Place diamond near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and let the vibration pass around it for one to two minutes. This method is useful when you want to cleanse multiple stones simultaneously or for stones set in jewelry that cannot be soaked.

### Sunlight

Short exposure to direct sunlight (15 to 30 minutes) carries no risk to diamond. Unlike some colored stones (amethyst, for example, may fade) or coated stones, diamond is photostable — its color does not change with light exposure. Prolonged sun exposure is unnecessary but harmless.

### Dry salt or selenite

Placing diamond in a bowl of dry salt or on a selenite slab overnight is used in crystal tradition for grounding and resetting. Salt will not damage diamond physically; rinse the stone afterward if using loose salt to prevent any salt residue from affecting the metal setting. Do not use wet salt water directly on fine settings.

### Visualization and intention

Hold the diamond in both hands, breathe slowly, and focus attention on clearing any accumulated energy. This method requires no materials and is used when other methods are not available.

## How Often to Cleanse

Physical cleaning of diamond jewelry is recommended every one to two weeks for pieces worn daily, as oils accumulate quickly and reduce brilliance. For crystal practice, no fixed interval exists; a common guideline is to cleanse when the stone arrives, after any period of intense use, and monthly during regular display. Inspect set diamonds periodically for loose prongs, which can cause stones to shift or fall out.
