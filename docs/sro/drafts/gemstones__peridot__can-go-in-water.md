# Can Peridot Go in Water? What's Safe and What Isn't

Peridot can go in water briefly for routine cleaning — warm water with mild soap is the recommended cleaning method. Peridot is damaged by prolonged soaking, pool or salt water, acids, ultrasonic cleaners, and steam. The damage is physical: acids etch the surface, thermal shock can cause fractures, and ultrasonic vibration can expand existing internal inclusions.

## Brief Water Exposure: Acceptable

A short rinse in warm (not hot) water with a drop of mild dish soap is the standard way to clean peridot. This removes surface oils, dust, and handling residue without harming the stone. Use a soft cloth or very soft brush, rinse thoroughly, and dry immediately with a lint-free cloth. Do not leave peridot sitting in water or allow it to soak for more than a few seconds.

## What to Avoid

**Pool and hot-tub water.** Chlorine in swimming pools and hot tubs attacks gemstone surfaces and can also degrade metal settings. Peridot should be removed before swimming.

**Salt water and ocean water.** Salt water is corrosive to peridot's surface over time. It can also accelerate damage to prong settings and metal mounts. Peridot should not be worn in the ocean or cleaned with a salt-water solution. For guidance on which stones tolerate salt, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

**Hot water and steam.** Peridot is sensitive to thermal shock — a sudden change in temperature. Very hot water, steam cleaners, and steam jewelry cleaners all carry the risk of fracturing the stone, particularly along existing internal stress fractures (lily pads). Always use warm, not hot, water.

**Acids.** Household cleaners, vinegar, lemon juice, chlorine bleach, and many commercial jewelry cleaning solutions are acidic enough to etch peridot's vitreous surface. Even mild acids leave dull patches visible under light.

**Ultrasonic cleaners.** The vibrations in ultrasonic jewelry cleaners can cause peridot's characteristic lily-pad inclusions (disk-shaped fractures around chromite crystals) to expand and may split the stone. Professional jewelers do not ultrasonically clean peridot.

## Peridot in Water for Energetic Cleansing

Some crystal practices use water — rivers, rainwater, saltwater — as an energetic cleansing method. For peridot, a very brief rinse in clean water is the safest option if you want to use water this way. A few seconds under running water, followed by immediate drying, is unlikely to cause damage. Extended submersion or salt-water cleansing methods carry real physical risk to the stone. ## Why Peridot Is Acid-Sensitive

Peridot is a magnesium iron silicate — formula (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄ — with a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7. Its vitreous surface, while relatively hard, is attacked by acids because silicate minerals in general react with acidic solutions. This is not a defect unique to peridot; it applies to many silicate gemstones. The practical result is that household cleaning chemicals and even natural acids in sweat can, over years, leave a stone looking duller than when new if it is not rinsed and dried regularly.

## Sun Exposure After Water Cleaning

Peridot does not need sun to dry and should not be left in direct sunlight for extended periods after washing. Brief sun exposure — a few minutes — does not harm peridot. Prolonged exposure in strong direct sun carries a low risk of fading in any stone, though peridot is not strongly susceptible.
