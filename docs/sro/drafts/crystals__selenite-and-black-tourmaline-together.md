# Selenite and Black Tourmaline Together: Benefits & Care

[Selenite](/gemstones/selenite) and [black tourmaline](/gemstones/black-tourmaline) are one of the most commonly recommended pairings in crystal practice. The combination appears in traditions focused on protective space-clearing: selenite is associated with cleansing and clarity, black tourmaline with protection and grounding. Practitioners describe the two as working together to clear and shield a space or energy field. The physical care rules for this pair are among the most important to understand before storing or using both stones together.

## Physical Compatibility: A Significant Hardness Gap

**Hardness difference.** Selenite is a variety of gypsum (calcium sulfate dihydrate, CaSO₄·2H₂O) with a Mohs hardness of 2 — soft enough to scratch with a fingernail. Black tourmaline has a Mohs hardness of 7.5. The gap of 5.5 points is one of the largest between any commonly paired stones. Any contact between them — loose storage in the same bowl, one placed on top of the other, the two knocking together in transit — will scratch and gouge selenite. Selenite's surface is easily damaged. The physical rule for this pair is: never store them loose together, and never place black tourmaline directly on a selenite slab or plate.

**How people store them safely.** The most common approach is to give each stone its own space — separate trays, dishes, or shelves. Many practitioners place selenite wands or plates as standalone pieces in a room and position black tourmaline chunks or tumblestones separately in corners or near doorways. The pairing works as a room combination or intention pairing rather than a stones-in-contact arrangement.

**Selenite and water.** Selenite must not go in water. Gypsum is slightly water-soluble — prolonged contact with water dissolves the stone's surface and causes deterioration. Even brief immersion repeated over time will damage selenite. Salt water is even more damaging. Do not place selenite in any water-based cleansing method. Do not place selenite in salt, either: selenite is a sulfate mineral and the ionic exchange with salt damages the surface. Cleanse selenite with sound, moonlight, or by placing it on dry soil briefly.

**Black tourmaline and water.** Black tourmaline tolerates brief rinsing under running water. Prolonged soaking or salt-water immersion is not recommended, as it can dull the surface over time.

**Fading.** Neither stone has documented fading concerns under normal indoor conditions. Selenite is naturally colorless or white; its appearance comes from its crystal structure, not pigment. Black tourmaline's opaque black color is stable.

**Selenite's other fragility.** Beyond hardness, selenite is brittle and has perfect cleavage in one direction. It fractures easily along cleavage planes if dropped or struck. Handle selenite pieces with care, keep them away from pets and children, and place them on stable surfaces where they will not be knocked.

## Traditional Goals This Pairing Addresses

Selenite is traditionally described as a clearing stone — one that practitioners use to refresh and purify energy in a space, an object, or an aura. It is associated with the crown chakra and described as promoting clarity, calm, and access to higher guidance. Many practitioners also use selenite to "charge" or "cleanse" other crystals by placing them near (not on top of) a selenite plate.

Black tourmaline is among the most widely used protective stones in crystal practice. It is associated with the root chakra and described as creating a barrier against disruptive, draining, or negative influences, and with grounding excess or unsettled energy into the earth.

The pairing is described as covering two complementary functions: selenite clears what is already present; black tourmaline shields against what might enter. Practitioners use this combination most often for the home — particularly entryways, bedrooms, and workspaces — where both qualities are considered useful together.

See the [selenite combinations page](/gemstones/selenite/combinations) and the [black tourmaline combinations page](/gemstones/black-tourmaline/combinations) for other pairings for each stone.

## How People Use This Pair

**Spatial placement.** The most common use is room placement rather than physical contact. Selenite is placed in a central location — on a shelf, a windowsill (indirect light), or a table — while black tourmaline pieces are placed at corners, doorways, or entry points. Practitioners describe this arrangement as creating a cleared-and-protected space.

**Separate display pieces.** Selenite towers, wands, or palm stones and black tourmaline raw chunks or tumblestones are both popular display formats. Placing them in the same room on separate surfaces is the standard approach. They work as an environmental pairing rather than a stones-in-contact combination.

**Crystal grid.** In [crystal grid](/learn/crystal-grids) work, selenite and black tourmaline can be used in the same grid with care — black tourmaline at the outer points (protection perimeter), selenite at interior positions (clearing). Ensure the grid surface holds each stone in place so they cannot slide into contact.

**Not commonly worn together.** This pairing is rarely worn as jewelry. Selenite is too soft and fragile for most wearable jewelry formats, and the risk of black tourmaline contacting and scratching selenite is high in bracelets or layered necklaces. If worn, selenite needs a fully enclosed protective setting, and even then, this is an unusual choice.

## What to Avoid

Never store selenite and black tourmaline in the same container unless each is padded and fully separated. Never place selenite in water — it will dissolve and the damage is irreversible. Never place selenite in salt. Do not use black tourmaline or any other harder stone to scratch or scrub a selenite surface. Do not drop selenite; it cleaves and chips readily. Avoid placing selenite in prolonged direct sunlight, as heat can affect delicate crystal formations, though color fading is not the main concern — structural damage from thermal stress is.

## Alternatives to Consider

For a cleansing stone that can be stored more freely alongside black tourmaline, **clear quartz** (Mohs 7) is used in many traditions for energy clearing and amplification, and at Mohs 7, it is substantially closer to black tourmaline's hardness — though still harder; store separately. **Fluorite** (Mohs 4) offers some clearing and protective associations in tradition and is a more moderate hardness, though still softer than black tourmaline. For those who want the protective properties of black tourmaline but in a softer, lower-maintenance format, **shungite** (Mohs 3.5 to 4) is used in some traditions for protection and grounding and poses less risk to selenite than tourmaline does.
