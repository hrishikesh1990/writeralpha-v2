# Garnet Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Garnet prices span one of the widest ranges of any gemstone group: common red almandine or hessonite sells for under $20 per carat, while fine demantoid or tsavorite reaches $10,000 or more per carat for top-quality material. The variety, color quality, size, and origin are the four main price drivers. Price ranges below are per carat for faceted gemstone material, checked against retail market data as of September 2026.

## Price Ranges by Variety

| Garnet variety | Color | Approximate retail range (per carat) |
|---|---|---|
| Almandine | Dark red to brownish red | $20–$150 |
| Pyrope | Deep red to purplish red | $20–$100 |
| Rhodolite | Purplish red | $50–$300 |
| Hessonite | Orange to yellowish brown | $8–$120 |
| Spessartite | Orange to reddish orange | $200–$500 |
| Tsavorite | Vivid green | $400–$4,200 |
| Demantoid | Green to yellowish green | $200–$18,000 |
| Color-change garnet | Varies | $80–$6,000 |
| Uvarovite (druzy) | Bright green | $1–$3 per carat (matrix pieces) |

Source: GemSelect retail data, June 2025; checked September 2026. Prices vary by clarity grade, cut quality, carat weight, and seller. Fine specimens or certified stones command premiums above these ranges; heavily included or off-color material falls below the lower end.

## What Drives the Price

### Variety

The species is the single biggest price variable. Common red almandine — the variety most people picture when they hear "garnet" — is abundant and affordable. Tsavorite and demantoid are rare, occur in limited deposits, and are actively collected by gemologists and collectors, pushing prices dramatically higher. This variety premium is not about quality within a variety; it is structural scarcity.

### Color quality

Within any variety, saturated, vibrant color commands the highest price. Overly dark stones — too much iron in almandine, for example — appear nearly opaque and are less desirable. Brownish modifiers in any variety reduce value. The most prized red garnets are medium to medium-dark with strong, pure hue and no grey or brown.

### Clarity

Most red garnets are eye-clean — no inclusions visible to the unaided eye — and clarity is expected rather than rewarded. Demantoid is an exception: its characteristic horsetail inclusions (associated with chrysotile fibers) are considered an identifying feature and, when present, may actually support the premium value. Hessonite often has a distinctive roiled or granular internal appearance that is normal for the variety, not a defect.

### Size

All garnet varieties become rarer and disproportionately more expensive above about 3 carats. Tsavorite and demantoid above 1 carat carry significant per-carat premiums because large, clean material is uncommon. Common red almandine is available in large sizes with less dramatic price escalation.

### Treatments

Most garnets are not treated. No heat, filling, or coating is applied to the overwhelming majority of commercial garnet material. This is a buyer advantage: the price you pay is for the natural stone as found. Confirm with any reputable seller that the stone is untreated — it should be — and be cautious of any garnet described as "enhanced" without a specific explanation.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Identify the variety before comparing prices.** A "garnet" label without a variety name is ambiguous. Red almandine at $80 per carat is priced normally; tsavorite at $80 per carat is suspiciously cheap. Know which variety you are buying.

**Check for glass imitations.** Common red glass is the most frequent substitute for inexpensive garnet in fashion jewelry and crystal shops. A standard hardness test or loupe check catches most glass imitations. For the identification process in detail, see [how to identify garnet](/gemstones/garnet/how-to-identify).

**Expect no treatment.** If a seller charges a premium for "untreated" garnet without explaining why this particular stone would otherwise be treated, that framing is unusual for garnet and worth questioning.

**Compare like with like.** Per-carat prices for a 0.5-carat stone and a 5-carat stone of the same variety will differ substantially. Calibrated commercial material (standard sizes cut for mass-market settings) trades at lower per-carat prices than custom-cut or collector-grade material.

**Use certification for expensive purchases.** A gemological report from GIA, AGL, or a national lab is warranted for any garnet stone above roughly $500. For demantoid or fine tsavorite, origin documentation also matters.
