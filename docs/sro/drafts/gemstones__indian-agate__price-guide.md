# Indian Agate Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Indian Agate is one of the most affordable stones in the crystal market. Tumbled stones sell for approximately $1–5 each at retail (USD, prices observed September 2026); bead bracelets for $2–8; loose beads in wholesale packs for $1.20–3.00 per bead. The stone's abundance — India produces it in very large quantities from the Deccan Traps basalt region — keeps prices low across all formats.

## Typical Retail Price Ranges (September 2026)

| Format | Retail price range (USD) |
|---|---|
| Single tumbled stone | $1–5 per piece |
| Pack of 5 tumbled stones | $3.95–6.00 per pack |
| Beaded bracelet (6–8mm) | $2–8 per bracelet |
| Loose beads (wholesale strand) | $1.20–3.00 per bead |
| Palm stone or worry stone | $4–12 per piece |

Prices vary by seller, country, and whether the listing is wholesale or retail. The figures above reflect single-item retail purchases. Buying in bulk consistently reduces the per-unit cost.

## What Drives the Price

**Pattern and color quality.** Indian Agate with distinct, symmetrical banding, clear moss or dendritic inclusions, or a rich contrast between colors commands a higher price than plain or mottled pieces with low visual interest. Sellers who grade pieces often mark the most attractive examples at a premium.

**Size.** Larger tumbled stones, palm stones, and statement specimens cost more than small tumbled chips. A well-formed 40mm palm stone will typically cost more than a 20mm tumbled piece, even from the same material batch.

**Polish quality.** Pieces with a high, even polish fetch more than roughly polished or matte-finished stones.

**Treatment status.** Natural, undyed Indian Agate in attractive earthy tones may be priced similarly to or slightly above dyed pieces in the same format, though dyed material is often sold more cheaply in bulk craft contexts. If a seller explicitly markets undyed natural material with verified provenance, that typically carries a small premium.

**Sourcing and retail context.** Crystal boutiques and metaphysical shops typically sell individual pieces at $3–5 each. Wholesale bead suppliers sell by the strand or pack at much lower per-unit prices. Online marketplace listings vary widely; comparing several listings is the fastest way to find a fair price.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

Indian Agate is not rare. Any listing priced significantly above $5–8 per plain tumbled stone should be accompanied by a clear justification — exceptional pattern, large size, or specialty carving. For plain small tumbled pieces, $1–3 is a normal market price at retail.

Verify that you are buying agate and not a substitute material. Soft stones such as serpentine (Mohs 3–4) are occasionally sold in markets as "green agate" or with trade names that imply agate. Real Indian Agate at Mohs 6.5–7 scratches glass easily; a soft substitute will not. See [how to identify Indian Agate](/gemstones/indian-agate/how-to-identify) for hands-on tests.

Durability is a value factor for jewelry. Indian Agate at Mohs 6.5–7 is suitable for everyday bracelets and pendants; the [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale) explains what that rating means in practice for wearability.

## Ethical Sourcing Considerations

India produces agate in very large volumes from artisanal and small-scale mining operations, primarily in Gujarat and Maharashtra. Working conditions and environmental practices vary between operations. Buyers who prioritize supply-chain transparency can look for sellers who disclose their sourcing country and supply chain; some retailers hold fair-trade certifications. For a broader guide to evaluating sourcing claims, see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing).
