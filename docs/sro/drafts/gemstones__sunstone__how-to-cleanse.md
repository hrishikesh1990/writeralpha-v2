# How to Cleanse Sunstone Without Damaging It

Sunstone is a feldspar mineral with a Mohs hardness of 6 to 7.2 and no significant water-solubility, which makes it one of the more forgiving stones to cleanse. Brief rinsing under cool running water is safe, as is smoke, sound, moonlight, and dry earth burial. Extended soaking and salt water are worth avoiding. Each method is covered below.

## Water Cleansing

Sunstone can go in water for a brief wash — typically 30 to 60 seconds under cool, running water. The stone is not water-soluble and does not contain reactive metals like the iron sulfide in pyrite. A mild, fragrance-free soap may be used to remove surface oils or grime; rinse thoroughly and pat dry with a soft cloth immediately afterward.

Avoid prolonged soaking (more than a few minutes) because sustained water contact can gradually affect the polish of feldspar surfaces over time, and feldspar has two cleavage planes that make it somewhat more vulnerable to moisture working into micro-fractures. Salt water is not recommended: salt is mildly abrasive and can dull a polished surface with repeated use.

For a full breakdown of Sunstone's water compatibility, see [can Sunstone go in water](/gemstones/sunstone/can-go-in-water). For general guidance on water-safe versus water-sensitive stones, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Smoke Cleansing

Hold Sunstone in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle flame for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the stone slowly so that smoke reaches all surfaces. Ensure the room is ventilated. This method carries no physical risk to the stone and is widely used in crystal practice. It is a practical alternative when you want to avoid any moisture contact.

## Sound

Place Sunstone near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and let the vibrations pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing is contact-free, which makes it useful for polished cabochons or faceted pieces where surface abrasion is a concern. A Tibetan singing bowl, a small bell, or even a recorded frequency played near the stone are all used in practice.

## Moonlight

Set Sunstone on a clean, dry surface — indoors on a windowsill or outdoors on a clear night — and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the weather first; outdoor placement on a damp or dewy night risks moisture accumulating on the stone's surface. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms. In crystal tradition, moonlight is used for a full reset alongside recharging.

## Sunlight

Brief morning sunlight of 15 to 30 minutes poses no physical risk to an opaque or translucent feldspar stone. Sunstone is traditionally associated with solar energy, and sunlight exposure is commonly used in practice both for cleansing and recharging in a single step. Avoid extended hours of direct sun on a faceted, highly transparent piece, as prolonged UV and heat can slowly affect surface finish over years of repeated exposure — though this is a long-term consideration, not an immediate concern.

## Dry Earth Burial

Bury Sunstone in a small pot of dry, indoor potting soil or clean sand for 24 hours, then brush off with a dry cloth. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp. This method is grounding in traditional practice and poses no risk to the stone.

## Breath and Visualization

Hold Sunstone in both hands, breathe in slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while mentally focusing on releasing any stale energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and is commonly used for a quick reset between uses.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal traditions treat cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing, Sunstone can be placed in brief morning sunlight, near a cluster of clear quartz, or on a dry selenite slab. For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals). The [Sunstone hub page](/gemstones/sunstone) covers all physical properties in one place.

## How Often to Cleanse

How often to cleanse is a matter of personal practice, not physical maintenance. A common guideline is to cleanse Sunstone when it arrives, after any period of heavy or emotionally significant use, and once a month during routine display. Physically, Sunstone benefits from an occasional wipe with a dry or barely damp cloth to remove surface oils and fingerprints.
