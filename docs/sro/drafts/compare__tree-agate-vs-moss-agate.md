# Tree Agate vs Moss Agate: Key Differences

Tree agate and moss agate are both varieties of chalcedony (microcrystalline SiO₂) but differ in transparency, inclusion type, and where those inclusions form. Tree agate is opaque, with a white base and dendrite patterns of manganese or iron oxides that grew in surface fissures. Moss agate is translucent, with three-dimensional inclusions of chlorite, hornblende, or related minerals locked inside the chalcedony host during formation. Transparency is the fastest field test.

## Mineralogy: What They Share and Where They Diverge

Both stones are chalcedony — the fibrous, microcrystalline form of quartz with the formula SiO₂. They share a Mohs hardness of approximately 6.5–7 and a specific gravity around 2.60. Neither is toxic or water-soluble.

The differences lie in their inclusions:

| Property | Tree Agate | Moss Agate |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical base | SiO₂ (chalcedony) | SiO₂ (chalcedony) |
| Mohs hardness | ~6.5–7 | ~7 |
| Transparency | Opaque | Translucent |
| Base color | White to cream | Clear, gray, or lightly milky |
| Inclusion type | Manganese or iron oxide dendrites | Chlorite, hornblende, iron oxides |
| Inclusion location | Formed in surface fissures | Trapped inside host during formation |
| True agate? | Yes (may show some banding) | No — technically chalcedony, not true agate |

Moss agate is, strictly speaking, not a true agate because it lacks the concentric banding that defines agate. It is translucent chalcedony with mineral inclusions — a distinction recognized in mineralogical literature (Wilner & Rossman, Caltech GPS, 2025).

## How Each Forms

**Tree agate** dendrites form when manganese or iron oxide solutions crystallize within fractures and fissures in white, opaque chalcedony. The process is similar to frost forming on a window — minerals precipitate in branching patterns driven by crystal growth physics. Because the dendrites form in fissures that reach the stone's surface, they can be more susceptible to damage from acidic or abrasive chemicals that enter the micro-cracks.

**Moss agate** inclusions form when mineral-rich fluids (carrying chlorite, hornblende, or related iron-bearing silicates) infiltrate silica gel during chalcedony's formation in volcanic rock cavities. The green minerals crystallize within the gel in three-dimensional branching patterns while the silica is still solidifying — locking the inclusions inside rather than in surface fissures. As a result, moss agate's inclusions are well-protected from surface wear.

## Identifying Each Stone by Sight

**Transparency test**: Hold the stone up to a light source. Moss agate transmits light; the inclusions appear to float within the stone. Tree agate transmits no light — the white base is fully opaque.

**Inclusion structure**: Tree agate dendrites are flat and sharp-edged, resembling ink-painted branches, frost, or river-delta outlines. Moss agate inclusions are three-dimensional, fibrous, and diffuse — more like clumps of lichen than precise branching.

**Base color**: Tree agate has a bright, solid white or cream-colored base, like porcelain. Moss agate's base is clear to slightly milky gray.

For hands-on identification tests on each stone individually, see the [tree agate identification guide](/gemstones/tree-agate/how-to-identify) and the [moss agate identification guide](/gemstones/moss-agate/how-to-identify). Hardness context is at the [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale).

## Care Differences

Because tree agate's dendrites form in surface fissures, prolonged exposure to harsh chemicals, acids, or ultrasonic cleaners can force material into those micro-cracks. Clean tree agate with lukewarm water and mild soap only.

Moss agate's inclusions are sealed within the chalcedony host, so they are more resistant to surface treatments. However, direct sunlight over extended periods may fade the green color in some specimens. Both stones are safe to rinse in water.

## Origin and Availability

[Tree agate](/gemstones/tree-agate) is primarily sourced from India and Brazil. [Moss agate](/gemstones/moss-agate) is found in India (Deccan Plateau), Brazil, the United States (Montana, Oregon), and Uruguay. Both are widely available and relatively affordable. Fine scenic specimens from Montana are particularly sought-after for their vivid inclusions.

## Which to Choose

Transparency and pattern type are the practical guide. If you want a stone you can hold to the light and see the inclusions float in depth — moss agate. If you prefer a contrasting, forest-silhouette pattern on a clean white background — tree agate.

For wearability, both stones handle daily use well at Mohs 6.5–7. Moss agate's sealed inclusions make it slightly more forgiving of wear; tree agate's surface dendrites can be affected by harsh cleaning agents. Both are suitable for rings, pendants, and beads when properly set.

## Metaphysical Traditions

Tree agate is traditionally associated with patience, stability, and connection to the natural world. Moss agate is used by practitioners as a stone of new beginnings, abundance, and emotional release. Both are associated with the heart chakra in crystal traditions. These are cultural associations, not evidence-based health claims.
