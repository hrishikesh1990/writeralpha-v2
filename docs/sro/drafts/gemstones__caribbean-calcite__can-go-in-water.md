# Can Caribbean Calcite Go in Water?

No. Caribbean Calcite should not be placed in water. It is calcium carbonate (CaCO3) with a Mohs hardness of 3, and calcite fizzes on contact with weak acids and is softened by prolonged water exposure. Brief accidental contact with water is unlikely to cause immediate visible damage, but intentional soaking, water baths, or wearing it in rain or while washing will progressively pit and dull the surface.

## Why Water Damages Caribbean Calcite

Calcite's chemical formula is CaCO3. It is the same mineral that makes up limestone and chalk. Water alone, especially slightly acidic water (including tap water in many areas), slowly dissolves the calcium carbonate. This process is gradual but cumulative — the same mechanism that dissolves limestone caves over geological time operates on your specimen at room temperature, just far more slowly.

The reaction is much faster with acids. Calcite fizzes visibly when a drop of vinegar or lemon juice is placed on it, because even dilute acids dissolve the carbonate immediately. Soaps, many cleaning sprays, and sweat are all mildly acidic and will dull a polished Caribbean Calcite surface with repeated exposure.

At Mohs 3, Caribbean Calcite is also soft. Wet surfaces pick up micro-abrasions more easily than dry ones, and any sediment in water acts as an abrasive against the stone's surface.

### What happens in salt water

Salt baths are doubly harmful. Dissolved salt creates a mildly acidic solution that reacts with the carbonate surface, and salt crystals left behind when the water evaporates draw ambient moisture back to the stone and concentrate on any exposed grain boundaries. Do not use salt water, salt baths, or wet salt burial to cleanse Caribbean Calcite.

### What happens with brief splashing

A brief splash of water — accidentally dropping it in a sink, or wiping it with a barely damp cloth — is unlikely to cause permanent damage if you immediately dry the stone with a soft, dry cloth and let it air dry completely. The danger is prolonged contact, soaking, and repeated exposure over time.

## Safe Cleaning Methods (Physical Dirt)

If Caribbean Calcite picks up dust or surface grime, use these approaches:

1. **Dry brush:** Use a soft, dry paintbrush or a makeup brush to sweep loose dust and debris from the surface. This is sufficient for most display pieces.
2. **Barely damp wipe (emergency only):** If grime cannot be removed dry, dampen a soft cloth minimally, wipe the surface once, and immediately follow with a dry cloth. Allow the stone to air dry completely before storing. Use this method sparingly.
3. **Avoid:** Running water, soaking, steam, any acidic or chemical cleaner.

## Sun Exposure

Brief sunlight exposure (15 to 30 minutes) does not bleach or physically harm opaque Caribbean Calcite under normal conditions. Sun charging after a dry cleanse is fine. Extended hours in direct summer sun could potentially affect delicate surface polish on very pale specimens — use common sense and avoid all-day outdoor exposure.
