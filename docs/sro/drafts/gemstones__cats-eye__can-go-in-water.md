# Can Cat's Eye Go in Water? A Physical Safety Guide

Cat's Eye chrysoberyl can go in water. It rates 8.5 on the Mohs hardness scale, is not water-soluble, and is not affected by common acids, which means brief contact with water poses no physical risk to the stone. Brief rinsing and mild soap-and-water cleaning are endorsed by gem trade sources as the correct maintenance method.

## Why Cat's Eye Is Water-Safe

Cat's Eye is a variety of chrysoberyl, a beryllium aluminum oxide mineral (BeAl2O4). The relevant physical properties for water safety are hardness, solubility, and chemical reactivity with water or dilute acids.

Chrysoberyl scores 8.5 on the Mohs hardness scale — harder than quartz (7), which is used as the baseline for "generally safe in water." Stones below Mohs 5 can be scratched or softened by water, and some minerals (halite, selenite/gypsum) are measurably soluble in water. Chrysoberyl is neither. Mindat confirms it is not affected by acids, which is a stronger indicator of chemical stability than water alone.

The chatoyant inclusions inside cat's eye — fine needles of rutile or hollow tubes — are fully enclosed within the crystal and are not accessible to water from the surface. Brief water exposure does not dislodge or dissolve them.

For a broader guide to which gemstones can safely go in water, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## How to Clean Cat's Eye with Water

The standard gem-trade recommendation for chrysoberyl cat's eye is mild soap and lukewarm water:

1. Add a small drop of mild dish soap to a bowl of lukewarm (not hot) water.
2. Submerge the stone and let it soak for one to two minutes to loosen surface oils and dust.
3. Use a soft toothbrush or cloth to gently scrub the surface and the back of the cabochon.
4. Rinse under cool running water until all soap residue is removed.
5. Dry with a soft, lint-free cloth and allow to air-dry fully before storing.

GemSelect and AGTA both confirm this method as appropriate for chrysoberyl. Gem dealers routinely use this approach for routine maintenance.

## What to Avoid

Although Cat's Eye tolerates water well, several situations warrant caution:

**Ultrasonic cleaners:** GemSelect notes that chrysoberyl is generally durable enough for ultrasonic cleaning, but caution is recommended. Vibration can loosen inclusions in heavily included specimens, and the setting (metal, adhesive) may not tolerate the ultrasonic bath even if the stone does. If in doubt, stick to hand-washing.

**Steam cleaning:** High-temperature steam is not recommended for any cabochon-set stone, as thermal shock can stress the stone or loosen the setting.

**Prolonged soaking:** There is no benefit to extended soaking for cat's eye. Brief immersion is sufficient. Long soaks in tap water containing chlorine or mineral salts are unnecessary and best avoided as a general habit.

**Harsh chemicals:** Bleach, acetone, and acid-based cleaners are not needed and should be kept away from any gemstone.

**Salt water:** Salt is not harmful to chrysoberyl itself, but prolonged exposure to salt water can corrode metal settings and adhesives used in mounted pieces. For loose stones, it poses no risk at moderate exposure. For a guide to which stones are safe with salt, see [salt safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## Water and the Jewelry Setting

Water safety depends partly on the mount, not just the stone. If Cat's Eye is set in silver or gold, brief water exposure is generally fine. If the stone is held by adhesive (glue-set cabochons in lower-cost jewelry), prolonged soaking may loosen the bond over time. Check the setting type before soaking mounted pieces.

## After Washing: Sunlight and Charging

After washing, some practitioners like to air-dry Cat's Eye in morning sunlight briefly before recharging. Chrysoberyl does not fade with brief sun exposure, so this is physically safe.
