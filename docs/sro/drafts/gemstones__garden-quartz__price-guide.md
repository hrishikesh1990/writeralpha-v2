# Garden Quartz Price Guide: Cost, Value Drivers, and Buying Tips

Garden Quartz tumbled stones typically retail for $10 to $50 per piece for small to medium sizes. Polished points and spheres range from $20 to $250 depending on size and inclusion quality. Premium collector specimens with exceptional clarity and a striking interior scene can exceed $500. Prices checked against retail market data, September 2026.

## Typical Retail Price Ranges

| Form | Typical retail range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Small tumbled stone (18–30 mm) | $5–$30 per piece | Common; widely available |
| Medium polished point (5–10 cm) | $20–$80 per piece | Price rises sharply with clarity |
| Large polished point (10–20 cm) | $60–$250 per piece | Inclusion scene drives the premium |
| Sphere (5–8 cm diameter) | $40–$150 per piece | Even more variation by scene quality |
| Premium collector specimen | $150–$500+ per piece | Exceptional clarity and inclusion depth |
| Cut/polished (per carat) | $2–$20 per carat | Less common; collector market |

These ranges reflect the retail market. Wholesale prices from Brazilian and Chinese suppliers run significantly lower; $7–$14 per piece for wholesale crystal points is cited in trade data. Retail markup reflects curation, selection, and provenance documentation.

## What Drives the Price

**Inclusion scene quality.** This is the dominant price driver. A Garden Quartz piece with a vivid, layered interior — distinct green chlorite plumes, reddish iron-oxide clouds, visible depth and three-dimensionality — commands significantly more than a piece with muddy, low-contrast inclusions. The scene must be visible from the stone's "sweet spot" viewing angle; pieces where the inclusions only appear under specific lighting are priced lower.

**Clarity of the host quartz.** The clearer the quartz matrix, the more visible the inclusions. Milky or heavily included host quartz obscures the interior. Pieces with a transparent "window" that allows an unobstructed view of the interior scene are priced higher.

**Size.** Larger pieces cost more, all else equal. A 10-cm polished point with the same inclusion quality as a 5-cm point will be priced noticeably higher because large, clear quartz with scenic inclusions is harder to source.

**Origin.** Minas Gerais, Brazil is the primary and most reputable source. Madagascar material is also well-regarded. Provenance from a named mine or region adds confidence for collectors and may support a modest price premium.

**Form.** Spheres and carved forms take more labor to produce, which adds to price. Raw clusters or natural points are sometimes priced lower than heavily polished pieces of equivalent inclusion quality.

## Why Price Varies So Much

Garden Quartz is sold under several trade names (lodolite, scenic quartz, landscape quartz, shaman quartz) and the inclusion composition varies widely — no two pieces are the same. Unlike a faceted diamond with a standardized grading system, there is no industry-wide grading for Garden Quartz. Price is largely set by what the seller believes the piece is worth visually, and what buyers are willing to pay for that specific interior scene. This creates wide variation between sellers and platforms.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Buy the stone, not the story.** Some sellers charge premium prices for Garden Quartz based on metaphysical claims or product names (e.g., "shamanic attunement points"). The stone's value is in its visual and mineralogical quality, not its label.

**Compare multiple sellers.** Price-shop across platforms before purchasing a high-ticket specimen. Similar inclusion quality is available at different prices from different sellers.

**Check for authenticity.** Glass imitations and assembled fakes are present in the market, particularly in bead and smaller tumbled form. A genuine piece at $30 is better value than a glass fake at $10. See [how to identify Garden Quartz](/gemstones/garden-quartz/how-to-identify) for practical checks.

**Request photos and video.** For any piece over $50, ask for video footage showing the stone under natural light from multiple angles. The interior scene of Garden Quartz must be evaluated dynamically — a single edited photo cannot convey it honestly.

**Check sourcing.** Ethical sourcing — transparent supply chain, worker welfare, environmental responsibility — matters beyond price. See [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing) for guidance on evaluating sellers.

## Durability and Value Retention

Garden Quartz rates 7 on the [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale), the same as all quartz varieties. It is durable in normal use but points and polished edges can chip if knocked against harder surfaces or other stones. Proper storage — individual pouches or a tray with dividers — protects value over time. The [Garden Quartz hub](/gemstones/garden-quartz) covers all physical properties and care.
