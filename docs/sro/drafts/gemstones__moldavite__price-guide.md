# Moldavite Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Genuine moldavite retails at roughly $20 to $80 per gram for standard raw specimens (September 2026); premium-locality and highly etched pieces command more. Faceted stones for jewelry run approximately $200 to $600 per piece. Prices rose substantially after 2019 as demand increased and Czech mining access tightened. Material priced below $10 per gram at retail is very likely imitation glass.

## Why Moldavite Is Expensive

### Finite, non-renewable supply

Moldavite formed in a single event — the Ries crater impact approximately 15 million years ago — and was deposited across a defined strewn field in the Czech Republic, with minor material in parts of Germany and Austria. No new moldavite is being created. The total available material is finite, and the best-quality sites have been collected for decades. Czech authorities have increasingly restricted excavation, shrinking the supply of new legally sourced material entering the market.

### Origin exclusivity

Moldavite can only come from one geographic source. Any stone described as originating outside Central Europe's Ries strewn field is not moldavite by definition. This geographic exclusivity, combined with genuine scarcity, means no substitute material can replace it.

### Grade and quality variation

Within authentic moldavite, prices vary substantially based on several physical characteristics:

- **Surface texture**: Deeply etched, complex worm-track or mossy surfaces command the highest prices. Smooth or poorly etched pieces sell for less.
- **Color depth**: Deep forest-green pieces are generally valued above pale olive or brownish-green material.
- **Size**: Very large intact pieces are uncommon and attract premium pricing. Most commercial material runs well under 10 grams.
- **Locality**: Besednice (southern Bohemia) is among the most prized collection areas; pieces with documented Besednice provenance typically command a premium over unlocalized Czech material.
- **Form**: Raw natural pieces, polished pieces, and faceted gemstones each have different markets. Faceting moldavite is difficult and wasteful because the glass is brittle (Mohs 5.5, conchoidal fracture), so finished faceted stones carry additional labor cost.

### Demand growth

Moldavite's surge in popularity in online crystal communities — particularly after around 2020 — drove demand well above the rate at which new material could be sourced. This demand increase, combined with shrinking legal supply, pushed per-gram prices upward and simultaneously made the fake trade more profitable.

## Price as a Fraud Signal

Price is one of the most reliable identification tools for moldavite. A per-gram price below roughly $8 to $10 at retail cannot cover the cost of legally sourced, certified-authentic Czech moldavite, export logistics, and dealer margin. Deep discounts and "moldavite" priced at $10 or $15 for a pendant with free international shipping are effectively certain to be imitation glass. For a full guide to telling genuine from fake material, see [how to identify moldavite](/gemstones/moldavite/how-to-identify).

## Durability and Its Effect on Value

Moldavite's Mohs hardness of 5.5 means it scratches easily against most household surfaces and against other common stones. Chips, scratches, and edge damage reduce value and cannot be polished out easily without losing material. Pieces in pristine condition, particularly large raw specimens with intact etching, are rarer than their weight alone might suggest. For context on how hardness affects gemstone use and durability, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale). The stone's full physical profile is at [moldavite](/gemstones/moldavite).

## Where to Buy and What to Verify

Reputable moldavite sellers photograph every individual piece, provide gram weights, and state the specific Czech locality. They do not use generic stock images or sell multiple "unique" pieces with identical outlines. For higher-value purchases, an independent gemological laboratory report — one that documents the specific piece with its weight, dimensions, photograph, and test results — provides meaningful protection.

The ethical sourcing landscape for moldavite is complicated by the prevalence of fakes and by variable documentation standards.
