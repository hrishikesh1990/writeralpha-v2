# How to Identify Real Lava Stone — Tests and Red Flags

Real lava stone is porous vesicular basalt: black to dark gray, lightweight for its size, and covered with natural pits and cavities visible to the naked eye. The key test is the pore structure — genuine lava stone has irregular, naturally formed holes that absorb water and essential oils. Smooth, uniformly colored beads with no porosity are not lava stone, regardless of what the label says.

## What Real Lava Stone Looks Like

Lava stone is a fine-grained igneous rock, dark gray to black in color. The most distinctive visual feature is the vesicular texture: irregular pits and holes of varying sizes across the surface, left by gas bubbles as the lava cooled. These are not uniformly spaced and vary in depth. The surface is matte, with a dull or slightly greasy lustre — never glassy or vitreous.

Under normal indoor light:
- Color is consistently black or charcoal gray; natural lava stone does not come in red, blue, green, or other bright colors
- Surface texture is rough and pitted even on polished beads; the polish smooths high points but does not fill the pores
- No internal transparency — lava stone is fully opaque; holding it up to a light source reveals no glow or translucency

Size inconsistency is normal. Natural basalt varies in pore density and mineral distribution, so beads from the same batch will look slightly different from one another. Perfectly uniform beads with no surface variation are a signal to look more closely.

## The Porosity Test

Drip a single drop of water onto the surface of a bead or tumbled piece. Real lava stone absorbs water into its pores within a few seconds — the drop will be drawn in or at least pull clearly toward the surface. On a glass bead or plastic imitation, the water will bead up and sit on the surface as a sphere.

The same test works with a small drop of essential oil: real lava stone will absorb it immediately, which is why it is used as a diffuser medium. Imitation material will not.

## Weight Test

Pick up a lava stone bead and compare it to a glass bead of similar size. Glass is significantly denser than vesicular basalt — natural lava stone beads feel lighter than you might expect for their size, while glass beads feel heavier. This is not a definitive test alone, as some plastics are also lightweight, but it is a useful first check.

## Temperature Test

Hold the bead against your cheek or lips for a few seconds. Real stone feels cold to the touch, even after sitting in a warm room; it warms slowly. Plastic heats up quickly to body temperature and may feel slightly warm at the start. Glass also starts cold but warms faster than stone. This test is most useful for comparing suspect beads to a known genuine piece.

## Dyed Lava Stone

The porous structure of lava stone absorbs dye readily. Brightly colored beads — purple, pink, blue, red — that are sold as "lava stone" are natural basalt beads that have been dyed. Dyeing is not fraud if it is disclosed, but it is often not disclosed clearly. Signs of dye:
- Color that does not exist in natural basalt (anything other than black or gray)
- Color that transfers onto a white cloth when the bead is rubbed while wet
- Uneven color pooling in the deepest pores compared to the surface

Dyed lava stone functions identically as a diffuser bead; the dye does not affect porosity in most cases. Whether you want dyed material depends on preference, not function.

## Imitation Materials to Watch For

**Black glass beads** are the most common substitute. They are glassy, have no surface porosity, are heavier than basalt, and do not absorb liquids. They may be sold under vague labels like "volcanic glass" (which is a real material — obsidian — but is not the porous lava stone used in crystal practice).

**Plastic beads** are sometimes dyed black and roughened artificially. They are lightweight, but their surface roughness is mechanical rather than porous — the texture does not absorb water.

**Ceramic beads** are occasionally sold as lava stone. Unglazed ceramic is also porous and may pass the water absorption test. Unlike basalt, ceramic has a uniform, manufactured texture and no natural pitting variation. If bead texture looks too consistent across a large batch, ceramic is worth considering.

## Price as an Identification Signal

Lava stone is abundant and inexpensive. If beads or a bracelet are priced significantly higher than market rate with claims of rarity or special origin, review what is actually being sold.
