# How to Cleanse Hematoid Quartz Without Damaging It

Hematoid Quartz can go in water for a rinse or quick wash — its quartz host is Mohs 7 and chemically stable. The iron oxide (hematite) inclusions introduce one practical limit: prolonged soaking or salt water can promote surface rust staining. For routine cleansing, water, smoke, sound, moonlight, and dry earth are all viable; the method depends on your preference and what you have to hand.

## Water Cleansing

Hematoid Quartz is safe to rinse under running water or wash with mild soap and a soft cloth. Use cool or lukewarm water and keep contact brief — a wash lasting a minute or two is sufficient and leaves no risk of damage. Dry the stone thoroughly afterward with a soft cloth, paying attention to any crevices in rough or natural-point specimens where water can collect and sit against the iron-rich zones.

Avoid extended soaking (hours or overnight), as water sitting on iron-bearing inclusions can encourage surface rust staining over time. Salt water is not recommended for the same reason: salt residue on iron-bearing mineral surfaces can draw moisture and cause discoloration. For a full discussion of water safety for this stone, see [can Hematoid Quartz go in water](/gemstones/hematoid-quartz/can-go-in-water). For guidance on which other stones are and are not water-safe, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Smoke Cleansing

Hold Hematoid Quartz in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating the piece so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This method carries no physical risk to the stone. Ensure the room is ventilated. Smoke cleansing is one of the most widely used methods for any crystal and is particularly practical for polished pieces kept in jewelry where water contact with a metal setting should be minimized.

## Sound Cleansing

Place Hematoid Quartz near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass over it for one to two minutes. Sound works at a distance, so the stone does not need to be moved through anything wet or subjected to temperature change. This method is especially useful for large display specimens or carved pieces where surface contact with other materials might risk scratching.

## Moonlight

Set Hematoid Quartz on a dry surface — a windowsill, shelf, or outdoor table on a clear night — and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast: dew or high overnight humidity will deposit moisture on the stone. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms. Moonlight cleansing also recharges the stone in one step in traditional practice.

## Dry Earth

Bury Hematoid Quartz in a small pot of dry, indoor potting soil or sand for 24 hours. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which is often damp. Brush off loose material with a dry cloth or soft brush when retrieving the stone. This method is grounding in symbolic terms and carries no physical risk when the soil is genuinely dry.

## Breath and Visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while directing attention toward clearing any accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This requires no materials and is useful for a quick reset during the day or when other methods are not available.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal tradition treats cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing Hematoid Quartz, options include placing it in morning sunlight for 15 to 30 minutes (brief sun exposure poses no risk to this opaque iron-bearing mineral), setting it near a clear quartz cluster, or resting it on a dry selenite slab. For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals). The [Hematoid Quartz hub page](/gemstones/hematoid-quartz) covers all physical properties in one place.

## How Often to Cleanse

Cleansing frequency is a matter of personal practice with no physical basis for any fixed schedule. A common guideline in crystal tradition is to cleanse Hematoid Quartz when it arrives, after any period of sustained focused use, and monthly during regular display. If you keep it in a workspace where it is handled frequently, more regular cleansing is often suggested.

As a physical maintenance note: inspect Hematoid Quartz periodically for any rust-colored staining, particularly around iron-rich zones. If staining develops, it indicates the stone has been exposed to prolonged moisture and should be dried and stored in a drier location going forward.
