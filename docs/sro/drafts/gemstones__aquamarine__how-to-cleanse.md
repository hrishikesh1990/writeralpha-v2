# How to Cleanse and Recharge Aquamarine

Aquamarine is safe to cleanse with water. Its Mohs hardness of 7.5 to 8 means water will not scratch or degrade the surface, and beryl is not water-soluble. Warm water with a small amount of mild soap is the method GIA lists as always safe for aquamarine. Other effective options include smoke, sound, moonlight, and dry breath — all covered below.

## Why Aquamarine Is Water-Safe

Aquamarine is a beryl mineral — chemically stable, non-toxic in finished stone form, and not prone to oxidation or corrosion from water contact. A Mohs hardness of 7.5 to 8 places it well above most household abrasives. Unlike pyrite, hematite, or selenite, aquamarine does not react with water in ways that damage the surface.

One caveat applies to fracture-filled aquamarine. Some lower-grade stones are clarity-enhanced by filling surface-reaching fractures with resin or glass. Harsh chemicals, ultrasonic cleaners, and steam can displace fillers in these stones. For fracture-filled material, warm soapy water and a soft cloth are the safest approach, and ultrasonic or steam cleaning should be avoided. If you are unsure whether your stone has been fracture-filled, check with the seller or a gemologist.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Warm water and mild soap

Run lukewarm water over the stone or place it in a bowl of warm water with a small drop of dish soap or gentle hand soap. Let it soak for two to five minutes, then gently brush with a soft toothbrush to remove any residue from settings or surface texture. Rinse thoroughly with clean water and pat dry with a lint-free cloth. This is the most thorough physical cleansing method and poses no physical risk to unfilled aquamarine.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold aquamarine in the smoke of burning herbs or incense for 30 to 60 seconds, moving the piece slowly so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is the most common ritual cleansing method for stones across traditions and carries no physical risk to aquamarine. Ensure ventilation — you are in the smoke too.

### Sound

Place aquamarine near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to wash through it for one to two minutes. The stone does not need to be touched or moved through anything. Sound cleansing is particularly useful for pieces mounted in jewelry where water immersion around the setting is undesirable.

### Moonlight

Set aquamarine on a dry surface in view of moonlight overnight. Check overnight weather first: dew or rain will wet the stone, which is harmless physically but may interfere with the ritual intention. Bring the stone indoors before morning condensation forms if you are leaving it outside.

### Breath and visualization

Hold aquamarine in both hands, take a full breath in, and exhale slowly across the surface while holding the intention to clear accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. No materials are required, and the method works anywhere.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal tradition treats cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing, aquamarine can be recharged by:

- **Moonlight** — leaving it out under a full moon overnight is the most common method. The stone's water and Neptune associations make moonlight a natural pairing in tradition.
- **Sunlight** — brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes) poses no physical risk to natural unfilled aquamarine. Prolonged sun exposure is not recommended for any heat-treated or fracture-filled stone as a precaution, even though natural aquamarine's heat-treated color is generally described as stable at ordinary temperatures.
- **Clear quartz cluster** — resting aquamarine on or near a clear quartz cluster is a traditional recharging method that requires no light source.
- **Selenite slab** — a dry selenite slab is also commonly used. Keep the slab itself dry; selenite dissolves slowly in water.

For a full recharging guide covering all methods and stones, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary, but common guidelines suggest cleansing aquamarine when it first arrives, after intensive use or emotionally significant events, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — frequency is a matter of personal practice. ## Storage After Cleansing

Store aquamarine in a cloth pouch or a jewelry box lined with soft fabric. At Mohs 7.5–8, it can scratch softer stones and can itself be scratched by harder materials such as corundum or diamond. Keep it away from direct prolonged sunlight during storage as a general precaution. The hub page at [aquamarine](/gemstones/aquamarine) covers storage and care alongside full physical properties.
