# How to Cleanse Larimar Without Damaging It

Larimar can be briefly rinsed under cool running water, but prolonged soaking is not recommended. Safe cleansing methods include smoke, sound, moonlight, selenite, and breath or visualization. Prolonged direct sunlight risks fading the blue color irreversibly, and salt-water or chemical baths are best avoided. Each method is covered below.

## Why Larimar Needs Careful Cleansing

Larimar is a blue variety of pectolite (NaCa₂Si₃O₈(OH)) with a Mohs hardness of 4.5–5 and perfect cleavage in two directions. It is not chemically reactive in the way pyrite is, but its moderate softness and perfect cleavage provide pathways for water and salt to work into the structure over time. Prolonged soaking can weaken the surface of porous or treated material, and Larimar is sometimes wax- or resin-impregnated at the trade level to stabilize softer rough — soaking such pieces may affect the stabilizer.

Larimar's blue color is also UV-sensitive. Laboratory tests have shown that extended heating (around 250°C) reduces the blue color, and long-term exposure to direct sunlight is associated with gradual fading. For practical cleansing, this means: no prolonged sunlight as a cleansing or recharging method.

## Quick Water Rinse (Brief Use Only)

A brief rinse under cool or lukewarm running water — 10 to 30 seconds — is acceptable for solid, untreated Larimar. Dry the stone immediately and thoroughly with a soft lint-free cloth. Allow any drill holes, settings, or cord to dry fully before storage.

Do not soak Larimar in water. Do not use salt water: salt is mildly abrasive, hygroscopic, and can damage both the stone surface and any silver setting. Do not put Larimar in an ultrasonic cleaner or steam cleaner, as vibration and heat can aggravate existing fractures or affect resin treatments.

## Smoke Cleansing

Hold the Larimar piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the piece slowly so all surfaces are exposed. This is one of the most widely used methods for Larimar in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to the stone. Ventilate the room well.

## Sound

Place Larimar near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone is never exposed to water, heat, or abrasion. This method works equally well for jewelry pieces that cannot be immersed, such as pieces with adhesive settings or natural-fiber cords.

## Moonlight

Set Larimar on a dry surface on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Any moon phase is used in crystal tradition. Check the weather before leaving Larimar outside: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms.

Moonlight is the most commonly recommended recharging method for Larimar in crystal practice, given that sunlight is a risk for color fading. Many practitioners treat a moonlight overnight as a combined cleanse-and-recharge step.

## Selenite Plate or Bowl

Place Larimar on or beside a dry selenite slab or in a selenite bowl for several hours. Selenite is traditionally considered a self-cleansing stone used to clear and recharge others in crystal practice. There is no physical interaction between the minerals that would harm either stone. Keep the selenite itself dry, as selenite dissolves in water.

## Breath and Visualization

Hold the Larimar in both hands, breathe in slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing on releasing any accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials, poses no physical risk, and is commonly used as a quick reset between longer cleansing sessions.

## Methods to Avoid

| Method | Risk to Larimar |
|---|---|
| Prolonged water soaking | Surface weakening; stabilizer damage in treated pieces |
| Salt water or dry salt | Abrasive; hygroscopic; can damage surface and settings |
| Prolonged direct sunlight | Blue color may fade irreversibly |
| Ultrasonic cleaner | Vibration can aggravate fractures and loosen settings |
| Steam cleaner | Heat and pressure risk fracturing or discoloring the stone |
| Harsh chemicals, acids, perfume | Surface damage; dye or treatment removal |

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps. After cleansing, place Larimar in moonlight (the most recommended method), on a dry selenite slab, or near a clear quartz cluster. Brief morning light (15 to 30 minutes, not the midday sun) is sometimes used in tradition, but given Larimar's color sensitivity, moonlight is the safer and more widely recommended choice.

For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals). Larimar's hub page at [Larimar](/gemstones/larimar) covers all physical properties.

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary. A common guideline is to cleanse Larimar when it arrives, after any period of intense use, and monthly during regular display or wear. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice. As a maintenance note, inspect Larimar periodically for any surface dulling, which may indicate abrasion, and store it separately from harder stones to prevent scratching.
