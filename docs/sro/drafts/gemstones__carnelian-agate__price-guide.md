# Carnelian Agate Price Guide

Carnelian Agate is an affordable semi-precious stone. Tumbled stones and beads commonly sell for $1–5 per piece at retail. Quality cabochons run roughly $2–30 per carat depending on color intensity, translucency, and size. Collector-grade pieces with exceptional banding or very deep, uniform color can exceed $50 per carat. All prices checked September 2026 and will vary by seller and market.

## Price Ranges by Form

| Form | Typical retail range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tumbled stone | $1–5 each | Common quality; most beads and display stones |
| Beads, per strand | $5–50 per strand | Wider range reflects bead size, cut, and color |
| Small cabochon (1–5 ct) | $2–15 per piece | Standard orange, decent translucency |
| Quality cabochon (good color) | $10–30 per carat | Deep uniform color, good translucency |
| High-quality cabochon (exceptional) | $30–100+ per carat | Top color, clarity, large size |
| Simple jewelry | $20–100 per piece | Setting metal and craftsmanship add cost |
| Designer or fine-metal jewelry | $100–500+ per piece | Brand premium applies |
| Carvings and collector pieces | $50–500+ per piece | Artistry, size, and pattern rarity |

These are retail ranges. Wholesale or bulk pricing is substantially lower — B2B bead strands can be found at under $1 per strand in large lots.

## What Drives the Price

**Color** is the dominant factor. The most prized carnelian agate shows a deep, saturated red-orange to red color that is even across the stone. Pale, washed-out, or patchy color depresses value significantly. Deep sardius-red material (the darker brownish-red end of the spectrum) commands the highest prices for single-color material.

**Translucency.** Carnelian agate ranges from translucent to opaque. Translucent material that glows warmly when held to light is more valuable than opaque or cloudy white-patched material. For banded carnelian agate specifically, the contrast and definition of the banding adds appeal.

**Treatment status.** Most commercial carnelian agate has been heat-treated — pale agate heated to develop orange-red color. This is long-accepted trade practice and does not meaningfully reduce price for standard retail material. However, genuinely untreated natural carnelian with strong color carries a premium with knowledgeable buyers who seek it. Dyed material (agate colored with artificial dyes) should be priced lower than heat-treated or natural material and should be disclosed; when it is sold without disclosure, buyers who later discover the treatment feel deceived.

**Size.** Larger stones of consistent quality are rarer and priced per-carat at higher rates. A 20-carat cabochon with excellent color is worth substantially more per carat than a 3-carat piece of the same quality, simply because large material is scarcer.

**Cut quality.** A smooth, high-polish finish that maximizes the stone's translucency and color is worth more than a rough or poorly shaped piece.

**Origin.** Indian carnelian (historically from Gujarat) and Brazilian material are the most common commercial sources. Certain origins are associated with higher-quality natural material, but origin alone is not a reliable quality indicator without seeing the stone.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Buy from sellers who disclose treatment.** An honest "heat-treated" or "heat status unknown" disclosure is a sign of a reputable seller. Unsupported claims that every piece is completely natural and untreated should be questioned. For help distinguishing genuine from dyed material, see [how to identify Carnelian Agate](/gemstones/carnelian-agate/how-to-identify).

**Compare color in natural light.** Artificial display lighting, especially yellow-warm retail lighting, flatters orange stones significantly. Request photos in natural daylight or check in person by daylight before paying for high-quality pieces.

**Be cautious with "rare" claims at mass-market prices.** Carnelian agate is abundant globally. Sellers who charge premium prices on rarity claims without providing origin documentation or grading certificates are offering unverified assertions.

**Understand the Mohs scale context.** Carnelian agate at Mohs 7 is durable enough for most jewelry uses — rings, bracelets, pendants, earrings — without special care beyond avoiding hard knocks. Its durability is appropriate to its price point. For more on hardness and durability, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale).

**Consider ethics of sourcing.** Most carnelian agate is mined in India, Brazil, and other producing countries with variable labor and environmental standards. For guidance on evaluating sourcing claims, see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing).

## Price Expectations by Budget

- **Under $10:** A tumbled stone or bead strand of ordinary quality. Useful for daily carry, learning, or general crystal practice. Heat-treated material is typical at this price.
- **$10–50:** A small to medium cabochon with good color, or simple jewelry. Expect heat-treated material; natural or untreated material at this price is possible but should be verified.
- **$50–200:** A quality cabochon with strong color and translucency, or well-made jewelry in silver. This range rewards more careful seller selection.
- **$200+:** Fine jewelry, large high-quality specimens, or collector-grade banded pieces with exceptional pattern. Provenance and treatment documentation become important at this level.
