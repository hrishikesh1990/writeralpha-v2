# Fluorite Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Fluorite is one of the most affordable gemstones and crystal minerals available, with rough material as low as a few cents per carat and common tumbled stones costing $2 to $15 each. At the high end, top-grade gem-quality fluorite with vivid colour and no visible inclusions reaches $50 to $100 per carat or more. The spread between these figures is large, and colour is the single biggest price driver.

## Price Ranges by Form (September 2026)

Prices below are approximate retail ranges in USD, checked September 2026, and vary significantly by grade, seller, and origin.

| Form | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Rough fluorite | $0.05–$3 per carat |
| Tumbled stones (20–40 g) | $2–$15 per stone |
| Cabochons | $1.50–$5 per carat |
| Faceted (commercial grade) | $3–$8 per carat |
| Faceted (high quality, vivid colour) | $30–$50 per carat |
| Exceptional (deep purple/blue, eye-clean, precision cut) | $50–$100 per carat |
| Colour-changing fluorite | $10–$25 per carat |
| Bracelets (standard grade beads) | $5–$30 per bracelet |

Fluorite is mined in large quantities primarily in China, Mexico, and South Africa, which keeps prices low across most grades. Abundant supply means fluorite rarely appreciates significantly as an investment, but also means genuinely affordable quality is readily available.

## What Drives Fluorite Price

### Colour

Colour is the most influential price factor. Deep, saturated purple, vivid blue, and intense green specimens command the highest prices. Pale, washed-out, or off-colour material falls at the lower end. Multicoloured or banded specimens — marketed as rainbow fluorite — carry a premium if the colour contrast is strong and distinct.

Colour-changing fluorite, which shifts between blue/teal and lilac or pale green depending on the light source, is rarer and priced accordingly.

### Clarity

Eye-clean, transparent fluorite — with no visible inclusions when held to the light — is always more expensive than translucent material with clouds, fractures, or mineral inclusions. Fluorite is classified as a Type II clarity gemstone, meaning inclusions are common in the species but the best specimens are inclusion-free.

### Cut

Precision-faceted fluorite requires skilled cutting. The stone's perfect octahedral cleavage (four directions) means it can split during faceting, increasing waste and labour cost. A well-proportioned faceted stone that shows no damage along cleavage edges commands a premium over a rough or basic cabochon. Cabochons and tumbled stones are the most affordable forms.

For context on how hardness affects cutting and durability in use, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale).

### Origin

Most commercial fluorite is from China and is priced at the lower end of the market. Collectors particularly value fluorite from specific localities: the Rogerley Mine in County Durham, England produces vivid green fluorite with strong UV fluorescence and daylight fluorescence, and these specimens sell at a collector premium well above standard material. Illinois (USA) fluorite, the state's designated gemstone, also carries regional interest. Origin premiums are most significant at the collector and specimen level, less so for tumbled or commercial gem material.

### Size

Larger stones are rarer and priced higher per carat. A 10-carat faceted fluorite is not simply ten times the price of a 1-carat stone — the price-per-carat typically increases with size for clean material.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

Several common situations lead buyers to pay more than a stone is worth:

**Buying generic material with premium branding**: Fluorite labelled with specific colour names (ocean fluorite, glacier fluorite, spirit quartz adjacent) is sometimes ordinary fluorite marketed at an inflated price. Compare the actual colour and clarity to the price range above.

**Mistaking imitations for fluorite**: Glass, dyed calcite, and plastic are sometimes sold as fluorite at suspiciously low or oddly variable prices. If a tumbled stone costs less than $0.50 and lacks the density and cool feel of real mineral, treat it with scepticism. For how to tell genuine fluorite from substitutes, see [how to identify fluorite](/gemstones/fluorite/how-to-identify).

**Paying gemstone premiums for crystal-use material**: If you are using fluorite for crystal practice rather than jewellery, there is no need to pay for gem-quality clarity or precision cutting. Commercial-grade tumbled stones at $3 to $10 are fully adequate for the purpose.

## Buying Responsibly

Fluorite is mined in several countries with variable labour and environmental standards. China supplies the majority of the market; conditions vary by mine and operator. Asking sellers about origin and supply chain is reasonable, and responsible sellers will have information to share.
