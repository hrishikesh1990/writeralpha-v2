# How to Cleanse and Recharge Agate

Agate is safe to cleanse with water, moonlight, smoke, sound, or sunlight for brief periods. At Mohs 6.5 to 7, it is durable enough for most methods. The one caution is dyed agate: prolonged soaking can loosen artificial colorants. Natural or undyed agate has no significant cleansing restrictions.

## Is Agate Safe to Cleanse With Water

Yes. Agate is a variety of chalcedony (silicon dioxide, SiO2) with a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7 and no solubility in water under normal conditions. A brief rinse under cool running water — 30 to 60 seconds — is safe for polished, natural agate. Pat dry afterward and allow it to air-dry fully before storage.

**Dyed agate:** Much commercially sold agate is dyed because the stone is naturally porous and absorbs colorants readily. If your stone has unnaturally vivid colors — bright blue, neon green, hot pink — it is likely dyed. Prolonged soaking in water can leach the dye and may alter the appearance. For dyed pieces, keep water contact brief or use a non-water method.

For a detailed treatment of water safety, see [can agate go in water](/gemstones/agate/can-go-in-water). General guidance on water cleansing across stone types is at [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Water rinse

Hold agate under cool running water for 30 to 60 seconds. Visualize the water carrying away accumulated energy if you follow that practice. Pat dry with a soft cloth. Do not use hot water, which can stress some polished surfaces, and avoid prolonged soaking of dyed stones.

### Smoke cleansing

Pass agate slowly through the smoke of burning herbs or incense for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating the piece so all surfaces are exposed. This method works for all agate varieties — natural, dyed, or treated — and poses no physical risk to the stone. Ensure the space is ventilated.

### Sound

Place agate near a singing bowl, tuning fork, or bell and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing is entirely contact-free, which makes it ideal for fragile, polished, or intricately shaped pieces. Distance from the sound source does not significantly reduce its effect within a room.

### Moonlight

Set agate on a dry surface — a windowsill or outdoors — on a clear night and leave it overnight. Agate is stable in ambient conditions and will not be harmed. Bring it in before morning dew forms. Full moon nights are traditionally preferred in crystal practice, though any clear night is used.

### Sunlight

Agate can be placed in direct sunlight for brief periods (15 to 30 minutes) without damage to the mineral itself. However, very prolonged exposure may cause some dyed or artificially colored stones to fade. Natural agate colors — gray, red, white, brown — are generally stable. Use sunlight as a short-burst recharge method rather than all-day exposure.

### Dry earth

Bury agate in a small pot of dry indoor soil or sand for 24 hours, then brush off with a soft cloth. This grounding method is used in some crystal traditions for deep reset. Use dry indoor soil rather than damp garden soil.

### Selenite slab or clear quartz cluster

Set agate on a dry selenite slab or inside a clear quartz cluster for several hours. This is a no-contact, no-moisture method used widely in crystal practice. Selenite itself is water-soluble, so keep the slab dry.

## How to Recharge Agate After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing removes accumulated energy and recharging restores the stone's intended purpose. Common recharging methods for agate:

- **Morning sunlight:** 15 to 30 minutes on a sunny windowsill.
- **Full moonlight overnight:** Set on a dry surface the night of or around a full moon.
- **Clear quartz cluster:** Place agate on or beside a cluster for several hours or overnight.
- **Intention setting:** Hold the stone in both hands, breathe slowly, and focus on a clear intention for what you want the stone to support.

For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary. Common recommendations include: when the stone arrives, after intense use (in a difficult meeting, after a stressful event), and monthly during regular display. There is no physical indicator that tells you when cleansing is needed — this is a matter of personal practice. As a practical maintenance note, agate benefits from occasional dusting and a quick rinse to keep its waxy luster visible.

## Storage After Cleansing

Agate is stable at normal household humidity and temperature. Store it away from sharp objects that could scratch the surface (hardness 6.5 to 7 means it will scratch glass but can be scratched by harder minerals such as topaz or corundum). A cloth pouch or a display case is sufficient. The [agate hub page](/gemstones/agate) covers full physical properties.
