# How to Cleanse White Agate Without Damaging It

White Agate is safe to cleanse with water. A chemically stable silica mineral (SiO₂) at Mohs 6.5–7, it does not dissolve or corrode with brief water contact. The main care note is dye: many commercial pieces have been dyed, and prolonged soaking can cause leaching. Undyed material can be rinsed or briefly soaked without issue. Smoke, sound, moonlight, and breath are equally safe dry alternatives.

## Water Cleansing

### Plain water rinse

Hold White Agate under cool to lukewarm running water for 30 to 60 seconds. This is the simplest method and carries no risk to structurally sound, polished specimens. Pat dry with a soft cloth afterward and allow to air-dry fully before storage.

### Mild soap wash

For pieces that have been worn as jewelry and have accumulated skin oils or residue, add a small amount of mild dish soap to lukewarm water and clean with a soft cloth or soft-bristled brush. Rinse thoroughly and pat dry. Do not use jewelry cleaning solutions that contain ammonia, bleach, or acids, as these can attack metal settings and may affect surface treatments.

### What to avoid with water

- **Steam cleaning**: White Agate is sensitive to high temperatures and rapid temperature changes. Steam can cause thermal shock, particularly in specimens with existing fractures. Do not use steam cleaners.
- **Ultrasonic cleaning**: Can loosen dye, resin impregnation, or surface coatings on treated specimens. Avoid for any agate whose treatment history is unknown.
- **Prolonged saltwater soaking**: Salt is mildly abrasive and can dull a polished surface over time. It is not harmful in a brief dip, but plain water is the safer choice for regular cleansing. For more on water safety in detail, see [can White Agate go in water](/gemstones/white-agate/can-go-in-water).

## Smoke Cleansing

Pass White Agate slowly through the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, turning the piece so all surfaces are exposed. Ventilate the room during and after, as you will be breathing the smoke too. This method is widely used in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to the stone.

## Sound Cleansing

Place White Agate near a singing bowl, tuning fork, or bell and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound works at a distance, so the stone does not need to be touched or moved. This is particularly convenient for jewelry pieces in settings you do not want to get wet.

## Moonlight

Set White Agate on a dry surface — a windowsill or outdoors on a dry night — and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check overnight weather: dew or rain will expose the stone to moisture, which is not harmful to the stone itself but may leave water marks on a polished surface. Bring it inside before morning condensation forms if outdoors.

## Dry Earth

Bury White Agate in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or fine sand for 24 hours. Retrieve the piece, brush off loose soil with a dry cloth, and inspect the surface. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which tends to hold moisture. This is a traditional grounding method used in crystal practice.

## Breath and Visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, breathe in slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and can be done anywhere.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In most crystal traditions, cleansing and recharging are separate steps. After cleansing White Agate, common recharging methods include placing it in brief morning sunlight (30 minutes is typical; natural white agate does not fade noticeably with moderate sun exposure, though dyed specimens may fade over longer periods), setting it near a clear quartz cluster, or resting it on a dry selenite slab. For a full guide to recharging options, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary on frequency. Common guidance is to cleanse White Agate when it first arrives, after any period of intensive use or emotional intensity, and once a month during regular display or storage. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice.
