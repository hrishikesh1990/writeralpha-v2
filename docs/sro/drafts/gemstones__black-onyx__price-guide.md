# Black Onyx Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Black onyx is an affordable gemstone. Dyed chalcedony cabochons and tumbled stones — which represent the vast majority of what is sold — typically retail at $1–$10 per carat for standard grades, and $10–$50 per carat for well-cut, high-polish pieces (prices checked September 2026). Finished jewelry ranges from about $50 for a simple setting to $500 or more for elaborate designs in precious metals. Understanding what drives the price makes it straightforward to avoid overpaying.

## Why Most Black Onyx Is Affordable

Black onyx is not a rare mineral. It is a variety of chalcedony (SiO2), and the uniform black color almost universally present in commercial material is achieved by dyeing — an accepted, disclosed treatment with a history of over 2,000 years. Because dyed black chalcedony can be produced from relatively abundant raw material, the stone occupies the lower-to-mid tier of the gemstone price spectrum.

Factors that push prices up within that tier:

- **Color uniformity and depth**: Pieces with an even, deeply saturated black surface command a premium over patchy or grayish material
- **Polish quality**: A high mirror finish adds value; surface haze or micro-scratching reduces it
- **Cut precision**: Well-proportioned cabochons with smooth domes or cleanly faceted stones are more valuable than poorly shaped material
- **Size**: Larger stones are proportionally more expensive per carat, as with most gems, because consistent color across a large area requires quality raw material
- **Treatment status**: The rare, genuinely untreated natural black onyx commands a premium over dyed material — but any claim of "natural" warrants gemological certification (see [how to identify black onyx](/gemstones/black-onyx/how-to-identify))

## Retail Price Ranges (September 2026)

| Form | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Tumbled stones (small, dyed) | $1–$5 per carat; $2–$15 per stone |
| Polished cabochons (jewelry grade) | $5–$50 per carat |
| Natural (undyed) black onyx, certified | $5–$20+ per carat |
| Simple black onyx ring or pendant | $50–$200 |
| Elaborate jewelry in precious metal | $500+ |
| Beaded bracelet (common retail) | $10–$40 |

These are retail ranges from multiple sources; wholesale prices are substantially lower. Prices vary by seller, origin, and grading criteria.

## The Treatment Question and What It Means for Value

Virtually all black onyx on the market is dyed chalcedony — this is standard and should be disclosed, not hidden. A reputable seller will state whether a stone is treated. Dyed material is not inferior for crystal practice, jewelry use, or display; it is simply the expected form of the stone. Paying a significant premium for "untreated" material without independent gemological certification is a common way to overpay.

For context on hardness and durability — relevant to long-term value in jewelry use — see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale). Black onyx at 6.5–7 is suitable for most jewelry uses but benefits from protective settings in high-wear applications such as rings.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

1. **Expect dyed material and price accordingly**: The standard retail price for quality dyed black onyx is modest. If a seller is asking significantly more for standard pieces, ask why.
2. **Compare per-carat to per-piece pricing**: Some retailers list per-piece prices that obscure high per-carat rates; others list per-carat prices that look high but are reasonable for larger stones.
3. **Request disclosure of treatments**: Reputable sellers disclose dyeing. Reluctance to discuss treatment is a warning sign.
4. **For high-value purchases, get certification**: Any piece presented as rare or natural black onyx and priced accordingly should come with a report from a recognized gemological laboratory.
5. **Source ethically**: For guidance on responsible purchasing, see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing).

## Price Relative to Similar Black Stones

Black onyx is typically less expensive than black spinel or black tourmaline cut to gemstone quality, and far less expensive than black diamond. It is similarly priced to black obsidian and other common opaque black materials. If a retailer prices black onyx at a comparable level to black spinel without explanation, verify what you are actually buying.
