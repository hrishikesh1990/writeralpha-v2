# How to Cleanse Obsidian — Safe Methods Explained

Obsidian is a volcanic glass with a Mohs hardness of 5–5.5. It can tolerate a brief rinse in plain water but should not be soaked or placed in salt water, which can enter micro-fractures and dull the surface over time. Safe cleansing methods include smoke, sound, moonlight, dry earth, a short water rinse, and breath or visualization.

## Water and Obsidian

Obsidian is not water-soluble. A quick rinse under cool running water is safe for a polished tumbled stone or sphere. However, obsidian's conchoidal fracture means raw or rough pieces have micro-fractures at edges, and prolonged soaking lets water enter those fractures and increase the risk of cracking. Salt water is more abrasive than plain water and more likely to widen micro-fractures, so avoid it.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold obsidian in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, rotating it slowly so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is the most commonly used method in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to obsidian. Ventilate the room while you work.

### Sound

Place obsidian near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and let the vibration move through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone does not need to be touched or moved through any substance. It is useful for raw pieces with sharp edges that are awkward to hold.

### Moonlight

Set obsidian on a dry windowsill or outdoor surface on a clear night and leave it for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast first: dew, rain, or high humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it inside before dawn to avoid morning condensation. In crystal tradition, full moonlight is favored for a combined cleanse-and-recharge step.

### Dry earth burial

Place obsidian in a pot of dry indoor potting soil or fine sand for 24 hours. Dig a shallow hole, set the stone in, and cover it. Retrieve and brush off any loose material with a dry soft cloth. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may hold moisture. Practitioners in many traditions use this method to return the stone to a grounded, neutral state.

### Brief water rinse

For polished obsidian (tumbled stones, spheres, palmstones), hold it under cool running water for 20 to 30 seconds, then dry thoroughly with a soft cloth. Do not soak. Do not use this method on raw or rough-edged pieces unless you intend to dry them completely and promptly.

### Breath and visualization

Hold obsidian in both hands, take a slow breath, and exhale steadily across the surface while directing your focus toward releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and leaves no residue. It is used for a quick reset between uses.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal traditions treat cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing obsidian, place it in brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes is ample for an opaque volcanic glass), near a clear quartz cluster, or on a dry selenite slab. ## How Often to Cleanse

There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule; this is a matter of personal practice. Common guidelines in crystal traditions suggest cleansing obsidian when it first arrives, after any period of intensive use, and monthly during regular display. Inspect polished pieces periodically for any scratches or surface dulling; obsidian at 5–5.5 Mohs is harder than most minerals but softer than quartz and can be scratched by quartz-rich rocks or abrasive surfaces.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store obsidian away from harder minerals, particularly quartz (Mohs 7) and diamond, which can scratch the surface. A soft cloth pouch or a lined box keeps the surface protected. Raw obsidian pieces should be stored with care: the conchoidal fracture produces razor-sharp edges that can cut skin. Wash hands after handling freshly broken or chipped obsidian to remove any fine glass particles.
