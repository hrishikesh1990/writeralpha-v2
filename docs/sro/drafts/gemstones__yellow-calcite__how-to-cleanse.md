# How to Cleanse Yellow Calcite Without Damaging It

Yellow Calcite must not be soaked in water or exposed to acids. As a calcium carbonate mineral (CaCO3) with a Mohs hardness of 3, it dissolves slowly in even mildly acidic solutions and its polished surface is easily etched by prolonged moisture. Safe cleansing methods are smoke, sound, moonlight on a dry night, visualization, and brief contact with selenite or dry earth. Each is explained below.

## Why Water and Acids Are Off-Limits

Yellow Calcite is a carbonate mineral. Acids — including weak acids such as vinegar, carbonated water, citrus juice, and naturally acidic rainwater — dissolve it by reacting with the carbonate ion. Even plain water, especially if slightly acidic, will slowly etch a polished calcite surface with repeated contact. Salt water is more damaging than plain water and should be avoided entirely.

Wet salt on the surface is similarly harmful: salt absorbs ambient moisture and prolongs acid contact. Dry salt burial, a method used for some harder stones, is not safe for yellow calcite.

For a full explanation of the chemistry and what happens when calcite goes in water, see [can Yellow Calcite go in water](/gemstones/yellow-calcite/can-go-in-water). For a general overview of safe cleansing by stone type, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold or place the yellow calcite piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the piece slowly so the smoke reaches all surfaces. Ensure the room is ventilated. This is the most widely used method for water-sensitive stones and carries no physical risk to calcite. The stone does not need to touch flame — proximity to smoke is sufficient.

### Sound

Place yellow calcite near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone is never exposed to moisture or friction. This method is particularly useful for larger raw specimens or delicate crystal formations where handling is risky.

### Moonlight

Set yellow calcite on a dry indoor windowsill or a dry outdoor surface on a clear night and leave it for several hours or overnight. Check the weather forecast before placing it outside: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it in before morning condensation forms. Crystal tradition uses this method for a combined cleanse and recharge in one step.

### Visualization and breath

Hold the yellow calcite in both hands, breathe in slowly, then exhale steadily across the stone's surface while picturing stagnant energy leaving it. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and poses zero physical risk. It is used when other methods are not convenient or for a quick reset between uses.

### Selenite or quartz cluster proximity

Place yellow calcite on a dry selenite slab or beside a clear quartz cluster for several hours. In crystal tradition, both selenite and clear quartz are described as self-cleansing and capable of clearing nearby stones. Neither method introduces moisture or abrasion. Ensure the selenite slab itself is dry — selenite also dissolves in water.

## What to Avoid

| Method | Why to avoid |
|---|---|
| Water soaking | CaCO3 etches in water, especially acidic water |
| Salt water | Corrosive; accelerates surface damage |
| Dry salt burial | Salt is hygroscopic and draws moisture |
| Vinegar or acid rinse | Dissolves the stone's surface vigorously |
| Steam cleansing | Hot moisture damages the polished surface |
| Ultrasonic cleaner | Vibration + water; unsuitable for Mohs 3 material |

## Recharging After Cleansing

Cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps in crystal tradition. After cleansing, yellow calcite can be placed in brief morning sunlight — 15 to 30 minutes is common practice. Note that prolonged UV exposure may gradually bleach the yellow color, which in calcite typically comes from iron-bearing or organic impurities; brief daily exposure is unlikely to cause visible change, but extended hours of direct sun over weeks is not recommended.

Alternatives to sunlight: place the stone beside a clear quartz cluster, on a dry selenite slab, or under moonlight during a full moon. For a full guide to recharging methods and their trade-offs, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary widely on frequency. A commonly suggested guideline is: cleanse when the stone arrives, after any period of intense use, and once a month during regular display. There is no physical basis for a fixed cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice.

As a maintenance note, inspect the stone periodically for any surface haziness or dulling of the polish. If the surface appears etched or less reflective than when new, the stone may have had unintended contact with moisture or acids. Store it away from bathrooms and kitchen areas, in a cool, dry location.

## Physical Cleaning (Dust and Surface Grime)

To remove dust or surface grime without cleansing ritually, wipe the stone with a soft, dry cloth. A very slightly damp cloth (not wet) can be used briefly and the stone should be dried immediately afterward. Never submerge or rinse under running water.
