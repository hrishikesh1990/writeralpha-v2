# How to Identify Real Angel Aura Quartz

Real Angel Aura Quartz is natural quartz permanently coated with vaporized metals — typically platinum and silver — in a vacuum chamber. The iridescence is on the surface, not inside the crystal, and the coating is uniform and anchored, not painted, pooled, or peeling. Glass imitations, dyed pieces, and other aura varieties are the most common alternatives to recognize.

## What Genuine Angel Aura Quartz Looks Like

The starting material is natural clear quartz in trigonal crystal form — you will usually see the characteristic hexagonal prism shape with pyramidal terminations on points, or the rounded form of tumbled pieces. The rainbow iridescence sits on the outer surface: as you tilt the piece, colors shift across the flat crystal faces, with the effect strongest on smooth areas and weaker on chipped edges or rough zones where the coating thins.

At any chip, nick, or fresh fracture, you will see plain clear or slightly milky quartz under the coating — a small transparent or white window through the rainbow film. This is normal and expected, and it is one of the clearest indicators that the coating was vapor-deposited on real quartz rather than applied as paint or dye.

The surface under the metallic sheen will show growth features typical of quartz: horizontal striations on prism faces, natural inclusions, phantoms, and occasional cloudiness within the crystal body. The quartz itself is real; only the color is engineered.

## How to Test It

### Weight and temperature

Pick up the piece. Quartz is denser (2.65 g/cm3) and colder to the touch than glass or resin. A piece that feels light and room-temperature rather than cool and solid is likely glass. This is a rough check rather than a definitive test, but glass imitations commonly fail it noticeably.

### Hardness scratch test

Quartz scratches glass (a window pane or a glass bottle base works). Drag a corner of the piece firmly across glass: genuine quartz leaves a visible scratch on the glass surface. Glass-based imitations rated around 5.5 on the Mohs scale will scratch softer materials but not glass itself — or will scratch much less deeply than quartz. Opalite, a common Angel Aura look-alike, is glass and will not scratch standard glass reliably.

### Coating uniformity

Genuine vapor-deposited coatings are extremely thin and adhere tightly to crystal faces. Look closely at crevices, growth lines, and any cracks: the rainbow effect on genuine pieces is concentrated on smooth faces and thins out naturally at edges and recessed areas, but it does not pool or puddle in cracks. If color is concentrated inside cracks and crevices rather than on the flat faces, the iridescence may be a dye or paint application rather than true vapor deposition.

### Peeling or flaking

Properly bonded aura coatings do not peel. If you see any lifting, flaking, or areas where the iridescent layer is separating from the surface, the piece was not produced through genuine vacuum vapor deposition.

## Common Look-Alikes

| Opalite glass | Milky blue-white glow; feels warm and light; will not scratch glass; no crystal structure visible |
| Coated clear glass points | No natural crystal growth features; feels lighter than quartz; may scratch with fingernail |
| Dyed/painted quartz | Color pooled in cracks; may flake; coating may rub off on cloth with pressure |
| Other aura varieties (titanium, aqua, rainbow aura) | Same process, different metals; titanium aura is typically bolder and more metallic; aqua aura has a gold coating producing bright electric blue |
| Mystic topaz | Similar surface iridescence but on topaz, not quartz; different crystal shape; much rarer and priced higher |

## The "Natural Angel Aura" Red Flag

All Angel Aura Quartz is treated — the iridescent finish does not occur in nature on quartz. Any seller describing a piece as "naturally occurring angel aura" or claiming the rainbow is an internal property of the stone is using the term inaccurately. The quartz is natural; the iridescent coating is entirely human-made. This is a known fact about the material, not a defect — but if a seller presents it otherwise, treat that as a signal to scrutinize the rest of their descriptions.
