# Ruby Zoisite Price Guide

Ruby Zoisite is an affordable ornamental stone. Tumbled stones typically retail from about $3 to $12; standard cabochons from $15 to $60; larger ruby-rich cabochons from $50 to $200. Sterling silver jewelry ranges from approximately $40 to $250. These are broad asking-price ranges from retail listings observed in July–September 2026 and are not appraisals. Prices vary by ruby coverage, size, quality, and seller.

## What Drives Ruby Zoisite Price

Several factors determine where any given piece falls within the price range:

**Ruby coverage and distribution:** Pieces where red ruby patches are prominent, well-distributed, and clearly visible against the green matrix command higher prices than pieces that are mostly green zoisite with scattered red specks. Both are genuine Ruby Zoisite, but the more visually striking specimens are more sought after.

**Black hornblende proportion:** A high proportion of black hornblende relative to green and red lowers perceived value. Pieces with small, well-placed black highlights are preferred over those that are predominantly dark.

**Size and format:** Ruby Zoisite is an abundant material from Tanzania, so price scales more by size and craftsmanship than by rarity. A detailed large carving commands more than a rough piece of the same weight simply because of the labor involved.

**Polish and craftsmanship:** Well-executed cabochons with a tight polish that shows the ruby zones sitting slightly raised above the zoisite surface attract higher prices than flat or dull finishes.

**Setting:** Sterling silver pendants and rings add the cost of metalwork. The stone itself is typically affordable even in jeweler-made pieces; the metalwork and labor are usually the primary cost drivers for finished jewelry.

## Retail Price Ranges (September 2026)

| Format | Typical retail asking range |
|---|---|
| Small tumbled stone | $3–$12 |
| Standard cabochon | $15–$60 |
| Large or strongly ruby-rich cabochon | $50–$200 |
| Bead strand | $10–$60 |
| Palm stone or small egg | $15–$60 |
| Small sphere or carving | $25–$125 |
| Large sphere or detailed carving | $100–$750 |
| Sterling silver jewelry | $40–$250 |
| Fine collector specimen (prominent crystals) | $100–$1,000+ |

These are broad asking prices from retail sellers, not confirmed resale values. Wholesale and rough prices are considerably lower: rough anyolite has been listed at approximately $0.10–$2 per carat, and anyolite cabochon material at $0.50–$3 per carat for a fairly even ruby-and-zoisite mix.

## Per-Carat Pricing Context

Faceted anyolite appears at approximately $2–$25 per carat in wholesale gem listings. This range reflects the variable quality of the opaque ruby inclusions. The ruby in anyolite is not comparable to transparent, gem-quality ruby used in fine jewelry — opaque ruby in a zoisite matrix is priced as an ornamental material, not a precious stone. Any appraisal that assigns gem-ruby per-carat values to anyolite specimens is misrepresenting the material. For guidance on recognizing genuine material and inflated value claims, see [how to identify Ruby Zoisite](/gemstones/ruby-zoisite/how-to-identify).

## How to Avoid Overpaying

Price alone is a useful sanity check: the stone is abundant and genuinely affordable. If a seller is asking more than a few hundred dollars for a single tumbled stone or small rough piece, investigate the claim carefully. Legitimate gem lab reports confirm material identity but say nothing about market value; appraisals come separately and should be from a credentialed, independent appraiser.

Buy from sellers who describe the material accurately as anyolite or ruby-in-zoisite, give the origin (Tanzania, Longido or Mundarara mine region), and do not compare opaque ruby inclusions to faceted gem ruby. For guidance on sourcing ethically and accurately described crystal material, see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing).
