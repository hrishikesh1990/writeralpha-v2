# Can Turquoise Go in Water? What Happens and What to Do

Turquoise should not go in water. The mineral is naturally porous and absorbs liquids into its internal structure, which can cause discoloration, chemical staining from soaps or dissolved salts, and — in untreated material — progressive physical damage. Brief, accidental contact is unlikely to cause immediate visible harm, but regular exposure will degrade turquoise over time.

## Why Turquoise Absorbs Water

Turquoise is a hydrated copper-aluminum phosphate with the chemical formula CuAl₆(PO₄)₄(OH)₈·4H₂O. Its crystal system is triclinic and its Mohs hardness is 5 to 6. The mineral forms in dry, arid regions as a secondary mineral where copper-bearing groundwater reacts with aluminum- and phosphorus-containing rocks. This formation process produces a fine-grained, microcrystalline structure with significant porosity — the internal spaces that make the mineral prone to absorbing surrounding liquids.

Specific gravity runs 2.6 to 2.8, and the surface lustre ranges from waxy to sub-vitreous depending on the specimen. The same porosity that gives turquoise its waxy feel when polished also means it readily takes in anything it sits in contact with: water, oils, sweat, lotions, and chemical cleaning agents all penetrate, and most cannot easily be removed once absorbed.

## What Water Does to Turquoise

Water alone does not dissolve turquoise or react with it chemically in the way acids do. The primary risks are:

**Discoloration.** Water carries dissolved minerals and salts even when it appears clean. As water evaporates from inside a porous specimen, it can deposit material that alters the color — turquoise may shift from blue toward green or develop uneven patches. Tap water with high mineral content poses a greater risk than pure water.

**Chemical absorption.** Any substance dissolved in the water — soap, detergent, shampoo, conditioner, chlorine — penetrates the pore structure. Turquoise has a strong affinity for oils and organic compounds, which can permanently shift its color toward green or gray.

**Salt water damage.** Salt water combines the risk of moisture absorption with direct ionic interaction at the mineral surface. Dissolved sodium chloride deposits within the pore structure as the water evaporates, accelerating surface degradation and potentially causing micro-cracking in fine material.

**Pool water.** Chlorinated swimming pool water is particularly aggressive. Chlorine at pool concentrations can react with the copper component of turquoise and cause color changes that are not reversible.

**Hot water and steam.** Heat combined with moisture can cause cracking in porous material. Ultrasonic cleaners — which vibrate jewelry in water — are not suitable for turquoise as they can fracture the pore structure and dislodge resin treatments.

## Does Treatment Grade Affect Water Sensitivity?

Yes. Most turquoise sold in jewelry and crystal retail has been treated to reduce porosity and improve durability:

| Treatment grade | What it means | Water risk |
|---|---|---|
| Natural (untreated) | No added resins or wax; original porosity intact | Highest — absorbs water readily |
| Wax stabilized | Surface sealed with wax under pressure | Moderate — wax can dissolve with heat or detergents |
| Resin stabilized | Pores impregnated with colorless resin | Lower — but micro-cracks still allow entry over time |
| Dyed / reconstructed | Color added or material reconstituted with binders | Variable — dye can leach in water |

Even resin-stabilized turquoise is not safe for prolonged soaking. The treatment improves durability against casual contact but does not make turquoise waterproof.

## Accidental Contact: What to Do

If turquoise gets wet accidentally — caught in rain, splashed, or briefly rinsed — dry it immediately with a clean, dry, soft cloth. Blot rather than rub. Let it air-dry fully at room temperature before storing. Do not use a hairdryer or place it in direct sunlight to speed drying; heat can cause surface cracking.

For turquoise set in silver: moisture trapped between the stone and the setting is more damaging than surface moisture, as it can cause tarnishing of the metal in contact with the stone and stain the turquoise from beneath. Dry set jewelry thoroughly and store in a dry location.

## Safer Cleaning Alternatives

For surface cleaning:
- Wipe with a dry soft cloth (microfiber or chamois)
- For set jewelry, a very slightly dampened cloth — wrung out so no water can pool — followed immediately by thorough drying
