# Moonstone Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Moonstone prices in 2026 range from approximately $3 per carat for opaque commercial material to $500 or more per carat for rare, transparent, fine blue-adularescent stones from Sri Lanka. The driver is not carat weight but the quality of the adularescent glow: a small, vivid blue-flash moonstone routinely outprices a large milky specimen by a wide margin.

## What Drives Moonstone Price

**Adularescence quality** is the single most important factor. The glow should be strong, centered on the cabochon, and move fluidly as the stone is tilted. A dull, patchy, or fixed sheen drastically reduces value. Blue adularescence commands a premium over white or silver sheen because it requires thinner and more precisely formed internal feldspar layers and is far less common.

**Transparency and body color** matter most in blue moonstone, where a near-colorless, transparent body allows the blue flash to appear most vivid. For peach, gray, and rainbow varieties, an even, attractive body color is preferred over muddy or uneven tone.

**Clarity:** Natural moonstone comes with characteristic inclusions called centipedes — short, parallel internal tension cracks visible under magnification. Stones with heavy visible cracking or cloudiness that obscures the adularescence are worth less. Eye-clean material with subtle natural inclusions is preferred.

**Carat weight and size:** Fine blue moonstone is rarely found in large, clean specimens. A blue-flash moonstone above 5 carats with strong adularescence commands disproportionately higher prices per carat because the quality required to produce that effect in larger material is scarce. Commercial white and peach material comes in a wider range of sizes with more predictable per-carat pricing.

**Origin:** Sri Lankan moonstone — particularly the classic blue-sheen variety — commands the strongest premium among serious buyers. Burmese material is also valued. Indian origin is common for commercial and rainbow material. Madagascar produces rainbow moonstone of varied quality.

**Cut quality:** Moonstone is almost always cut as a cabochon. The dome height and orientation matter: the flat base must be cut parallel to the adularescence plane, and the dome must be high enough to concentrate the glow centrally. A poorly oriented cut can eliminate visible adularescence from an otherwise fine stone.

**Treatments:** Most commercial moonstone is untreated. Some lower-grade material is filled, coated, or assembled as a doublet. Treatment disclosure affects value; ask sellers for a statement.

## Price Ranges by Quality Tier (September 2026)

Prices reflect natural, untreated loose moonstone. Jewelry settings and craftsmanship add significantly to retail cost beyond stone value alone. Prices vary by seller and market.

| Quality tier | Characteristics | Approximate price per carat |
|---|---|---|
| Entry commercial | Opaque to milky, weak or absent sheen, visible inclusions | $3–$15 |
| Good commercial | Translucent, visible white or silver adularescence, acceptable cutting | $15–$40 |
| Fine white / silver | Strong floating sheen, good coverage, clean appearance | $40–$100 |
| Premium blue moonstone | Transparent to highly translucent, strong centered blue adularescence | $100–$300 |
| Exceptional collector quality | Electric-blue adularescence, high transparency, larger size, fine cutting | $300–$500+ |

**Rainbow moonstone** (technically adularescent labradorite, sold under the moonstone trade name) commonly ranges from $15 to $100 per carat, with very fine transparent material exceeding that level.

**Peach and gray moonstone** typically ranges from $5 to $40 per carat; saturated material with a strong glow at the upper end.

**Set jewelry** adds a craftsmanship premium: a sterling silver moonstone ring or pendant retails for $25 to $800 depending on stone quality, size, and maker. Gold settings move prices to $150 and beyond for fine pieces.

## How to Evaluate a Moonstone Before Buying

Hold the stone under a single light source and rotate it slowly. A strong moonstone shows a smooth, gliding glow that follows your movement across the dome. Patchy, scattered, or static light indicates weaker layering quality.

In photographs, adularescence is difficult to assess because the effect depends on viewing angle and movement. Vendors who photograph moonstones at the single best angle can make average stones look exceptional. Where possible, request a video clip of the stone rotating under a point light.

Very low prices for pieces described as "blue moonstone" or "rainbow moonstone" are a signal to check identification. Opalite glass — the most common imitation — may sell for less than $1 per piece. See [how to identify moonstone](/gemstones/moonstone/how-to-identify) for field tests.

## What Affects Durability and Long-Term Value

Moonstone's Mohs hardness of 6 to 6.5 and two directions of perfect cleavage make it more fragile than many other gemstones at similar price points. A stone that cracks in a ring setting is not repairable in most cases. For durability context, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale).
