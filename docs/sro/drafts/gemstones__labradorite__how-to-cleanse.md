# How to Cleanse Labradorite Without Damaging It

Labradorite can be rinsed briefly under cool running water, but prolonged soaking is not recommended: the stone's polish can dull over time, and salt water is more abrasive than plain water. The safest cleansing methods — smoke, sound, moonlight, dry earth burial, and breath — pose no physical risk to the specimen. Each is described below.

## What Labradorite Can and Cannot Handle

Labradorite is a plagioclase feldspar with a Mohs hardness of 6 to 6.5. It is not water-soluble, so brief contact with water does not chemically damage it. What causes wear over time is the combination of water, abrasion, and any salts or cleaning agents that can lodge in small surface cracks and dull the polish. Labradorite's labradorescence — the iridescent play of color it is known for — can appear muted if the polished surface is degraded.

Specifically:
- **Brief rinse under cool water:** acceptable for polished specimens, but dry the stone completely immediately afterwards.
- **Salt water:** not recommended. Salt is abrasive and can lodge in the stone's perfect cleavage planes, dulling the surface over repeated use.
- **Prolonged soaking:** not recommended. Extended water exposure softens the polish gradually, which can affect the visibility of labradorescence.
- **Ultrasonic cleaners or steam:** not recommended. Labradorite's perfect cleavage makes it vulnerable to the vibrations in ultrasonic cleaning, and steam can cause thermal stress.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the labradorite piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the piece slowly so all surfaces pass through the smoke. Smoke cleansing is the most widely used method for labradorite in crystal practice: it is quick, poses no physical risk to the stone, and leaves no residue. Ventilate the room — you are breathing the smoke as well.

### Sound

Place labradorite near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the sound vibration to move through it for one to two minutes. Sound works at a distance, so the stone need not be touched or passed through any liquid. This method suits large or display specimens that are difficult to move.

### Moonlight

Set labradorite on a dry surface — a windowsill or an outdoor spot — on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check conditions first: dew, rain, or high humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms. In crystal tradition, moonlight is considered a fitting method for labradorite given the stone's traditional association with lunar and intuitive qualities.

### Dry earth burial

Bury labradorite in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or sand for 24 hours. Dig a shallow hole, place the stone, cover it, and retrieve it the next day. Brush off loose soil with a dry soft cloth. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may retain moisture. This method is used in tradition to return the stone's energy to a neutral state.

### Breath and visualization

Hold labradorite in both hands, take a slow breath in, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing mentally on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and is used when others are unavailable or for a quick reset between practice sessions.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are separate steps. After cleansing, labradorite is commonly placed in brief morning sunlight (15 to 30 minutes), near a cluster of clear quartz, or on a dry selenite plate. Prolonged direct sun exposure is not recommended, as it can cause some surface fading over time, though short periods pose no significant risk to a stone with labradorite's generally dark gray base.

For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice. Common guidelines in crystal tradition suggest cleansing labradorite when it first arrives, after any extended period of intensive use, and monthly during regular display. If you wear labradorite daily, more frequent cleansing is often suggested by practitioners.

As a practical maintenance note: inspect your labradorite periodically for any cloudiness or dullness that could indicate surface wear. Store it away from harder stones (diamonds, sapphires, quartz) that can scratch its surface, and keep it in a soft cloth or padded box.
