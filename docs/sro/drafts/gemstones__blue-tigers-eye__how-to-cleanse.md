# How to Cleanse Blue Tiger's Eye Without Damaging It

Blue Tiger's Eye can be briefly rinsed under lukewarm water, but prolonged soaking and salt water should be avoided. For a ritual cleanse, smoke, sound, moonlight, and dry earth burial are all safe and widely used. The steps below cover each method, with the physical reasoning for any limits.

## Why Water Matters for Blue Tiger's Eye

Blue Tiger's Eye is a quartz-family stone (SiO2) with a Mohs hardness of 6.5–7. This hardness means the surface resists scratching well, and quartz itself does not dissolve or corrode in fresh water. A quick rinse under cool or lukewarm tap water — 20 to 30 seconds — is physically safe and removes surface dust and oils effectively.

What to avoid:

- **Prolonged soaking**: Extended immersion can work moisture into any micro-cracks or fracture planes in the stone over time, and it softens or degrades adhesives used in settings.
- **Salt water**: Salt is mildly abrasive and corrosive to metal settings, and a salt-water soak combined with time can penetrate hairline fractures that plain water would not.
- **Gem elixirs (indirect method preferred)**: Some practitioners make crystal-infused water. Blue Tiger's Eye should not be placed directly in drinking water intended for consumption — use the indirect method (stone placed outside a glass container) if you use this practice.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Water rinse (physical cleaning)

Hold the piece under lukewarm tap water for 20–30 seconds. Use your fingers or a very soft cloth to wipe the surface gently. Pat dry immediately with a clean cloth. Do not leave the stone sitting in pooled water or in a wet cloth. This method cleans surface residue but is generally not the method practitioners use for ritual cleansing — it is most useful after the stone has been handled a lot or stored in a dusty place.

### Smoke cleansing

Pass Blue Tiger's Eye slowly through the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a stick for 30 to 60 seconds, ensuring all surfaces are exposed. This is among the most common ritual cleansing methods used for this stone. It poses no physical risk to the specimen. Ensure the room is ventilated.

### Sound

Place the stone near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound works at a distance from the stone, so nothing touches or enters it. This method suits pieces that are set in jewelry (where liquid methods could affect the metal or adhesive) and large display pieces.

### Moonlight

Set Blue Tiger's Eye on a dry windowsill or on a dry surface outdoors on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the weather first: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will introduce unwanted moisture. Bring the stone indoors before morning condensation forms. Practitioners use a full moon for what they describe as a more complete reset.

### Dry earth burial

Place the stone in a small container of dry indoor potting soil or coarse sand for 24 hours. Bury it fully, then retrieve and brush off any residue with a dry cloth. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp. This is particularly popular in traditions that emphasize grounding.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, take a slow breath in, and exhale steadily across its surface while focusing on clearing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and is suitable when other options are unavailable.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal traditions treat cleansing and recharging as separate steps. After cleansing Blue Tiger's Eye, you can:

- Place it in morning sunlight for 15–30 minutes. Brief sun exposure poses no physical risk to this opaque stone, and blue tiger's eye does not fade significantly under short exposure.
- Set it near a clear quartz cluster.
- Place it on a dry selenite slab overnight.

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary widely on frequency. A commonly suggested approach is to cleanse when the stone arrives, after any period of concentrated use, and monthly for stones on regular display. These are matters of practice, not physical maintenance requirements. As a physical note: inspect the surface periodically for any buildup of dust or oils and rinse briefly as needed.
