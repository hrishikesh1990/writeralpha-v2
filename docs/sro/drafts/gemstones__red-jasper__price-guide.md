# Red Jasper Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Red Jasper is one of the more affordable stones in the crystal market. It is abundant, widely sourced, and not subject to the scarcity that drives prices for rarer minerals. A single tumbled piece at retail runs roughly $3–$16 depending on quality and seller; cabochons for jewelry range from $4 to $30. Prices checked September 2026; they vary by grade, origin, and seller.

## Retail Price Ranges by Form

| Form | Typical retail range | Unit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single tumbled stone (A grade) | $3–$16 | per stone | Higher end for ethically sourced, well-polished specimens |
| Tumbled stones (bulk) | $9–$15 | per pound | Wholesale/bulk suppliers; wide variation by grade |
| Rough stone | $5–$20 | per pound | Color consistency and size drive price |
| Cabochon (jewelry grade) | $4–$30 | per piece | Shape, size, and finish are the main variables |
| Beads (strand of 15–20) | $2–$15 | per strand | Uniformity and hole alignment affect yield |
| Decorative slab (polished) | $6–$28 | per square foot | Used for inlay or display |

## What Drives the Price of Red Jasper

**Color consistency.** Deep, even red throughout the stone commands more than mottled or yellowish material. The most valued pieces have a clean, saturated red-brown with no cloudy patches.

**Pattern and markings.** Plain red jasper is common and cheap. Material with distinctive banding, orbicular patterns, or contrasting inclusions (a form sometimes called brecciated red jasper) is rarer and priced higher.

**Cut and finish.** Rough or minimally processed material costs least. Tumbling adds labor and time. Cabochon cutting, sphere shaping, or carving adds further skill and equipment cost, reflected in higher prices.

**Grade.** Most lapidary suppliers grade their tumbled stones (A, B, EX grades are common). A-grade material is hand-checked for color and surface quality; B-grade accepts more natural imperfections. The same stone weight costs less in B grade.

**Origin.** Red jasper from India and some Chinese sources is generally the least expensive on the market. Brazilian and some African material often sits at a mid-range. Specialty American varieties (from Oregon, Idaho, or Arizona) can command premiums.

**Seller type and sourcing claims.** Retail crystal shops, ethical sourcing certifications, and small independent sellers all add markup over wholesale prices. A single ethically sourced, A-grade tumbled stone from a specialist retailer may cost $15–$16 where the same weight of B-grade material from a bulk wholesaler costs $1–$2.

**Treatment.** Untreated natural Red Jasper has no color enhancements. Dyed, stabilized, or resin-impregnated material should cost less, but is sometimes sold at the same price as natural stone without disclosure. See [how to identify Red Jasper](/gemstones/red-jasper/how-to-identify) for tests that help distinguish natural from treated material.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Know what's normal.** Red Jasper is abundant. A price much above $20 for a single standard tumbled stone (about 1–1.5 inches, without special carving or setting) is above the typical retail ceiling unless the piece is unusually large, shaped, or from a specialist supplier with documented ethical sourcing.

**Ask about treatment.** Dyed material is not inherently inferior for decorative use, but you should not pay natural-stone prices for it. Ask sellers directly whether the stone has been dyed or stabilized.

**Buy by weight for bulk.** If you need multiple tumbled stones, buying by the pound from a lapidary wholesale supplier is more economical than buying individual stones from a crystal shop.

**Compare across stone types.** Red Jasper's hardness (6.5–7 on the Mohs scale) and durability make it practical for everyday wear and handling. For the same price point, it generally outlasts softer stones. For how hardness affects durability and value, see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale).

**Check sourcing.** Some Red Jasper is mined in regions with variable labor and environmental standards. Retailers who document their supply chain typically charge more but offer more certainty about what you are buying. For a full discussion, see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing).
