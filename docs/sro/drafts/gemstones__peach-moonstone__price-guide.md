# Peach Moonstone Price Guide — Cost and Value Factors

Peach moonstone in the commercial to fine range typically retails for approximately $5–$50 per carat for loose cabochons (September 2026). The most important value driver is the quality of adularescence — the internal glow that moves as the stone tilts — followed by transparency, cut orientation, and size. Peach, gray, and similar-toned moonstone varieties occupy a lower price tier than the rarest blue-flash moonstone, making peach moonstone one of the more accessible varieties.

## Price Ranges by Quality

The following ranges are approximate U.S. retail prices for natural, loose peach moonstone cabochons, based on data from mid-2026. Prices vary by seller, region, and individual specimen.

| Quality tier | Characteristics | Approximate retail price |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial | Opaque or milky, weak or offset adularescence, visible surface blemishes | $3–$15 per carat |
| Good | Visible white or peach adularescence, acceptable cutting, some inclusions | $15–$40 per carat |
| Fine | Strong floating sheen, good coverage across the stone face, clean surface | $40–$100 per carat |
| Exceptional | Unusually strong adularescence, high translucency, superior cut and size | $100+ per carat |

As a rough guide for the retail crystal market: a small tumbled peach moonstone (1–3 grams) typically sells for $2–$8 per piece; a larger shaped cabochon or palm stone may range from $10–$40 depending on quality and size. These per-piece prices reflect that most crystal-market stones are commercial to good grade.

## What Drives Peach Moonstone's Price

### Adularescence quality

The adularescence — the billowing internal glow — is the primary value factor for all moonstone, including peach varieties. A strong, centered effect that remains visible across a wide range of viewing angles commands a premium. A narrow flash that only appears when the stone is tilted to a precise angle is less valuable because it may not be visible during normal jewelry wear.

In peach moonstone, the adularescence is typically white to silvery over a warm peach body color. A stone with both a pleasing body color and clear adularescence is more valuable than one where the milky tone overwhelms the glow.

### Transparency and clarity

More transparent material allows the adularescent glow to appear suspended within the stone, increasing its visual depth and appeal. Opaque or heavily included material shows a flat, surface-level sheen at best. Characteristic inclusions in [peach moonstone](/gemstones/peach-moonstone) include "centipede" cracks (tension cracks visible under magnification) and twinning planes — these are natural and expected, but heavy inclusion concentrations reduce value.

### Cut and orientation

Peach moonstone is almost always cut as a cabochon — a smooth, domed form — because this shape displays adularescence far more effectively than faceting. The cutter must orient the stone so the internal feldspar layers are correctly aligned; poor orientation pushes the glow toward one edge or eliminates it. A well-cut stone with centered adularescence is worth more than the same material cut off-axis, even if the rough quality is identical.

Mohs hardness of 6–6.5 (see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale)) means the stone takes a good polish but is susceptible to scratching in wear, particularly in rings.

### Size

Moonstone prices per carat rise with size because larger crystals that preserve all premium qualities are uncommon. A 10-carat peach moonstone cabochon with strong adularescence costs significantly more per carat than a 2-carat stone of equivalent quality. Most commercial peach moonstone in the crystal market is under 10 carats.

### Origin

India produces most of the commercial peach-toned moonstone in the market. Sri Lanka and Myanmar produce finer-quality material but at lower volumes. Origin affects price primarily at the fine and collector end of the market.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

- **Buy from sellers who disclose country of origin and treatment status.** Moonstone is sometimes stabilized (resin impregnated) to improve durability of fragile specimens; untreated natural material is standard but disclosure matters.
- **Ask to see adularescence in person or in video.** A photo of a moonstone under fixed studio lighting cannot show whether the sheen moves — that movement is what you are paying for.
- **Compare prices across several sellers before buying fine-quality material.** Commercial peach moonstone is widely available; fine material with strong adularescence is not. If a price seems far below market for claimed quality, inspect the stone closely.
- **Check for imitations.** Glass and synthetic spinel can mimic the peach color. For identification tests, see [how to identify peach moonstone](/gemstones/peach-moonstone/how-to-identify).
