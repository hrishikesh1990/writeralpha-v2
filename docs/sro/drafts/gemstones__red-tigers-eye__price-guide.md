# Red Tiger's Eye Price Guide

Red Tiger's Eye is one of the more affordable stones in the crystal market. Tumbled stones retail at $1–4 per piece and bead bracelets at $8–16. The stone is abundant and widely traded, and most commercial material is heat-treated, which keeps prices low. Overpaying is less about rarity and more about buying from sellers who omit treatment disclosures or mislabel dyed material.

## Current Retail Price Ranges (September 2026)

The following ranges are drawn from North American retail sellers checked in September 2026. Prices vary by grade, size, origin, and seller.

| Form | Typical retail range | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Tumbled stone (small, 1/2"–3/4") | $1–2 | per piece |
| Tumbled stone (medium, 7/8"–1.25") | $2–4 | per piece |
| Tumbled stones by weight | ~$36 | per pound (medium size, India origin) |
| Bead bracelet (8mm beads) | $8–16 | per bracelet |
| Loose beads (wholesale, single pieces) | $1.75–4.50 | per bracelet unit |

These figures are for retail consumers; wholesale beads and bulk tumbled lots are considerably cheaper per unit.

## What Drives the Price of Red Tiger's Eye

### Chatoyancy quality

The intensity and sharpness of the moving light band (chatoyancy) is the primary quality factor. A stone with a strong, well-defined, centered eye that shifts cleanly under light commands a premium over one with a faint or diffuse effect. In beads and bracelets, the chatoyancy is often visible even in seller photos if you look for it.

### Color depth and uniformity

Deep red-brown with well-defined dark banding and contrast reads as higher quality than flat, uniformly dyed red with no tonal variation. Most commercially sold Red Tiger's Eye has been heat-treated to achieve the red color; that is standard practice and does not constitute fraud, but the quality of the heat treatment affects the outcome.

### Size

Larger tumbled stones and larger bead diameters (10mm, 12mm versus 6mm) cost more per piece, though not per gram. A 12mm bead bracelet retails at the higher end of the range compared with a 6mm bracelet.

### Origin disclosure

Red Tiger's Eye is produced primarily in South Africa, India, Australia, and Thailand. South African material is often considered the benchmark, though Indian-origin material dominates the affordable retail market. Origin is sometimes disclosed in seller listings, but its effect on retail price at the lower end of the market is minimal.

### Treatment disclosure

Most Red Tiger's Eye is heat-treated. Sellers who disclose this are being accurate; sellers who describe the red color as "natural" without qualification may be conflating naturally occurring red tiger's eye (which does exist geologically but is less common commercially) with heat-treated material. Neither is inferior as a stone, but transparency on treatment is a sign of a reliable seller.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Know typical prices.** At the retail ranges above, a tumbled Red Tiger's Eye stone selling for $15–25 as a single tumbled piece with no special size or quality justification is overpriced. The stone is abundant and widely traded.

**Check for chatoyancy in photos or in person.** A stone sold as Red Tiger's Eye that shows no chatoyancy is either very low grade or not tiger's eye at all. Chatoyancy is the defining optical property and the main quality driver.

**Ask about treatment.** A seller who confirms heat treatment is being honest about standard market practice. A seller who claims the red color is entirely natural in all pieces without qualification may not be accurately informed about their stock.

**Compare form-specific prices.** Bracelets cost more per gram of stone than tumbled pieces because of labor, elastic, and jewelry assembly. That premium is normal. What is not normal is paying bracelet prices for beads, or tumbled-stone prices for raw chunks with no visible quality.
