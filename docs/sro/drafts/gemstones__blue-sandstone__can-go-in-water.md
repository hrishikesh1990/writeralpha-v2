# Can Blue Sandstone Go in Water?

Blue Sandstone can go in water briefly without damage. It is a manufactured glass (aventurine glass), and glass does not dissolve, leach, or react with plain water in normal handling. A short rinse is safe for cleaning a polished tumbled stone or jewelry piece. Extended soaking is unnecessary and is not recommended, partly because setting metals in jewelry may be affected, and partly because salt water or acidic water can slowly etch glass surfaces.

## Why Blue Sandstone Is Water-Stable

Blue Sandstone is not a natural mineral. Correctly called blue goldstone or aventurine glass, it is a silica-based glass colored by cobalt ions and containing metallic copper platelets sealed within the glass matrix. See the [Blue Sandstone hub page](/gemstones/blue-sandstone) for full physical properties.

Glass does not dissolve in water at normal temperatures and pressures — this is one of its defining characteristics as a material. The copper inclusions that give Blue Sandstone its sparkle are completely enclosed inside the glass and do not contact water or leach into it. The stone is non-porous, so it does not absorb water the way porous minerals such as selenite or raw calcite do.

The Mohs hardness of approximately 5.5 is the relevant limitation. Water itself does not scratch glass, but abrasive particles suspended in water can, and salt water introduces a chemical that can slowly attack the glass surface — particularly along micro-scratches — over repeated or prolonged exposure.

## Water Situations: What Is Safe and What Is Not

| Situation | Safe for Blue Sandstone? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brief rinse (≤1 minute, plain water) | Yes | Standard physical cleaning method |
| Washing with mild soap and water | Yes | Rinse thoroughly; do not soak |
| Prolonged soaking (hours or overnight) | Not recommended | No evidence of acute damage, but unnecessary |
| Salt water bath | No | Salt can etch polished glass surfaces over time |
| Ocean water | No | Salt and sand abrasion both damaging |
| Hot water or steam | No | Thermal shock can crack glass |
| Ultrasonic cleaner | No | Vibration can cause micro-fractures |
| Wearing in the shower briefly | Generally safe | Avoid soap residue buildup; rinse and dry after |
| Swimming (pool or sea) | Not recommended | Chlorine and salt both affect polished glass with repeated exposure |

## How to Rinse Blue Sandstone Safely

1. Hold the piece under cool or lukewarm running water for 30 seconds or less.
2. If needed, use a soft cloth or cotton bud dampened with plain water to remove residue.
3. Pat dry immediately with a lint-free cloth.
4. Leave to air-dry fully in a safe, stable spot before storing or wearing.

Never use abrasive scrubbing pads, toothpaste, or household chemical cleaners on Blue Sandstone. All of these can scratch or dull a polished glass surface.

## Is Blue Sandstone Toxic in Water?

No. Polished Blue Sandstone does not release harmful substances into water in normal handling. The cobalt that colors the glass and the copper inside the matrix are both sealed within the glass. For the same reason, Blue Sandstone is not used to make gem waters or crystal-infused water — not because it is toxic, but because the practice is a traditional one and the stone's benefits are ascribed by tradition, not chemistry.
