# How to Identify Real Blue Sandstone

"Real" Blue Sandstone is genuine aventurine glass (blue goldstone): a manufactured silica-based glass containing metallic copper inclusions suspended in a cobalt-colored matrix. Imitations include plastic or acrylic copies, resin with glitter inclusions, plain dyed glass without metallic sparkle, and surface-coated glass. A combination of visual inspection, the temperature test, and loupe magnification identifies genuine material reliably without specialist equipment.

## Understanding What "Real" Means for Blue Sandstone

Blue Sandstone is not a natural mineral. The correct technical name is blue goldstone or aventurine glass. It is a manufactured product — a fact that must be disclosed under trade standards of the GIA and major gem-trade bodies. Any seller describing it as a natural crystal or natural stone without qualification is misrepresenting the material.

Because it is manufactured, the identification question is not "natural vs. synthetic" (it is always synthetic) but "genuine aventurine glass vs. inferior imitation." Genuine blue goldstone has consistent, visually striking metallic sparkle from real copper platelet inclusions sealed inside the glass. Inferior imitations substitute cheaper materials that do not reproduce this effect accurately.

## Step-by-Step Identification

### Step 1: Visual inspection of sparkle

Genuine blue goldstone produces aventurescence — a bright, directional metallic flash that moves across the stone as you tilt it. The sparkle is deep within the material, not on the surface. As you rotate the piece in directional light (a single lamp or sunlight, not diffuse room light), the glitter shifts angle and depth.

Imitation red flags:
- Flat, surface-only shimmer: suggests a painted or coated surface, not embedded inclusions
- Iridescent rainbow effect: suggests a thin-film coating, not copper inclusions
- Static or non-directional sparkle: suggests glitter in resin, not oriented copper platelets
- Perfectly uniform, too-regular sparkle: resin with glitter has a different, more uniform distribution than genuine goldstone

### Step 2: Temperature test

Glass feels distinctly cool against the skin and warms slowly. Pick up the piece and hold it briefly.

- Genuine blue goldstone (glass): feels cool, takes 5–10 seconds to reach skin temperature
- Plastic or acrylic: warms to skin temperature almost immediately, feels slightly lighter

This test is reliable and requires no equipment.

### Step 3: Weight and density

Genuine blue goldstone has a specific gravity of approximately 2.5 to 2.8 — similar to quartz. A comparable piece made of plastic or acrylic will feel noticeably lighter for its size. This test is most useful when comparing a suspect piece side-by-side with a known genuine piece of similar dimensions.

### Step 4: Loupe magnification (10x or 20x)

Under a jeweler's loupe at 10x to 20x, genuine blue goldstone shows:
- Triangular or hexagonal metallic copper platelets scattered at various depths and orientations within the glass
- Occasional small gas bubbles (a hallmark of glass manufacture)
- Copper inclusions that appear warm gold-toned or slightly silvery, filtered by the blue glass

Imitations show:
- Round or irregular glitter particles (resin with cosmetic glitter)
- No depth variation — all sparkle at the same surface plane
- Absence of gas bubbles in plastic/resin

### Step 5: Hardness check (optional, last resort)

Genuine blue goldstone (Mohs ~5.5) will scratch easily with a steel file or knife blade (Mohs 5.5–6.5). Plastic and acrylic are softer (Mohs ~2–3) and will scratch with a fingernail. Only perform a scratch test in an inconspicuous area of an unset piece, and only when the other tests have not resolved the question.

## Key Properties of Genuine Blue Sandstone

| Property | Genuine Blue Goldstone |
|---|---|
| Material type | Man-made glass (aventurine glass) |
| Mohs hardness | ~5.5 |
| Specific gravity | 2.50–2.80 |
| Refractive index | ~1.53 (isotropic; singly refractive) |
| Inclusions | Metallic copper platelets |
| Blue color source | Cobalt ions in glass matrix |
| Fracture | Conchoidal |
| Temperature | Cool to touch |

## Buying Guidance

Reputable sellers disclose that Blue Sandstone is a man-made glass. If a listing describes it as a natural crystal or natural mineral without qualification, that is a misrepresentation. Low price alone does not confirm or deny quality — Blue Sandstone is an affordable manufactured material, and genuine pieces are widely available at modest prices.
