# Can Crazy Lace Agate Go in Water?

Yes — Crazy Lace Agate is safe in water. It is silicon dioxide (SiO2) at Mohs hardness 7, chemically stable and non-soluble. A brief rinse or wash is perfectly fine. Prolonged soaking in salt water is not recommended because salt can dull the polished surface and crystallize inside surface fractures, not because the mineral itself dissolves.

## Why Crazy Lace Agate Is Water-Safe

Crazy Lace Agate is a variety of chalcedony — microcrystalline quartz. Quartz-family minerals are among the most chemically inert in common use. Silicon dioxide does not react with water at room temperature and does not dissolve under normal household conditions. Crazy Lace Agate's hardness of 7 on the Mohs scale means it is harder than glass and resists surface abrasion from typical cleaning.

Minerals that are unsafe in water tend to be soft (selenite at Mohs 2 dissolves readily), chemically reactive (pyrite oxidizes and forms sulfuric acid), or porous in ways that allow water to cause internal damage. Crazy Lace Agate has none of these properties. It has no cleavage — fractures in the material are conchoidal rather than planar — and its dense chalcedony structure limits water penetration.

For a broader reference on which crystals can safely contact water, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## Fresh Water: Safe

Running a piece of Crazy Lace Agate under lukewarm tap water is safe. Use your fingers or a soft brush to remove surface dust or oils. Pat dry with a soft cloth and let the stone air-dry completely before storage. Hot water used repeatedly can create minor thermal stress over a long period, but a normal lukewarm rinse carries no risk.

Mild liquid soap is acceptable for physical cleaning. Rinse thoroughly and dry promptly.

## Salt Water: Use Caution

Crazy Lace Agate itself is not destroyed by salt water, but regular exposure is not recommended for two reasons:

1. **Polish dulling.** Salt is mildly abrasive and can gradually dull a waxy or vitreous polish with repeated contact.
2. **Fracture crystallization.** Salt dissolved in water can travel into hairline surface cracks. As the stone dries, salt crystalizes inside those cracks and can widen them over time, particularly in pieces that have minor existing fractures.

A brief accidental dip — rinsing with fresh water immediately afterward and drying fully — is unlikely to cause visible damage. Routine salt-water soaks as a cleansing ritual carry cumulative risk to the finish and structural integrity of pieces with surface fractures. For a guide to which stones are salt-tolerant, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## Cleaning Crazy Lace Agate Without Water

Water is the most convenient method, but other options work equally well for ritual or practical cleansing. Smoke, sound, moonlight, and brief sunlight exposure are all physically safe for Crazy Lace Agate. The stone is not photosensitive: its iron-oxide color bands (reds, oranges, browns) are thermally produced and stable in normal sunlight.

For sun safety details, see [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals). For the full range of cleansing approaches, see [how to cleanse Crazy Lace Agate](/gemstones/crazy-lace-agate/how-to-cleanse).

## Physical Cleaning vs Ritual Cleansing

A distinction worth making: physical cleaning removes dust, skin oils, and residue. Ritual cleansing is a traditional practice intended to clear accumulated energy. Water handles both at once, but if the goal is only physical cleaning, a dry soft cloth or brush is sufficient for lightly soiled pieces.
