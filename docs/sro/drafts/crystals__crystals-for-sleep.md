# Crystals for Sleep: Which Stones Practitioners Use

Crystals are used as part of sleep routines in two main ways: placed in the bedroom to shape the environment, and placed under the pillow or beside the bed for closer proximity during sleep. The stones most commonly chosen are [amethyst](/gemstones/amethyst), [selenite](/gemstones/selenite), [lepidolite](/gemstones/lepidolite), [howlite](/gemstones/howlite), [rose quartz](/gemstones/rose-quartz), and [black tourmaline](/gemstones/black-tourmaline). Crystals are a complementary practice; they do not treat insomnia or sleep disorders.

## What Practitioners Look For in a Sleep Crystal

Evaluation in crystal tradition centers on two questions: does the stone's energy quality read as calming rather than stimulating, and is it physically safe to have in a sleep space?

**Calming vs. stimulating:** Practitioners generally classify purple, pale blue, white, and gray stones as calming. Orange, bright yellow, and red stones are considered energizing and are typically moved out of the bedroom.

**Physical safety in the sleep space:** This matters more than most guides acknowledge. Any stone placed under a pillow must survive compression without shattering; softer stones and layered minerals can crack and leave debris. Stones placed on nightstands or in the room do not carry this risk.

## Amethyst

Amethyst is purple quartz (SiO2), colored by Fe3+ ions and natural irradiation, Mohs 7. At that hardness it can be safely placed under a pillow without breaking under normal adult body weight. Color may fade with prolonged direct sunlight, so keep it out of sun-facing windowsills.

In crystal tradition, amethyst is the primary sleep stone. Practitioners associate it with quieting an overactive mind, easing restlessness, and supporting dream recall. It is also one of the most affordable and widely available stones. A small tumbled piece under a pillow or on a nightstand is the most common application.

## Selenite

Selenite is a variety of gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O), Mohs 2. That hardness means a fingernail can scratch it. **Do not put selenite under a pillow:** pressure can crack or powder the surface, and the soft gypsum creates debris. Selenite also dissolves in water — see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water) before cleaning it.

Practitioners place selenite wands or plates on a nightstand, windowsill, or shelf beside the bed. In tradition it is associated with clarity, energetic cleansing of a space, and a quiet, still quality suited to sleep environments. Because selenite does not need cleansing in the same way harder stones do (by some accounts, it self-clears), it is popular for those who want a low-maintenance stone.

## Lepidolite

Lepidolite is a lithium-bearing mica, K(Li,Al)3(Al,Si)4O10(F,OH)2, Mohs 2.5–3. Like selenite, its softness makes it unsuitable for under-pillow placement — it can flake and chip. Use it on a nightstand or in a small dish beside the bed.

Tradition associates lepidolite with emotional balance and transition, making it a specific recommendation for people whose sleep difficulty stems from anxiety or racing thoughts. The mineral contains lithium in its crystal structure (not a bioavailable pharmaceutical source), and practitioners sometimes cite this when recommending it for mood-adjacent uses — that connection is symbolic, not physiological.

## Howlite

Howlite is a calcium borosilicate hydroxide, Mohs 3.5. It is chalky white with gray veining and is frequently dyed blue or [turquoise](/gemstones/turquoise) to simulate turquoise or blue howlite — if you want natural howlite, buy from a source that specifies undyed. At Mohs 3.5 it is softer than most quartzes; use it on a nightstand rather than under a pillow for the same safety reason as lepidolite.

Practitioners associate howlite with slowing down racing thoughts and promoting patience. It is one of the stones specifically recommended for people who lie awake replaying the day.

## Rose Quartz

Rose quartz (SiO2, Mohs 7) is durable enough for under-pillow use. Its pink color comes from microscopic inclusions or lattice-level defects depending on the specimen. Some specimens fade with extended sun exposure — [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals) covers which stones to keep away from light.

In tradition, rose quartz is associated with emotional comfort and nurturing. Practitioners recommend it for people whose sleep difficulty is tied to emotional stress, grief, or relationship worry.

## Black Tourmaline

Black tourmaline (schorl) is a complex boron silicate, Mohs 7–7.5, with no significant cleavage. It is one of the more durable stones in this group and withstands under-pillow or under-mattress placement.

Tradition frames black tourmaline as a protective boundary stone — practitioners place it at corners of the room or under the bed to create what they describe as a shielded sleep space. It is the standard recommendation for people whose sleep is disrupted by a sense of unease or worry about external threats.

## [Moonstone](/gemstones/moonstone)

Moonstone is a variety of orthoclase or [labradorite](/gemstones/labradorite) feldspar, Mohs 6–6.5, with two directions of cleavage. The adularescence (floating glow) comes from thin alternating layers of feldspar that scatter light. **Practical note:** feldspar cleaves cleanly if struck at the right angle — avoid placing moonstone where it could be knocked off a hard surface.

In tradition moonstone is deeply connected to sleep, dreams, and cycles. Practitioners associate it with intuitive dreaming and emotional processing during sleep, and it often appears in sleep-focused crystal grids placed around the bed rather than under the pillow.

## Placing Crystals in the Sleep Space

| Placement | Best for | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Under pillow | Hard stones: amethyst, rose quartz, black tourmaline (Mohs ≥ 6) | Soft stones: selenite, lepidolite, howlite |
| Nightstand | Any stone | Nothing — this is the safest spot |
| Bed corners or under bed | Larger pieces: black tourmaline, [smoky quartz](/gemstones/smoky-quartz) | Brittle or flaky specimens |
| Window / shelf | Decorative clusters, moonstone | Amethyst, rose quartz (fade risk from sun) |

## Building a Sleep Routine with Crystals

A consistent ritual matters more than the specific stone chosen. Many practitioners use crystals as a physical anchor for a winding-down sequence:

1. Set the stones in their chosen position before dimming the lights.
2. Spend two to five minutes with slow breathing, holding a palm stone if preferred.
3. If using crystals in [crystal meditation](/learn/crystal-meditation), keep the session shorter and slower at night than in a daytime practice.
4. Leave the stones in place overnight.

Clean the stones periodically. Selenite and lepidolite are water-sensitive; see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals) for water-free methods such as sound cleansing or moonlight.
