# Obsidian Price Guide — What It Costs and Why

Common black obsidian is one of the least expensive stones in the crystal market. A typical tumbled stone sells for $1 to $10 per piece. Special varieties — rainbow, fire, gold sheen, silver sheen — cost more, sometimes significantly, because the visual effects depend on rare formation conditions and skilled cutting. Prices checked September 2026.

## Price Ranges by Variety

| Variety | Typical retail price (per tumbled stone or piece) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Black obsidian (tumbled) | $1–$10 | Most common; price rises with size and polish quality |
| Mahogany obsidian | $3–$15 | Reddish-brown streaks from hematite and limonite inclusions |
| Snowflake obsidian | $3–$15 | White cristobalite inclusions; clean pattern = higher price |
| Silver sheen obsidian | $5–$25 | Microscopic bubble layers; alignment to cutter matters |
| Gold sheen obsidian | $5–$30 | Same mechanism as silver sheen; golden colour commands a premium |
| Rainbow obsidian | $10–$60+ | Iridescent layered effect; vibrancy and pattern width affect price sharply |
| Fire obsidian | $20–$100+ | Rarest variety; vivid structural colour; premium for strong colour play |

*All prices are per individual tumbled stone, cabochon, or small polished piece of typical retail size (approximately 2–5 cm). Larger carvings, spheres, or sculpted pieces command multiples of these figures. Prices vary by grade and seller.*

## What Drives the Price

**Variety and optical effect:** Plain black obsidian forms abundantly across volcanic regions worldwide and requires no special cutting to show its character. Rainbow and fire obsidian only reveal their colour play when cut and polished at the exact right angle to the internal layers — a skilled lapidary decision that affects yield and therefore cost. Fire obsidian is geologically rare; major deposits are limited.

**Size:** Price scales roughly with size and weight. A palm-sized black obsidian sphere will cost considerably more than a small tumbled stone of the same material.

**Polish quality:** The reflective finish of obsidian is a selling point; a well-polished mirror surface commands a premium over a rough or matte-finished piece. Any surface scratches or inclusions that interrupt the polish reduce value.

**Form:** Tumbled stones are the cheapest form. Carved figures, skulls, spheres, obelisks, and arrowheads are cut to shape and polished individually, which adds labour cost.

**Origin:** Obsidian is found worldwide — Mexico, Iceland, the United States (Oregon, California, Wyoming), Turkey, Ethiopia, Japan — and origin is rarely a primary price driver for common black obsidian. However, certain regionally distinctive varieties (such as Oregon rainbow obsidian or Mexican fire obsidian) are sometimes sold with provenance information that can affect price.

**Mohs hardness and durability:** At 5–5.5 on the Mohs scale (see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale)), obsidian is a relatively soft glass. It can scratch if stored with harder minerals. Durability affects how well a polished piece holds its finish over time.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Know the variety:** If you are buying black obsidian, the market price is low. Any seller charging $30 or more for a small black tumbled stone without documented reason (exceptional size, artist carving, certified origin) is worth questioning.

**Check for imitations:** Industrial slag glass, man-made glass, and resin are all sold as obsidian. See [how to identify obsidian](/gemstones/obsidian/how-to-identify) for tests you can do at home. Genuine obsidian is affordable enough that sellers of real material have no need to inflate prices dramatically for common varieties.

**Buy from reputable sellers:** For sourcing guidance, see [ethical crystal sourcing](/learn/ethical-crystal-sourcing). Request information on origin and any treatments applied. Obsidian is not typically treated (it requires no heat, irradiation, or coating to achieve its natural appearance), so a seller claiming treatment benefits is a red flag.

**Sheen varieties:** For rainbow, gold sheen, and silver sheen obsidian, ask to see the piece at different angles or request multiple photos. The sheen only appears when light hits the internal layers correctly — a piece that shows minimal sheen in a straight-on photo may still be genuine, but strong consistent sheen in multiple angles justifies a higher price. The flip side: a piece marketed as "rainbow obsidian" with no visible iridescence at any angle may be black glass.
