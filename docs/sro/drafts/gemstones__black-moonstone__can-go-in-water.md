# Can Black Moonstone Go in Water?

Black Moonstone can go in water for brief rinsing. The mineral — a feldspar — is not water-soluble, and short contact does not damage the stone. Prolonged soaking is not recommended because water can penetrate micro-cracks along feldspar cleavage planes and weaken them over time. Salt water, steam, and ultrasonic cleaning are the three methods to avoid.

## Why Black Moonstone Is Broadly Water-Safe

Black Moonstone is a silicate mineral belonging to the feldspar group, with Mohs hardness between 6 and 6.5 and a density around 2.55 to 2.63 g/cm3. Feldspars are insoluble in water under normal conditions — the mineral does not dissolve, leach, or react with plain water at room temperature.

This puts Black Moonstone in a different category from stones that genuinely should not get wet, such as pyrite (which oxidizes), selenite (which dissolves), or halite (table salt). A brief wash does not harm the mineral chemistry of Black Moonstone.

For a general reference on which crystals can and cannot tolerate water, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## What Limits the Water Contact

While the mineral itself is water-stable, two physical factors justify keeping contact brief:

**Cleavage planes.** Feldspar cleaves in two directions at approximately right angles. Polished specimens often have micro-cracks or inclusions along these planes, especially tumbled pieces that have been through abrasive processing. When water enters a crack and dries, repeated wetting and drying can slowly widen the fracture. For a smooth, uncracked specimen this is a minor concern. For a piece that already shows inclusions or fine tension cracks, prolonged soaking accelerates any existing damage.

**Jewelry settings.** Metal settings — particularly silver and base metal — can corrode slowly in water. Glued settings can loosen if water penetrates behind the stone. Pieces set with adhesive rather than pressure-set should not be soaked. For jewelry cleaning, a quick wipe with a damp cloth is safer than immersion.

## Safe Water Practices

For a loose tumbled stone or cabochon: rinse briefly under lukewarm running water, use a drop of mild soap if needed, scrub gently with a soft cloth or soft toothbrush, rinse clean, and pat dry immediately. Do not leave in a bowl of water to soak.

For a pendant or bracelet: wipe the stone with a damp soft cloth. Avoid getting water behind the setting or in crevices where it will sit. Dry thoroughly.

For a ring: brief rinsing is acceptable, but frequent daily washing while wearing the ring will accelerate edge wear on a 6 to 6.5 Mohs stone. Feldspar edges chip more readily than quartz.

## Salt Water and Salt Soaks

Salt water is not recommended for Black Moonstone, primarily because of its effects on settings and surface integrity rather than on the mineral itself. Dissolved salt leaves deposits in micro-cracks and accelerates corrosion of metal components. Prolonged dry salt burial on a polished stone also carries some risk, because salt is hygroscopic — it draws moisture from the air — and can concentrate it at the stone's surface. See [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals) for broader guidance on which stones tolerate salt cleansing.

## Sun Drying After Washing

After rinsing Black Moonstone, pat it dry and let it air dry in a shaded spot rather than setting it in direct sun to speed drying. Brief sunlight exposure does not harm feldspar mineralogically, but direct, prolonged sun is not necessary and some practitioners prefer to keep lunar-associated stones out of strong direct sunlight as a matter of tradition. For general guidance on sun exposure, see [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals).

## Cleaning vs. Energetic Cleansing

Physical cleaning with water removes surface dirt from Black Moonstone.
