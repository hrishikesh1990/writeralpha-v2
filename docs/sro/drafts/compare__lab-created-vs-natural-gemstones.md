# Lab-Created vs Natural Gemstones: Key Differences

Lab-created gemstones have the same chemical composition, crystal structure, and physical properties as their natural counterparts. The difference is origin: natural gemstones form in the earth over millions of years; lab-created stones are grown in controlled facilities over days to weeks. Both are real gems — but they differ in price, detectability, rarity, and what drives their value.

## What "Lab-Created" Actually Means

"Lab-created," "synthetic," and "lab-grown" mean the same thing under U.S. Federal Trade Commission guidelines: a stone that has essentially the same chemical composition and physical structure as a natural gem but was produced by humans. These terms are legally required to accompany the gem name (e.g., "lab-created [sapphire](/gemstones/sapphire)," not simply "sapphire") when selling.

"Simulated" or "imitation" stones are different: a cubic zirconia simulates a [diamond](/gemstones/diamond) but is chemically and physically distinct from it. Lab-created gems are not imitations — they are the same mineral, grown in a different place.

## Same Chemistry, Different Origin

| Property | Natural | Lab-Created |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical composition | Identical to natural | Same as natural |
| Crystal structure | Identical to natural | Same as natural |
| Mohs hardness | Identical | Same |
| Refractive index | Identical | Same |
| Color range | Wide natural variation | Controlled; often more consistent |
| Inclusions | Natural mineral inclusions | May have growth-related inclusions (flux, gas bubbles, curved growth lines) |
| Formation time | Millions of years | Days to weeks |
| Detectability | Requires lab for some | Professional equipment required |

Because lab-created gems have the same [Mohs hardness](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale), refractive index, and density as natural ones, visual or simple physical tests cannot tell them apart. The [citrine vs yellow topaz comparison](/compare/citrine-vs-yellow-topaz) is a good example of how even natural gem pairs with similar colors require lab equipment to distinguish. Professional identification requires spectroscopy, inclusion analysis, or photoluminescence testing in a gemological laboratory.

## How Different Gems Are Grown

The main production methods vary by gem type:

**Corundum ([ruby](/gemstones/ruby), sapphire)**: The Verneuil (flame fusion) method has been used since 1902 and is the oldest and cheapest process. Flame fusion stones often show curved growth lines under magnification. Flux and hydrothermal growth produce higher-quality material with fewer visual tells but at higher cost.

**[Emerald](/gemstones/emerald)**: Flux and hydrothermal methods produce lab emeralds. Hydrothermal lab emeralds may show jagged "two-phase" inclusions rather than the three-phase inclusions typical of Colombian natural emerald.

**Diamond**: Chemical vapor deposition (CVD) and high-pressure high-temperature (HPHT) methods. CVD diamonds have largely driven the price of lab-grown diamonds down significantly since 2020.

**Quartz ([amethyst](/gemstones/amethyst), [citrine](/gemstones/citrine), [rose quartz](/gemstones/rose-quartz))**: Hydrothermal growth. Lab quartz has been commercially available for decades and is chemically indistinguishable from natural without spectroscopy.

## Price Difference

Lab-created gemstones are substantially less expensive than comparable natural stones:

- Lab-created sapphires typically retail for 60–90% less per carat than matched natural sapphires of equivalent color and clarity.
- Lab-created emeralds are similarly discounted compared to Colombian or Zambian naturals of comparable grade.
- Lab-grown diamonds have seen the largest price compression — a 1-carat lab diamond that sold for $4,000–5,000 in 2016 was available for under $1,000 in some markets by 2024, though prices fluctuate with production volumes.

Natural gems carry a scarcity premium that lab-created material does not. Whether that premium is worth paying depends on the buyer's priorities: rarity and investment value favor natural; budget and environmental considerations often favor lab-created.

## Detectability: Can You Tell Them Apart?

Without laboratory equipment, you generally cannot distinguish a high-quality lab-created stone from a natural one of the same gem type. Experienced gemologists can identify some lab-grown material from certain tells:

- Curved growth lines (Verneuil flame-fusion ruby and sapphire, visible under microscopy)
- Gas bubbles or chevron patterns (some flux-grown material)
- Absence of natural inclusions that are expected in natural material from known localities
- Spectroscopic signatures specific to synthetic growth methods

For most consumers buying jewelry, only a GIA or equivalent laboratory report can confirm natural vs. lab-created origin.

## Ethical and Environmental Considerations

The ethical case for lab-created gems rests on reduced mining impact: no open-pit excavation, no disruption of ecosystems in mining regions, and reduced labor concerns. However, lab-created gems require significant energy, and some production methods use energy-intensive processes in facilities powered by fossil fuels.

The ethical case for natural gems includes fair-trade certified mining operations that support local economies in gem-producing regions (e.g., Columbia, Zambia, Sri Lanka, Myanmar), though verification of ethical sourcing remains difficult without certification.

Neither option is straightforwardly superior on environmental or ethical grounds without knowing specifics of production and supply chain. Buyers who prioritize ethical sourcing should look for certification programs (Fairmined, Fairtrade Gold for metals; GIA reports for origin traceability on natural stones) and ask suppliers directly.

## Metaphysical Traditions and Lab-Created Gems

A common question in the crystal community is whether lab-created gems "work the same" as natural ones for metaphysical purposes. Practitioners are divided: some hold that lab-created gems carry the same energetic properties as natural ones because the chemistry is identical; others favor natural formation as part of what gives a stone meaning. Both positions are cultural traditions. No scientific study has demonstrated measurable energetic or healing effects from gemstones of either origin.

## Which to Choose

Choose **natural** if: long-term value retention, rarity, or investment potential matters; the stone is for a major heirloom piece; or origin story is part of its significance to you.

Choose **lab-created** if: budget is the primary factor; you want a larger or higher-clarity stone for the same spend; or you prioritize reducing mining impact.

For either option, buy from vendors who provide a certificate from an independent gemological laboratory (GIA, AGL, Gübelin) and clearly disclose whether the stone is natural or lab-created.
