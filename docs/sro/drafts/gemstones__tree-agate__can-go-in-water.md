# Can Tree Agate Go in Water?

Yes. Tree Agate is quartz-based — a variety of chalcedony with a Mohs hardness of 7 and a silicon dioxide (SiO₂) composition — and it does not dissolve or corrode in contact with water. Brief rinsing and normal handling around water are fine for a natural, untreated piece. Prolonged soaking, salt water, and soaking of dyed or set pieces carry specific risks and are not recommended.

## Why Tree Agate Is Generally Water-Safe

Tree Agate's mineral backbone is microcrystalline quartz — the same fundamental material as agate, jasper, and chalcedony. Silicon dioxide does not react chemically with water at normal temperatures. The green dendritic inclusions (typically manganese or iron oxide minerals, or chlorite-group silicates) are also physically stable under brief water contact.

At Mohs 7, Tree Agate resists surface abrasion well. A hardness above 5.5 is the broad threshold above which a stone is generally considered durable enough for water contact without structural risk, and Tree Agate sits well above that threshold.

For a general guide to which stones can go in water and why hardness alone is not always sufficient, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## When Water Becomes a Problem

### Prolonged soaking

Tree Agate does not need to soak in water for any physical purpose. Prolonged soaking is unnecessary for cleaning and adds risk to settings, strung cord, any adhesive used in assembled pieces, and dyed surface treatments. A rinse of 20 to 30 seconds achieves all physical cleaning benefits.

### Salt water

Salt water is not recommended for Tree Agate, particularly set or polished pieces. Salt is mildly abrasive and can dull a polished chalcedony surface over time. As water evaporates, salt crystals can deposit inside micro-fractures. Metal settings corrode in salt water. Plain fresh water is sufficient for cleaning and carries none of these risks. For a broader guide to which stones tolerate salt, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

### Dyed pieces

Some Tree Agate sold commercially has been dyed to intensify the green inclusions or whiten the host chalcedony. Dyed pieces are more sensitive to prolonged soaking and should be cleaned with a damp cloth rather than immersed. Signs of dye: unusually vivid, uniform green color; color pooled in fractures or drill holes; green that does not follow natural dendritic branching patterns.

### Jewelry settings and strung beads

The stone tolerates water; the setting and cord may not. Metal settings can corrode or discolor in repeated water exposure. Strung bead cords absorb water and weaken over time, especially with prolonged soaking. Clean set pieces with a damp cloth rather than immersion.

## How to Clean Tree Agate with Water

Hold the piece under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 to 30 seconds. Use a soft cloth or soft-bristle brush to remove surface debris if needed. Dry immediately and thoroughly with a clean cloth. Do not leave Tree Agate resting in standing water.

For all cleansing methods — including non-water options such as smoke, sound, and moonlight — see [how to cleanse Tree Agate](/gemstones/tree-agate/how-to-cleanse).

## Sunlight After Water Cleansing

Tree Agate is generally tolerant of brief sun exposure. An opaque chalcedony with green mineral inclusions does not fade rapidly in moderate sunlight. However, prolonged intense sun on a dyed piece may affect the color treatment over time. For a brief outdoor air-dry in diffuse light, there is no meaningful risk.
