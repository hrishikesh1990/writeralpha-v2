# Tree Agate Price Guide: What It Costs and Why

Tree Agate is an affordable gemstone. Tumbled stones retail at approximately $2–8 per piece; polished specimens and small display pieces at $10–30; larger collector-grade display pieces at $20–80. Exceptional pieces with very clear, densely branched inclusions against a crisp white host occasionally reach higher prices, but Tree Agate is not a premium market stone. Price is driven primarily by pattern quality, form (tumbled versus shaped), and whether the piece is natural or treated.

## Typical Retail Prices (September 2026)

| Form | Typical retail range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tumbled stone (small) | $2–8 per piece | Most common entry form; widely available |
| Tumbled stone (large) | $5–15 per piece | Larger pieces with good pattern coverage |
| Polished freeform / palm stone | $10–30 | Polish quality and pattern clarity drive the upper end |
| Cabochon (for jewelry) | $3–20 per cab | Size and pattern quality vary widely |
| Sphere or egg | $15–50 | Larger display forms |
| Collector display piece | $20–80 | Large, high-contrast, well-patterned specimens |
| Raw / rough | $1–5 per piece | Lower visual impact; used in lapidary |

These ranges reflect single-piece retail prices. Bulk and wholesale pricing is lower. Prices vary by seller geography, platform (dedicated gem retailers versus general marketplaces), and individual piece quality.

## What Drives the Price of Tree Agate

**Pattern quality** is the primary price driver. Pieces with dense, well-defined dendritic branching against a clear white or cream ground command higher prices than those with faint, sparse, or blurry inclusions. The clarity of the contrast between the green inclusions and the white host body matters more than the intensity of the green alone.

**Inclusion depth and three-dimensionality.** Inclusions that appear to float at different depths within the stone are more visually compelling — and more expensive — than surface-only patterns. A loupe or magnifying glass reveals depth quickly.

**Form and finish.** Shaped, polished pieces (spheres, palms, carvings) cost more than tumbled material of similar quality due to the labor of cutting and polishing. The stone's hardness of 7 makes it workable but requires proper equipment; see [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale) for context on what hardness 7 means for durability.

**Origin.** Tree Agate is commercially sourced primarily from India (Gujarat and Maharashtra), Brazil, Uruguay, and the United States. Indian material dominates the mass market and the lower price ranges. Brazilian and American material tends toward higher prices partly due to higher processing costs.

**Treatment.** Natural, untreated Tree Agate commands a small premium over dyed material when both are correctly disclosed. Dyed pieces are not inherently inferior for display, but they should be priced and labeled as treated. A dyed piece sold at natural prices is a mislabeling issue, not a quality issue. For how to detect dyed material, see [how to identify Tree Agate](/gemstones/tree-agate/how-to-identify).

## Red Flags and Overpaying

**Prices significantly above the ranges above** for common tumbled material (for example, $25–50 for a single small tumbled stone) are not justified by Tree Agate's market position. Very high prices on common forms sometimes indicate mislabeling as a rarer stone, or a retail context (spa, boutique, tourist market) where margins are high.

**Unusually low prices** are worth scrutiny if the piece is sold as "rare" or a premium variety. Tree Agate is not a rare stone; if a seller is combining rarity claims with low prices, inspect the piece carefully for glass, resin, or surface coating.

**No disclosure on treatment.** A seller who cannot confirm whether a piece is dyed or natural when asked directly is a sourcing concern.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

Buy from sellers who describe the origin, form, and treatment status of each piece. Compare prices across multiple platforms for the same form and quality level before purchasing. A $5 tumbled stone from a reputable gem retailer is generally equivalent in mineral quality to a $5 tumbled stone from an online marketplace, assuming both are correctly labeled as natural.
