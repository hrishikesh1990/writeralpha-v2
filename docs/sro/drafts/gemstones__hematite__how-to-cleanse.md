# How to Cleanse Hematite Without Damaging It

Hematite should not be placed in water. It is an iron oxide mineral (Fe₂O₃) and water contact can degrade polished surfaces and promote corrosion in specimens that contain unoxidized iron components. Safe cleansing methods include smoke, sound, moonlight on a dry surface, dry earth burial, and breath or visualization.

## Why Water Should Be Avoided

Hematite is iron oxide — its iron content is approximately 70% by weight. While pure Fe₂O₃ is already fully oxidized, real-world specimens often contain other iron-bearing minerals such as goethite or magnetite, and prolonged water contact can degrade polished surfaces and promote corrosion in those components. The site's dedicated page on [can hematite go in water](/gemstones/hematite/can-go-in-water) explains this in full and confirms that water can cause rusting and surface damage.

Salt water is more aggressive than plain water. Wet salt on a specimen's surface acts as an ongoing source of corrosion. Dry salt burial is also not recommended: salt is hygroscopic and draws ambient moisture toward the stone.

For a broader guide to which stones are water-safe and why, see [how to cleanse crystals](/learn/how-to-cleanse-crystals).

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Hold hematite in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, moving the piece slowly so all surfaces are exposed. This is the most widely used cleansing method for hematite and carries no physical risk to the specimen. Ventilate the room well during and after.

### Sound

Place hematite near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. The stone never needs to touch anything liquid, making this method ideal for polished specimens or heavy tumbled pieces that are awkward to pass through smoke. Sound cleansing is also well suited to large display specimens.

### Moonlight

Set hematite on a dry windowsill or outdoor surface on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast first: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms. In crystal tradition moonlight is used to both cleanse and reset a stone's energy in a single step.

### Dry earth burial

Place hematite in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or sand for 24 hours. Bury it shallowly, cover it, and retrieve it the following day. Brush off any loose soil with a dry cloth or soft brush. Use indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp and introduce moisture to the specimen.

### Breath and visualization

Hold hematite in both hands, breathe in slowly, then exhale steadily across the stone while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and is useful for a quick reset between longer cleansing sessions.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps in most crystal traditions. After cleansing hematite, place it in brief morning sunlight for 15 to 30 minutes — short exposure poses no physical risk to this opaque, metallic mineral — near a clear quartz cluster, or on a dry selenite slab. For a full guide to recharging methods, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

Hematite is traditionally used in [grounding](/crystals/crystals-for-grounding) and [protection](/crystals/crystals-for-protection) practice. Its full physical profile — hardness, chemistry, cleavage, and varieties — is on the [hematite hub page](/gemstones/hematite).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary on frequency. A common guideline is to cleanse on arrival, after any period of intense use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice. As a maintenance note, inspect hematite periodically for dull patches or discoloration on polished surfaces, which may indicate prior moisture exposure.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store hematite in a cool, dry location away from bathrooms, kitchen windowsills, or any area prone to steam or condensation. A small silica gel packet inside a display case helps control ambient humidity without touching the stone. Do not store hematite alongside hygroscopic materials such as salt crystal lamps.

Wash hands after handling hematite, particularly after touching freshly broken or rough material. Avoid inhaling any dust produced by cutting or grinding the stone.
