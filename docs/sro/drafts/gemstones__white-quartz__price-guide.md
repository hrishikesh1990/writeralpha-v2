# White Quartz Price Guide: What It Costs and Why

White Quartz is one of the most affordable and widely available crystals on the market. Retail prices for tumbled stones ranged from approximately $8 to $11 per pound at multiple US and Canadian retailers as of September 2026, putting individual tumbled pieces at roughly $0.50 to $1 at wholesale and $1 to $3 at retail. Quality, size, form factor, and retailer type are the main price drivers.

## Typical Retail Price Ranges (September 2026)

The prices below come from retail and wholesale listings observed at multiple online crystal retailers. All prices are in USD unless noted.

| Form | Typical retail price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tumbled stones, small to medium | $8–$11 per pound (30–40 pcs) | Bulk bags; US and Canadian retailers |
| Tumbled stones, individual | $1–$3 per piece | Retail; polished, palm-stone size |
| Raw rough, tumbling grade | $10–$14 per pound | Unpolished; South African origin common |
| Wholesale bulk (5 lb bags) | Significantly below retail | Varies by supplier; per-unit cost drops sharply |
| Small specimens, clusters or points | $5–$20 | Varies by size and formation |
| Collector or display pieces | Price varies | Not surveyed in this research pass |

These figures represent the lower end of the crystal market. White Quartz is abundant globally — Brazil, South Africa, Madagascar, and the US all produce it — so supply consistently exceeds decorative demand, keeping prices low.

## What Drives the Price of White Quartz

**Abundance.** Quartz is among the most common minerals in the Earth's crust, and milky or white quartz is a particularly widespread variety. High supply keeps prices low compared with rarer stones.

**Form factor.** Tumbled stones are machine-polished in bulk and are cheap to produce. Carved pieces, shaped spheres or wands, and large statement specimens require more labor and carry higher prices. A carved White Quartz sphere will cost more than a simple tumbled piece of similar weight.

**Quality of finish.** Tumbled stones sold for their appearance may be graded informally by the uniformity and intensity of white colouration, the absence of surface pitting, and smoothness of polish. Premium-grade tumbled stock carries a modest markup over standard bulk material.

**Size.** Larger pieces cost more. A 100 g polished palm stone will typically cost more than three 30 g tumbled stones even at similar total weight, because of the additional labor required to shape it.

**Origin labeling and branding.** Stones labeled with specific origins (Brazilian, Madagascan) or sold by boutique retailers with strong branding may carry a higher price. In most cases, the physical properties of milky quartz do not differ significantly by origin, and origin premiums are mainly a marketing distinction.

**Retailer type.** Wholesale and bulk suppliers price significantly below specialty crystal boutiques, Etsy sellers, or gift shops that add margin for curation, packaging, and presentation.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

- **Buy by weight for tumbled stones.** Per-pound pricing gives better value and makes it straightforward to compare sellers.
- **Check for realistic pricing.** Extremely low prices can indicate glass or plastic imitations — see the [White Quartz identification guide](/gemstones/white-quartz/how-to-identify) for the physical tests.
- **Recognize that White Quartz has no meaningful rarity.** If a seller describes it as rare or limited, that is marketing language. Milky quartz is common worldwide.
- **Ask about treatments.** Genuine milky quartz does not require bleaching, dyeing, or coating. Ask sellers whether any treatment has been applied; most reputable sellers disclose this.

## Durability and Long-Term Value

At Mohs 7, White Quartz resists everyday scratching well. It will not dissolve, tarnish, or corrode with normal care. See [Mohs hardness scale](/learn/mohs-hardness-scale) for durability context. A well-kept piece retains its appearance indefinitely — the low initial price is accompanied by a long useful life.
