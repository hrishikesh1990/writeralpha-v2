# How to Cleanse Druzy Quartz Without Damaging It

Druzy Quartz can be cleansed safely with smoke, sound, moonlight, selenite, or a brief rinse under running water — provided the piece is untreated and thoroughly dried afterward. Treated specimens with dye, metallic coatings, or resin backing should avoid water entirely. Identify what you have before choosing a method.

## Know Your Piece Before You Cleanse

Druzy Quartz is quartz (SiO2, Mohs 7) but it almost always includes other materials: an agate or chalcedony matrix, and often surface treatments. Common treatments include dye applied to the matrix, metallic coatings (titanium, gold) deposited by vapor or electroplating, resin backing, or adhesive.

The quartz points themselves tolerate water. The treatments may not. If your piece displays electric blue, rainbow, iridescent gold, silver, or any unusually vivid color, it has almost certainly been coated. Water and soap can dull or lift these coatings over time. If the substrate is porous or the piece shows visible glue or resin, soaking introduces moisture that may weaken the bond.

## Methods Safe for All Druzy Quartz (Including Treated Pieces)

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the druzy piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds. Move the piece slowly so all exposed crystal surfaces pass through the smoke. This is the most widely available method and carries no physical risk to quartz, coatings, dye, resin, or matrix material. Ventilate the room; you are also breathing the smoke.

### Sound

Place the druzy piece near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing involves no physical contact with the stone beyond placing it nearby, so it is safe for all specimen types and all treatments. This method works well for fragile geodes that would be awkward to pass through smoke.

### Selenite slab or cluster

Set the druzy face-up on a dry selenite slab or next to a selenite cluster for a few hours or overnight. In crystal tradition, selenite is used to clear accumulated energy from nearby objects. No moisture is involved. Keep both stones away from humidity — selenite itself dissolves in water.

### Moonlight

Place the piece on a dry surface — a windowsill, a covered balcony, or a shelf near a window — on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours. Check the weather first. Overnight dew or rain would expose the piece to moisture, which may be problematic for treated specimens. Bring it indoors before morning condensation forms.

### Visualization and breath

Hold the piece in both hands, take a slow breath in, and exhale steadily across the surface while mentally focusing on clearing it. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials, poses no physical risk, and is the appropriate choice when other methods are unavailable.

## Water Cleansing — Untreated Pieces Only

For untreated natural druzy (earth tones: white, gray, cream, tan, brown, iron-stained orange), a brief physical wash is possible when the piece is genuinely dirty.

1. Fill a bowl with lukewarm distilled water and one drop of pH-neutral dish soap. Use distilled rather than tap water — tap water leaves calcium and mineral deposits in the crystal crevices as it evaporates.
2. Use a soft-bristled brush (a baby toothbrush works well). Move the bristles gently in one direction along the crystal points rather than scrubbing across them; back-and-forth motion catches between points and can snap tips.
3. Rinse briefly under slow-running lukewarm water.
4. Allow water to drain from the cavity by placing the piece face-down or tilted on a dry cloth for several minutes.
5. Use compressed air or a gentle puff of breath to push remaining moisture from between crystals.
6. Pat the matrix dry. Do not press cloth into the crystal bed.
7. Allow to air-dry completely in a warm, dry location before storing.

Do not soak druzy. Trapped moisture between crystals can leave mineral residue and, in specimens that contain iron-bearing minerals in the matrix, may promote surface oxidation.

## What to Avoid

- **Ultrasonic cleaners**: The vibration can dislodge crystal points from the matrix.
- **Steam cleaners**: Steam forces moisture between crystals and may loosen coatings.
- **Salt water or dry salt**: Salt is hygroscopic, drawing moisture toward the specimen. It can also dull coatings.
- **Harsh chemicals**: Bleach, vinegar, and commercial cleaners attack dye, metallic coatings, and resin.
- **Extended sunlight during energetic cleansing**: Druzy has no known fading risk under brief outdoor exposure, but prolonged direct sun on resin-backed pieces can soften adhesive over time.

## Recharging After Cleansing

In crystal tradition, cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps. After cleansing, practitioners place druzy in brief morning sunlight (15–30 minutes; quartz is not light-sensitive), next to a clear quartz cluster, or on a dry selenite slab. ## How Often to Cleanse

There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice. Common guidance in crystal tradition is to cleanse a piece when it first arrives, after periods of heavy use, and monthly during regular display. Inspect druzy periodically for accumulated dust or lint between the crystal points; a gentle brush (dry, for non-dirty pieces) keeps the surface sparkling.
