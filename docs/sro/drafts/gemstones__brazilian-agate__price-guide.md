# Brazilian Agate Price Guide

Brazilian Agate is one of the most affordable stones in the crystal market, owing to the enormous supply from Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil. Small tumbled pieces retail for roughly $2–$8 each; individual polished slices cost around $5–$20; display bookend pairs typically run $20–$140 depending on size and quality. Vivid "dyed" material does not reliably command higher prices than natural-color material — color intensity reflects treatment, not rarity.

## What Drives Brazilian Agate's Price

**Size.** Price scales with weight and dimensions more than any other factor for bulk agate. A small slice (3–4 cm) retails for a fraction of what a matched display pair of 15 cm nodules costs.

**Clarity and pattern quality.** Pieces with crisp, well-defined banding patterns, clean centers (no clouding or inclusions), and uniform polish command higher prices than irregular or heavily pitted specimens.

**Color.** Counterintuitively, dyed vivid colors are extremely common and do not indicate rarity or premium quality. Natural-color grey and white agate can sometimes fetch similar or higher prices from buyers who specifically want untreated material. Deep blue, red, and green dyed slices are abundant and typically priced at the lower end of the market.

**Form factor.** Matching bookend pairs — where a nodule is sliced and the two halves mirror each other — command a premium over random individual slices because they require selection and are sold as decorative sets.

**Finish quality.** High-polish pieces cost more than rough or semi-tumbled material. Lapidary finishing adds labor cost.

**Abundance of supply.** Brazilian Agate from the Salto do Jacuí region of Rio Grande do Sul is mined in very large quantities. This makes it one of the least scarce decorative stones on the market, keeping prices low across all grade levels.

## Retail Price Ranges (September 2026)

Prices below are drawn from retail sources checked in September 2026. Prices vary by seller, country, and grade.

| Form | Typical retail price (USD) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Small tumbled stones (~2–3 cm) | $2–$8 each | Most common form; sold singly or in sets |
| Small slices (3–4 cm) | $1–$5 each | Wholesale as low as $0.50–$0.90; retail mark-up applies |
| Medium slices (5–9 cm) | $5–$20 each | Popular for jewelry-making and display |
| Display bookend pair (small) | $20–$60 | Matched natural halves on a stand |
| Display bookend pair (large) | $60–$140+ | Specimens 15 cm+ with strong banding |
| Set of 10 slices (~3–6 cm) | $25–$40 | Common retail configuration |

These are retail reference ranges; wholesale pricing is lower, often $0.50–$3 per piece depending on size and quantity. Prices can be higher from premium retailers or lower from bulk importers.

## How to Avoid Overpaying

**Know what is common.** Dyed blue, red, and purple slices are extremely abundant at wholesale. A seller charging $30+ for a single small dyed slice of unremarkable quality is pricing above market. Compare across multiple sources before buying.

**Check the form factor.** A "matched bookend pair" label on two similar but unmatched pieces is a common inflation tactic. True bookend pairs are cut from the same nodule and mirror each other when opened — the banding on each half is a mirror image of the other.

**Understand dyeing.** A vivid color does not mean higher quality or rarer material. If you want untreated agate and are paying extra for it, ask the seller explicitly whether the piece has been dyed. Natural Brazilian Agate is grey-toned; vivid colors are almost always treated.

**Buy in sets for practice use.** If you want agate for crystal practice rather than display, sets of tumbled stones or small slices offer the best value. A set of 10 slices for around $25–$35 gives far more value than individual pieces at $10 each.

## Does Durability Affect Value?

Brazilian Agate's Mohs hardness of 6.5–7 makes it durable for jewelry and handling — harder than steel and scratch-resistant under normal use. This durability is why it has been a workhorse lapidary stone for over a century, and it contributes to its long-term value as a piece that does not need replacing.
