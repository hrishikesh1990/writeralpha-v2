# Moldavite and Citrine Together: Benefits & Care

[Moldavite](/gemstones/moldavite) and [citrine](/gemstones/citrine) are paired in crystal traditions focused on transformation and abundance. The combination is described as one of action and manifestation: moldavite is traditionally associated with initiating rapid change, while citrine is associated with optimism, willpower, and attracting prosperity — making this a pairing aimed at forward movement in practical life goals.

## Physical Compatibility: Hardness, Water, and Fading

**Hardness difference.** Moldavite is a natural silica glass (a tektite) with a Mohs hardness of 5 to 5.5. Citrine is a yellow-to-orange variety of quartz with a Mohs hardness of 7. That gap of 1.5 to 2 points means citrine can scratch moldavite if the two are stored loose together or knocked against each other. Keep each stone in its own cloth pouch or box. When wearing both as pendants, avoid settings that allow the stones to knock against one another.

**Moldavite and water.** Moldavite is glass and is not water-soluble. Brief rinsing is safe. Prolonged soaking and salt water are not recommended: salt can abrade the textured surface over time, and long immersion in mineral-rich water can etch rough moldavite specimens. Do not use salt bowls for cleansing moldavite.

**Citrine and water.** Citrine tolerates brief rinsing. Prolonged soaking or salt-water immersion is not recommended, as salt can dull the surface over extended use.

**Fading.** Fading is a genuine care concern for citrine and not for moldavite. Most citrine sold commercially is heat-treated amethyst — the yellow or orange color is produced by heating amethyst to around 470–750°C. This heat-treated citrine is sensitive to prolonged direct sunlight: sustained UV exposure can gradually shift the color back toward a pale or colorless tone. Natural citrine (far rarer) is also susceptible to some color change with extended intense light exposure. In practice: do not display citrine on a sunny windowsill or in direct outdoor light. Moldavite's green color is stable under normal conditions.

**Moldavite's fragility.** As a glass, moldavite has conchoidal fracture and is brittle. It chips and cracks on impact more easily than most gemstones. Pendant and earring settings are appropriate; ring settings are not advised unless the stone is fully protected by metalwork. For grid or shelf use, stationary placement is safest.

## Traditional Goals This Pairing Addresses

Moldavite is traditionally described as a catalyst for transformation — a stone that accelerates personal change, confronts practitioners with what needs to shift, and opens the heart and third-eye chakras. It is associated with the element of fire and with intense, rapid shifts in life circumstances.

Citrine is one of the most widely used stones for prosperity and abundance in crystal practice. It is associated with the solar plexus chakra and described as promoting optimism, confidence, creativity, and the attraction of opportunity. In many traditions it is called a "merchant's stone" — a stone carried to support business, financial intention, and motivated action.

The pairing is described as complementary for anyone working on a specific material or life-change goal: moldavite is said to create the opening and momentum for change; citrine is said to direct that energy toward tangible outcomes with confidence. Both are associated with fire energy in some traditions, which practitioners describe as amplifying their combined effect.

See the [moldavite combinations page](/gemstones/moldavite/combinations) and the [citrine combinations page](/gemstones/citrine/combinations) for other pairings for each stone.

## How People Use This Pair

**Carried together.** Practitioners carry both stones in a pocket or bag for periods when they are working on a specific goal — a job change, a creative project, or a business launch. Because the stones cannot be stored loose together (due to the hardness gap), a divided pouch or two separate small pouches are used.

**Prosperity altar or workspace.** Placing moldavite and citrine together on a desk or work surface is a common arrangement. Citrine is often placed on or near financial paperwork, contracts, or a business card; moldavite is placed nearby as a catalyst stone. Neither stone should be left in a window receiving direct sunlight.

**Crystal grid.** In [crystal grid](/learn/crystal-grids) work, citrine is typically placed at anchor points for abundance or manifestation intentions, with moldavite at the center to represent the transformation behind the goal. Quartz points are sometimes added to direct the energy.

**Meditation.** Holding or placing both stones during a visualization practice focused on a specific life goal is common. Practitioners describe moldavite as bringing urgency and clarity about what needs to change, and citrine as reinforcing the positive expectation needed to pursue it.

## What to Avoid

Do not store moldavite and citrine loose in the same container. The hardness gap guarantees surface damage to moldavite over time. Do not place citrine in direct sunlight for display — color loss is irreversible. Do not use salt water to cleanse either stone. Avoid placing moldavite in rings or other high-contact jewelry settings. Because both stones are associated in tradition with active, forward-moving energy, some practitioners note that this pairing is not the best choice for rest or sleep contexts — it may be better suited to daytime, goal-focused use.

## Alternatives to Consider

For a prosperity-focused combination without the hardness incompatibility, **pyrite** (Mohs 6 to 6.5) is traditionally associated with abundance and resilience. It is closer to moldavite in hardness, though still harder; the same storage rules apply. **Green aventurine** (Mohs 7) shares citrine's association with luck and opportunity and the same hardness issue with moldavite. If the goal is a gentler transformation-and-abundance pairing that reduces the fragility concern, **green jade** or **malachite** are used in prosperity traditions — though malachite requires its own care (it should not go in water or be used in powdered form).
