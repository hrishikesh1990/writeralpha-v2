# Who Should Be Cautious About Wearing Black Obsidian

Two types of caution apply to wearing black obsidian: physical and traditional. Physically, the stone's brittleness and sharp fracture make it unsuitable for rings and bracelets worn during active use. In some crystal and astrological traditions, practitioners suggest caution for people going through acute emotional distress or grief without support, though these are matters of personal practice rather than established fact.

## Physical Cautions: Durability and Safety

Black obsidian is a volcanic glass with a Mohs hardness of 5 to 5.5 and no cleavage. It fractures conchoidally, producing very sharp curved edges — the same property that made it prized for ancient blades. These physical characteristics create specific wear risks:

**Rings and bracelets**: Gem reference sources note that obsidian's low toughness makes it inappropriate for high-impact jewelry such as rings and bracelets. A knock against a hard surface can chip or shatter a piece. Anyone who works with their hands — in trades, cooking, or sports — faces a realistic risk of damaging both the stone and their skin if it breaks while being worn.

**Sharp edges on raw or broken pieces**: Raw obsidian and any chipped polished piece can have razor-sharp edges. Handle raw specimens carefully; do not let children handle broken or rough pieces unsupervised.

**Children**: Tumbled obsidian pieces smaller than a golf ball are a choking hazard for young children and should be kept out of reach. Sharp-edged raw or chipped pieces are an additional injury risk.

**Dust**: Cutting, grinding, or shaping obsidian produces silica-bearing dust. This is an occupational concern for lapidaries, not a risk from wearing finished jewelry. Lapidaries should use wet cutting methods and appropriate respiratory protection.

## Traditional and Metaphysical Cautions

Crystal traditions are not uniform on this point, and nothing below is a statement of medical or psychological fact. These are views held in various crystal-practice communities:

**Intensity of the stone's associations**: In many crystal traditions, black obsidian is associated with shadow work — the process of examining difficult or unconscious patterns. Practitioners in these traditions sometimes suggest that people in acute grief, severe anxiety, or emotional crisis approach black obsidian cautiously and ideally alongside other grounding support, rather than as a standalone tool. This is a matter of individual sensitivity and belief, not a documented effect.

**Beginners to crystal practice**: Some practitioners recommend starting with gentler stones before working with black obsidian, on the basis that its traditional associations are considered intense. This is a preference within the practice, not a health claim.

**Astrological cautions**: In some Vedic astrology traditions, black obsidian is associated with Shani (Saturn) or Rahu, and some practitioners advise consultation with an astrologer before extended wear. Western crystal astrology associations vary and are not standardized. These are traditional frameworks, not predictive systems.

Crystals are a complementary practice. They are not a treatment for anxiety, grief, or any other health condition. If you are experiencing mental health concerns, please speak with a qualified healthcare professional. ## Who Wears Black Obsidian Without Issue

The majority of people who choose to wear black obsidian do so without concern. The physical cautions above are primarily relevant to setting, activity level, and form: pendants, earrings, and brooches in protected settings are far less likely to chip than rings and bracelets. Tumbled stones carried in a pocket or bag are generally low-risk.
