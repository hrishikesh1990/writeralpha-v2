# Can Blue Onyx Go in Water?

Blue Onyx can tolerate brief contact with clean water. It is chalcedony — microcrystalline quartz (SiO₂) — with a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7, which means water does not dissolve or significantly erode it. The important qualification is that most commercial Blue Onyx is dyed, and prolonged soaking can fade or bleed that dye. A short rinse is fine; a long soak is not.

## Why Blue Onyx Is Physically Water-Tolerant

Chalcedony is a variety of quartz (SiO₂), one of the most chemically stable minerals found at the Earth's surface. Silicon dioxide does not react with water at normal temperatures or dissolve in it. The stone's hardness of 6.5–7 also means that water at normal pressure does not scratch or pit the surface. These two facts together mean that water contact per se is not harmful to the mineral.

This distinguishes Blue Onyx from genuinely water-unsafe stones such as selenite (which dissolves), pyrite (which oxidizes and corrodes), or malachite (toxic dust risk if abraded when wet). Blue Onyx carries none of those concerns.

For a comparison of many stones and their water safety, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## The Dye Issue: Why Prolonged Soaking Is Risky

Almost all commercially sold Blue Onyx has been dyed to achieve or deepen the blue color. Natural blue chalcedony exists — the color arises from the Tyndall effect, in which light scatters from fine colloid-sized particles in the stone — but it is rarer and more expensive. The vibrant, uniform blue of most retail pieces comes from added pigment.

Dyes can leach, fade, or bleed when the stone is soaked in water for extended periods, especially warm water, soapy water, or salt water. The rate depends on the dye used and the quality of the finishing, neither of which is typically disclosed by sellers. To avoid color damage:

- Keep water contact to under a minute for cleaning purposes
- Use cool, plain water only — no soap, no salt
- Dry the stone immediately and thoroughly with a soft cloth
- Do not leave it in a water bowl overnight

## Salt Water: Avoid

Salt water is more corrosive than plain water for dyed surfaces. The salt is mildly abrasive and can act as a vehicle for moisture penetrating any micro-cracks in the polish where dye sits. Salt water baths are not recommended for Blue Onyx. For guidance on which stones tolerate salt, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## Sunlight After Washing

If you dry Blue Onyx with a brief time in the sun after a water rinse, keep the exposure short — under 30 minutes. Prolonged sunlight fades the dye in most treated specimens over time with repeated exposure. For guidance on sun-safe charging, see [sun-safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals).

## Physical Cleaning vs. Metaphysical Cleansing

Two different uses bring Blue Onyx into contact with water:

**Physical cleaning** — removing dust, skin oils, or residue from a worn bracelet or pendant. A quick wipe with a damp cloth, or a brief rinse under cool running water followed by immediate drying, is safe and effective.

**Metaphysical cleansing** — resetting the stone's energy in crystal practice. Water is one method practitioners use, but it is not the only one and not the most recommended for dyed stones. Smoke, sound, moonlight, and selenite carry no risk to the surface. The full range of options is covered in [how to cleanse Blue Onyx](/gemstones/blue-onyx/how-to-cleanse).
