# How to Cleanse Cat's Eye Safely

Cat's Eye chrysoberyl is one of the more forgiving stones to cleanse. With a Mohs hardness of 8.5 and no sensitivity to water or mild acids, it tolerates most physical cleaning methods without damage. All standard ritual cleansing methods — smoke, sound, moonlight, sunlight, water, and dry earth — are physically safe for this stone.

## Physical Safety Baseline

Understanding the stone's physical properties lets you cleanse confidently without worrying about damage.

Cat's Eye (cymophane) is a variety of chrysoberyl, formula BeAl2O4. It rates 8.5 on the Mohs hardness scale, making it harder than nearly all other materials it is likely to contact. It is not water-soluble, is not affected by common acids, and does not contain metals that corrode or oxidize on exposure to moisture. Brief exposure to household cleaning agents is unlikely to cause permanent damage, though harsh chemicals are unnecessary and best avoided.

The stone does have one distinct cleavage direction, so avoid dropping it on hard surfaces or applying sharp impact. The chatoyant inclusions that create the cat's eye effect are internal and not affected by surface cleaning.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Water rinse

Hold Cat's Eye under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 to 30 seconds. Gently rub the surface with your fingers or a soft cloth. Rinse and dry thoroughly with a soft, lint-free cloth. This is the simplest physical clean and removes dust, skin oils, and any surface residue without risk to the stone.

Avoid boiling water or sudden temperature extremes, which can stress any gemstone through thermal shock.

### Mild soap and water

Add a small amount of mild dish soap to a bowl of lukewarm water. Place Cat's Eye in the solution for one to two minutes, then clean with a soft brush (a soft toothbrush works well), rinse thoroughly under running water, and dry. GemSelect and AGTA both confirm this method as appropriate for chrysoberyl. This is the standard recommendation from gem dealers and jewelers for routine maintenance.

Do not use bleach, ammonia-based cleaners, or abrasive materials.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold Cat's Eye in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, moving the piece slowly to expose all surfaces. This is a widely used ritual cleansing method and poses no physical risk to the stone. Ensure the room is ventilated.

### Sound

Place Cat's Eye near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing requires no physical contact with liquids or any other substance, making it a good option for jewelry pieces where the setting or metal band may warrant care.

### Moonlight

Set Cat's Eye on a dry surface on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours or overnight. This is one of the most common methods in crystal practice for a combined reset and recharge. Cat's Eye has no light-sensitivity that would cause fading, so moonlight or mild sunlight exposure poses no physical risk.

### Sunlight

Place Cat's Eye in morning sunlight for 15 to 30 minutes. Unlike some colored stones (amethyst can fade in prolonged UV exposure; some treated stones are UV-sensitive), chrysoberyl cat's eye does not fade with brief sun exposure. Do not leave colored gemstone cabochons in intense midday sun for extended periods as a general precaution.

### Dry earth burial

Bury Cat's Eye in a small pot of dry indoor soil or sand for 24 hours. Retrieve, brush off any residue with a dry cloth, and rinse if desired. This is used in crystal tradition as a grounding method. Use indoor potting soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps in crystal practice. After cleansing, Cat's Eye can be placed in brief sunlight, near a cluster of clear quartz, or on a dry selenite slab. In Vedic tradition, the stone is sometimes held in cupped hands while setting a specific intention aligned with its astrological purpose (Ketu-related themes of clarity and protection).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal traditions vary widely. A common practice is to cleanse Cat's Eye when it arrives, after any period of concentrated use, and once a month during regular display or wear. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is a matter of personal practice. For physical maintenance, wipe the stone with a soft dry cloth after each wearing to remove skin oils, which can dull the surface over time.

## Storage After Cleansing

Cat's Eye is hard enough to scratch most other minerals and gemstones. Store it separately from softer stones in a cloth pouch or a compartmentalized jewelry box to avoid surface scratching on both sides. A cool, dry, dust-free location is ideal. No special humidity control is required, as the stone is not hygroscopic and does not react with moisture.
