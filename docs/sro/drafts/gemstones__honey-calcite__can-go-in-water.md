# Can Honey Calcite Go in Water? No — Here's Why

Honey Calcite should not go in water. It is calcium carbonate (CaCO3) with a Mohs hardness of 3 — soft enough to be scratched by a copper coin — and it reacts with acids, including the mild acids present in salt water and carbonated tap water. Prolonged water contact etches the polished surface, and any acid dissolves the stone. Use a dry cloth for physical cleaning instead.

## The Chemistry Behind the Damage

Calcite — the mineral that makes up Honey Calcite — is calcium carbonate (CaCO3). This is the same material as limestone. It reacts with acids in a well-documented reaction: acid + calcite produces calcium salt, water, and carbon dioxide gas. The fizzing you see when a geologist drops dilute hydrochloric acid on a rock in the field is this exact reaction. Even a mild acid such as vinegar (acetic acid) or the carbonic acid that forms when tap water absorbs atmospheric CO2 will etch a calcite surface given enough contact time.

Plain water without acid does not trigger the carbonation reaction, but calcite has slight water solubility over time, and at Mohs 3 the polished surface is soft enough that water molecules at cleave planes (honey calcite has perfect cleavage in three directions) will gradually degrade the finish. Repeated soaking — even in clean water — leads to progressive surface dullness.

For a comparison with other stones and which are genuinely water-safe, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## Salt Water Is Worse Than Plain Water

Salt water is more damaging than plain water for two reasons. First, dissolved salt ions can accelerate the degradation of calcite surfaces. Second, if any of that salt dries on the specimen, it becomes a mildly acidic residue that continues attacking the surface as it draws ambient moisture. Dry salt burial — sometimes used as a cleansing method for harder stones — is also not recommended for Honey Calcite, because salt is hygroscopic and will pull moisture toward the stone's surface.

For a guide to which stones are safe around salt, see [salt-safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals).

## What Happens If Honey Calcite Gets Wet Briefly

A brief, accidental splash of plain water is unlikely to cause visible damage. If Honey Calcite gets wet, dry it immediately with a soft lint-free cloth, pat gently rather than rubbing (rubbing a wet Mohs-3 surface can cause fine scratches), and allow it to air-dry fully before storing in a closed case. Do not use paper towel or rough fabric.

The risk is cumulative: a single splash is low risk; repeated short soaks over weeks will visibly dull the surface.

## How to Clean Honey Calcite Physically

For dust and fingerprints, wipe with a soft dry cloth. For light dirt, use a cloth barely dampened with plain water and wipe once, then dry immediately. Never soak, submerge, or run under a tap. Do not use any cleaning product — household cleaners, dish soap, and vinegar-based cleaners are acidic and will cause immediate damage. Avoid ultrasonic cleaners, which create cavitation (micro-bubbles that implode against the surface) and are too aggressive for a soft stone with perfect cleavage.
