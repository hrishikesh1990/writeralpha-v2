# Can Cherry Quartz Go in Water?

Yes — Cherry Quartz is safe to rinse with cool or lukewarm water. It is man-made glass (silica-based), chemically stable, and does not corrode, dissolve, or shed material in normal water contact. Brief immersion for cleaning is fine; the precautions that apply are about temperature and prolonged exposure, not water reactivity.

## Why Cherry Quartz Is Water Safe

Cherry Quartz is not a natural mineral. It is produced by melting silica (SiO₂) — the same base material as natural quartz — together with a red coloring agent (cinnabar in some production, synthetic dyes in most commercial pieces today) and cooling the melt into a non-crystalline glass. The resulting material has an approximate Mohs hardness of 7 and a conchoidal fracture pattern, like all glass.

Glass does not react with water the way chemically reactive minerals do. Minerals that are unsafe in water fail for specific physical reasons: iron-bearing stones like pyrite oxidize and corrode; salt minerals (halite) dissolve; fibrous minerals like some serpentines can release hazardous particles. Cherry Quartz has none of these vulnerabilities. Its silica-glass matrix is stable at ambient temperatures and ordinary pH levels.

For a full reference covering which crystal types are and are not water safe, see [what crystals can go in water](/learn/what-crystals-can-go-in-water).

## What to Do and What to Avoid

**Safe:** Cool or lukewarm running water, 20 to 30 seconds; mild dish soap if the piece is dirty; patting dry with a soft cloth.

**Avoid:** Boiling water or steam — sudden, intense heat causes thermal shock in glass and can crack it. Ultrasonic cleaning devices — the vibration intensity can fracture glass with internal inclusions. Harsh chemical cleaners — these can etch and dull the surface over time.

**Salt water:** Cherry Quartz glass will not corrode in salt water, so a short salt-water rinse will not damage it. However, salt itself offers no benefit beyond a plain water rinse for cleaning glass, and residue can dull the finish if not rinsed thoroughly. See [salt safe crystals](/learn/salt-safe-crystals) for the broader category context.

## Is Cinnabar in Cherry Quartz a Water Safety Concern?

Some Cherry Quartz production uses cinnabar (mercury sulfide) as the coloring agent. Mercury is toxic, but in finished Cherry Quartz the cinnabar is fused within the glass matrix and does not leach into water under normal conditions. Multiple published sources on this material confirm that casual handling, wearing, and rinsing of finished pieces does not pose a mercury exposure risk. Most budget-priced commercial pieces do not contain cinnabar at all — they use synthetic red dyes, making mercury content moot.

If you are concerned about the specific composition of a piece you own, contact the supplier. For routine cleaning, water rinsing of finished Cherry Quartz is safe regardless of which coloring method was used.

## How to Clean Cherry Quartz at Home

1. Hold the piece under cool running water for 20 to 30 seconds.
2. If there is visible grime, apply a small amount of mild liquid soap and gently rub with your fingers or a soft cloth.
3. Rinse thoroughly under running water until no soap remains.
4. Pat dry with a lint-free cloth.
5. Allow to air-dry fully before storing in an enclosed container.

For sun exposure after washing: Cherry Quartz is sun-stable. Brief drying in morning sunlight — 15 to 30 minutes — will not cause fading. For extended sun exposure guidance, see [sun safe crystals](/learn/sun-safe-crystals).
