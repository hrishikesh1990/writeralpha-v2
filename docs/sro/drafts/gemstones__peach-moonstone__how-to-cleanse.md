# How to Cleanse Peach Moonstone Without Damaging It

Peach Moonstone can be briefly rinsed under cool, clean water for physical cleaning — it is not soluble and not corroded by plain water. What damages it is prolonged soaking, which can infiltrate hairline cracks and cleavage planes, and ultrasonic cleaning or steam, which create vibrations and heat that chip or split the stone. For energetic or ritual cleansing, moonlight, smoke, and sound are the safest choices.

## Why Water Needs to Be Used Carefully

Peach Moonstone is orthoclase feldspar with a Mohs hardness of 6–6.5 and two directions of perfect cleavage. The cleavage planes are the key issue: microscopic cracks along these planes can admit water, and repeated soaking causes those cracks to widen over time. The stone is not chemically reactive with plain water the way pyrite or malachite is, but the physical structure makes extended immersion unwise.

A brief rinse under cool running water — ten to thirty seconds to remove surface dust or oil — carries little practical risk for a solid, uncracked specimen. A salt-water soak, however, carries double risk: salt is mildly abrasive against a 6–6.5 hardness stone, and salt solutions can deposit crystals in surface pores as the water evaporates, gradually expanding micro-fractures.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Moonlight

Set peach moonstone on a dry windowsill or outdoors on a clean, dry surface on a clear night and leave it under moonlight for several hours. This is traditionally the most appropriate method for [peach moonstone](/gemstones/peach-moonstone) given its association with lunar energy in crystal tradition — practitioners treat a full moon exposure as both cleansing and recharging in a single step. Check the weather forecast: dew or rain will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it inside before sunrise to avoid morning condensation forming on the surface.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the stone in the smoke of burning herbs or incense for 30 to 60 seconds, moving it slowly so all surfaces pass through the smoke. Ensure the room is ventilated. Smoke does not touch the stone's surface in any way that risks cleavage or scratching, making this one of the most reliably safe methods for fragile gems.

### Sound

Place peach moonstone near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing requires no physical contact with a liquid or abrasive surface. It is useful for multiple pieces at once and for delicate or cracked specimens that should not be handled frequently.

### Dry earth burial

Bury the stone in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or sand for 24 hours. Retrieve it, brush off soil with a dry soft cloth. Use indoor potting soil rather than garden soil, which may be damp. This method is used in crystal tradition to symbolically ground and clear the stone.

### Breath and visualization

Hold peach moonstone in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale steadily across the surface while focusing on releasing accumulated energy. Two or three slow breaths is a common practice. This method is used when other methods are unavailable or for a quick reset.

## Methods to Avoid

- **Ultrasonic cleaners:** The vibrations propagate along cleavage planes and can split the stone.
- **Steam cleaning:** Heat and pressure expand micro-fractures; feldspar gems are specifically excluded from steam cleaning in gem trade practice.
- **Salt-water soaks:** Salt deposits in surface pores and gradually widens fine cracks.
- **Harsh chemical cleaners or bleach:** Not appropriate for any polished gemstone.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Crystal tradition treats cleansing and recharging as two separate steps. After cleansing, common recharging options for peach moonstone include extended moonlight exposure (a full night rather than a few hours), placing it on a dry selenite slab, or setting it near a clear-quartz cluster. Brief morning sunlight — 15 to 30 minutes — is unlikely to cause fading in an opaque peach-toned feldspar, but extended direct sun exposure is avoided by cautious practitioners.

For a full guide to recharging practices, see [how to charge crystals](/learn/how-to-charge-crystals).

## How Often to Cleanse

Crystal tradition offers no single answer. A common guideline is to cleanse when the stone arrives, after any sustained or intense use, and monthly during regular display. There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule — this is personal practice. As a care note, inspect peach moonstone periodically for new surface chips or widening cracks; those indicate the stone needs careful storage away from impact, not more frequent cleansing.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store peach moonstone wrapped in a soft cloth or in a lined box, separate from harder stones such as quartz (Mohs 7) or topaz (Mohs 8) that will scratch it. Keep it away from high-humidity locations such as bathroom windowsills. Handle it by the sides rather than the top dome to avoid pressing a finger ring or watch against the surface.
