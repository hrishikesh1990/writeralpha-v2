# Who Should Be Cautious About Wearing Larimar

There is no population for whom Larimar poses a chemical or toxicological risk in normal wear: the mineral is non-toxic in its solid form and safe against skin. The real cautions are physical — its Mohs hardness of 4.5–5 makes it prone to scratching — and traditional, covering practitioners who believe certain temperaments or astrological placements call for a different stone. This page covers each category in turn.

## Physical Cautions: Hardness and Durability

Larimar is a blue variety of pectolite (NaCa₂Si₃O₈(OH)) with a Mohs hardness of 4.5–5, placing it on the softer end of gem materials used in jewelry. Quartz — found in common dust and many harder stones — scratches anything below Mohs 7. This means Larimar will develop surface scratches over time in any setting with high abrasion exposure.

**Who this affects most:**

- People who work with their hands — gardening, construction, cooking, or any activity involving impact, grit, or friction — should remove Larimar rings and bracelets before the work begins. Pendants and earrings are better protected and endure daily wear more easily.
- People who store jewelry in shared compartments where Larimar sits against harder stones (quartz, topaz, corundum, diamond) risk surface damage.
- Children under roughly eight years of age should not have unsupervised access to small Larimar beads or cabochons, which are a choking hazard.

Larimar also has perfect cleavage in two directions, meaning a sharp blow at the wrong angle can split the stone along a plane even without scratching it. Remove Larimar before any sport or impact-heavy activity.

Prolonged exposure to direct sunlight or sustained heat is also a practical concern: laboratory heating tests have shown that Larimar's blue color can be reduced by extended heating, and UV sensitivity means color fading is possible with very long-term sun exposure. This is relevant for people who habitually leave jewelry in car dashboards, on sunny windowsills, or under strong display lamps. For most normal wear this risk is low, but it is worth noting for people who wear the same piece constantly outdoors.

## No Toxicological Risk in Normal Wear

Larimar is not a soluble mineral, does not release copper or sodium in quantities harmful to skin, and produces no irritants in normal handling. There is no medical or chemical basis for recommending that any age group, health condition, or circumstance avoid wearing Larimar for safety reasons. Crystal dust is a general mineral hazard to avoid inhaling; do not grind or cut Larimar without respiratory protection, but this applies to all mineral dust and is not specific to Larimar.

## Traditional Practice: Temperament Fit

Crystal practitioners sometimes suggest that certain personal temperaments or circumstances may not be the best match for Larimar's traditionally calm, water-like energy. These are matters of personal tradition, not physical facts.

Larimar is traditionally associated with calm, emotional release, and receptivity — qualities linked to its throat, heart, and crown chakra associations in crystal practice. Some practitioners suggest that people who are already very low in energy, motivation, or groundedness may find a stone with more activating associations (such as carnelian or citrine) more useful in a given period. This is framed not as a prohibition but as a question of fit: if Larimar makes a person feel more disengaged than at ease, setting it aside or pairing it with a grounding stone is the conventional recommendation.

This is the extent of traditional temperament caution. Crystal practice does not hold that Larimar is harmful to any personality type; it simply recognizes that different stones are traditionally matched to different needs. For a full account of what Larimar is traditionally used for, see [Larimar meaning](/gemstones/larimar/meaning).

## Astrological Tradition

In crystal healing tradition — not in mainstream Vedic or Western astronomy — Larimar is most commonly paired with water signs (Pisces, Cancer, Scorpio) and air signs (Aquarius, Libra), where its associations with calm communication and emotional balance are considered a complementary fit. These associations are traditional and not supported by scientific evidence.

No zodiac sign is conventionally warned away from Larimar in the crystal trade literature. The tradition is inclusive: practitioners who write about zodiac and Larimar typically note that anyone may work with it but that certain signs may find a particularly strong resonance.

If you consult a Vedic astrologer about gemstone selection, be aware that classical Vedic jyotish assigns planetary gemstones based on birth chart analysis (navratnas system) and that Larimar is not one of the traditional nine planetary gems. Any specific planetary caution would come from an individual chart reading, not from Larimar's properties alone.

## Pregnancy and Nursing: No Physical Risk

There is no evidence that wearing Larimar poses any physical risk during pregnancy or nursing. Crystals are a complementary practice, not a medical treatment, and should not replace prenatal care. For night-time use considerations, see [sleeping with Larimar](/gemstones/larimar/sleeping-with).
