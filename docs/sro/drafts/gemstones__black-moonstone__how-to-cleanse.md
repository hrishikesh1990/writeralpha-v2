# How to Cleanse Black Moonstone Without Damaging It

Black Moonstone is a feldspar at Mohs 6 to 6.5 and tolerates brief water rinsing without damage. Prolonged soaking, salt soaks, and ultrasonic or steam cleaners are the methods to skip. Safe cleansing options include a quick rinse with mild soap, smoke, moonlight, sound, and dry earth burial — the same methods used across most feldspar gemstones.

## Physical Cleaning: Soap and Water

For removing surface dirt, the standard approach for Black Moonstone is lukewarm water with a drop of mild liquid soap and a soft cloth or soft-bristled brush. Work gently, rinse thoroughly, and pat dry immediately. Do not leave the stone submerged or soaking; prolonged water contact can work into existing micro-cracks along the feldspar cleavage planes and weaken them over time.

**What to avoid:**

- **Salt soaks:** Salt is abrasive at the surface level and, when dissolved, can leave deposits in surface cracks. Avoid salt water baths for any piece with chips or fine inclusions. For jewelry with metal components, salt solution can accelerate corrosion of silver or base-metal settings.
- **Ultrasonic cleaners:** The vibration can propagate along feldspar cleavage and cause internal fractures, particularly in stones that already have small inclusions or tension cracks.
- **Steam cleaners:** The rapid temperature change and high moisture is unsuitable for feldspar jewelry. Reserve steam cleaning for harder, tougher stones.
- **Acidic or chemical cleaners:** Not needed and can etch the polish on softer feldspar surfaces.

## Smoke Cleansing

Hold Black Moonstone in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, moving the piece slowly so all surfaces are exposed. This is the most commonly used method in crystal practice for stones of any hardness and carries no physical risk to the specimen. Ensure good ventilation; you are breathing the smoke too.

Smoke cleansing is traditional — practitioners use it to clear accumulated energy before setting a new intention. There is no physical mechanism by which smoke alters a stone's mineral properties.

## Moonlight

Set Black Moonstone on a dry windowsill or a dry surface outdoors on a clear night and leave it under moonlight overnight. Black Moonstone's strong traditional association with lunar energy makes moonlight a particularly popular cleansing and recharging method in crystal practice.

Check the weather before leaving the stone outside: dew, rain, or condensation are the physical risks. Bring the stone indoors before morning condensation forms. A dry surface indoors near a window that receives moonlight is the safest arrangement.

## Sound

Place Black Moonstone near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and let the vibration wash over it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing works at a distance, so the stone does not need to be touched or moved through any liquid. This method works well for fragile or highly included pieces that you prefer not to handle too much.

## Dry Earth Burial

Bury Black Moonstone in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or fine sand for 24 hours, then retrieve and brush clean with a soft dry cloth. Use dry indoor soil rather than garden soil, which can be damp. This grounds the stone physically in the earth it formed from — a method traditional practitioners use for a deep reset.

## Recharging After Cleansing

Cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps in most crystal traditions. After cleansing, place Black Moonstone under moonlight (the most common choice, given its associations), on a dry selenite slab, or near a clear quartz cluster overnight. Brief morning sunlight — 15 to 30 minutes — is sometimes used, though prolonged sun exposure is not necessary and some practitioners prefer to keep lunar-associated stones out of strong direct sun. ## How Often to Cleanse

No physical maintenance schedule applies to Black Moonstone beyond periodic inspection for chips or surface scratches. In crystal tradition, cleansing is typically done on arrival, after any period of heavy use, monthly during the full or new moon, or whenever the stone is felt to need it. This is a matter of personal practice rather than mineralogical necessity.

Inspect the stone periodically for edge chips or small cracks opening up. If the surface polish dulls noticeably, professional re-polishing by a lapidary restores it; a home re-polish attempt risks further damage on a 6-6.5 Mohs stone.
