# How to Cleanse Black Onyx Without Damaging It

Black onyx is physically durable — Mohs hardness 6.5–7, no cleavage, non-toxic — but most commercial specimens are dyed, and prolonged water exposure or salt water can lift or streak the dye and dull the polish. A quick wipe with a damp cloth is safe; extended soaking and salt baths are not. Dry methods (smoke, sound, moonlight, breath) carry no physical risk at all.

## What Makes Black Onyx Sensitive to Some Methods

Black onyx is a variety of chalcedony (SiO2), and virtually all of the uniformly black material sold in the crystal and jewelry trade has been dyed — either by the traditional sugar-and-acid carbonizing technique or with cobalt or other dyes. The dyeing is accepted practice and widely disclosed, but it means the stone's surface is not inherently black and can be affected by:

- **Prolonged water immersion** — extended soaking can cause dye migration or surface dulling over time
- **Salt water** — salt is more corrosive than plain water and can accelerate dye breakdown and pitting on polished surfaces
- **Dry salt burial** — salt is hygroscopic, drawing moisture to the surface; best avoided on dyed material

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Quick damp-cloth wipe

Dampen a soft cloth with plain tap water, wipe the surface of the black onyx piece, and dry immediately with a second dry cloth. This removes surface dust, skin oils, and residue without prolonged contact. Do not soak, submerge, or use soap with the stone regularly; mild soap on a soft brush is acceptable for occasional cleaning of jewelry settings, followed by a thorough rinse and immediate drying.

This is purely a physical cleaning step, not a metaphysical cleanse. For energetic cleansing, use one of the dry methods below.

### Smoke cleansing

Hold the black onyx piece in the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, turning it so all surfaces pass through the smoke. This is among the most widely used energetic cleansing methods in crystal practice and carries no physical risk to the stone. Ensure the space is ventilated.

### Sound

Place black onyx near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. The stone does not need to be touched or moved, making this method ideal for display pieces or multiple stones at once.

### Moonlight

Set black onyx on a dry surface — a windowsill or outdoor table — on a clear night and leave it for several hours or overnight. Check the forecast first: dew, rain, or high overnight humidity will expose the stone to moisture. Bring it inside before morning condensation forms. In crystal tradition, moonlight is used for both energetic cleansing and recharging in a single step.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the stone in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale steadily across its surface while directing your focus toward releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials and is used when other methods are unavailable or for a quick reset between uses.

### Dry earth or potting soil

Bury black onyx in a small pot of dry indoor potting soil or sand for 24 hours. Brush off loose soil with a dry cloth when you retrieve it. Use dry indoor soil rather than garden soil, which may hold moisture. In traditional practice, earth burial is associated with grounding and resetting a stone's energy — qualities linked to [crystals for grounding](/crystals/crystals-for-grounding) and black onyx's own traditional associations with steadiness and protection.

Cleansing and recharging are treated as separate steps in most crystal traditions. After cleansing black onyx, common recharging options include placing it in brief morning sunlight (an opaque stone with no light-sensitive dye concerns for short exposures), near a clear quartz cluster, or on a dry selenite slab. ## How Often to Cleanse

There is no physical basis for a cleansing schedule; this is a matter of personal practice. Common guidelines in crystal tradition suggest cleansing when the stone first arrives, after any period of intense use, and monthly during regular display. If black onyx is worn daily in jewelry, more frequent cleansing is often recommended. As a maintenance note, inspect the stone periodically: any streaking, patchiness, or dulling of the finish may indicate dye wear rather than anything energetic, and a new piece may be worth sourcing if the treatment has degraded significantly.

## Storage After Cleansing

Store black onyx away from prolonged direct sunlight (extended exposure can fade dyes over time), and keep it separate from harder stones — anything above Mohs 7 can scratch the surface. A fabric-lined box or soft pouch is sufficient. Avoid humid storage areas such as bathrooms.
