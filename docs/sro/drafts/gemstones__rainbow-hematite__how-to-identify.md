# How to Identify Real Rainbow Hematite

Natural Rainbow Hematite has a red-brown streak, is non-magnetic or only weakly magnetic, and displays iridescent color only on its surface — not through the stone. A significant proportion of the material sold commercially as "rainbow hematite" is titanium-coated synthetic hematite, which looks similar but behaves differently under testing.

## What Real Rainbow Hematite Is

[Rainbow Hematite](/gemstones/rainbow-hematite) is a variety of hematite — iron oxide (Fe2O3) — whose iridescent colors are produced by an ultra-thin natural surface coating of aluminum phosphate nanocrystals, typically 200 to 500 nanometers thick (Rossman & Ma, 2025). This coating acts like a diffraction grating, splitting white light into shifting bands of blue, green, purple, gold, and red depending on the viewing angle.

The colors exist only at the surface. The underlying material is ordinary hematite: metallic grey, heavy, with a diagnostic red-brown streak.

For context on what the stone is traditionally associated with, see [Rainbow Hematite meaning](/gemstones/rainbow-hematite/meaning). For information on whether a price signals genuine material, see the [price guide](/gemstones/rainbow-hematite/price-guide).

## The Most Important Distinction: Natural vs. Titanium-Coated

The single most commercially significant identification issue with Rainbow Hematite is the difference between natural iridescence and artificial titanium coating. Much of the bulk "rainbow hematite" sold in crystal shops and online is titanium-coated hematite — hematite that has been treated with a thin titanium oxide layer to produce brilliant iridescence.

| Feature | Natural Rainbow Hematite | Titanium-coated hematite |
|---|---|---|
| Source of color | Natural aluminum phosphate nano-film | Applied titanium oxide coating |
| Color character | Colors shift subtly; often include earthy tones | Often very vivid, intense, uniform; may include electric blue/purple |
| Magnetism | Not strongly magnetic | Often strongly magnetic (magnetic hematite is the typical base) |
| Streak | Red-brown | Red-brown (same base mineral) |
| Price | Slightly higher per piece for natural specimens | Often cheaper in bulk |
| Legitimacy | Genuine natural material | Not fake, but treated/enhanced — should be disclosed |

Titanium-coated hematite is not a fraud in itself, but it should be labeled accurately. Some sellers describe it as "natural" when it is not. A listing that says "non-magnetic rainbow hematite" is more likely to be natural; a listing offering bulk bags of strongly magnetic material at very low prices is almost certainly titanium-coated.

## Identification Tests

### Streak test

Draw the stone across the back of an unglazed ceramic tile (a streak plate). Genuine hematite — whether natural rainbow or titanium-coated — will leave a red-brown streak. This is one of hematite's most reliable diagnostic properties. Glass and most imitations leave a white or no streak; some do not scratch the tile at all if they are softer than the ceramic.

### Magnetism test

Hold a small magnet next to the stone. Natural Rainbow Hematite is not strongly magnetic. Hematite is anti-ferromagnetic (essentially non-magnetic) under normal conditions. If the stone is strongly attracted to a magnet, it is almost certainly titanium-coated magnetic hematite, not natural Rainbow Hematite. This is the fastest field test.

### Weight

Hematite is dense — approximately 5.26 g/cm3 (Mindat). Pick up the stone: it should feel noticeably heavy for its size, significantly heavier than glass, resin, or most quartz. Very light pieces that look like Rainbow Hematite are likely glass, plastic, or resin imitations.

### Surface and color behavior

Tilt the stone under a light and observe the iridescent colors. In natural Rainbow Hematite, the colors shift with angle but tend toward subtler, more complex tones — purples, greens, and golds. In titanium-coated material, the colors are typically more vivid and uniform. In glass or resin imitations, the "rainbow" effect is often a static coating visible as a flat layer rather than a shifting surface film.

Check the edges and any chips: a glass imitation will look glassy at a chip; hematite will look metallic grey.

### Hardness test

Rainbow Hematite has a Mohs hardness of 5 to 6. It will scratch glass (Mohs ~5.5) and will not be scratched by a copper coin (Mohs ~3). Use a steel nail (Mohs ~6.5) with care: some hematite specimens at the lower end of the range may show a faint scratch from steel, while harder specimens will not.

## Buying Guidance

When purchasing Rainbow Hematite online, look for sellers who clearly state whether the material is natural iridescent hematite or titanium-coated, and who describe it as non-magnetic if it is natural.
