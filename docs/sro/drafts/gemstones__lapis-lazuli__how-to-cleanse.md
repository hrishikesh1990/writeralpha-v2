# How to Cleanse Lapis Lazuli Without Damaging It

Lapis lazuli must not go in water. The stone is a porous composite rock: its calcite component dissolves slowly on water contact, its pyrite inclusions oxidize and produce irreversible orange rust staining, and many pieces are dyed — which runs when wet. Safe cleansing methods are smoke, sound, moonlight, selenite, and breath or visualization. Each is explained below.

## Why Water Damages Lapis Lazuli

Lapis lazuli is an aggregate of several minerals — primarily lazurite, calcite, and pyrite — and each reacts to moisture in a way that causes damage.

Calcite dissolves slowly in water and more rapidly in acidic liquids. Prolonged or repeated water contact etches the calcite areas of lapis, producing a dull or chalky surface. Acids — vinegar, lemon juice, or acidic jewelry cleaners — cause faster etching and should never be used.

Pyrite (iron disulfide, FeS2) oxidizes when it comes into contact with water and oxygen. The reaction produces iron sulfate minerals and can create orange-brown rust staining on the surrounding blue lazurite surface. This staining is permanent and cannot be removed without abrading the stone itself. Even brief water contact can initiate this process.

Lapis is also porous. Much commercial lapis is dyed to deepen weak color or disguise pale calcite, and the dye runs in water. Wax and oil coatings common on polished pieces are also stripped by water contact.

Salt water is more corrosive than plain water and should not be used. Dry salt burial — used for some harder stones — is also inadvisable for lapis lazuli because salt is hygroscopic and draws ambient moisture toward the specimen.

## Safe Cleansing Methods

### Smoke cleansing

Pass lapis lazuli slowly through the smoke of burning herbs, incense, or a candle for 30 to 60 seconds, ensuring all surfaces are exposed. Smoke cleansing is the most widely used method for water-sensitive stones in crystal practice and poses no physical risk to the specimen. Ventilate the space — you are breathing the smoke too.

### Sound

Place lapis lazuli near a singing bowl, bell, or tuning fork and allow the vibration to pass through it for one to two minutes. Sound cleansing requires no physical contact with the stone beyond placing it nearby, making it particularly suited to dyed, waxed, or fragile pieces that should not be handled repeatedly. The stone does not need to rest inside or touch the bowl.

### Moonlight

Set lapis lazuli on a dry indoor surface — a windowsill with good moon exposure works well — on a clear night and leave it for several hours or overnight. Outdoor placement risks dew forming on the stone as temperatures drop; keep it indoors. Bring it in before morning condensation forms. Crystal tradition uses moonlight for a full overnight energetic reset.

### Selenite plate

Place lapis lazuli on a dry selenite slab or plate and leave it for several hours or overnight. Selenite is widely used in crystal practice as a passive cleanser requiring no preparation or supervision. Ensure the selenite surface is dry — selenite itself dissolves slowly in water and should not be damp.

### Breath and visualization

Hold the lapis lazuli in both hands, inhale slowly, and exhale steadily across the stone's surface while focusing mentally on releasing accumulated energy. Repeat two or three times. This method requires no materials, leaves no physical residue, and is appropriate when other methods are unavailable.

## What to Avoid

| Method | Safe for lapis lazuli? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Water soak | No | Calcite etching, pyrite oxidation, dye runs |
| Salt water | No | More corrosive than plain water |
| Dry salt burial | Avoid | Salt draws moisture to the stone surface |
| Steam or ultrasonic cleaner | No | Moisture and vibration damage the composite structure |
| Vinegar or acidic cleaners | No | Attacks calcite; etches surface permanently |
| Prolonged direct sunlight | Avoid | UV can fade lazurite's blue color over time |

Brief morning sunlight — up to 30 minutes — is considered low risk in crystal practice for an opaque stone.

## Physical Cleaning

For surface dust or fingerprints, use a soft dry microfiber or cotton cloth only. If more cleaning is needed, test a very slightly damp cloth on a hidden area first and stop immediately if any blue color transfers. Never use chemical cleaners, ultrasonic machines, or steam.
